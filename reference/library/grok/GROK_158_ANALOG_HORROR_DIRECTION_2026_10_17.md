# ANALOG HORROR AND CLAYMATION DIRECTION

## PAOLO SAID IN THIS CHAT

Paolo said he needs research on the top 10 analog horror games of all time. He needs a metric ton of research on Mike the art style of that one creepy HBO child show that was like claymation animatronic shit. He loves that. He wants an awesome file to help the master coordinator direct the art coordinator. He said Analog horror.

## MY PROPOSAL

This is a direction sheet for the art coordinator. It is not a mechanic lock. Analog horror is already the tone for every pixel and every sound. This adds the games and the claymation reference so the cooks have something to look at.

The creepy HBO claymation show is Moral Orel. It is stop-motion claymation. It aired on Adult Swim and is on HBO Max. It looks like a children's religious show and then it is not. The bodies are simple clay. The faces are stiff. The movement is a little wrong. The sets are small and handmade. That is the look he named. Don't Hug Me I'm Scared is the puppet cousin. It starts like Sesame Street and then the objects come alive and it gets gore and existential. It mixes puppets, claymation segments, and live action. Both are the vibe. Not a filter. The grime is baked in.

### TOP 10 ANALOG HORROR GAMES

These are the ones that keep coming up. They use low fidelity, VHS grain, old interfaces, or chunky retro art so the fear lives in what you cannot quite see.

1. Iron Lung. You are sealed in a rusted sub at the bottom of a blood ocean. No windows. You navigate by instruments and a delayed camera. The fear is sound and the photos you take. PS1 style. Markiplier played it. https://gamerant.com/best-analog-horror-games/

2. Faith: The Unholy Trinity. 8-bit pixels. A priest with a crucifix. Satanic Panic 1980s. Rotoscoped cutscenes. The graphics are so chunky they become the horror. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith:_The_Unholy_Trinity

3. Home Safety Hotline. You work a 1990s call center. Callers describe things in their houses. You look them up on a green CRT. The monsters are uncanny and the job is the dread. https://gamerant.com/best-analog-horror-games/

4. Mouthwashing. Low poly PS1 look. A crashed space freighter. Body horror, workplace rot, and a crew that is already broken. The faces are just off. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouthwashing_(video_game)

5. Signalis. Pixel survival horror. Android in a totalitarian future. Limited inventory, save rooms, melancholy. Retro sci-fi dread.

6. The Complex: Found Footage and other Backrooms games. Liminal empty rooms. VHS. You walk and something is wrong with the space.

7. Assessment Examination. In the Mandela Catalogue world. You take a test. Reality is not stable.

8. Fears to Fathom series. Short found-footage episodes. Real-feeling. Ironbark Lookout is one.

9. Puppet Combo games. Murder House, Nun Massacre. VHS slasher. Chunky, mean, handmade.

10. Paratopic and Who is Lila? and Stories Untold. Disturbed VHS, unanswered questions, old tech interfaces. Amanda the Adventurer is the puppet one if the list needs a children's show that turns.

### WHAT TO STEAL FOR BOHEMIA

Steal the restraint. Low fidelity so the player's head fills the gaps. Grain that is baked, not a filter slapped on. Interfaces that look like old machines. Faces that are simple and a little wrong. Movement that is not smooth. Bodies that look handmade, clay or puppet or chip. Sets that feel small and real. Horror that starts as a normal job or a normal street and then does not.

Do not steal a specific character or a named series he has not named. Moral Orel and Don't Hug Me I'm Scared are the style references he asked for. The games are the proof that this look works in a game. The art coordinator can cook faces, tiles, and loading screens against this sheet. The main character is still downed, never dead. Square house tiles stay. Nothing glows. Analog horror is the light and the stillness, decided at the source.
