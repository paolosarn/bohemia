# ASK 30. THE MAP IN COLOUR

## PAOLO SAID IN THIS CHAT
The map still looks like shit. I don't want a grey yellow sand color to flood all of our art.

## MY PROPOSAL
Battle Brothers makes its world map readable at a glance by using strong contrast between terrain types, clear outlines on roads and coasts, and landmarks that stay large at every zoom. Snow in the north is pale. Desert in the south is warmer. Forests are dark green. Mountains are grey with white edges. Settlements have banners and attached location icons that remain readable when the player zooms out. The party marker is a small character that stays the same size while the map scales. Roads are lighter than the ground they cross. Fog of war is a separate layer that does not wash out the colors underneath.

The real Las Vegas valley from above is not one beige color. Red rock and basalt appear on the western edge. Lawns and golf courses are bright green against the desert. Pools and Lake Mead are blue. Concrete and asphalt are cool grey. Rust and abandoned metal are orange-brown. Neon on the Strip is pink, cyan, and yellow at night. From the street the same places show cracked sidewalks, faded paint, and pool water that is not clean. Photos of the valley from satellite and from the ground show these colors side by side. A map that uses only grey-yellow sand loses the green, the blue, the rust, and the neon. The player cannot tell a road from a yard or a settlement from open desert.

Bohemia's map uses the same contrast method. Roads are lighter than the dirt they cross. Settlements keep their banners and attached icons large enough to read at the far zoom. The party marker stays a fixed size. Green appears where lawns and golf courses exist. Blue appears at pools and the lake. Rust and concrete appear on the edges of blocks. Neon appears only on the Strip at night and only as small lights, never as a glow that covers the ground. The far stop still shows the whole valley. The near stop shows individual house roofs and sidewalks. Nothing is flooded with one sand color.

Sources: Battle Brothers developer blog on worldmap rework and village screens; satellite and street-level descriptions of the Las Vegas valley; Paolo's direct statement on the map color.
