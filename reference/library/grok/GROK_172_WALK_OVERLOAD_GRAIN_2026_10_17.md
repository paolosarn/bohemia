# WALK OVERLOAD GRAIN

## PAOLO SAID IN THIS CHAT

Paolo said more. He wants more analog horror and assets so the sprites and animations look way better. Claude makes a really bad graphic artist.

## MY PROPOSAL

Walk cycle. The 4-frame minimum is contact, passing, contact, passing. Frame 1 is contact left. Frame 2 is down or passing. Frame 3 is the mirror of frame 1. Frame 4 is the mirror of frame 2. Body dips 1 or 2 pixels on the down pose. Arms opposite the legs. Hold each frame 100 to 150 ms. Feet stay planted. No ice skating. Preview at the real walk speed. Variable holds sell weight. No tween. Stiffness is allowed. The Crashbox feel holds the poses.

Crashbox machine. The power goes out every episode in Season 1. An unnamed flashlight robot fixes it. He plugs wires, hits a button with a hammer, bangs his head on the panel, pulls a lever, or drops a thermometer. He complains. Oh no not again. I thought I fixed this. The cafeteria and maintenance sketches show the robots eating and working. The place is a junk factory that is always almost broken. Donuts appear. Wires spark. The machine is a workplace.

Grain and analog. Bake the grain into the pixels. Light random dither or intentional noise on rust, clay ridges, and junk. Do not add a VHS filter later. Scanlines and tracking are optional and rare. Keep contrast low. Dirty greens, dull rust, sick clay. Nothing glows. Light is flat or one hard source. The steps stay. The stiffness stays. The parts that do not fit stay.

### COOK

One 4-frame walk with contact and passing poses and variable holds. One flashlight robot that fixes wires or hits a panel. One cafeteria tile with a green clay cartridge on a rusty table. One surface with baked intentional grain. Lock these against the earlier sheets. The art coordinator can cook them. The main character is still downed, never dead. Square house tiles stay. Nothing glows. Analog horror is the light the stillness and the parts that do not fit, decided at the source.
