# ASK 107: The Anti Zach D Films

## PAOLO SAID IN THIS CHAT:

AI videos like Zach D Films, but instead of safe fun facts, political things to get people thinking. Break down Zach D. Films format exactly. Then a plan for the opposite: short AI-made videos about money, power and how economies really break.

## MY PROPOSAL:

Zach D Films is a locked format of short vertical videos with a calm deadpan narrator, a new visual change roughly once per second, a mid-story hook that creates an immediate question, and almost no hard cuts. Videos run 25 to 40 seconds from 60 to 80 word scripts. The voice is patient and matter-of-fact. Posting is frequent enough to build a channel of 28 million subscribers since 2021. Tools that can make this today include Starpop for the exact format skill, Taletok for the Zackie voice, Atlabs or Synthesia for script-to-video, and OpusClip for turning longer pieces into shorts.

### Ten First Video Ideas with First-Second Hooks

1. The dollar dies when everyone already owns it. Hook: A man pays for bread with a stack of paper that used to buy a house.
2. Debt is just a promise that someone else works for you. Hook: A worker hands his paycheck to a bank that already owns his house.
3. Bubbles pop when the last buyer runs out of money. Hook: A room of people buying the same house from each other until the door locks.
4. The 2008 crash started with loans that could never be paid. Hook: A family signs papers for a house the bank already knew they could not afford.
5. Circular vendor financing is the supplier lending you the money to buy his own product. Hook: Lucent hands a check to a phone company that spends it the same day on Lucent gear.
6. Nortel and Lucent collapsed when the customers they financed went bankrupt. Hook: A telecom giant watches its own loans vanish as the network it built sits empty.
7. Power moves when the people who print the money also decide who gets it. Hook: A quiet room where three people decide which cities keep the lights on.
8. Batteries become the new crowns when the old money stops working. Hook: A clerk refuses paper and only takes a charged pack.
9. Clout is just the number of people who still answer when you call. Hook: A mayor watches the phone list shrink one name at a time.
10. An economic apocalypse starts the day the shelves stay empty after the trucks stop. Hook: A store light is still on but every shelf is bare.

Each video stays under 40 seconds, uses the once-per-second visual rule, and ends on a question that makes the next one necessary. The sources for the format are the Starpop and Taletok breakdowns plus the YouTube analysis of the one-second rule. The sources for the 2008 and Lucent material are the BIT circular financing page and the 2000 Street article on vendor loans.
