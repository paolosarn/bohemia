## MY PROPOSAL

This sheet is the practical rules the art coordinator and Claude can follow so the sprites stop looking bad. Lock the palette first. Soft & Dirty or Dry Sea or a custom set of rust dull metal sick green clay. 16 to 32 colors max. Value does the work. Squint and the shape still reads. Shadows shift cooler. Highlights shift warmer. Grain is baked into the pixels not a filter. Nothing glows. Light is flat or one hard source.

### ANIMATION RULES

Animate on twos. 12 fps base. Hold each pose. 3 or 4 frames for a walk. 4 to 6 for most actions. Hold the key poses longer than the in-betweens. No tween. Draw the key poses by hand. Copy the previous frame and move only the parts that change. Use onion skin. Preview every frame at the real speed. Smear frames are allowed for fast hits but the hold is the Crashbox feel. Sub-pixel is not needed. Stiffness is the point. Bodies are assembled. Heads sit off. Mouths can be wrong. Parts can fall and rebuild. The stiffness is the analog.

### ASSET RULES

Author at target resolution in Aseprite. Point sampling. Integer scaling. Export with 1 pixel padding. Tags for each animation. Linked cels for parts that do not move. Cook order stays cartridge then robot then person then tiles then UI. First cook set remains the dented green clay cartridge, the rusty mismatched robot, the clay face with wrong mouth, the 4-frame walk, the junk street tile, the scrap metal loader that falls apart. Add the cafeteria tile and the maintenance arm that reattaches if the set grows. The main character is still downed, never dead. Square house tiles stay. Nothing glows. Analog horror is the light the stillness and the parts that do not fit, decided at the source.
