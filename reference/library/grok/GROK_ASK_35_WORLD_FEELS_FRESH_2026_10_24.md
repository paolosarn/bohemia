# ASK 35. A WORLD THAT FEELS MADE FRESH

## PAOLO SAID IN THIS CHAT
How does Battle Brothers generate its world and choose the start (settlement placement, roads, factions, names)? What makes players FEEL a world is new at the first look? Cite each.

## MY PROPOSAL
The Battle Brothers wiki page on the Global Map states that the world map is randomly generated at the beginning of each game. Every world is filled with settlements aligned to one of three noble factions. A typical setup involves a total of 17 settlements. Settlement distribution among the noble houses can range from roughly equal to lopsided. The developer blog on the worldmap states that the map covers the scope of a single continent with geography and climate similar to a middle-European country. It is procedurally generated at the start of every new game. Terrain is painted according to rules. Mountainous terrain often appears in a connected line forming a ridge. A village is usually surrounded by farmland. Villages are placed near fertile land and rivers. Bigger towns, guardposts, and keeps are placed at positions of strategic importance. Roads connect these places and often snake around dark forests or lead through them. Fog of war covers unexplored areas except around the party and major settlements.

The developer blog on the strategic worldmap states that different factions exist with their own agenda and resources. Factions own locations and send out parties from them with objectives such as patrolling, hunting, or raiding. The simulation runs even without the player. A village sends a caravan along the road. A nearby bandit camp can ambush it. The village may then issue a contract. The wiki does not say the exact algorithm that chooses settlement names or the precise rules that decide the player's starting settlement. Player and modder descriptions note that one side of the map is usually dedicated to wilderness where late-game threats spawn, and that roads connect settlements so that travel is faster on them.

Design writing on procedural worlds states that a world feels new at first look when the layout changes the paths the player can take, when landmarks and barriers force different routes, and when the first view shows a mix of open land and obstacles that the player has not memorized. Controlled variation keeps the world coherent while still different. Pure randomness without rules feels empty. The sources do not give a single visual feature that always works. In Bohemia the map generates with the same rules. Settlements sit near resources and roads. Factions own places and send parties. The first view shows a different arrangement of the valley each time. The player sees roads, barriers, and settlements whose placement changes the first path.

Sources: Battle Brothers wiki Global Map page; developer blogs 7, 52, and the strategic worldmap post; player and modder descriptions of seed variation; design writing on what makes a procedural world feel fresh.
