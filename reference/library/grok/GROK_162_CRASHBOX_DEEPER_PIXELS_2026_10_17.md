# CRASHBOX DEEPER PIXEL DIRECTION

## PAOLO SAID IN THIS CHAT

Paolo said more research. He said help with our direction and like pixels and how to make shit look like fire, sprites, and shit for the whole enchilada. He said he has been saddened by the art direction that the chat has been doing on Claude. He said Analog horror. He said Crashbox.

## MY PROPOSAL

Cuppa Coffee Studios built Crashbox in about seven to eight months for the first block. They do not do clean CGI. They use clay, puppets, physical models, cutouts, and mixed media. The computer is low-tech on purpose: wheels, string, train tracks, scrap metal, rubber bands. Green cartridges are sculpted clay. Rusty tin robots load them. The whole factory looks assembled from junk. That is the source. The pixels have to feel the same way.

### PIXEL RULES FROM THE SOURCE

The clay is visible. Even at 16 or 32 pixels a green cartridge should read as soft clay, not a hard plastic block. Use two or three greens. One base, one darker for the dents, one lighter for the fingerprint ridges. Do not smooth the edges. Leave the steps. The robots are tin and rust. Use dull metal gray and three rust colors. The rust sits on the edges and the joints. A highlight is a scratch, not a clean shine. Nothing glows.

Bodies are built, not drawn. A person sprite is a head that sits a little high or a little off, a torso that is one color, arms that are a different tone or a different shape. The Revolting Slob was a puppet that looked wrong. Captain Bones was a skeleton that fell apart and rebuilt. Do the same in pixels. A hire can have a clay face made of simple shapes. A robot can have one arm that is clearly a different part. A drone can look taped or wired.

Movement is stop-motion. Three or four frames for a walk. Hold each pose. Do not tween. The stiffness is the analog. It matches the chip bodies. A turn is a snap, not a spin. An idle is a slight shift that holds.

Tiles are junk. A street tile is cracked asphalt with one piece of scrap or a faded line. A wall is boards and rust with one green clay cartridge stuck in it. A building face is not clean. It has seams and patches. The tactical map stays house-sized tiles. The art on them looks handmade inside a machine that is already falling apart.

UI is the same material. A menu plate is a green clay cartridge shape with rusty edges. A button is a robot part. Text sits a little off. Grain is in the pixels, not a filter on top. Light is flat or from one hard source. No soft glows. No clean anti-alias on the important edges.

### COOK ORDER

One green clay cartridge sprite. Soft, dented, fingerprint ridges. One rusty tin robot that loads it. Mismatched parts, dull metal, rust on the joints. One person sprite with a clay face and a stop-motion walk. One street tile and one wall tile that look junk-built. One UI plate that is the cartridge shape. The art coordinator locks these. Everything else grows from them.

This is the harder handmade version. The Claude chat has been soft. Crashbox was not soft. The main character is still downed, never dead. Square house tiles stay. Nothing glows. Analog horror is the light and the stillness, decided at the source.
