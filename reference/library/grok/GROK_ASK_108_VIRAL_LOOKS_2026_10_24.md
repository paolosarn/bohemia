# ASK 108: How Bohemia Can Look Viral

## PAOLO SAID IN THIS CHAT:

I definitely need Grok to research how our game can look really good to people if they were to view it on social media. I am trying to make it go viral. Right now I do not think it is looking too good.

## MY PROPOSAL:

Indie games go viral on TikTok, Reels, Shorts and X when a 15 to 30 second clip explains the game with no context through physics fails, chaotic co-op, or a single unexpected moment. Content Warning sold one million paid copies in about two weeks after a free day flooded the platforms. Sledding Game sold over 100,000 copies in five days from one developer's clips of a penguin crashing. Megabonk hit high concurrent players from months of self-deprecating TikToks. A scroller stops in the first 0.5 to 1 second for motion, high contrast, a face or a number that should not be that high, or a pattern interrupt like mid-action with no setup. A LITTLE THING FROM Content Warning. A LITTLE THING FROM Sledding Game.

### What Bohemia Must Change to Stop a Scroll

The first frame must show a clear action or contrast: a chip-body arm rearranging mid-fall, a car-shot blast at house-tile range, or a bare shelf under a still-lit shop sign. Vertical framing keeps the person door-high and the tiles readable. High contrast between dirty analog-horror textures and one bright element such as a charged battery or a neon remnant avoids the grey-yellow flood. Faces must perform with the squiggle voice so the mouth moves when someone talks. Text overlays stay short and readable at phone size. Sound hits on the beat of the 120 BPM track. Clips of a stomp that ends in four minutes or a single unexpected free attack work better than long chess-puzzle views. Sources for the examples are the Everything PR case studies and the Wardrome shortform discovery piece. Sources for the stop-the-scroll rules are the Trapplan and Eklipse guides on the first second.
