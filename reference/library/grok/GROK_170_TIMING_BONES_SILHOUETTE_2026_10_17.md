# TIMING BONES SILHOUETTE

## PAOLO SAID IN THIS CHAT

Paolo said more. He wants more analog horror and assets so the sprites and animations look way better. Claude makes a really bad graphic artist.

## MY PROPOSAL

Timing rules. Frame duration is the secret. Most frames 80 to 120 ms. Holds 200 ms or longer. Anticipation and impact hold longer. Walk can be 4 frames at 100 to 150 ms. Attack holds the impact frame. Idle holds the base pose. Preview at 12 fps. No tween. Variable hold times make 4 frames feel weighted. Export the durations so the code keeps them.

Captain Bones. He is a pirate skeleton on a shipwreck. He collapses his bones and rebuilds them into math problems or pictures. He is the most fluid stop-motion character in the show. He falls apart and puts himself back together. He is cursed to sail forever even after his body is gone. The body is parts. That is the translation for chip bodies. A downed hire can fall into parts and rebuild. The stiffness and the reassembly are the analog.

Silhouette rules. Fill the sprite with one flat color. If the shape does not read, fix the shape. Break symmetry. Leave gaps so limbs do not fuse. One signature detail that survives at the real scale. 32 px for small items. 64 px for people. 128 px for bosses or portraits. Squint test before shading. Value does the work. Limited palette. Grain baked in. Nothing glows.

### COOK

One Captain Bones style body that falls into parts and rebuilds. One 4-frame walk with variable holds. One silhouette-tested clay face. Lock these against the earlier sheets. The art coordinator can cook them. The main character is still downed, never dead. Square house tiles stay. Nothing glows. Analog horror is the light the stillness and the parts that do not fit, decided at the source.
