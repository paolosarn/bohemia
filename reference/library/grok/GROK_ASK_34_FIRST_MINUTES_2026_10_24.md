# ASK 34. THE FIRST MINUTES THAT MAKE A PLAYER CARE

## PAOLO SAID IN THIS CHAT
What do studies and the best games say makes a player care about a new playthrough in the first five minutes? Give sources. How does Battle Brothers start each origin, and how do the best run-based and sandbox games open (cutscene, text, straight in)? Rank what works.

## MY PROPOSAL
Studies and design writing on the first five minutes say that players decide whether a game is worth their time before they understand every system. One analysis of early session data found that games which prompt interaction in the first minute retain more players than games that delay it. Another design note states that the first five minutes should prove the experience is worth the player's limited time and that early emotional experiences shape long-term memory through the primacy effect. A third source lists three rules that hold in successful openings: make the player feel competent rather than a beginner, make the early game feel abundant with small rewards, and keep the experience clean so confusion does not cause churn. The sources do not name a single number of seconds that works for every genre.

Battle Brothers starts most origins with a short flavor text that names the company and its situation, then drops the player onto the world map with a small starting party and some resources. The wiki page on Origins states that each origin comes with a flavor introduction, different starting characters, equipment, resources, and special rules. Rebuilding a Company is recommended for new players and includes some guidance. A New Company starts with three experienced mercenaries and no particular advantages. Lone Wolf starts with one strong fighter. Other origins change the starting gear, the starting location, or the rules that last the whole campaign. The official tutorial video and player guides show that the player is on the map within the first minute and can begin hiring or taking contracts. The wiki does not say the exact length of any opening text or cutscene.

Run-based and sandbox games that keep players usually open straight into play. A short text or a single image can set the tone, but the player is moving or making a choice within the first minute. Long unskippable cutscenes and multi-screen tutorials that delay the first meaningful action lose more players. Straight into the world with a clear next action ranks highest. A short flavor text followed by immediate play ranks second. A long cutscene ranks last for this type of game.

In Bohemia the first five minutes put the player on the map with one clear person to talk to and one place to walk. The opening text is two sentences. The player is moving before the minute ends.

Sources: Battle Brothers wiki page Origins; official tutorial video on starting a campaign; player guides on early game; design analyses on first-five-minute retention and the primacy effect.
