# CRASHBOX DIRECTION

## PAOLO SAID IN THIS CHAT

Paolo said Crashbox. He said the creepy HBO child show was like claymation animatronic shit. He loves that. He wants an awesome file to help the master coordinator direct the art coordinator. He said Analog horror.

## MY PROPOSAL

The show is Crashbox. It ran on HBO Family from 1999 to 2000. It is stop-motion claymation mixed with puppets, cutouts, and other styles. The framing device is the inside of a game computer. Green cartridges are sculpted out of clay. Rusty tin robots load them. The computer looks low-tech and handmade: rubber bands, conveyor belts, gears, and junk. Cuppa Coffee Studios animated it. People remember it as off-the-wall and a little unsettling even though it was an educational game show. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crashbox

### THE LOOK TO STEAL

Handmade and tactile. Clay that still looks like clay. Faces that are simple and a little wrong. Movement that is stop-motion, not smooth. Bodies that feel sculpted or assembled. Sets that look built from real junk. The whole thing lives inside a machine that is broken and organic at the same time. Segments change style but the grime and the oddness stay. Captain Bones is a skeleton pirate made of bones that move. The Revolting Slob is a puppet who explodes. Haunted House Party uses cardboard cutouts and lightning. Nothing is clean. Nothing glows.

### FOR BOHEMIA

This is the art reference for the analog horror direction. The chip bodies, the robots, the drones, the streets, and the loading screens can all borrow the handmade clay and junk look. The grain is baked in, not a filter. Faces are stiff and a little off. Movement is not smooth. The world looks like it was built by hand inside a machine that is already falling apart. That matches the economic apocalypse and the chip-in-the-body undead. The art coordinator can cook against this sheet. The main character is still downed, never dead. Square house tiles stay. Nothing glows. Analog horror is the light and the stillness, decided at the source.
