WHAT WAS ACTUALLY DONE
----------------------
Not 228 images redrawn. 236 of 237 canon garments and ALL 36 hairstyles are
GENERATED, by 13 functions totalling 1,213 lines. Hair is generated too -- every
`layer:'hair'` entry calls genHair -- so both halves of what he asked for are
code, not art.

Every pixel DISTANCE in those 13 functions now reads a scalar derived from the
grid it was handed:

    function rsc(){ var s=(CW/56)|0; return s<1?1:s; }

and every generator opens `var S=rsc()`. Part ids, ramp keys and colour indices
are NOT distances and do not scale -- that is the whole classification, and there
were 752 integer literals to sort. Sorting them by eye would have been a guess, so
the method was measure-fix-measure, one generator at a time, until the pixels
agreed.

THE THREE MISTAKES THE MEASUREMENT CAUGHT, WHICH READING WOULD NOT HAVE
------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. AN INCLUSIVE BOUND IS OFF BY S-1. `yy>=tb-4` is FIVE rows, not four. Scaling
   the 4 gives nine rows at 112 where the waist needs ten, and the garment
   silently creeps. Same bug in eight places (the shoe shaft, the hat line, the
   skirt cut, the hair's brow ceiling).
2. A FRACTION OF A SPAN IS A CELL COUNT. Every generator places features as
   anchor + round(span * fraction) -- the knee at 45% of the leg, the eye line at
   35% of the face. At 112 the same span is 2n+1 pixels rather than 2n, so the
   rounding lands on a DIFFERENT cell. The dust mask started two rows below his
   mouth. Fixed once, in a shared helper, across all 23 sites:
       function fr(a,b,f){ var S=rsc(); return Math.round(((b-a+1)/S-1)*f)*S; }
3. A MAX EDGE IS THE BOTTOM OF ITS CELL, NOT THE TOP. tMx and tb are the last
   pixel of a cell, so "one column in from the hip" starts S-1 earlier. Getting
   this backwards put the satchel inside its own rim.

None of the three is visible in the source. All three are obvious in the numbers.
