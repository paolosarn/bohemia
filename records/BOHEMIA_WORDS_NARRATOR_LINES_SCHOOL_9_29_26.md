## FINDING 1: REAL AUTOMATED ANNOUNCEMENTS SELF-IDENTIFY AND NEVER CONTRACT
The US Emergency Alert System's standard script: "This is a National Emergency Message.
All broadcast stations and cable systems shall transmit this National Emergency Message."
Transit and building PA scripts follow the same shape: "This is an emergency," never
"this's an emergency." Three real features, all directly usable: (1) THE MACHINE NAMES
WHAT IT IS DOING before it does it ("this is a message," "this is an alert") -- a person
never announces their own speech act, a machine always does; (2) ZERO CONTRACTIONS,
zero exceptions, in every real script found; (3) REPETITION OF THE FRAME, not the
content -- the identifying sentence repeats verbatim across otherwise different messages,
which is a real, testable ruler for a future gate leg (a narrator line that varies its
self-identification is wrong).

## FINDING 2: THE WRONGNESS IS A MISMATCH, NOT A DECORATION -- WORDS' JOB IS TO STAY BORING
Research on synthetic-voice horror converges on ONE mechanism: unease comes from an
EXPECTANCY VIOLATION, a mismatch between what is said and how flatly it is delivered --
not from scary word choice. This is the load-bearing finding for this round: THE WORDS
SHOULD NOT TRY TO BE CREEPY. A narrator line that reaches for dread ("something is
wrong...") is doing SOUNDS' and DIRECTION's job with worse tools and duplicating effort
that belongs to the audio treatment (too even, tape, room). The line's job is to be
CORRECT AND PROCEDURAL; the one wrong DETAIL is still rule 20h's job (a fact that is
true and off, stated flat, never flagged), not a wrong TONE. This also matches BB'S OWN
tooltip register, found in [bb words] this same round: a bare, unstyled fact reads as
"machine" for free; reaching for style is what breaks it.
