# HOW A SENTENCE SOUNDS — the research the questbook does not contain
# (8/27/26, WORDS lane, on his order: "do big brain online research if you need to")

## WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
`questbook/` is 244 files, 152 studied quests and 3,672 citable findings, and
every one of them is about **WHAT HAPPENS** — hooks, reversals, choices,
consequence. The WORDS brief named the hole in it exactly: *"Nothing in 3,672
findings tells you why one line lands and the next one dies."* This is the
missing layer, researched on 8/27 and written down so it is citable like
anything else.

## FINDING 1 — A REFUSAL IS NEVER JUST "NO", AND THE PROOF IS IN MILLISECONDS
This is the most useful thing found, because it is measurable and we fail it.

Conversation analysts have timed this. A **preferred** response (yes, agreement,
acceptance) lands at a median of about **269 ms**. A **dispreferred** one (no,
refusal, disagreement) lands at about **561 ms** — more than twice the gap. The
delay is so reliable that listeners read a pause itself as the beginning of a
no, before a single word arrives.

And the refusal does not arrive alone. It comes in a package, in this order:
1. a **delay** — a breath, a click, a beat of nothing
2. a **preface or hedge** — "well", "look", "it's just", "the thing is"
3. an **appreciation** — acknowledging what was asked
4. an **account** — the reason, usually before the refusal itself
5. often a **trail-off** that implies the no WITHOUT EVER SAYING IT

**THE RULE FOR US:** when somebody in Bohemia says no, they never lead with it.
When somebody says yes, they answer instantly. That difference is free
characterisation and it costs one word.

## FINDING 2 — CLASS AND REGION LIVE IN SYNTAX, NEVER IN SPELLING
Eye dialect (respelling words to show an accent) makes the reader work harder
for nothing and carries a long history of making the speaker look stupid. It is
already banned for Spanish (THEY SPEAK SPANGLISH, 8/25). The research says the
ban should be total, and gives the positive technique:
- **grammar patterns**: "I seen him", "I been waiting", "I says to him",
  double negatives — non-standard, not wrong
- **dropped helping verbs and articles**
- **regional idiom and proverb**, the phrasing only somebody from here uses
- **rhythm and syntax**, which is where a voice actually lives
Never a respelled word. Not one.

## FINDING 3 — CONTRACTIONS ARE THE BASELINE MARKER OF SPOKEN LANGUAGE
Every practitioner source says the same thing in the same breath: contractions
are what makes written dialogue sound spoken. Our quest scenes were measured at
**2.2%** against our own street barks at **75%**. This is not a stylistic
preference, it is the single loudest tell we have.

## FINDING 4 — SUBTEXT IS THE CRAFT, AND WORD CHOICE CARRIES IT
"The most important thing in dialogue is the thing that is not said." The
technique is specific rather than mystical: a character picks the word that
serves them. Somebody says "indentured citizen" so nobody has to say "slave".
Our fixer says "I don't steal it, I move it" for the same reason. The polite
word for the ugly thing, offered as a dare.

## FINDING 5 — VARY THE SENTENCE, AND GIVE THE BODY SOMETHING TO DO
Mix short and long deliberately. And **action beats**: a line lands harder when
a body does something in it. For us that means a line can point at a prop, and
Avellone's rule from the brief still governs — the best line might be somebody
refusing to answer, and the second best might be a prop.

## WHAT WE FAILED, MEASURED AGAINST FINDING 1 (8/27, our own corpus)
    NPC speeches in the quests                          504
    that open with any hedge or preface                  24   (4.8%)
    that lead with a bare negation                       16   (3.2%)
Real talk is full of prefaces. Ours has almost none, because every one of our
people is fluent, composed and already finished thinking. Nobody buys a second.
This is TELL 8 in records/BOHEMIA_VOICE_DIAGNOSIS_8_26_26.md and it is now
counted on every run.

## WHAT THIS CHANGED IN THE CARD
Rule 3 (SOMEBODY HAS TO ASK, SOMEBODY HAS TO FUMBLE) gains the refusal package
as a concrete technique instead of a vibe. Rule 5 gains the syntax-not-spelling
clause. Nothing else moved; the research confirmed the card rather than
rewriting it, which is the honest outcome to report.
