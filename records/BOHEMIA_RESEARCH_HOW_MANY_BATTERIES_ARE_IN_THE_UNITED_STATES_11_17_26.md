# HOW MANY BATTERIES ARE IN THE UNITED STATES THAT COULD BE CHARGED AGAIN (research for Paolo, 11/17/26; VAMILY rule 459(a))

HIS ASK: 'how many batteries have ever been made in the United States that are still possibly usable if they are able to get
recharged, right now, in the factories, in people's houses, in the garages, what do you think that real estimate is... rechargeable
batteries, what is the capacity, how many people have bought rechargeable battery stations... solar power.'
Done by web research on 11/17 (49 lookups). EST = a labelled estimate where no source was found. The building lanes cite THIS file.

## THE ANSWER
- Counting each thing as one battery (a phone is 1, a car battery is 1, an electric car is 1): ABOUT 2.3 BILLION (low 1.6, high 5).
- Counting the small cells inside them: ABOUT 22 BILLION (low 13, high 37). About 70% of all cells sit inside electric cars.
- What they hold when full: ABOUT 910 GWh (low 750, high 1,150).
- Per person (340 million): about 7 things, 66 cells, 2.7 kWh. Per survivor if 1 in 100 is left: about 680 things, 6,600 cells,
  267 kWh (about 17,800 phone charges).
- Confidence: the energy total is good to about 20% (three well-sourced kinds are 94% of it); the count could be off by two.
  Weakest numbers: household rechargeable AA cells and tool packs (no source), then the cells per electric car.

WHERE THE ENERGY IS: electric cars and hybrids 503 GWh (55%); lead-acid 193 (21%); grid and home 165 (18%); everything a hand
can carry 48 (5%). The things people own most of hold almost none of the energy.

## THE FACTS A PRICE IS BUILT ON
- A rechargeable AA (NiMH): about 27 g, about 2.4 Wh. A throwaway alkaline AA: 23 g, about 3.5 to 4 Wh, used once.
- An 18650 lithium cell: about 47 g, 11 to 12.7 Wh. A full phone charge: about 15 Wh.
- IN AA'S (2.4 Wh each): a phone charge is about 6; a tool pack (50 Wh) about 20; a power station (500 Wh) about 200; a car's
  starter battery (600 Wh) about 250; one electric car (75 kWh) about 31,000. A Tesla Model 3/Y long-range pack holds 4,416 cells.
- One Las Vegas solar roof (about 35 kWh a day) fills about 2,300 phones or about 14,600 AA's a day, before charger losses.
- 3,000 AA's weigh about 80 kg (175 lb); 1,000 about 27 kg (60 lb).

## WHAT THE COORDINATOR SET BESIDE IT (general battery knowledge, NOT from the lookups, and NOT canon until he says)
- THE COUNT IS TODAY'S. Left alone they die: a lead-acid battery left flat is ruined within about a year; a lithium cell left
  empty too long will not take a charge again; heat ages every kind faster, and a Las Vegas garage is hot. Years into a collapse
  the working ones are a small part of the 2.3 billion. That, not the count, is why a battery can be money.
- A BATTERY IS WORTH ITS CHARGE, and the sun is the mint: a working roof fills thousands a day. So in this world working solar
  has to be rare and held by somebody, or a charged AA could never buy a bag of rice. Who holds the roofs is the next question.
- MOST CELLS ARE LOCKED IN ELECTRIC CARS. One wrecked pack is thousands of cells for whoever has the tools to open it.

## CAVEATS
Two government sites (FHWA, AFDC) were blocked from the research machine, so those figures are from search snippets. The EPA
3-billion figure is from a 2009 page. The 137 GWh grid figure is given by some outlets as utility-scale only.
