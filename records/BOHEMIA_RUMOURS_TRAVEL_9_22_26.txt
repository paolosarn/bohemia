WHAT WAS BUILT
================================================================================

A. THE PASS ASKS THE WORLD, NOT THE CAMERA.
   THE FIX IS NOT TO LOOSEN THE ROOM. Two people standing a cell apart in the
   world ARE a cell apart in the world; we simply do not paint both of them, and
   how far apart their pictures are is a question about the screen. ctGossipPass
   now reads ctAt(), the same reader the renderer itself uses.
   AND IT CLOSES THE OLDER HALF OF THE SAME DEFECT: on the draw list, two
   neighbours could only ever talk WHILE THE PLAYER WAS LOOKING AT THEM. The
   file's own comment caught that about the CLOCK in 9/6 and fixed the clock; the
   PLACE was still being asked of the camera.
   ON THE ALPHA AFTER: 61 people, closest pair 1 cell, 46 pairs within talking
   distance, 41 timers live. The pass costs 0.56 ms, against a render of 119 ms.

B. THE WORLD MAKES ITS OWN NEWS. Two people who have stood together forty-five
   minutes sometimes did something worth repeating, raised through the ONE door
   (witness()), witnessed by whoever the WORLD has within sight.
   *** AND THE FIRST CUT MADE AN OUTFIT THE PRICE OF ADMISSION AND MEASURED ZERO
   *** STORIES IN A DAY:

       people in the world                          61
       of them with any outfit at all               15
       outfits on the block                          1
       pairs within talking distance                46
       pairs where BOTH had an outfit                4
       stories made in a day                         0

   Two kills in one line. Most of a residential block runs with nobody, and with
   ONE outfit on the block the "different outfits" story could never happen
   anywhere near his house. A NEIGHBOURHOOD'S GOSSIP IS ABOUT NEIGHBOURS, NOT
   ABOUT GANGS. The outfit now chooses WHICH act it was, never whether there was
   one.

C. A STORY CHANGES IN THE TELLING, and the three changes are the ones that are
   actually replicated: Bartlett's serial reproduction (1932) and Allport and
   Postman (1947).
     LEVELLING     the place slides, the hour goes vague. Detail goes fast, and
                   WHERE and WHEN go before WHAT.
     SHARPENING    the act climbs a rung and never descends, because nobody
                   repeats the smaller version.
     ASSIMILATION  the blame moves onto somebody the teller already carries. The
                   famous one: the razor changes hands.
   And a fourth that is not Bartlett: the SOURCE goes before the claim (Hovland's
   sleeper effect). `from` already rides on every retelling since 9/5, so what
   levelling takes here is the place and the hour, never the fact somebody said it.
   THE ROLL IS HASHED OFF THE STORY AND THE TELLER, NOT RANDOM: the same
   retelling always comes out the same way, so a rumour is not a slot machine and
   a gate can put one story through one mouth twice and get one answer.

D. A STORY KEEPS ITS OWN NAME. The "have I heard this" test compared
   actor + kind + turn, and distortion changes both, so a drifted story came back
   around as NEWS and one event could have bounced between two neighbours for
   ever, each copy wronger, until the ledger was nothing else. YOU RECOGNISE THE
   STORY, NOT THE TIMESTAMP: a seed is stamped where the story starts and
   survives everything that happens to it.

E. *** AND TWO PEOPLE WHO STAND TOGETHER SEE EACH OTHER, WHICH NOTHING IN THIS
   *** GAME HAD EVER DONE.
   MEASURED: 61 minds, and ONE sighting in the entire city. bohemia_memory has
   been a real witness organ since 8/2 -- sightings, familiarity, clarity that
   decays -- and the only thing ever written into it was the PLAYER walking past.
   Nobody on the street had laid eyes on anybody else.
   It cost the row its most important effect. Assimilation bends a story toward a
   FAMILIAR FACE, and the first cut read the DEED LEDGER for that:

       minds holding no actor at all      37
       minds holding exactly one          20
       minds holding two                   4
       stories that changed hands          0

   A mind holding exactly one actor has an EMPTY pool once you exclude the real
   one, so the razor could never change hands and the claim about it could never
   have failed. A sighting list is always full. One see() call each, at the moment
   the pass already knows about the pair.
   AFTER: 27 stories blamed on the wrong person, and five people in the valley now
   have a reputation for something they never did.

F. A MOUTH SAYS IT, per rule 19(c): a head, a name plate, and the sentence. The
   bark sits BELOW the reaction to his own act (that one is more urgent) and
   ABOVE the ambient chatter (news is louder than a remark about the weather),
   fires once per speaker per story so it can never become the street's whole
   voice, and leaves the register-bearing ambient organ untouched.
   ENGLISH ONLY, ON PURPOSE. Paolo 8/26: "enough is enough... stop spending
   rounds on this Spanish shit", and the language gate holds a hard cap of 532
   register lines nobody may raise without a newer quote. The Spanglish law says
   a Spanglish speaker HAS Spanglish lines, not that every sentence is one.

THREE MORE THINGS THE ROUND'S OWN CONTROLS CAUGHT
================================================================================
1. THE WALK DID NOT WALK. stepOnce() takes a DIRECTION INDEX into DIRS, not
   (dx, dy). The probe passed (dx, dy) and moved ONE step in four hundred, and
   reported "nobody said anything" about a player who had not gone anywhere.
   Fixed, and then the street spoke.
   *** AND THE SAME MISTAKE IS IN A GATE THIS LANE ALREADY SHIPPED ***:
   walking_companion_gate.js line 196 calls stepOnce(0, 1). It still steps (in
   DIRS[0], north) so its claim is not wrong, but the second argument means
   nothing and the comment says "eight walked steps" about eight steps in one
   direction. Named here rather than quietly repaired inside another row.

2. A BODY CAN BE DRAWN AND STILL BE UNNAMEABLE. ctPersonName scans ctEveryone(),
   which is the three-by-three around the player, while the renderer draws from
   wider. A story about somebody outside that box returns null, and the bark
   correctly says NOTHING rather than a sentence with a hole in it. Not fixed
   this round: the actors of city-made stories are all inside the box, so it
   costs nothing today, and widening the name reader is not this row.

3. THE SUBJECT WAS BEING CALLED THE SPEAKER'S OWN NAME. ctPersonName falls back
   to a TRADE when the player has not asked somebody's name, which is right for a
   speaker's own plate and wrong mid-sentence: the glass read "WATCH squeezed
   somebody" over the head of a person whose own plate also read WATCH. A trade
   now gets an article, and a trade that collides with the speaker's plate
   becomes "the other watch". Two more caught the same way: the acts are past
   tense, so "I watched {who} {act}" produced "I watched the other worker never
   paid somebody back" on the real glass; and {who} at the head of a second
   sentence was lower case.
