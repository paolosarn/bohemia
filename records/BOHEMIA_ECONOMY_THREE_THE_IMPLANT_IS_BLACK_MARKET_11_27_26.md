## 1. WHAT REAL MEDICINE BUILDS, AND WHAT IT COSTS (US)

- POWERED ARM: sensors on the stump read the muscles and drive the hand's motors; a socket, no surgery. A bebionic-class hand GBP
  15,000 to 25,000 [Mondaq UK 2022]; US multi-grip hands $40,000 to $100,000+ [ScienceInsights, secondary]; the LUKE arm $100,000+
  [Popular Science 2016]. The Hero Arm's battery 7.4 V 2,600 mAh, charged every night [Open Bionics FAQ].
- MICROPROCESSOR KNEE: the knee's resistance set every step. The VA bought 149 C-Legs for $5.3M, about $35,600 each [VA 2008];
  Genium GBP 59,250, X3 85,750, X4 91,750 [Keoghs 2024]; the motorized Power Knee $120,000 [URI c.2008, dated], 25 hours a charge
  [Ossur brochure].
- COCHLEAR IMPLANT: a wire in the inner ear and a processor outside; coarse sound, months to learn. $30,000 to $50,000 an ear all
  in [UCSF]; average $51,072, range $39,475 to $98,195 [CareCredit 2025 via ThePricer].
- CAMERA EYE (Argus II): a camera on glasses feeds a 60-electrode chip on the retina; flashes and edges, not faces. About $150,000;
  one patient's whole bill $497,000. Its maker phased it out from 2019 and 350+ users were left with no repairs, one crowdsourcing
  parts [IEEE Spectrum 2022]. 24 of 30 still working at five years [da Cruz et al., Ophthalmology 2016].
- PACEMAKER: about $8,200 to $11,100 all in [Medicare, secondary]; its battery 10 to 15 years.
- BRAIN-COMPUTER INTERFACE: BrainGate decoded attempted speech at 62 words a minute [Willett et al., Nature 2023]; Neuralink users
  move a cursor, 21 enrolled by January 2026 [Reuters 2026]; the first user lost about 85 percent of his threads [Popular Science
  2024]. Never sold: it needs a lab to train the decoder. IN THE GAME: a story, never a shelf.

## 2. THE REAL RISKS

Socket limbs: no cut, but 23 percent of adults give up an electric arm even with full care [Biddiss and Chau 2007], 44 percent in
an Austrian survey [Salminger et al. 2022]. A limb bolted to bone: superficial infection 38 to 52 percent [Banducci et al. 2023].
Cochlear: adult infection 0.4 to 5.1 percent, about 1.2 percent removed [Stanford cohort]; revisions 5 to 10 percent. Argus II:
12 of 30 had a serious event [da Cruz 2016]. Pacemaker: infection 1 to 5 percent; reused pacemakers 3.86 against 2.23 percent
events across 2,302 devices, not significant [Sinha et al., PACE 2018]: salvage works. Without follow-up care: the Argus users.

## 3. THE BLACK MARKET, AS DOCUMENTED

Kidneys: buyers pay $50,000 to $120,000, sellers get under 10 percent [Library of Parliament Canada citing UNODC]; often under
$5,000 [Global Financial Integrity 2011]; about 10,000 a year [WHO via Advisory Board 2012]. Bought abroad: wound infection 16.7
against 2.0 percent, graft failure at three years 11.7 against 0.8 percent [Avicenna J Med 2022]. Stolen devices before
sterilization [Boston Scientific via FierceBiotech]; over 250,000 counterfeit Ethicon units [MSU 2021]; counterfeit hernia mesh
resold in the US [MassDevice]; $83M of fake devices and drugs seized in 2012 [MassDevice]. Back-room silicone injections kill and
disfigure [NBC News].
