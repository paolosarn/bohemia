=============================================================================
THE FINDING (two of them)
=============================================================================
1. AN ACTION'S COST IS FIXED, IN "MOVES". YOUR CONDITION CONVERTS MOVES INTO
   TIME. AND THE CONVERSION CAN NEVER EXCEED 4x, BECAUSE SPEED HAS A FLOOR.
   The cost table stays one stable number per action; the FELT cost varies with
   how wrecked you are; a bad day can never become an infinite one.

2. A STEP IS AN ACTION, PRICED IN THE SAME CURRENCY. A plain step is
   run_cost( 100, false ) — 100 moves, one second at base speed — and bonuses
   are capped so a step can never cost LESS than 100. Walking and doing are ONE
   clock in their model with two kinds of purchase. Bohemia's are deliberately
   TWO clocks (clause 17 of the mobile-camp law: the buff burns on steps, the
   day burns on every action), so this is a real divergence, chosen, not missed.

--- THE FUNDAMENTAL UNIT ---
MOVES_PER_TURN 100 — calendar.h:289 `return to_turns<int>( duration ) * 100;`
  inside the friend to_moves() declared at calendar.h:160. This is the whole
  reason the model works: an action is priced in a currency 100x finer than the
  tick, so a cost can be divided by speed and still land on a whole tick.
SECONDS_PER_TURN 1 — calendar.h:232 `from_seconds( const T t ) { return
  time_duration( t ); }` reads identically to calendar.h:223
  `from_turns( const T t ) { return time_duration( t ); }`. A duration's raw
  unit IS the turn, and one second constructs the same duration as one turn, so
  a turn is a second. calendar.h:227 `from_moves( t ) { return
  time_duration( t / 100 ); }` closes the loop from the other direction.
BASE_SPEED 100 — creature.cpp:189 `speed_base = 100;` in Creature::Creature()
  (constructor opens at creature.cpp:183). The member is declared
  creature.h:1276 `int speed_base = 0;` and the accessor is creature.h:927
  `virtual int get_speed_base() const;` — the value 100 lives at the
  constructor, which is why the citation is creature.cpp and not character.cpp.

=============================================================================
CONTENT — theirs, and deliberately thin
=============================================================================
The page's ACTIONS list is FOUR GENERIC ROWS (a quick thing / a short task / a
real piece of work / an hour on something) built from the duration ladder above.
It is deliberately NOT a borrowed task list. Cataclysm's per-task times live in
its JSON data tree; enumerating them would have produced a document that reads
as a PROPOSAL for Bohemia's action costs, and clause 4 of the time law reserves
that table to Paolo. MECHANISM-MINE / CONTENTS-PAOLO'S: the shape is mine to
bring, the list and the numbers are his.

The ERRANDS rows pair their four real block lengths with their three real
payout rates. That pairing (which rate goes with which block) is the page's
arrangement, not a claim about their content — their rates attach to specific
missions, not to block lengths.

=============================================================================
THE ARITHMETIC, CHECKED
=============================================================================
A 30-minute job = 30 * 60 * 100 = 180,000 moves, always.
  at speed 100 (fresh):            180000 / 100 = 1,800 s = 30 min
  at speed 50 (100% overloaded,
    pain 15, thirst 50):           180000 / 50  = 3,600 s = 60 min
  at the floor, speed 25:          180000 / 25  = 7,200 s = 120 min
  worst case multiplier:           100 / 25 = 4x, and never more.
A plain step = 100 moves: 1.00 s fresh, 4.00 s at the floor.
  (For scale, Bohemia's own crossing maths puts a step at 3.52 s — see clause 16
  of the mobile-camp law. Their WORST step and our AVERAGE step are the same
  number, which is a coincidence, but a useful one to feel.)
Travel: 0 away = 20 min; 1 = 30; 3 = 50; 8 = 100 min.
Errands: 3x1 = 3, 4x4 = 16, 5x10 = 50, 5x20 = 100.
Sleep ladder: 2880 / 1440 = 2 days to the first rung; 20160 = 14 days to the top.
