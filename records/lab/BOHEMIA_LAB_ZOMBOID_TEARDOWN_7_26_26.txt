==============================================================================
MECHANIC 1 — LOOTING: a two-stage procedural distribution
==============================================================================
THE WHOLE SYSTEM IN ONE SENTENCE: the ROOM and the CONTAINER TYPE together pick
which named loot tables are in play, and every table rolls its good items AND a
separate junk table.

KEY               VALUE   SOURCE                      WHAT IT MEANS
CHANCE_BASE       100     Distributions.lua           weightChance is out of 100
GUARANTEED_MIN    98      Distributions.lua           min>=98 is their "always" sentinel

STAGE 1 (Distributions.lua:10972, the real kitchen counter):
  kitchen.counter.procList = {
    {name="KitchenBottles",    min=0, max=1, weightChance=40},
    {name="KitchenBaking",     min=0, max=1, weightChance=40},
    {name="KitchenBreakfast",  min=0, max=1, weightChance=80},
    {name="KitchenCannedFood", min=0, max=1, weightChance=100},
    {name="KitchenDishes",     min=0, max=1, weightChance=80},
    {name="KitchenDryFood",    min=0, max=1, weightChance=100},
    {name="KitchenPots",       min=0, max=1, weightChance=80},
    {name="KitchenRandom",     min=0, max=1, weightChance=20},
  }
STAGE 2 (ProceduralDistributions.lua, the real KitchenCannedFood):
  rolls = 2, items = { "TinOpener", 8, "CannedChili", 4, ... "Vinegar_Jug", 0.05 },
  junk  = { rolls = 1, items = { "DeadMouse", 1, ... } }

THE FIVE DESIGN FACTS UNDER IT:
1. THE CONTAINER TYPE IS THE KEY, NOT THE ROOM. A kitchen COUNTER and a kitchen
   CRATE draw completely different tables. The same crate in a bedroom draws a
   third set. Place a container and you have authored an expectation.
2. JUNK IS A SEPARATE ROLL WITH ITS OWN ROLLS COUNT. That is the mechanical
   reason rummaging feels like rummaging instead of shopping: the game is
   deliberately spending your time on a dead mouse.
3. WEIGHTS SPAN FIVE ORDERS OF MAGNITUDE IN ONE LIST. In LivingRoomShelf a
   skill book sits at 0.005 next to entries at 8. Rarity is not a separate
   system, a separate roll, or a separate table: it is the same weighted list
   with a small number in it.
4. A TABLE IS REUSED EVERYWHERE. KitchenCannedFood appears in the kitchen
   counter, the kitchen shelves, the overhead cupboards and the livingroom
   counter, at different weightChances. One authored table, many places.
5. THE MIN/MAX IS AN INSTANCE COUNT, NOT AN ITEM COUNT. min=0,max=4 on
   KitchenDryFood means the whole table can roll FOUR TIMES in one cupboard.

CONTENT: the tables themselves are theirs, and the emulation embeds the REAL
data, trimmed and declared: 4 rooms, 18 container slots, 65 tables, each keeping
its top 12 items by weight and top 6 junk entries. No weight was altered and
nothing was invented. Item WEIGHTS are ours (see below).

==============================================================================
MECHANIC 2 — RUMMAGING: the time economy
==============================================================================
THE WHOLE SYSTEM IN ONE SENTENCE: every item you touch costs time, and the cost
is set by how heavy it is and by which two containers you are moving it between.

KEY               VALUE   SOURCE                                WHAT IT MEANS
T_BASE            120     ISInventoryTransferAction.lua:785     moving between two things on you
T_CONTAINER       50      ISInventoryTransferAction.lua:795     taking from / putting into the world
T_WEIGHT_CAP      3       ISInventoryTransferAction.lua:810     w = min(weight, 3)
T_DELTA_FLOOR     0.4     ISInventoryTransferAction.lua:805     the fullness term's floor
T_SELF_DROP       0.1     ISInventoryTransferAction.lua:820     dropping from your own hands
T_FLOOR_DROP      0.2     ISInventoryTransferAction.lua:822     dropping out of a container
T_DEXTROUS        0.5     ISInventoryTransferAction.lua:828     the trait
T_ALLTHUMBS       2.0     ISInventoryTransferAction.lua:831     and its opposite

THE FORMULA, verbatim in shape:
  maxTime = 120
  if src is your inventory and dest is also on you:  delta = destWeight/destMax
  elif src is your inventory:                        maxTime = 50
  elif dest is on you:                               maxTime = 50
  delta = max(0.4, delta)
  w = min(item.actualWeight, 3)
  maxTime = maxTime * w * delta

THE FOUR DESIGN FACTS UNDER IT:
1. WEIGHT IS TIME. A 3kg pot costs 3x a 1kg can and 30x a 0.1kg bandaid, capped
   at 3kg so nothing takes forever. Looting time is a function of WHAT you loot,
   not a flat search timer. Almost every game gets this wrong.
2. ORGANISING COSTS MORE THAN TAKING. 120 base to move something between two
   things you carry, 50 to take from or stash into the world. The game charges
   you 2.4x for fiddling with your own inventory.
3. TRAITS ARE FLAT MULTIPLIERS ON THE WHOLE ECONOMY. 0.5 and 2.0. One number
   makes a character a hoarder or a fumbler across every interaction they have.
4. DROPPING IS ALMOST FREE (x0.1 out of your hands). Panic-dumping is always
   available, which is what makes encumbrance a decision and not a trap.

==============================================================================
MECHANIC 3 — ENCUMBRANCE, AND THE THING I GOT WRONG FIRST
==============================================================================
I ASSUMED "a full bag is a slow bag" applies to everything. IT DOES NOT, and the
source is unambiguous: destCapacityDelta is only computed in the branch where
BOTH containers are on your body (ISInventoryTransferAction.lua:790-792).
GRABBING OFF A SHELF IS FLAT. The fullness tax lands when you PACK.

Measured on the shipped page, 3kg item, ticks:
  grab from a container (any fullness)      150
  pack into a 0%-full bag                   144
  pack into a 95%-full bag                  342     <- 2.4x
So the emulation models THE REAL TWO-STEP LOOP a Zomboid player actually does:
take into your hands (flat), then pack into the bag (taxed by fullness).

THE DESIGN FACT, and it is the best one in the file: ZOMBOID CHARGES YOU FOR
ORGANISING, NOT FOR GRABBING. Snatching is cheap; deciding what to keep is
expensive. That is exactly the right way round for a looting game, and it is one
if-branch.

NOT IMPLEMENTED, ON PURPOSE: the movement/stamina penalty for being overloaded
(Java-side, not in the Lua I read), loot-rarity sandbox settings, zombie density
scaling (`ignoreZombieDensity` is read and cited but not modelled), container
capacity limits, item condition, and the whole noise/attention system.
