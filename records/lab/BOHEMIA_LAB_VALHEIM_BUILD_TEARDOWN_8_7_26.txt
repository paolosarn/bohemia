================================================================================
THE FINDING
================================================================================

A WORKBENCH NEEDS A ROOF AND 70% COVER TO CRAFT. IT NEEDS NEITHER TO CLAIM ITS
BUILD RADIUS OR TO SUPPRESS ENEMY SPAWNS.

That is one design decision and it is the entire feeling he described. The moment
the bench touches bare dirt you own the two properties that make a camp a camp --
a zone you may build in, and a zone nothing spawns in. The house is an optional
later improvement. YOU DO NOT BUILD A CAMP, YOU PLACE ONE.

The second finding, which is why it feels fast rather than merely being fast:
DECONSTRUCTION REFUNDS WHAT IT COST, so being wrong is free, so nobody hesitates.
Hesitation is what actually makes a build system feel slow -- not menu depth.

WHY VALHEIMPLUS COUNTS AS A SOURCE AT ALL: it is a HarmonyX mod that patches the
live game's own classes by name. Its [HarmonyPatch(typeof(X), "Y")] attributes,
its field and property references, and its per-setting DEFAULTS are the real
game's field names and the real game's vanilla values -- a mod that got them
wrong would not load. So a name or a vanilla default read off ValheimPlus is
genuinely sourced. A FORMULA read off ValheimPlus is the MOD'S formula, and where
that distinction matters it is stated on the row.

================================================================================
MECHANISM vs CONTENT
================================================================================

MECHANISM (copied exactly, and what a port would take):
  - the asymmetry: roof+cover gate CRAFTING only, never building or suppression
  - one radius doing both jobs, so there is no second number to tune
  - the visible circle being the same variable as the build range
  - placement answering with a three-value STATUS, not a boolean
  - one tool, three verbs, no build mode
  - refund gated on "did you place it", and the refund being the full cost
  - level = 1 + distinct extensions in attach range; radius grows per level
  - support having to reach the ground, with loss on two axes

CONTENT (Valheim's, standing in as ours, and replaceable wholesale):
  - every recipe (what a workbench, a wall, a club costs)
  - the piece list and the extension list
  - the material names
  - the starting inventory (not in the constant block at all -- page furniture)

WHAT IS NOT HERE, ON PURPOSE:
  - snapping, rotation, roof-piece geometry, wall-by-wall architecture. Valheim
    has TWO systems inside one hammer. PLACING A CAMP is the one he praised;
    CONSTRUCTING A BUILDING is the one with a Steam thread titled "Building Feels
    Terrible" and a manual-snapping feature added in 2023 to apologise for it.
    A page that modelled the second would answer a question nobody asked.
  - real-time swing timing, stamina drain per placement, durability decay over
    days, weather damage, and the ward/PrivateArea permission system.
  - the vanilla per-material integrity figures. The STRUCTURE of integrity is
    sourced; the per-step base loss is declared ours because those numbers are
    not readable and this page will not pretend otherwise.
  - ANY Bohemia number. Not one. NO DAMAGE BEFORE THE DIAL is intact.

================================================================================
THE FIVE LOOPS, AND HOW EACH ONE CLOSES
================================================================================

1. BUILDING. Select a piece, tap the ground. Status answers Valid / Invalid /
   NoBuildZone with a reason. Cost leaves the pack, the piece exists, repeat.
   The loop's real content is the ONE LINE that is the finding:
       if (!d.station && !d.anywhere && !stationCovering(cx, cy)) -> Invalid
   A station is exempt from needing a station. That exemption is the reason your
   first tap on bare dirt always works.

2. CRAFTING. Stand in a bench's range, roof the bench, cover 7 of the 9 tiles
   over it, press CRAFT. Materials leave, the item lands in "made", repeat. With
   fewer than 7 roofed it refuses and says the count, so the 70% is legible
   instead of mysterious.

3. DECONSTRUCTING. Place anything, switch to REMOVE, tap it: the full cost comes
   back and the inventory returns to exactly where it started. Tap the ruin
   instead and nothing comes back, because IsPlacedByPlayer is false.

4. UPGRADING. Park CHOP BLOCK / TAN RACK / ADZE / TOOL SHELF inside the dashed
   5 m ring. Level goes 1 -> 5, radius goes 20 -> 36, and the white circle grows
   on screen each time. Level caps and radius caps together.

5. SPAWN SUPPRESSION. Press SPAWN ROLL. Rolls inside the circle produce nothing
   (green dots), rolls outside produce a monster (red dots). Take the bench down
   and roll again: the same cells now spawn. That is what the circle was for.

AND STILL PHYSICAL: switch to REPAIR and tap a piece to read its support. A wall
with no chain to the ground draws red and reads UNSUPPORTED. Fast to use is not
weightless, and that is why it reads as real instead of as a menu.

HOW THIS WAS VERIFIED — TEN PLANTED MISTAKES, TEN CAUGHT
================================================================================

lab_gate.js runs 573 checks with LAB-10's row added, 35 of them B0-B32 driving
this page's own functions in a real browser at 390x844. Green is not evidence, so
every claim above was mutation-tested by breaking the page on purpose and
checking the gate noticed:

  M1  a station stops being exempt from needing a station     -> B1, B3, B4, B7
  M2  the BUILD path starts requiring a roof                  -> B27, B28, B30 (+10)
  M3  spawn radius decoupled from build radius (two knobs)    -> B24
  M4  refund halved                                          -> A16, B14, B16
  M5  the ruin becomes player-placed                          -> B15
  M6  NoBuildZone collapsed into plain Invalid                -> B4, B5
  M7  extensions attach from any distance                     -> B21
  M8  cover requirement dropped to 0.6                        -> A16, B10
  M9  roofTheBench's redundant skip removed                   -> NOTHING. Correctly.
  M9b roofTheBench counts new placements (the REAL bug)       -> B9a, B10, B11

TWO OF THOSE ROWS ARE THE ONLY REASON THIS SECTION IS WORTH READING.

M9 IS A CONTROL THAT FAILED TO BE A CONTROL. It was meant to reproduce a bug this
page really had -- roofTheBench(n) treating n as "place n more roofs" instead of
"reach n roofed cells", which made the 6-of-9 case silently test 7-of-9 so the
cover checks agreed with each other while both were wrong. M9 removed the wrong
line and the gate stayed green, and a green run against a mutation is either a
weak check or a bad mutation. It was a bad mutation: the fix was seeding the count
from the roofs already there, not the skip. M9b broke the right line and B9a
caught it. A CONTROL THAT DOES NOT REPRODUCE THE FAILURE PROVES NOTHING, and the
only way to find that out is to check which line the fix actually lives on.

M2 FIRST RAN AS A CRASH, NOT AS A FAILURE. It made pieces unplaceable, so B8 read
a null piece, threw, and killed the run at check 8 of 32 -- which meant B27, B28
and B30, the three checks that measure the actual finding, never executed and
could not be shown to catch anything. B8 was hardened to return nulls instead of
throwing and M2 was re-run: all three then failed loudly, and B28's failure
message names the tell by itself ("Build false->true, spawns-off true->true,
craft false->true" -- roofing moved TWO answers where the finding says it moves
exactly one). A GATE THAT DIES EARLY IS A GATE WHOSE LATER CHECKS ARE UNPROVEN.

AND THE MUTATION HARNESS ITSELF WAS BROKEN THE FIRST TIME. It reverted each
mutation with `git checkout -- <page>` on a file that had never been committed, so
nothing ever reverted, eight mutations stacked on top of each other, and every
result after the first was garbage that still looked like a clean table of
FAILs. Caught by the revert printing "did not match any file(s) known to git" and
by the failure lists being identical run to run. Fixed by committing the page
first and asserting the revert with `git diff --quiet`. THE TOOL THAT CHECKS THE
WORK NEEDS ITS OWN CHECK, and "the output looked plausible" is not one.


================================================================================
THEN I OPENED THE PNG, AND FOUND FOUR THINGS 572 GREEN CHECKS COULD NOT SEE
================================================================================

VERIFY ON THE REAL SURFACE (7/18) says look at the rendered pixels before
shipping. Every one of these was invisible to the model checks, because every one
of them is about SIZE, and not one check asked about a size.

1. *** THE WHITE CLAIM CIRCLE WAS NOT ON THE SCREEN AT ALL. *** "The claim draws
   itself" is one of the five findings, and the grid was 15x33 at 26px cells, so a
   20 m radius was 8 cells and a 36 m radius was 14.4 -- the circle fell entirely
   off a 15-cell-wide board. The only ring visible was the player's 8 m reach, in
   the wrong colour, reading as the claim to anybody looking at it. THIRTY CHECKS
   WERE GREEN AND THE CENTRAL VISUAL WAS MISSING.
   Fixed by the grid, not by the shot: 26x33 at 15px, same 390px wide.
   New checks B31/B31b measure the circle's diameter against the canvas.

2. AND THAT UNDERSIZED GRID WAS THE ROOT CAUSE OF SOMETHING I HAD ALREADY
   "FIXED" THE WRONG WAY. B20 originally asserted the claimed CELL COUNT grows
   each level and failed at 195->257->281->285->285, saturating on the whole
   board. I changed the CHECK to measure area in m2 instead. That was papering:
   the count was right and the BOARD was too small. On the real grid the original
   assertion passes, and B20b now asserts it never swallows the board. FIX THE
   TARGET, NOT THE RULER -- and I did it backwards first.

3. *** THE PLAYER WAS INVISIBLE. *** The marker drew at CELL-16 wide, a literal
   tuned for 26px cells. At 15px that is MINUS ONE PIXEL and the canvas drew
   nothing. Two more literals in the same family: roof squares came out 3px, and
   the spawn dots were sized to swamp the terrain. Every draw size is now derived
   from CELL, and B32 asserts each stays visible.

4. THE LEGEND WAS LYING BY OMISSION. Spawned monsters were drawn in the same red
   as an unsupported piece, so two unrelated meanings shared one swatch, and the
   legend listed "unsupported" while never mentioning the dots at all. Monsters
   got their own colour and both dot states got legend entries.

WHAT LINKS ALL FOUR: the gate tested whether the MODEL agreed with itself, and it
did, perfectly. Nothing tested whether the model was VISIBLE. A finding about what
a player can see cannot be verified by a check about what a function returns.
