## THE FINDING

> **TEN YEARS COLD, MONEY IS NOT THE CURRENCY. STANDING IS.**

Ban money as a system and there is nothing left to trade *with*. What's left is
who owns the working thing and whether they'll deal with you. That isn't a
workaround for the ban — it's a better game, and New Vegas is the proof.

### And it works because of exactly one structural decision

**Fame and infamy are two separate counters that never cancel.** Not a slider
from hated to loved. Two numbers, tracked apart, and your title is read off
*both*.

That's why New Vegas has words no other game has — **Wild Child**, **Dark Hero**,
**Soft-Hearted Devil**. Titles that only exist because the game refused to average
you out. A single slider expresses "how good are you". Two counters express
"what kind of person are you", which is a different and much better question.

**One slider is a stat. Two counters is a person.**

Measured on the page: press "work both sides of the same fight" repeatedly and
your title goes **Soft-Hearted Devil → Dark Hero → Wild Child** while *neither
number ever goes down*. A single slider cannot do that.

### The hard gate is the best line in it

To be truly Idolized you need fame ≥ **90** *and* infamy under **4**. **You
cannot buy your way out of a bad name with good deeds.** That's a gate, not a sum,
and it's the most portable idea in the document.

## THE CITY-BUILDER HALF — which is what he was actually pointing at

> "FALLOUT NEW VEGAS **THAT ALSO DOUBLES AS A CITY BUILDER**"

With money banned, a building can't pay you. So what does it do?

> **YOU DON'T BUILD TO GET RICH. YOU BUILD TO BECOME SOMEBODY THEY HAVE TO DEAL
> WITH.**

That's the join. The builder isn't a separate mode bolted onto an RPG — **it is
how you earn standing**, and under the CENTURY RULE it compounds across three
acts. A spreadsheet city-builder asks "what does this produce." This one asks
"who does this make me."

And it gives Valheim's comfort curve a second job: in LAB-05 comfort bought a
longer buff; here it also buys **standing weight** — a nobody's favour is worth
less than a somebody's. Same curve, new use, and the new use is the join.

## THE THIRD FINDING

**The same deed is worth wildly different amounts.** The Brotherhood accepts you
at **3**. The Legion idolises you at **100**. The cheapest door in the game costs
3 and the dearest costs 100.

A small, insular, desperate outfit is cheap to impress. A huge bureaucratic one
barely notices. **That is how you make a small faction matter without buffing
it**, and it costs one number per faction.

## WHAT NOT TO PORT

- **Any economy. At all.** Not a price, not an exchange rate, not a display. That
  is now law and this note repeats it so a reader of one file can't miss it.
- **A 0–100 fame number the player reads.** His register is small legible numbers.
  The *two-counter structure* ports; a pair of percentage bars is another game's
  HUD.
- **New Vegas's specific thresholds.** They're calibrated to a 30-hour RPG with
  eight factions. Ours would have to be re-derived for a roguelite where runs
  reset — and **what happens to standing on death is the single biggest open
  question this page raises and does not answer.**
- **Zomboid's shutoff as a live mechanic.** Deliberately past tense here. Day 14
  is a date ten years ago, not an event. The page draws a line through it.
- **My deeds, my worth multiplier, and my act compounding.** All three are mine,
  all three are crude, and all three exist only so the page can be pressed. The
  CENTURY RULE's real mechanism is [PENDING Paolo] and its own law says so.

## HONEST LIMITS

- **One sourced number.** Everything else is `[DOC]`. New Vegas's reputation data
  lives in the game's ESM behind the GECK; both Fallout wikis and GitHub's code
  search return 403 at this gateway, so the numbers come from documented
  summaries and are tagged that way.
- **The two infamy marks (15 / 50) are my placement**, not their data. Their real
  infamy thresholds are per-faction like the fame ones and I did not find them.
  The page needed a scale to render; the teardown says so rather than implying I
  read them.
- **The 33x spread is the honest way to feel it, not a single documented ratio.**
  Dearest-idolized over cheapest-idolized is 5x; 100-over-3 is the cheapest door
  against the dearest. Both are in the teardown.
- **Five of their consequences aren't modelled** — vendor discounts, hired thugs,
  gifts, dialogue skill checks, and the endgame territory redistribution. That
  last one (who ends up owning the dam) is the most relevant New Vegas mechanic
  to a 100-year city-builder and is **flagged as the next thing worth studying**.
- **This is not my lane's system.** Factions belong to WORLD/QUEST and the city to
  CITY. The page touches none of their code and claims nothing.
- **I have not seen him play it.**

## WHAT THIS DOES NOT DECIDE

**Not one number here is Bohemia's**, and by law there is no economy on the page.
The THREE CURRENCIES stand: resources, electricity, clout, no fourth thing.

**Two questions:**

1. Is **standing** the thing you spend, and is **building** how you earn it?
2. Should standing be **two counters that never cancel** — so you can be loved and
   feared by the same people — rather than one slider?

And the one I'd want answered before anyone builds it: **in a roguelite, what
happens to standing when the run ends?**
