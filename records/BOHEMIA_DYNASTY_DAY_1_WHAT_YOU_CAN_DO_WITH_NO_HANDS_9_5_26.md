## 1. THE GAMES AISLE -- THE VERB SET IS THE SAME EVERY TIME
Read the control lists of the games where you are actually the animal, not a
person in a costume. They converge, and they converge hard. The whole
vocabulary is eight verbs:
```
MOVE            walk, and RUN at a cost you feel. running spends something,
                and you get it back by STOPPING. sitting down is a move.
BITE            and HOLD the bite. the hold is the skill, not the bite.
CARRY ONE THING in your mouth. while you carry it you cannot bite, and
                carrying costs you the same thing running costs.
CALL            a howl, a bark. other animals answer it. it is the only
                thing you can send further than you can walk.
MARK            you leave yourself on the ground and it stays there.
SMELL           a mode. the world is repainted as who has been here.
FEED            give what you have to something smaller than you.
REST            sleep, and the day moves without you.
```
**NOT ONE OF THOSE EIGHT NEEDS A HAND.** That is the finding: the verb set is
not "the human list minus things". It is its own complete list, and it was
arrived at independently by everybody who has built one.

### AND HERE IS EXACTLY WHERE IT GETS BORING. THREE FAILURES, ALL DOCUMENTED.
1. **THE GLOWING LINE.** Almost every one of these games solves "an animal
   cannot read a map" the same way: a smell mode that greys the world out and
   draws a coloured trail along the ground, and you follow it. The reviews of
   the ones that do it land on the same word, repetitive, and the same wish,
   that it would "evolve in some way". **A smell that draws you a line is not
   a sense. It is a quest marker with a filter over it.** It is the single
   most-copied and most-criticised mechanic in the whole aisle.
2. **A BODY WITH NOTHING UNDER IT.** The most common criticism of the
   prettiest ones is that the animal is a novelty and there is no system
   beneath it: "beautiful but surprisingly empty", "the lack of anything
   substantive to do with it". The body is not the game. The body is the
   input device.
3. **THEY ARE SHORT, AND THEY ARE STILL TOO LONG.** The well-reviewed ones
   run two to six hours, and reviewers STILL say there is not much reason to
   go back. **Eight verbs is a five-hour game.** That is not an insult to the
   genre, it is a measurement of a ceiling, and it is the single most useful
   number on this page for us.

## 2. *** THE ONE SENTENCE, WHICH IS WHAT A LANE CAN BUILD FROM ***
> **AN ANIMAL IS NOT A HUMAN WITH FEWER BUTTONS. IT IS A DIFFERENT SET OF
> FACTS ABOUT THE SAME STREET.**
The version that fails SUBTRACTS: no hands, no talking, no trading, no
building, so you get the human game with the buttons greyed out and a
walking speed. The version that works SUBSTITUTES: you give up the hands and
you get a sense that **nobody else in the game has**, and the street
reorganises around it. The player is not playing a weaker character. He is
being handed information the human generation will never get again.
And that is the strongest possible argument for putting the animal FIRST:
a thing you lose is worth more than a thing you never had.

## 3. THE REAL AISLE -- WHAT AN ANIMAL HAS INSTEAD OF HANDS
Four facts, and three of them are not what a designer would guess.

### (a) SMELL IS NOT A LINE. IT IS A CLOCK.
A tracking dog brought in at right angles to a trail does three things in
order: it searches for the track, it **decides which way the person went**,
and then it follows. The deciding is the interesting part. It needs only
**three to five consecutive footprints** and it takes **three to five
seconds**, and the mechanism is comparing how STRONG the smell is between
adjacent prints. Scent decays, so the older print is weaker. The animal is
not seeing a trail. It is reading a gradient in TIME.
> **A TRACK HAS A DIRECTION BECAUSE IT HAS AN AGE.**
That single sentence kills the glowing line without argument. A smell does
not tell you where somebody IS. It tells you **which way and how long ago**,
and those are two numbers a game can be built out of, and they get worse
while you stand there.

### (b) SMELL IS STEREO.
Airflow modelling of an actual nose says each nostril takes a **spatially
separate sample**, and the comparison between the two is what locates the
source. Two nostrils are two ears. So **turning your head is an action**, and
the direction of the wind is a fact about the board.

### (c) SNIFFING IS NOT BREATHING.
The air comes in the front and goes out the **side slits, backwards**, so the
animal never blows away the thing it is smelling -- and it can aim that
exhale at the ground to stir more up. Breathing in is taking a reading.
Breathing out is either staying quiet or **disturbing the ground on purpose.**

### (d) THE EYES TRADE DOWN AND THE DARK TRADES UP.
Two cones instead of three, so blue and yellow and effectively no red.
Acuity around 20/75. But far more rods, a reflective layer behind the retina,
and eyes further round the head: **much better in the dark, much better at
MOVEMENT, and a much wider field of view.**
> **COLOUR IS NEARLY GONE. DETAIL IS NEARLY GONE. MOTION AND DARK ARE FREE.**
That is a straight fit with a valley that opens at 06:00 in the dark, where
LIGHT=TERRITORY and NOBODY PATROLS THE DARK. The animal generation is the one
that is not afraid of our own night, and it costs no new art to say so.

### (e) AND THE ONE NOBODY EXPECTS: THE REAL SUPERPOWER IS READING PEOPLE.
A pet dog will follow a human's POINT to hidden food. Hand-raised wolves do
worse at it. Chimpanzees do worse at it. Domestication selected for treating
a human gesture as **information**, and that is a stronger social skill than
the ones our nearest relatives have.
> **THE ANIMAL'S DIALOGUE SYSTEM IS NOT TALKING. IT IS WATCHING WHAT A
> PERSON DOES WITH THEIR BODY AND KNOWING WHAT IT MEANS.**
And we already built the hard half of that on 8/26 and 8/27: the face
PERFORMS (mouth off the actual letters, blink, brow) and the portrait pops up
whenever somebody speaks. **The animal cannot hear the sentence and can read
the brow.** Same renderer, same portrait, the words withheld. That is a
dialogue system for Gen 1 with zero new art and zero new canon.

## 4. THE MEASUREMENT -- WHAT BOHEMIA HAS TODAY (all measured 9/5)
1. **THE WORD "ANIMAL" APPEARS IN THE DYNASTY ENGINE EXACTLY ONCE AND IT IS
   IN A COMMENT.** `const GEN_COUNT = 3; // Animal / Human / Angel`
   (engine/bohemia_engine.js:2488). That is the entire presence of Gen 1 in
   the machine that owns the dynasty.
2. **THE FOLD IS GENERATION-BLIND.** Across the whole fold block
   (engine/bohemia_engine.js:2467-2680) the variable `gen` is compared in
   exactly TWO places and both are bookkeeping: filtering which choices
   belong to this generation, and the `for` loop counter. **Nothing branches
   on which generation it is.** Gen 1 folds the same `standings`,
   `territory`, `builds`, `economyCapacity`, `invest`, `karma` and `virtues`
   as Gen 2. **A coyote inherits a productive economy.**
3. **`selectHeir` STILL HAS ZERO CALLERS.** Three mentions in the entire
   repo: the definition, and the module export line, which appears twice more
   as inlined copies inside two old slices. (Confirms the 9/4 scorecard.)
4. **AND `selectHeir` CANNOT PERFORM THE ONE FOLD GEN 1 EXISTS FOR.** Its
   body picks `rel === 'child'`, else `rel === 'sibling_child'`, else returns
   null with its own comment "dynasty in crisis". The gen-1-to-gen-2 handoff
   is **Animal to Human**. Under the only heir function this game has, an
   animal's heir is its cub.
5. **THERE IS NO SMELL IN BOHEMIA.** Across every engine module and every
   slice: `olfact` **0**, `sniff` **2**, and every hit for `smell`, `scent`,

## 5. *** THE FINDING THAT CHALLENGES US ***
### FIRST, THE SMALL ONE, WHICH IS FREE MONEY
**WE BUILT THE ANIMAL'S MODEL OF THE PLAYER AND NEVER BUILT THE PLAYER'S
MODEL OF THE ANIMAL.** Every species in the valley carries a number for how
close a person can get before it decides about them, and a second number for
when it goes. The valley already knows exactly what an animal thinks about
you. Gen 1 is that same file read the other way round, and there is nothing
on that side at all. Same standing shape as the seventeen invisible hats, the
four bright garments nobody wore, the face maker with no route in and the
twelve approved encounters that never fired: the material exists and never
reached the player. I have not numbered it, because nobody has ever counted
this class in one place and a confident count is as expensive as a confident
no (8/28).

### AND THE ONE THAT ACTUALLY BREAKS SOMETHING
**GEN 1 CANNOT USE THE FOLD, AND THE FOLD IS THE ONLY DYNASTY MACHINE WE
HAVE.** The fold's entire content is faction standings, territory, builds,
economy capacity, per-district investment, karma, virtues and a family tree.
An animal has **no standing with a faction** (it is not a person a faction
can hold an opinion about), holds **no territory** in the sense this game
means, **builds nothing**, and has **no economy**. Run the animal generation
through the fold exactly as written and it hands the human generation a block
of zeroes.
> **A HANDOFF THAT INHERITS NOTHING IS A RUN. THAT IS THE FIRST LINE OF
> CLAUDE.md, AND OUR OWN FOLD IS WHERE IT WOULD HAPPEN.**
The 8/28 ruling is explicit that a handoff INHERITS EVERYTHING and that is
precisely why THERE ARE NO RUNS never touched it. The engine as written
cannot honour that for generation one. This is not a bug to file, it is a
question nobody has asked: **what can an animal actually leave behind?**
The answer that survives our own laws is one this project already found
twice, from two other directions:
- day 16's **shadow of the future**: because we never reset, every
  relationship is a repeated game, and the man who lives is the one who
  tells people;
- day 4's **marks that persist**, and the ruling that attachment is built
  from interdependence, responsiveness, and being missed.
**AN ANIMAL LEAVES BEHAVIOUR IN OTHER ANIMALS AND MEMORY IN PEOPLE.** Ground
it knows. People who have seen it and what they decided about it. A pack that
exists because it made one. A body carrying what happened to it. Those are
four things the fold has **no field for** -- and the walked city already
half-models all of them in the five person-memory writes day 21 found sitting
OUTSIDE the hardened save (`minds`, `known`, `met`, `belong`, `deedweight`).
**So the gen-1 inheritance block and the people-save gap are the same hole
seen from two ends,** which is the argument for RUN's BB-THE-PEOPLE-RIDE-THE
-SAVE being worth more than its row currently says.

## 6. WHAT WE TAKE AND WHAT WE REFUSE
**TAKE**
- The eight verbs in section 1, as they stand. Not one needs a hand, every
  one is real, and they are already 120 BPM friendly: a step is a beat, a
  bite is a beat, a call is a beat.
- **Smell as a clock, never a line.** A track carries an AGE and a
  DIRECTION, read off a few consecutive prints, and the reading COSTS
  SECONDS you can be caught standing in. Under day 17's standing rule --
  words for the world, one number, and the number is theirs on you -- the
  animal's readout is words (WARM, COLD, HE WENT THAT WAY, TWO OF THEM) and
  the one number is **how old**, which is exactly "how much trouble am I in"
  translated into a nose.
- Stereo. Left and right differ, so turning your head is a move, and wind is
  a fact about the board.
- The eyes trade down and the dark trades up. Costs no new art; the valley
  already has a night and already says nobody patrols it.
- **Reading a person's body instead of their words.** The face performs
  already. Withhold the sentence, keep the brow.
**REFUSE**
- **A smell mode that paints a coloured line to the objective.** The most
  copied and most criticised mechanic in the aisle. It converts a sense into
  a quest marker and it is where every one of these games gets boring.
- A hunger bar, a thirst bar, any meter on the animal's body. Day 7 settled
  that and being an animal is not an exemption -- if anything it is the
  strongest test of it.
- Any new damage number for teeth. NO DAMAGE BEFORE THE DIAL, and section 1's
  verbs need none: the bite is a HOLD, which is time, not damage.
- A dialogue menu. An animal that picks a reply off a list is a human in fur,
  and it throws away the only mechanic in section 3 that is uniquely ours.
- **A long Gen 1.** Eight verbs is a five-hour game by measurement, and ours
  sits beside ninety-odd hours of a human one. Gen 1 should be short on
  purpose. **How long it actually runs is HIS and it is not decided here.**
- Running Gen 1 through the fold as written, which section 5 says returns
  zeroes.
- Any second animal generation, any "play as an animal again" mode, any
  animal-form toggle in Gen 2. Day 16's rule holds: it has to pay off THE
  FIRST TIME, and this one pays off by ending.

## 8. CONFIDENCE
- Sections 4.1 through 4.8, every count and every quoted line: **MEASURED**
  today, in engine/, slices/ and the decoded fight. The two positive controls
  are stated because both would otherwise have produced a false finding.
- Three to five footprints, a three-to-five second deciding phase, the three
  behavioural phases, and the intensity-comparison mechanism: peer-reviewed,
  two independent papers agreeing. **HIGH.**
- Separate per-nostril samples and the rearward side-slit exhale: from the
  computational fluid dynamics work on an anatomically accurate nasal model.
  **HIGH** for the anatomy; the bilateral-comparison localisation is the
  authors' own stated interpretation, so **MEDIUM-HIGH** as a mechanism.
- Two cones, ~20/75 acuity, rod-heavy retina, reflective layer, wider field:
  veterinary-clinical consensus, uncontroversial. **HIGH.**
- Pointing comprehension in dogs versus wolves and chimpanzees: a 2002
  Science paper, heavily cited and heavily argued about. The RESULT is solid;
  the DOMESTICATION explanation for it is contested in the later literature.
  I have used only the result. **HIGH on the result, MEDIUM on the cause,
  and the design does not need the cause.**
- The eight-verb list: assembled from published control lists and action
  documentation, **MEASURED** off the documents, not remembered.
- "Where it gets boring", the two-to-six hour figure, and the repetitiveness
  criticism: review and press consensus, several outlets agreeing. **MEDIUM
  -HIGH.** One of my two searches for a design-literature treatment of the
  non-human prologue returned nothing usable; I have not padded around that.
- Sections 2, 5, 6 and 7: **MY ARGUMENT AND MY ROUTING.**
