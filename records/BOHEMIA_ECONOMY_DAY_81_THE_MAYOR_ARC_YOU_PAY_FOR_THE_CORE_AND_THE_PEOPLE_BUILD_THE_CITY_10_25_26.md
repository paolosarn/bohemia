## 0. THE ANSWER, SHORT

1. The canon already has the whole shape and it still holds: three rungs (YOUR GROUND, then THE CITY BACKS YOU,
   then THE HOUSE everybody calls the mayor), zone and the people build, road, power and water first, three
   cities across three acts, nothing built where nobody holds it. What was missing is what each building is FOR,
   what it COSTS, and what the map SHOWS. This page writes those.
2. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG: every real city that came back from the dead was mostly rebuilt by its own
   people, by hand, a room at a time, once somebody gave them the hard core (water, a roof slab, light, safety).
   Quinta Monroy: the state paid for half a house and the families built the other half themselves inside two
   years. Havana 1995: 25,000 family plots grew most of the city's vegetables. Warsaw fired new bricks out of its
   own rubble. So "a big cost to revamp a whole city" is true in HANDS AND YEARS, but most of it is never the
   player's batteries. The player pays for the CORE; his standing unlocks the PEOPLE; the people pay the rest in
   labour. Today's builder does the opposite: the player pays one battery for every single lot and the people
   build nothing ("the society builds", 7/1, has zero code).
3. A COST THAT SOUNDS RIGHT is priced in HAND-DAYS, because after the dollar dies a day of somebody's work is the
   only honest unit, and a hand-day costs about 3 batteries all in (a labourer's wage, his food, his water). Every
   building below is that many hand-days, plus TAPE for the materials, plus the days it takes, plus the rung or
   act that lets you build it at all (earned AND paid).
4. Thirty-one building kinds in ten needs plus five grand works, each with its purpose for the company, the people and the money; its
   draft cost; when it unlocks; its look in each act (rule 159's palette: act one faded and rare, colour coming back
   as the city heals); and what changes on the map.
5. THE TEN FIRST, ranked by fun and purpose: the pump house over a well, the roof on a dead house, the lookout,
   the workshop, the garden plots, the first-aid room, the wind rig, the wall and gate, the swap stall, the
   bunkhouse.

## 2. THREE PLACES THE CANON FIGHTS ITSELF, AND HOW THIS PAGE READS THEM

**(a) "Earned, not afforded" (7/31) against "the costs have to sound right, a big cost" (10/24).** Newest wins, but
10/24 did not bring back upkeep or bankruptcy; it asked for COSTS. So: EARNED AND PAID. A building needs a
permission you went and got (the rung, the act, sometimes a quest or a person who knows how) AND a one-time price
(batteries, tape, hands, days). There is still no upkeep bill and no bankruptcy (7/31 stands on that). The hole
7/31 named, "neglect has to bite", is closed by a ruling he already made: THE FUTURE GOES BOTH WAYS (9/27, raided
falls). A base you never guard gets raided; a raided lot is bare ground again (lotbuild already does this). Neglect
bites with a fight at the gate, never with a bill.

**(b) "The map is for looks; you build in the settlement screen and nowhere else" (rule 86, 10/10) against
"there's a city grid for a reason, this is meant to implement changes to the city" (10/24).** Both hold. You ACT in
one place, the settlement screen of a base you hold. The MAP SHOWS the result, as looks only: the base's picture
re-rendered with what stands (rule 128's kept scene), lit windows at night where there is power, a cleared road
drawn as road, and between acts the city's outskirts grow on the grid by how much you built (section 6). Nobody
taps the map to build; nothing on the map is a button that was not one before.

**(c) Three currencies (7/26) against the six (rule 47, 9/29).** The six are what the COMPANY carries and spends;
the 7/26 three are what a BUILDING makes. They are the same stuff grouped two ways: RESOURCES are food, water and
tape (his 7/28 icon: an apple, a hammer, duct tape); ELECTRICITY is batteries; CLOUT is standing, which nobody can
buy. So a building is priced in the six and pays out in one of the three. Day 80 (the currency page) carries the
bar's half of this.

## 3. THE UNIT OF COST: THE HAND-DAY

After money dies, the honest price of a building is how many days of somebody's work it eats, plus what it is
made of. So every price on this page starts as HAND-DAYS and turns into batteries at one rate.

**What a hand-day costs, from what main already says:**
- His exchange, 10 crowns to 1 battery (reference/library/grok/GROK_31_TEN_TO_ONE_2026_10_01.md).
- A labourer's wage: Battle Brothers' daytaler 6 crowns a day, miner 6, mason 8, farmhand 10, all one battery a
  day at the floor (reference/library/grok/GROK_34_WAGE_BATTERIES_2026_10_01.md).
- He eats and drinks: one food and one water a man a day at the ration counter, 42 batteries a quiet plains week
  for three (records/BOHEMIA_ECONOMY_DAY_72_A_WEEK_AT_THE_COUNTER_IS_42_AND_EVERY_PURSE_ON_MAIN_IS_HARDER_THAN_BATTLE_BROTHERS_10_18_26.md).
- So ONE HAND-DAY = 1 battery wage + 1 food + 1 water = **about 3 batteries** bought at the counter (about 2 at the
  sack). Your own crew building costs no wage but still eats and drinks (2 a day) and is not out earning.

**What it is made of: TAPE.** Rule 47 makes tape the repair stack (Battle Brothers' tools); his 7/28 icon puts a
hammer and duct tape in the same mark. So tape is the materials line: salvaged lumber, sheet metal, wire, pipe,
fasteners, all one count. One tape point is worth about 2 batteries at the counter (Battle Brothers' tools are worth
200 crowns a stack, 20 batteries before the town's markup, GROK_31; the stack size is the library's, so the 2 is a
draft for TUNING). Salvage on the road and the scrap trade fill it.

**How long: DAYS ON THE MAP.** A building takes calendar days because only so many hands fit on a lot. Days pass
on the map clock, so a building rises while the company is out on a job.

**What the company earns, so the prices can be judged:** the board pays 30, 60 or 90 a job by the star today, and
the pay that holds a company of twelve is about 207 to 282 a won routine job
(records/BOHEMIA_ECONOMY_DAY_79_THE_GRIDS_MONEY_10_19_26.md). So a 40-battery building is a good week's work for a
small crew; a 1,000-battery work is a season for a big company; anything in the tens of thousands is a generation
and the city's labour, not one purse.

## 4. THE LADDER AS PLAY (the 6/30 rungs in today's game)

The rungs read off things the game already keeps: the fourteen home bases and who holds them
(engine/bohemia_homebases.js), standing with the factions (at least twelve, the 9/29 bestiary law's floor), the act, and the
families you housed. No new meter. The word "mayor" stays what people call you, never a title on a screen (6/30).

**RUNG 1, YOUR GROUND (act one, from the first base).** You hold a home base: the one your origin starts in, or
one you take. Inside its settlement screen you build on its lots, with your own crew's hands or hired ones, on the
company's batteries. Sixteen small kinds are open here, 8 to 50 batteries each hired (section 5). This is the
Battle Brothers company with a yard: water, food, a roof, a bench, a wall. What changes: the crew stops buying
water and food at the counter, gear gets fixed at home, a family moves into the house you roofed.

**RUNG 2, THE CITY BACKS YOU (act one, late, and act two).** His number: about half the factions friendly (6/30,
"about 49%"; six of twelve). What it unlocks is the thing the real record says rebuilds cities: **the people build.**
You stop paying for every room. You pay for a CORE (a pump, a half house, a fenced lot) and the families do the
rest with their own days: the half house doubles itself, the allotments feed the block, the housed families lend
hands to your works (a housed family is one free hand-day a day on your lots, a draft). Seven more kinds open,
32 to 102 batteries hired and far less with the people's hands; in act two eight bigger works, 170 to 1,300. You
may build in a base whose local faction is only cool to you, because the city has your back (6/30).

**RUNG 3, THE HOUSE (act two, late, and act three).** Enough of the city defers to your family that its works are
yours to call: the five grand works (section 5), each a generation's job, paid half in your batteries (the parts
nobody can make by hand) and half in the city's own labour (thousands of the people's hand-days). Nobody pays you a
tax; there is no office (6/30, the Texas lesson). People work on the pipe because the pipe is theirs too. A rung-3
discount, a draft: parts a quarter cheaper (traders come to you) and days halved (more hands).

**FALLING BACK DOWN (6/30 left it open; the default here).** A rung is held, not owned. Standing falls (a betrayal,
a faction turned) and the rung drops with it; a base raided and ruined is bare ground again for the act (lotbuild
already clears its lots, and 9/27's "raided falls" is the canon). That is how neglect bites, with no upkeep bill.

**ACROSS THE THREE ACTS (rule 31, the flip).** Each act holds its own rung: act one starts at rung 1, act two
starts at the rung act one ended on, act three at act two's. What you built in act one stands in act two (his
6/30 lean, "most things stay"), weathered by the era's dials (rule 128), and the city's growth between acts is
computed from it (section 6). The father's book shows his city (6/30).

Ten needs, in the order a dead city needs them: WATER, FOOD, POWER, SHELTER, SAFETY, HEALTH, TRADE, MAKE, MOVE,
BELONGING. The eight kinds already built on main (engine/bohemia_lotbuild.js, all one battery and one day today)
are marked. **HIRED** is the price with every hand hired; **OWN HANDS** is the price when your crew or the people put

banks/BOHEMIA_ECONOMY_THE_MAYOR_ARC_BUILDINGS_10_25_26.json, draft:true, never read by the game).

**Who may build what:** a kind needs its act AND its rung (earned), then its price (paid). A few need a person who
knows how, which ties the catalog to hiring: the water tower and the flats want a mason, the block grid an
electrician, the clinic a doctor (Battle Brothers' anatomist or monk), the station a rail man.

## 6. WHAT CHANGES ON THE MAP WHEN YOU BUILD

Rule 86 (the map is for looks) and rule 161 (the grid is where change shows) together: nothing on the map becomes a
button; everything you build SHOWS.

1. **The base's picture changes the same day.** A home base on the map is re-rendered from its kept scene (rule 128)
   with what stands on its lots: a tarp roof becomes a tin roof, a water tank appears on its stand, a lookout
   breaks the roofline. A LITTLE THING FROM Stardew Valley (rule 133 names it as the bar): the building rises
   over its days, scaffold first, so you see it coming.
2. **Night tells the truth.** Windows light where you have power (the solar rack, the wind rig, the grid), so at
   night a base you invested in glows and a dead block stays dark (the analog-horror dark is the default; light is
   earned).
3. **Lived-in signs.** Laundry on a roofed house, smoke from the workshop, a queue at the standpipe, people walking
   between your buildings (the settlement walkers already exist).
4. **A cleared road is drawn as road**, and travel on it is faster (roads faster than dirt, the 9/24 overworld law).
5. **The lookout lifts the fog farther round your base** (rule 155: the fog of war).
6. **Between acts the city grows from the outskirts, by how you helped it** (his numbers, rule 164's "for later":
   about 17% bigger in act two, about 33% in act three). The default: the growth is his percent times how much of a
   target you built, capped at his percent. Housing and water weigh most, because the real record says people
   come back where water, a roof and light come back first (New Orleans: 77% of households back four years on where
   the money reached them; a third in the Lower Ninth). A city nobody helped does not grow and may shrink (the
   future goes both ways, 9/27).
7. **Colour comes back with it.** Act one's palette is rule 159's (faded, dirty, rare, colour as an accent); the
   blocks you rebuilt get their colour back first in act two, the rest of the city in act three. So the colour on
   the map is a picture of what you did.
8. **A raided base draws as a ruin** for the rest of the act (6/30: what happens stays).

## 8. WHY THESE COSTS SOUND RIGHT: THE REAL RECORD

**The ratios are the real world's.** Prices are set so that what is dear in the real record is dear here, measured
against the pump house over a well (38 batteries hired), the cheapest real thing with a solid price:

| real thing | real price | ratio to a well | ours | ratio to the pump |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a hand-pump borehole, 222 to 1,080 people (charity: water project records, 2009 to 2011) | $3,100 to $3,400 | 1 | pump house, 38 | 1 |
| a core half house with land and services (Quinta Monroy, Chile, 2004) | $7,500 | 2.3 | the half house, 102 | 2.7 |
| a rural health post (Nepal, 2009, with the community's share) | $28,539 | 8.6 | the clinic, 300 | 7.9 |
| a family solar kit with lights and phone charging (UN-Habitat 2014; M-Kopa 2016) | $150 to $217 | 0.06 | the solar rack, 34 | 0.9 |
| tearing down one dead house (Detroit, 2014 to 2016) | $10,000 to $16,400 | 3 to 5 | a cleared road, 36 | 0.9 |
| a 53-shop market restored (Aleppo, al-Saqatiyah, 2019, eight months) | $400,000 | 121 | the swap meet, 46 (a parking lot, not a stone souk) | 1.2 |
| a solar-hybrid mini-grid (ESMAP 2022: about $3,900 a kW; 217,000 grids for $127 billion) | about $585,000 each | about 177 | the block grid, 960 | 25 |
| short-line rail rehabilitation, per mile (US CRISI projects) | $240,000 to $1,100,000 | 73 to 333 | the station 1,300; the line 25,000 | 34; 658 |

Where ours breaks the ratio, it breaks it on purpose and says why: the solar rack is dearer than the real kit
(a panel that makes money must not be a printing press, section 9); the big works are cheaper than the real ones
by about seven times, because the people's hands pay the rest at rung 2 and above and an act is only a year
(rule 84); the swap meet is act one's parking lot, and the souk's price is act three's market hall.

**The rebuilding is the people's, and the record says so every time.**
- **Quinta Monroy (Chile):** the state's $7,500 bought land, services and half a house (30 square metres: the wet
  room, the kitchen, the stair, the walls); 93 to 100 families built the other half themselves, about $750 each in
  two years, doubling their homes to an estimated $20,000 each.
- **Havana in the Special Period:** by 1995 about 25,000 family and small-group plots grew most of the city's fruit
  and vegetables; the urban sector grew 800,000 tonnes in 1999; the Alamar garden, 180 members, grows about 300
  tonnes a year.
- **Warsaw:** preparatory work 1945 to 1947, most of the Old Town rebuilt 1947 to 1951, opened 1953; kilns outside
  the city fired new bricks from the Old Town's own rubble (22 million cubic metres of it, by one curator's count).
- **Germany:** about 400 million cubic metres of rubble; crews salvaged bricks, beams and fittings and piled the rest
  into hills. (The famous rubble women are partly a myth: about 60,000 in Berlin, a small share of the women.)
- **Argentina after 2001:** workers restarted their own closed factories, about 130 by 2004 (10,000 workers), 300 to
  350 later.
- **New Orleans after Katrina:** $9 billion of Road Home grants to more than 130,000 residents; 77% of households
  back four years on; the poorest areas paid 30% of rebuilding themselves against 20% in the richest; a third of the
  Lower Ninth Ward came back.
- **Detroit:** a demolition cost $8,500 to $16,400 a house; Day 51 measured that the city reclaimed about 7 of 139
  square miles (records/BOHEMIA_ECONOMY_DAY_51_THE_TIERS_ARE_ALREADY_WRITTEN_AND_SEVEN_SQUARE_MILES_OF_ONE_HUNDRED_AND_THIRTY_NINE_9_24_26.md).
- **William Kamkwamba (Malawi, about 2002):** a 5-metre windmill from a broken bicycle, a tractor fan blade and blue
  gum poles, about 2,200 kwacha of parts, ran four bulbs and charged the neighbours' phones.

The life lesson underneath, never preached: **you cannot buy a city; you can only give people a reason to build
it.** That is also the 6/30 law's own line: legitimate power after collapse is earned by being the one who fed,
built and defended the city until everyone just looks to you.

## 9. THE FINDING, MEASURED AGAINST MAIN

The home-base builder that exists (engine/bohemia_lotbuild.js, LIFE+CITY 9/29; WHOLE GAME status THIN) was read
against this page:

1. **Every kind costs the same: one battery and one day** (`COST = { currency: 'electricity', amount: 1 }`,
   `DAYS = 1`, from EVERYTHING COSTS ONE). A wall costs what a solar panel costs. Nothing sounds right because
   nothing differs.
2. **The solar panel pays itself back in ONE day.** Its yield is the power building's ruled amount
   (engine/bohemia_powerbuild.js, `AMOUNT = 1` a day) and its price is one battery. Four lots of panels (a place has
   four lots, `BUILD_LOTS = 4` in the settlement screen) make 4 batteries a day for 4 spent once: 28 a week, two
   thirds of the 42 a quiet week costs a crew of three (Day 72). That is the farmable money the 9/5 law warned about
   ("a currency anyone can farm is the oldest way money dies"). The real kit pays back in about a year; this page's
   draft rack (34 batteries) pays back in about a month.
3. **The people build nothing.** No kind is built by anyone but the player; there is no zone, no core, no family
   that finishes its own half. The 7/1 law's "the society builds" has no code, and it is the one thing the real
   record says matters most.
4. **Four lots and eight kinds is a yard, not a city.** That is right for rung 1 and the first hours; the other two
   rungs and the act-two works do not exist.
5. **What already works and this page builds on:** the eight kinds each house a family or make one of the three
   (7/26), each names an existing picture, each is recorded in the century ledger so the next act counts it, and a
   raid that takes the base clears its lots. The skeleton is right; the prices, the people and the rungs are missing.

## 10. LITTLE THINGS FROM OTHER GAMES (rule 139, each marked)

- **Pocket City 2** (the named city-builder base, 7/1): unlocks come from levels and quests, never from money alone,
  which is why this page keeps "earned AND paid"; the road connects everything, which is why the cleared road is a
  building.
- **A LITTLE THING FROM Stardew Valley** (rule 133 names it as the bar): the abandoned community centre is rebuilt
  room by room by bringing bundles of goods (six rooms, thirty bundles), and each finished room repairs something
  you can see in town; a building from the carpenter costs money AND materials (a coop is 4,000 gold, 300 wood, 100
  stone) and rises over two or three days. Ours: a grand work is a set of deliveries you watch the city answer.
- **Fallout 4** (he named it on 7/1: "kinda like Fallout 4 but the society does it"): its settlements made the
  player place every wall, and players called it a chore; his correction is the people build, which is this page's
  rung 2.
- **Valheim** (named 7/31): comfort around where you rest makes resting worth more; ours: a base with a bunkhouse,
  a cool room and a lit room heals and feeds better than the road camp.
- **Battle Brothers** (the campaign's model): no building, but every settlement makes goods through the places
  attached to it, and those decide its prices (Day 68: buy x1.0 sell x0.15 where it is made). A base you build is
  your own settlement with attached places: build the workshop and tape is cheap at home.
