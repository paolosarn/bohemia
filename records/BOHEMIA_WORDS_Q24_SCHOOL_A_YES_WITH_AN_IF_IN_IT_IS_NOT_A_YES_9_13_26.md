## THE SHORT VERSION
**There are two different speech acts wearing the same word. Agreeing with somebody
is fast and bare, and real recorded speech measures it at 1.75 words. CLOSING A DEAL
is neither, and two hard doctrines say why: aviation made repeating the instruction
back mandatory because "roger" hides a misunderstanding, and contract law says an
acceptance that changes a term is not an acceptance at all. "I'll take it if it's
back by dark" does not accept the job. It re-opens it. "I'll take it. Back by dark?"
accepts it and asks. Same words, different deal, and a button has to know which one
it is.**

## 1. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG, AND IT IS A RAZOR
The instinct for a characterful yes in a hard valley is to give it a condition:
*I'll take it if it's back by dark.* That sounds like somebody protecting himself,
and it is the version I would have written.

**Under the common law's mirror image rule it is not an acceptance.** Acceptance must
be an unconditional replica of the offer, and a reply that purports to accept while
being conditional on additional or different terms **is a counter-offer**. The
textbook example is exactly our sentence's shape: *"I accept, if you include new
tires"* is a counter-offer.

And the doctrine draws the line in the one place a writer can act on:

- **"I accept, if you include new tires."** -> a counter-offer. The deal is back open.
- **"I accept. Could you include new tires?"** -> an acceptance plus a non-binding
  request. The deal is closed at the original terms, and the question rides along.

**The condition INSIDE the yes re-opens the negotiation. The condition BESIDE the yes
closes it.** That is not a legal technicality dressed up as craft: it is the
difference between a character who has taken the job and a character who is still
haggling, and the player pressing a button needs it to be the first one.

(The mirror image rule is common law; sales of goods under the UCC relax it. The
distinction between a condition and a request survives either way, and that is the
part this lane is using.)

## 2. WHAT THEY SAY INSTEAD OF YES: THEY SAY THE TERMS BACK
Aviation is the place where this was studied hardest, because people died of it.
**A readback is the verbatim repetition of the instruction back to the controller**,
and pilots are required to read back the safety-related part of all communications.
The reason it exists is the finding: **"roger" and "wilco" do not verify
understanding.** Reading the numbers back is a double check that catches a number
misheard or a number wrong.

**And the error rates are the part worth staring at.** In an FAA study of message
complexity, of the readback errors that occurred, **only 1.74% of omissions, 18.83%
of substitutions and 21.05% of transpositions were corrected** by the controller
listening. Most errors survived a system built entirely to catch them. An
uncorrected wrong readback is called a hear-back error and shows up later as an
aircraft somewhere it should not be.

**The writing rule falls out of it: the acceptance that means something contains the
terms.** Not "yes", not "roger", not "I'll take it". The number, the time, the place,
said back.

**AND PEOPLE ALREADY WROTE ONE WITHOUT THE RESEARCH.** The confirmation line on the
card is *"You took it. It is yours until the day is out."* That is a readback: it
says the duration back. It is the strongest of the two attempts and round two should
build on it rather than replace it.

## 3. WHAT THEY ADD ABOUT THE PRICE
Day-labour ethnography describes the corner deal as a negotiation over **price,
skills, location and duration**, struck in the few minutes before the worker gets in
the vehicle, and it notes that the worker is free to accept or decline and **free to
walk off the job**.

Two things follow for a valley with no law (Q20) and no enforcement (Q21):
- **The acceptance is not binding, so the words are not what makes it real.** What
  makes it real is that either side can leave. So an acceptance that swears loyalty
  is writing a contract nobody can enforce; an acceptance that names the first
  checkpoint is writing the thing that actually governs.
- **Four things can be read back, and which one a person picks IS the
  characterisation.** The man who reads back the price and the man who reads back the
  time and the man who reads back where it has to be left are three different people
  taking the same job.

## 4. OUR OWN BUILD, MEASURED
**4.1 THE BASELINE, FROM REAL RECORDED SPEECH.** From this lane's Q21 work on the
199,740 dialogue-act-tagged turns: **ACCEPT, n=11,123, mean 1.75 words, prefaced
12.7%.** Social agreement really is fast and bare, and any instinct to make every yes
in the game weighty is arguing with that.

**4.2 OUR OWN ACCEPTANCES ARE ALL THE SOCIAL KIND.** 45 lines of 3,093 open by
accepting. Read by hand: "Alright. It was a fall." "I will bring him home." "Fine.
Your block gets the light." These are people agreeing inside a scene, and they are
good.
- **Only 4 of the 45 restate any term of a deal.**
- **Only 1 carries an "if" at all.**
- **The best one in the set is already a price clause attached to a yes:** *"I'll
  take it for what it's worth to me, not what it's worth to you."*

**So the commercial acceptance is nearly absent from this game, exactly the way the
refusal was when Q21 measured it.** We have 3,093 lines of people agreeing with each
other and almost nobody closing a deal.

**4.3 AND A SEPARATE THING, NAMED SO NOBODY CHASES IT AS MINE.** The stranger's walk
lists a dead row on the day card, *"Half of it now, before I go"*. That is a haggle
row, not the take row this row is about, and whether either works is a matter for the
glass and not for this record. I have not walked the demo and I am not reporting on

## 5. WHAT SCHOOL LEAVES ME HOLDING FOR ROUND TWO
1. **A CONDITION INSIDE THE YES RE-OPENS THE DEAL; BESIDE IT, IT CLOSES.** The button
   must be the closing kind. Any "if" belongs in a separate sentence.
2. **THE ACCEPTANCE THAT MEANS SOMETHING IS A READBACK**, because bare acknowledgement
   hides misunderstanding.
3. **WHICH OF THE FOUR TERMS HE READS BACK IS THE CHARACTER**: price, skill, place,
   or time.
4. **NOTHING BINDS**, so an acceptance that promises loyalty is writing an
   unenforceable contract; one that names the first checkpoint is writing the real one.
5. **SOCIAL YES STAYS FAST AND BARE**, 1.75 words, and our 45 existing lines are the
   right register for that and must not be "improved" into deal talk.
6. **AND PEOPLE'S CONFIRMATION LINE IS ALREADY A READBACK** and should be built on.
