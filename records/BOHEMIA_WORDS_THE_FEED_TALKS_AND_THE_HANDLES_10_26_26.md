## 2. THE RESEARCH (217c asks for it first; the thinness is said where it is thin)
HANDLES
- When a clean name is taken, people add digits, usually two or four, and those digits look like a birth
  year or a date (arXiv 1101.5578, "How Unique and Traceable are Usernames?"). A study of game names found
  numbers in a name often gave the birth year (reported by Inverse; the authors were not named, so it is
  a lead, not a fact).
- People keep their first handle for decades, out of nostalgia: AOL's peak (about 35 million subscribers,
  several screen names each) left more than 100 million names, and adults still log in with them (Daily
  Beast; opinion and anecdote, the numbers unverified).
- Throwaway accounts: about 96 in 100 are used three times or fewer; far more common where people talk
  about something they will not sign (Reddit studies, Guo and Caine 2025; a mental-health study at about
  six times the base rate). From abstracts only.
- The crypto era's marks were profile pictures and pledges (laser eyes from 16 Feb 2021, "until $100K",
  fading by June as the price fell: Know Your Meme, Decrypt). The search found nothing on ".eth" names,
  so the ".eth" fossil here is our reading, marked ours.
- Accounts keep posting after their owners die: estimates of 5 to 300 million profiles of the dead,
  some updating automatically (VOA 2017); 205,068 comments on 1,369 MySpace profiles of the dead
  (Brubaker and Hayes 2011). The search found no study of bots on Twitter after death; the bots here
  are grounded in the automatic-posting finding, not in a measured Twitter fact.
- A meme is a group of items that share content, form or stance, made with awareness of each other, and
  imitated and transformed by many (Shifman, Memes in Digital Culture, MIT Press 2013/14).
- Nextdoor makes people post under the name they use and their legal surname, address-verified (a 2024
  court filing). So a neighbourhood feed has two kinds of name: the person and the page.
- The 702 is Las Vegas identity (thin, commercial sources; kept to one handle, bigken702_smoker).
FEEDS IN A CRASH
- Argentina 2001: neighbourhood barter clubs, the Red Global de Trueque, their own credits; from about
  400,000 people in 800 clubs (April 2001) to an estimated 2.5 million users 2001 to 2003; credits bought
  food and bus rides; then counterfeiting and collapse (CS Monitor, Ambito, Wikipedia mirror; the counts
  disagree). Here: the swap meet at the drive-in, "no money, only trades".
- Venezuela: scarce medicine and food found through social networks, posts offering a swap or asking for
  one blood-pressure drug by name; resellers working off informants and apps (Americas Quarterly,
  InSight Crime, Local10). Here: "ISO insulin. any kind. it's for my sister."
- Lebanon 2019 on: the black-market rate was posted on exchangers' apps and broadcast by single changers
  on WhatsApp, moving hour by hour (AP via KTVZ, Rest of World 2022). The search did not confirm daily
  group posts. Here: @nobodysgas posting every price he hears.
- Scanner pages relay the radio in real time; some post names and plates, police warn them, rumours take
  root (a triple homicide in Bangor that never happened), some pages ban names and house numbers, admins
  burn out (Bangor Daily News 2021, SunLive NZ). Here: @valleyscanner702's "no names, no house numbers on
  this page. we lost a man to a rumor once."

## 3. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG
**All eight of our handles were clean words with no digits and no trade but one.** In a valley of about
two million people, every clean name was taken decades before the crash; the research says the digits
are what a person adds. So a clean handle in this valley does not read as a neighbour, it reads as an
outfit or a bot. That is now the rule, and it is the one a reader can learn without being told:
**people carry digits and their trade; pages carry a clean name with their faction's word in it; the
bots carry a brand.** The eight old handles stay (REUSE-FIRST): they are the early accounts, made when
short names were still free, and each now has a person behind it (Benny Ortiz the lineman is @thecircuit).
