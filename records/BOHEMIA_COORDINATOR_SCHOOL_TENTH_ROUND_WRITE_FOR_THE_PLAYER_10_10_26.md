# BOHEMIA COORDINATOR SCHOOL, ROUND TEN OF TEN: WRITE FOR THE PLAYER (10/10/26)

## THE ANGLE
A bandleader's craft is knowing each hand's sound and writing so that hand can play it. Ellington wrote the growl for Cootie and the glissando for Hodges; Miles handed Kind of Blue over as sketches because he had chosen players who did not need charts; Blakey built young players and pushed them out to lead. The conductor spends rehearsal minutes on the weak section and trusts the principal in the strong one. The coordinator writes one kind of row to 26 lanes and judges them by one rule, as if they were interchangeable. Ten sweeps of record say they are men with sounds, and nobody has written one down.

## WHAT THAT WORLD KNOWS
1. Ellington: "You can't write music right unless you know how the man that'll play it plays poker." He wrote "Jeep's Blues" for Hodges and "Concerto for Cootie" for Williams; his aim, in his words, was to mold the music around the man. (https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Duke_Ellington ; https://www.mosaicrecords.com/the-great-jazz-artists/duke-ellington/) Bites here: no row on this board was ever written around what one lane has proven it can do.
2. Strayhorn's 1955 Ellington Effect: each member of the band is "a distinctive tone color and set of emotions," mixed with the others "to produce a third thing." (https://explorepahistory.com/odocument.php%3FdocId=1-4-89.html) Bites here: the board's output is 26 particular sounds, not 26 copies of one worker.
3. At Hodges's funeral in 1970: "Johnny is not replaceable. Because of this great loss our band will never sound the same." (https://jazz.com/encyclopedia/hodges-johnny-john-cornelius) Bites here: when COOK FOUR or EYES is on hold, their jobs do not pass to the next lane at par; the coordinator plans as if they do.
4. Kind of Blue: Davis gave the band "sketches of scales and melody lines" and almost no rehearsal (Bill Evans's liner notes). But "All Blues" had grown over six months of gigs, "So What" had been played live, and only "Flamenco Sketches" was a first take. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kind_of_Blue ; https://www.counterpunch.org/2009/08/14/kind-of-blue-at-fifty-stalked-by-bill-evans-ghost/) Bites here: the sketch works on a player with a proven record and fails on a stranger; the coordinator gives everyone the same row.
5. Miles to Hancock: "Don't play the butter notes." Hancock worked out it meant the obvious notes and left them out; Miles never explained. (https://jazzfuel.com/dont-play-the-butter-notes-herbie/) Bites here: a one-line correction to a strong lane beats a paragraph of spec.
6. Hancock on Miles: "I pay you to practice on the bandstand in front of the people." Tony Williams was hired at 17. (https://www.pbs.org/wnet/americanmasters/four-lessons-in-innovation-from-jazz-great-miles-davis/13460 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Williams_(drummer)) Bites here: a lane proves itself on the real surface, not in a page nobody plays.
7. "Blakey's University" graduated Byrd, Shorter and Marsalis; Blakey wanted them to "get out of here and form your own group." Betty Carter's Jazz Ahead did the same. (https://www.npr.org/transcripts/769311659 ; https://www.arts.gov/honors/jazz/betty-carter) Bites here: TUNING, MODS and QUESTS write pages that never leave the band; a finding graduates only when it lands as a named row on a building lane.
8. Goldin and Rouse 2000: women were 5 percent of the top five US orchestras in 1970 and about 25 percent by the late 1990s; the screen raised a woman's chance of advancing from preliminary rounds by about 50 percent and explains 30 to 55 percent of the rise in female new hires, with large standard errors. (https://www.nber.org/digest/jul97/orchestrating-impartiality ; https://repec-prod.princeton.edu/econlib/RePEc/pri/papers/728) Bites here: judge the work with the lane name hidden, or the judge sees the name and not the work.
9. Alsop, Chen and Slatkin on rehearsal: the skill is triage, knowing which problems "warrant the use of rehearsal time"; Slatkin lets some go. (https://www.jordanrsmith.com/blog/2024/7/18/rehearsal-technique-chen-alsop-slatkin) Bites here: the coordinator's minutes go to the weak section; the strong one gets a sketch and trust.
10. Belichick's scouting guide says the defense "doesn't work if players are freelancing"; Steve Kerr recalls Phil Jackson saying he ran the triangle "for the rest of the team," not for Jordan and Pippen; Popovich made Ginobili a sixth man for his aggressiveness with the second unit. (https://www.bostonglobe.com/2020/04/20/sports/old-scouting-guide-gives-us-fascinating-insight-into-bill-belichicks-defensive-philosophy/ ; https://readjack.substack.com/p/inside-the-triangle-what-players ; https://www.pressreader.com/usa/austin-american-statesman/20141128/282368332961351) Bites here: the system is shaped to the hands you have, and a role is set by what the man does.
11. Fit travels badly: NFL players who changed teams dropped and only "stabilized after a year"; punters, whose skill is not team-specific, showed no change. (https://ilp.mit.edu/node/13154) Bites here: a move costs a round of fit; move once, on proof, then leave it.
12. Hackman: "cookie-cutter formulas and prescribed leadership styles often backfire." Belbin's role test did not predict performance in Jackson (2002). (https://leadershipnow.com/leadershop/1333-2.html ; https://people.freebsd.org/~dch/pub/belbin/belbin.md) Bites here: do not type a lane with a label; write what it shipped.

## WHAT THE COORDINATOR DOES NOT DO
- It does not write a lane's sound down. Ten sweeps say EYES finds what nobody else sees and PLUMBER builds honest instruments; no line on the board says so, so no row is aimed at it.
- It does not move a job. The load problem sat with RUN while RUN and EYES posted 22 s and 48 s for one thing, and PLUMBER, who found the walk pressed against a wall, had already measured 85 s. The honest number belonged to PLUMBER on day one.
- It does not read a held claim as a sound. CHARACTER held two claims for weeks; the fourth version is the tell to stop, and the same row kept going to the same lane.
- It does not shorten the row for a proven lane or lengthen it for an unproven one. COOK FOUR passed the art director and gets the same brief as a lane on hold.
- It judges with the name showing. The registry files an item under its lane before the judge sees the pixels.

## THE ONE CHANGE
THE SOUND LINE. Every lane's section in the meeting hall gets one line under MODE and STATE, written and owned by the coordinator:
SOUND: <what this lane has shipped well, six to twelve words> | proof <sha or record> | tested <date> | not yet: <one thing it has not proven>.
Example: "SOUND: finds the break a stranger meets, on the glass, before anyone | proof 1bdc7023 | tested 10/9 | not yet: a fix." It is rewritten only from a SHIPPED line or a blind verdict, never from a promise.

THE RULE FOR SHAPING A ROW. The coordinator reads the SOUND line before writing. Inside the sound, the row is a SKETCH: the two-word label, Paolo's words, the ship test, nothing else; the lane picks the notes. Outside the sound, the row is a CHART: the full brief, the instrument, the first measurement, a check-in after one round. A lane with "not yet: a fix" never gets a fix as a sketch.

THE WORDS FOR MOVING A JOB. One line on both sections: "[two words] MOVED 10/10 to <LANE>: its SOUND is <quote>, proof <sha>. <OLD LANE> keeps <the half it has proven>." A move happens once, on proof, and the job then runs a full round before anyone judges it, because fit costs a round.

THE AUDITION BEHIND THE SCREEN. In the COOK registry a new item carries a blind number and no lane name until EYES posts PASS or BACK, one line, on the before-and-after at 1:1 against the item's one question. Only then does the registry show the lane. A lane whose blind pass rate beats the board for three rounds is a section principal and gets sketches; one below it for three rounds gets charts.

## ONE RULE
WRITE FOR THE MAN, NOT THE INSTRUMENT. Every row is shaped to what that lane has actually shipped well, that record is written on one line where everyone can read it, and a job goes to the lane whose line fits it.
