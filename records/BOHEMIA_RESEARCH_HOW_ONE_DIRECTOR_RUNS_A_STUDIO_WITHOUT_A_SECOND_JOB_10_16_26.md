# RESEARCH: HOW ONE DIRECTOR RUNS A STUDIO WITHOUT A SECOND JOB
# (10/16/26, coordinator research; Paolo's words: "there's almost 30 chats, I cannot
# individually ever enter them again, I'll never get nothing done, I can't be having
# an extra part-time job to do this.")

## THE QUESTION
One human creative director fronts 26 AI lane chats that act as departments. He will
read ONE coordinator chat and tap thumbs in ONE vote tab. Nothing else. So the
coordinator has to become what a real studio calls the producer, the studio head, and
the art director's filter, all at once. How do real creative directors and studio heads
run big multi-department teams without doing every review themselves? This file is the
answer, with sources, and it ends with the operating model we run from now.

A note on sources. The web fetch tool in this session could not open pages (DNS and

excerpts of the named articles, not on the full text. Where a source is a secondary
summary (a book summary, a news write-up of an interview), it says so. Years are the
publication years of the pages cited.

## PART 1. HOW THE FAMOUS DIRECTORS ACTUALLY REVIEW

### Pixar: dailies every morning, Braintrust every few months, and the director keeps the pen
- Dailies: the artists on a film meet at the start of the day and show work in
  progress, and the director gives feedback in the room. Everything shown is
  unfinished on purpose. Catmull's argument is that when everyone shows ugly work
  every day, people get over the embarrassment and get more creative. (Shortform
  summary of Creativity, Inc., https://www.shortform.com/blog/pixar-dailies/ , 2014
  book; Catmull quote via https://intenseminimalism.com/2011/and-when-you-get-over-the-embarrassment-youre-more-creative-ed-catmull-pixar/ , 2011)
- Who sits in dailies: the film's DIRECTOR. On Inside Out, Pete Docter watched shots
  for several days before approving them. (https://hapamama.com/getting-animated-with-pixar-inside-out/ , 2015)
- The filter under the director: a Pixar animator's advice is that before you pitch a
  change, you take it to your supervising animator first, who decides whether it is
  worth the director's time. (Animation Mentor newsletter,
  https://newsletters.animationmentor.com/newsletter/0909/feature_geek.html , 2009)
- Braintrust: a group of other directors sees the whole film every few months. It has
  NO power to mandate anything. Catmull: "Giving the Braintrust no power to mandate
  solutions affects the dynamics of the group in ways that I believe are essential."
  After the meeting, the director alone decides what to do with the notes.
  (GovExec summary of the Fast Company excerpt,
  https://govexec.com/management/2014/04/how-monsters-inc-met-priority-goals/82603 , 2014;
  Fast Company excerpt, https://www.fastcompany.com/3027135/inside-the-pixar-braintrust , 2014)
- Braintrust notes name the PROBLEM, not the fix: "The Braintrust's notes, then, are
  intended to bring the true causes of problems to the surface, not to demand a
  specific remedy." If a fix is offered, it is only to illustrate, never to prescribe.
  (Creativity, Inc. passages quoted at https://christopherming.com/?p=4095 , 2014 book;
  BYU Design Review, https://www.designreview.byu.edu/collections/making-products-that-dont-suck , n.d.)
- Catmull's HBR article (2008) describes the whole method as three things: give
  creative authority to the project leaders, build a culture where peers show
  unfinished work, and break walls between disciplines.
  (https://store.hbr.org/product/how-pixar-fosters-collective-creativity/R0809D , 2008)

THE LESSON FOR US: at Pixar there are TWO review loops. The director is in the room
every day with WIP. The studio-level reviewers (Braintrust, the president) see the
whole thing every few months and only point at problems. Paolo is not the Pixar
director. He is the Braintrust and the studio head. The coordinator is the director
who sits in dailies.

### Film studios: dailies for the director, curated dailies for the studio, test screenings for the money
- Dailies (rushes) are raw footage reviewed every day by the director, DP and
  producers to catch problems while reshoots are still possible. Studio and network
  people "may receive curated dailies": a selection, not the whole pile.
  (https://filmdaft.com/what-are-dailies-in-film-definition/ , 2024;
  https://fast.io/resources/film-dailies/ , 2024)
- Executives and financiers watch dailies "not engaged, day-by-day, in production but
  who must have assurances that the project is meeting their respective expectations."
  (Wikipedia, Dailies, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dailies , accessed 2026)
- About 90 percent of widely released studio films are test-screened, the average
  film about three times; the studio reads the audience, then gives notes.
  (Wikipedia, Test screening, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_screening , accessed 2026)

THE LESSON: the studio does not watch every take. It watches a curated cut on a
schedule, and it reads the audience. The director watches every take.

### FromSoftware: Miyazaki directs 95 percent of his time, and scaling meant teaching leads his LOGIC
- "About 95% of what I do at the company and how I spend my time is directed towards
  making games and directing games," despite being president.
  (VGC on the Guardian interview, https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/miyazaki-spends-95-of-his-time-directing-games-despite-fromsoftware-president-title/ , 2022)
- On Elden Ring he had to hand off more than usual, so "he took care to not just tell
  others how things should be concluded, but also share his logic, philosophies, and
  thought processes as well." (Weekly Famitsu interview via Gamebanshee,
  https://www.gamebanshee.com/xhhc6 , 2022)
- He said the studio could make its biggest game because there are "more excellent
  members that can be entrusted with various things." (same source, 2022)
- Later he said there is a "high possibility" he hands the director chair to others
  on future projects and gives them "full direction and full control."
  (IGN Nordic via Yahoo, https://tech.yahoo.com/general/articles/fromsoftware-soulslike-games-unlikely-directed-124548877.html , 2024)

THE LESSON: the way a single-taste director scales is not more reviews. It is writing
down WHY he decides what he decides, so the leads decide the same way without him.
That is what our laws folder is for, and why every ruling needs the reason attached.

### Nintendo: Miyamoto as PRODUCER, reviewing the screen at milestones, and retiring the table-flip
- The term: "upending the tea table" (chabudai gaeshi) is a late-stage reset by
  Miyamoto that can throw away months of work. Aonuma calls it the "Miyamoto Test."
  (https://zeldadungeon.net/wiki/Upending_the_Tea_Table , n.d.)
- Documented cases: Twilight Princess (opening village extended after localization
  had started), Super Mario Galaxy, Hyrule Warriors, New 3DS 3D.
  (Iwata Asks, https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/twilight_princess/0/4 , 2006;
  Game Informer, https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2015/03/23/new-3ds-enhanced-3d-was-a-last-minute-case-of-miyamoto-upending-the-tea-table , 2015)
- How he reviewed Galaxy from 400 km away: "I held meetings displaying the game screen
  using a camera, and talking about how we should change things in the game via video
  conferencing." And: "I don't think we would have been able to make this game if I
  was the director." He was producer; Koizumi directed.
  (Iwata Asks, https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/super_mario_galaxy/3/2 , 2007)
- His bar for a lead's report: he rejects "passable" as an answer because it gives
  no measurable sense of how close the thing is to done.
  (Iwata Asks, Skyward Sword, https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/zelda-skyward-sword/7/13 , 2011)
- In 2020 he said he has moved past the table-flip; he only does it when he can
  clearly see how the change improves the game, and now mainly influences controls
  and early concept. (Famitsu via Nintendo Life,
  https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2020/03/miyamoto_has_broken_his_habit_of_completely_reworking_games_mid-development , 2020)
- Nintendo's testing is a separate company, Mario Club Co., Ltd., spun out of an
  internal QA department in 2009, several hundred staff.
  (https://www.mcvuk.com/nintendo-spins-off-qa-division/ , 2009;
  https://www.mariowiki.com/Mario_Club_Co.,_Ltd. , accessed 2026)

THE LESSON: the famous late table-flip is the thing the man himself retired. The
sustainable version is: producer not director, review the SCREEN at set meetings,
demand measurable answers from leads, and keep a separate test arm so the producer's
eyes are not the only quality check.

### Kojima: keep the team small enough that one taste can touch everything
- At the studio's 2016 relaunch: "I'm aiming to keep the staff less than 100 people,"
  so he could connect to developers directly.
  (https://www.mcvuk.com/kojima-productions-to-keep-new-studio-under-100-staff/ , 2016)
- By 2025 the team had grown past 200 and he said "It was impossible to be together
  and polish everything." His stated ideal is Sandfall's "33 team members and a dog."
  (VGC, https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/hideo-kojima-says-clair-obscur-team-size-is-ideal-they-have-33-team-members-and-a-dog/ , 2025)

THE LESSON: the "director sees everything" model has a hard headcount ceiling, by the
admission of the man most famous for it. Past roughly 100 people it breaks. We have
26 departments. The director cannot see everything; the structure must not assume it.

### Supergiant: tiny team, decide fast, "the game is the design document"
- Kasavin on the small-team benefit: decisions are faster, "ideas don't need to be
  over-documented or over-vetted," a feature can be in at a base level the same day.
  (Gamebanshee Bastion interview, https://gamebanshee.com/kpydd , 2011)
- The team was 12 people in 2017 and "It's really important to us to stay small."
  (The Ringer podcast write-up, https://www.theringer.com/2017/07/28/uncategorized/pyre-greg-kasavin-achievement-oriented-podcast-dd948a4f109f , 2017)
- Amir Rao in 2025: "the game is the design document." One project at a time.
  (https://www.notebookcheck.net/Supergiant-Games-director-says-Hades-3-isn-t-a-sure-thing-despite-the-success-of-Hades-2.1125776.0.html , 2025)
- No source confirms a formal "no producers" rule at Supergiant; what is documented is
  that the team is small enough that the build itself is the review surface.

THE LESSON: the review is the build. Judgment happens by playing the thing, not by
reading a page about it. That matches our "he is the integration test" record.

### Larian: founder-director on top, studio managers under him, bottom-up teams
- Vincke never expected "to be 400 people to make BG3."
  (PC Gamer at GDC via WN Hub, https://wnhub.io/news/marketing/item-4459 , 2024)
- He credits growth to "a team of studio managers who oversee team management" and
  describes a "bottom-up development approach. We give our teams as much freedom and
  agency as possible." (80.lv coverage, https://cdn.80.lv/articles/larian-on-why-it-dropped-baldur-s-gate-3-sequel-how-it-scares-publishers , 2024)
- His scope rule: "if the team could handle it, then we're going to put it in."
  (Shacknews, https://www.shacknews.com/article/137186/larian-studios-ceo-interview-bg3-2023 , 2023)
- Seven offices across time zones so no single person has to stay up to fix a late
  problem. (Gamebanshee, https://gamebanshee.com/988a , 2022)

THE LESSON: one director's taste, a layer of managers under him, and teams that own
their own calls. The director does not approve each quest line.

### Blizzard: strike teams are a review PANEL that is not the director
- A strike team is about a dozen people from outside the project who play the build
  and send back feedback; Dustin Browder led the Diablo III one.
  (Gamebanshee Diablo III interview, https://gamebanshee.com/ku6kf , 2008)
- Bill Roper described them as "high-level sounding boards for the development teams
  so that we can keep as many minds on every project as possible."
  (Gamebanshee Bill Roper interview, https://gamebanshee.com/34uf , c. 2000 to 2003)
- They rotate across projects and date back to Diablo II.
  (StarCraft Wiki, https://starcraft.fandom.com/wiki/Blizzard_Entertainment , accessed 2026)

THE LESSON: the review that catches problems is a PANEL of outside eyes who play the
build, not the one director. Our version is the eyes-and-ears lane plus the gates.

### Valve: no managers, cabals, and a lead who is a clearinghouse not a boss
- The 2012 handbook: flat means no management and no reporting lines; the founder
  "is not anyone's boss"; employees can green-light projects and ship.
  (Valve handbook PDF, https://valvesoftware.com/aperturescience/employee_handbook.pdf , 2012;
  Engadget, https://www.engadget.com/2012-04-23-definitely-panic-if-theres-caviar-a-guide-to-working-at-valv.html , 2012)
- Cabals are temporary, multidisciplinary teams formed to ship a feature. A lead
  emerges informally and acts as an information clearinghouse; "Leads have no
  authority other than that everyone agrees it will help the project to have them
  doing coordination." (Field Notes summary, https://fieldnotesbynick.substack.com/p/valve-employee-handbook , 2012 handbook;
  SKMurphy excerpts, https://skmurphy.com/?p=6750 , 2012)
- Valve itself calls the model potentially unstable at scale. (Engadget, 2012)

THE LESSON: the lead's job is to be the one place information flows through, not the
one place approvals wait. A clearinghouse moves things; an approval desk stops them.

### Naughty Dog: no producers, a 70-row macro, and everyone tracks their own work
- "We don't have anyone at all at the studio who has the job title of producer,"
  and nobody, including the co-presidents, solely manages people. Developers organize
  themselves. Uncharted 2's design was "a 70-row-long spreadsheet," not a long
  document. (Game Developer on LeMarchand's DICE 2010 talk,
  https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/dice-2010-naughty-dog-s-lemarchand-on-why-the-studio-has-no-producers , 2010)
- Druckmann: documentation goes stale very quickly; Wells wants everyone to track
  their own responsibilities. (LA Times via PressReader,
  https://www.pressreader.com/usa/los-angeles-times/20160528/282084866051373 , 2016)
- In 2025 Druckmann described taking "more of a producer role" on a second project:
  mentor, give feedback, be the executive in the room.
  (Push Square, https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2025/05/neil-druckmann-confirms-involvement-in-second-unannounced-naughty-dog-project , 2025)

THE LESSON: "no producers" at Naughty Dog does not mean no production. It means the
leads carry it, and the plan is one short table everyone can see. Our backlog and
handoff files are that table. Every lane carries its own production.

### Insomniac: the founder NAMED himself the bottleneck and handed off direction
- Ted Price on holding CEO and director at once: "I was the bottleneck for a lot of
  things." The production team could not get questions answered, and the split made
  him worse at both jobs. He handed creative direction of Resistance to Marcus Smith.
  (Game Informer, https://www.gameinformer.com/games/resistance_3/b/ps3/archive/2010/10/25/interview-ted-price-talks-resistance-3-juggling-genres-and-going-multiplatform.aspx , 2010)
- Insomniac "had never worked under one person's directive on a project."
  (same source, 2010)

THE LESSON: the most direct quote in this whole file. A founder who is also the daily
approver becomes the thing the team waits on. The fix was a named proxy.

## PART 2. WHAT THE PRODUCER ACTUALLY DOES

### The producer is the shield and the translator
- The producer is "the link between the creative and the teams, converting designs
  into tasks and driving the teams forward."
  (https://speedrun.substack.com/p/game-producer-the-role-everyone-loves , 2024)
- Producers "track milestones, manage risk, protect the timeline and budget," and the
  worked example is a creative director asking for features that take a year.
  (VFS career guide, https://vfs.edu/node/8630 , 2025)
- The producer is "not the 'boss' of the people on the game development team, but the
  'boss' of the game." (https://wikipedia.classicistranieri.com/en/articles/g/a/m/Game_producer.html , accessed 2026)
- Producers safeguard "the team's time and output" by "acting as a shield against
  potential distractions," and the top complaint about producers is interrupting flow.
  (Game Production Alchemist, https://gameproductionalchemist.substack.com/p/top-7-complaints-about-game-producers , 2024)
- The anti-pattern: management as a "fast-forward button" that passes every
  interruption straight to the team. (Being Agile excerpt,
  https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/being-agile-eleven/9780133375640/ch01lev2sec6.html , 2013)

### How many people one manager handles
- There is no proven magic number. A 2021 evidence review found no universal ideal
  span and no support for the "rule of 10"; measured spans run from 9 to 77.
  (https://theindependent.co.zw/business-digest/article/200036823/what-is-the-optimum-number-of-subordinates-for-a-manager , 2021)
- McKinsey's role-based ranges: a supervisor of routine work 11 to 15, a coordinator
  15 or more; complex judgment work is lower.
  (https://www.mckinsey.com/business-functions/organization/our-insights/how-to-identify-the-right-spans-of-control-for-your-organization , 2019)
- Vendor and forum sources repeat 5 to 8 for complex work but cite no primary study.
  (https://blog.happily.ai/the-attention-formula-how-many-direct-reports-should-a-manager-really-have , 2024)

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR US: 26 lanes reporting to one human is far past any span for
judgment work. It is fine for an AI coordinator that reads everything, because the
limit on span is attention and the coordinator has no attention budget. The human
has a span of ONE: the coordinator.

### The one-page status and the milestone review
- Publisher-side producers run "weekly check-ins with development partners," keep a
  status tracking sheet current, and do "monthly milestone approvals with written
  reviews of deliverables." (Devolver Digital senior producer listing,
  https://workwithindies.com/careers/devolver-digital-senior-producer , 2025)
- Weekly status reports inform management but "shouldn't replace a comprehensive
  project review." (Chapter 13 of a game production text via Scribd,
  https://www.scribd.com/document/720878764/Chapter-13 , accessed 2026)
- Mullich: send the publisher a progress report BEFORE the milestone lands so nobody
  is surprised at review, and make sure the reviewer understands the milestone's
  intent. (https://davidmullich.com/2013/08/05/milestones-and-glass-houses-preventing-your-development-schedule-from-shattering-part-2/ , 2013)
- Sakurai, a working director, starts every day by writing a daily report to his
  team himself, then builds until 6:30 p.m. (Nintendo Life,
  https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2024/10/random-game-director-masahiro-sakurai-reveals-his-daily-work-routine , 2024)
  He also checks "the specifications behind graphics, sound, motion, characters,
  stages, modes, and UI," not only finished products. (Kotaku on his Famitsu columns,
  https://kotaku.com/smash-bros-director-doesnt-have-to-work-hard-but-he-1460009783 , 2013)

### The gate model: concept, pre-pro, vertical slice, alpha, beta, gold
- Stages: concept doc and lean prototype; vertical slice (also called first
  playable) as a go/no-go; pre-alpha; alpha (feature complete, rough); beta (content
  locked, optimizing); gold. (https://kb.heathen.group/guides/tips-and-tricks/fundamentals/development-phases , accessed 2026;
  https://www.wayline.io/blog/setting-game-development-milestones-concept-launch , 2024)
- The vertical slice decides "whether to continue with the current vision or go back
  to the drawing board." (Anno Union devblog, https://anno-union.com/?p=9660 , 2018)
- Publisher gates like First Playable and Vertical Slice sit in pre-production, and
  money is released only against "verified, working builds that meet predefined
  criteria." (https://salivity.github.io/game-development/article/publisher-milestones-in-game-development , accessed 2026)
- Internal green-light meetings: executives "assess development progress and
  determine whether development will continue"; several prototypes funded, the
  promising ones approved, the rest culled. (Ask a Game Dev,
  https://askagamedev.tumblr.com/post/746119896807653376/in-regard-to-the-vertical-slice-do-studios-work , 2024)

### Review versus check-in, and what reaches the director
- A REVIEW is the director seeing finished candidates and picking or killing. A
  CHECK-IN is someone asking the director how to do the work. Pixar formalizes the
  first and blocks the second: the Braintrust names problems and leaves the fix to the
  team (christopherming.com, 2014 book), and the supervising animator decides what is
  worth the director's time at all (Animation Mentor, 2009).
- Film studios get CURATED dailies, not the whole day's footage (filmdaft, 2024).
- Netflix's rule for the same thing: "context, not control." Leaders set the context
  so people decide on their own, and the leader does not approve each decision.
  (Culture deck via https://www.fastcompany.com/91146182/netflix-revamped-2009-memo-on-culture , 2024 article on the 2009 deck;
  Hastings book summary, https://www.supersummary.com/no-rules-rules/summary/ , 2020 book)
- Supercell: the CEO calls himself "the world's least powerful CEO," has "no creative
  control" over a cell once it exists, and his job "is not to command, but to remove
  obstacles." (tech.eu, https://tech.eu/2017/05/30/supercell-ilkka-paananen/ , 2017)

THE RULE WE TAKE FROM THIS: the director sees CANDIDATES, never questions. Anything
phrased "how should we..." dies at the coordinator. Anything that is not the best
two or three finished options for one decision dies at the coordinator. This is
already our law (NEVER ASK HIM A TECHNICAL QUESTION, 8/15; EVERYTHING IS A THUMB,
8/9). The research says every studio that scaled did the same.

## PART 3. DECISION BUDGETS: DECIDE LESS, DECIDE BIGGER

- Bezos, 2018: "If I make, like, three good decisions a day, that's enough," and
  Buffett says three good decisions a year. "Your job is not to make thousands of
  decisions every day." He does his hard thinking before lunch.
  (Quartz, https://qz.com/work/1390844/jeff-bezos-only-expects-himself-to-make-three-good-decisions-a-day/ , 2018)
- Why: decision quality falls across a session. Eight Israeli judges, 1,000-plus
  parole rulings: grants ran about 65 percent right after a break and fell toward
  zero before the next one. (Danziger et al., PNAS 2011; Columbia press release,
  https://business.columbia.edu/press-release/cbs-press-releases/how-extraneous-factors-impact-judicial-decisionmaking , 2011)
  Later critics say the effect size is overstated, but no one disputes that long
  runs of small decisions degrade. (https://kancelaria-skarbiec.pl/?p=20518 , 2013)
- Bezos's 2015 letter splits decisions into one-way doors (irreversible: slow,
  careful, consulted) and two-way doors (reversible: "use a light-weight process. For
  those, so what if you're wrong?"). The failure mode is running every two-way door
  through the one-way process. (https://blog.kindel.com/2019/06/27/one-way-and-two-way-doors/ , 2019)
- Batching: the Braintrust meets every few months and sees the WHOLE film at once
  (GovExec, 2014). Miyamoto reviewed Galaxy in scheduled screen-share meetings, not
  on demand (Iwata Asks, 2007). Milestone approvals are monthly, status weekly
  (Devolver listing, 2025).
- The art director as taste proxy: the art director "reviews all art assets in the
  game and thus ensures good consistent quality throughout"; the creative director
  owns "creative direction and artistic integrity" and does not review each asset.
  (https://retrostylegames.com/blog/role-of-art-director-in-game-development/ , 2024;
  https://www.krop.com/jennyf/resume , accessed 2026)
- The style bible is how hundreds of artists work without asking: it holds shape
  language, proportions, palette, lighting and do/don't examples; "consistency doesn't
  hold on its own past 30 to 40 assets and 2 to 3 artists," so "someone owns a review
  checkpoint against the bible at defined intervals."
  (Juego Studios, https://www.juegostudio.com/blog/maintain-visual-consistency-external-art-teams , 2025;
  Room 8, https://room8studio.com/news/who-needs-an-art-bible-game-art-direction-from-indie-to-aaa , 2023)
- Blizzard's Hearthstone art director (GDC 2015, "7 Habits of Highly Effective Art
  Directors"): hire to your weaknesses, cultivate successful failures.
  (Pluralsight write-up, https://pluralsight.com/blog/film-games/learn-7-habits-highly-successful-art-directors-blizzard , 2015)

OUR OWN MEASUREMENT AGREES. records/BOHEMIA_RESEARCH_THE_PRICE_OF_HIS_THUMBS_8_16_26.md:
the sound ballot grew six times and his keep rate halved, 366 buttons to keep 119
things, then a 34-of-35 kill sitting. A falling keep rate was answered with a BIGGER
ballot. That is the parole-judge curve on our own numbers. The director's thumbs
were spent compensating for a narrow generator. Decide less, decide bigger.

## PART 4. WHEN THE DIRECTOR IS THE BOTTLENECK, AND WHAT FIXED IT

### Documented failures
- Insomniac, Resistance 1 and 2: founder as CEO and director, "I was the bottleneck
  for a lot of things," the production team could not get answers. (Game Informer, 2010)
- Wideload, Stubbs the Zombie (GDC 2006 postmortem): a 12-person core jumped to 60
  with contractors; "not enough producers," the leads' time went to training, the
  team lacked bandwidth to review incoming work on time, and the project director was
  also the producer. (GameSpot, https://www.gamespot.com/news/6146662.html/ , 2006;
  Game Developer, https://gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-wideload-games-i-stubbs-the-zombie-i- , 2006)
- BioWare, Anthem: six-plus years, the game "only came together during the last 18
  months"; sources blamed "indecision and mismanagement" and meetings that
  "consistently ended with indecision as to the direction of the project"; stress
  leave and crunch followed. (Kotaku April 2019 via Push Square,
  https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2019/04/anthems_development_plagued_by_bad_management_technical_issues_and_unacceptable_working_conditions_says_new_report , 2019;
  TechSpot, https://static.techspot.com/news/79501-anthem-development-plagued-mismanagement-frostbite-challenges-report.html , 2019)
- BioWare, Mass Effect Andromeda: five years of development, most of the game built
  in the last 18 months, procedural planets cut in 2015, a dozen leadership departures.
  (Kotaku June 2017 via Destructoid, https://vip-develop.destructoid.com/?p=220012 , 2017;
  GamingBolt, https://gamingbolt.com/mass-effect-andromedas-development-troubles-laid-bare-frostbite-a-problematic-engine-for-rpgs-rushed-development-and-more , 2017)
- CD Projekt, Cyberpunk 2077: development rebooted in late 2016, management would
  "figure it out along the way," 13-hour crunch days, the E3 demo did not represent
  the real build. (Bloomberg January 2021 via PCGamesN,
  https://www.pcgamesn.com/cyberpunk-2077/report-crunch-e3 , 2021)
- Cloud Imperium, Star Citizen: 20 former staff described Roberts's "meticulous
  micromanagement of the game, down to the smallest details"; a senior engineer spent
  months on shield effects; an artist made five characters in 17 months and had the
  designs reversed. (Forbes May 2019 via CGMagazine,
  https://www.cgmagonline.com/news/star-citizen-development-woes-continue , 2019;
  TechSpot, https://www.techspot.com/community/topics/report-exposes-star-citizens-troubled-development.253761/ , 2019)
- 3D Realms, Duke Nukem Forever: twelve years, engine switched each time a better one
  appeared, every new effect seen elsewhere pushed in ("One day George started
  pushing for snow levels"), "this game must be perfect before it can be released,"
  no outside deadline because the money was there. (Wired December 2009 via Gizmodo,
  https://gizmodo.com/what-went-wrong-with-duke-nukem-forever-5431731 , 2009;
  Techdirt, https://www.techdirt.com/?p=58197 , 2009)
- Across 155 published postmortems, the most common problem class was the TEAM
  (communication, disagreement), then schedule and process, not technology.
  (Washburn et al., ICSE 2016, https://thomas-zimmermann.com/publications/files/washburn-icse-2016.pdf , 2016)

The pattern in every case: the direction lived in one head and came out late, in
pieces, or never. Work waited on the head, or was redone when the head changed. The
team paid in crunch.

### The fixes studios actually used
1. Name a proxy and hand them the pen. Insomniac gave Resistance to Marcus Smith
   (2010). Square Enix put Tabata on Final Fantasy XV after "reviewing its development
   structure" and announced it next to a playable demo (Game Informer,
   https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2014/09/17/tabata-final-fantasy-xv.aspx , 2014).
2. Move the founder from director to producer, review the screen at set meetings.
   Miyamoto on Galaxy (Iwata Asks, 2007), and his 2020 retirement of the table-flip
   (Nintendo Life, 2020).
3. Write down the logic so leads decide the same way without you. Miyazaki on Elden
   Ring (Gamebanshee/Famitsu, 2022). Netflix "context, not control" (2009 deck).
4. Put a layer of managers under the director and give teams agency. Larian's studio
   managers and bottom-up teams (80.lv, 2024). Supercell cells (tech.eu, 2017).
5. Cap what the director can touch. Kojima's under-100 rule (MCV, 2016), and his
   admission that past 200 "it was impossible to be together and polish everything"
   (VGC, 2025). Supergiant at 12 to 20 (Ringer, 2017).
6. Replace the director's eyes with a panel and a test arm. Blizzard strike teams
   (Gamebanshee, 2008), Pixar Braintrust (Fast Company, 2014), Nintendo's Mario Club
   (MCV, 2009), film test screenings (Wikipedia, accessed 2026).
7. Keep the plan short enough that everyone can see it. Naughty Dog's 70-row macro
   (Game Developer, 2010). Status weekly, approvals monthly (Devolver, 2025).

## APPLIED: THE OPERATING MODEL FOR ONE DIRECTOR OVER 26 AI DEPARTMENTS

The mapping, in one line each:
- Paolo is the STUDIO HEAD and the BRAINTRUST. He sees the whole thing on a schedule,
  points at what is wrong, picks between finished options, and plays the build. He is
  not the dailies director. He never sits in a department.
- The coordinator is the DIRECTOR WHO SITS IN DAILIES and the PRODUCER. It reads
  every lane every round, filters, kills, decides every two-way door, and writes the
  one page he reads.
- The 26 lanes are DEPARTMENTS WITH LEADS. Each carries its own production (its own
  handoff block, its own gates), like Naughty Dog's leads. None of them talks to him.
- The laws folder is the STYLE BIBLE and Miyazaki's "logic, philosophies, and thought
  processes." A ruling without its reason attached is not finished.
- The gates are MARIO CLUB. The eyes-and-ears lane is the STRIKE TEAM. A thing that
  did not pass them does not exist to him.

### His time: about 15 minutes a weekday, one sitting a week, about two hours total
- DAILY (10 to 15 minutes, phone, bottom-up): read the coordinator's ONE brief and
  tap the VOTE tab. The brief is the Sakurai daily report, written by the coordinator
  for him, not the other way around. Bezos's three good decisions a day is the
  ceiling on what we ask: the vote tab shows at most three decision GROUPS a day, each
  a thing he can see or hear, each with two or three finished options.
- WEEKLY (one 30 to 45 minute sitting, the Braintrust): he opens the one link, plays
  the newest build on his phone, and talks. Voice, stream of consciousness, as he
  already does. Every sentence becomes a ruling or a job in the coordinator before
  the reply ends. This is the only time the whole game is in front of him, and it is
  the only review that is allowed to be messy, because the Braintrust is supposed to
  be. Nothing else that week needs his voice.
- NEVER: entering a lane chat. The one word VAMILY is sent by the coordinator's
  routine, not by him. If he ever has to open a lane, the coordinator failed.

### What he sees
1. ONE BRIEF, bottom-up, eighth-grade, three parts: what shipped since the last brief
   and WHICH TAB it is in; what the fleet is doing next (one line, no choices); the
   WHAT I NEED FROM YOU block, which is "Nothing, I'm good" most days.
2. ONE VOTE TAB, in the alpha, opening on it. Only finished candidates, shown not
   described, animations and sounds playing, two or three options, asked once. Capped
   at about 20 items waiting at any time (Pixar's Docter approved a shot after seeing
   it several days; he does not get a hundred a day). When the cap is hit, the lanes
   keep cooking and the coordinator keeps culling; the tab does not grow.
3. ONE WEEKLY FLEET SCORECARD at the bottom of the weekly brief: 26 lines, one per
   lane, each one sentence: what it made, the tab, and one of three words: COOKING,
   STUCK, FROZEN. That is the "one-page status." He reads it in 60 seconds or skips it.

### What never reaches him
- Work in progress. Only finished candidates. (Pixar's director sees WIP; the studio
  head sees the cut.)
- Any question that starts with "how should we." That is a check-in, not a review.
  It dies at the coordinator, which answers it from the laws, from Battle Brothers,
  or by picking a default and building it.
- Any technical, prioritisation, or lane-ownership question. Already law (8/15).
- Anything that failed a gate, or has no reference check, or is a fourth version of
  something he already killed twice (STOP PRODUCING, 7/26).
- Duplicates, re-asks of his own words (NOTES ARE RULINGS, 7/19), and text-only
  items (THE FIRST VOTES, 9/21: text items are boring, make the pixels and the sound).
- Lane status, gate output, commit logs, file names, backlog rows. The machine's
  words stay in the machine.

### What the coordinator decides alone (every two-way door)
- The running order across all 26 lanes, what each lane does this round, which lane
  owns a system, and when a lane is frozen or thawed.
- Which candidates reach the vote tab and which die in the coordinator. The rule:
  the coordinator kills everything it would not bet he keeps. If the tab's keep rate
  drops under about 40 percent, the coordinator is passing too much through and the
  generator is too narrow; fix the generator, do not grow the ballot (8/16 record).
- Every fork that has a defensible default: pick it, say why in the record, build it,
  put it in the game where he meets it. He corrects after. (EVERYTHING IS A THUMB, 8/9)
- All numbers, all tuning, all pipeline, all tooling, all plumbing, all scheduling.
- Kills: a second rejection ends the feature for the session without asking him.

### What goes to him (every one-way door)
- Identity and names he reserved.
- A pillar, a reference game, an era, an ending. Anything in the first section of
  CLAUDE.md. These are the Bezos one-way doors: slow, with the research and two or
  three conclusions, one at a time from the visible queue (8/4 format).
- A genuine creative fork about the GAME with no defensible default. Rare. Plain words.
- Anything he asked to see.

### How often the whole fleet is reviewed
- The coordinator reviews every lane EVERY ROUND (the dailies). A lane with no real
  thing he can see or hear registered in the vote tab that round did not have a round
  (COOK EVERY ROUND, 9/21).
- He reviews the whole fleet ONCE A WEEK, in the sitting, by playing the build and
  reading the 26-line scorecard. This is the Braintrust cadence compressed from
  months to a week because our lanes ship daily.
- Once a MONTH the coordinator writes the milestone review for itself and him: where
  the demo stands against the gate ladder (vertical slice, alpha, beta), one page, and
  the one thing blocking everything. That page is the only place calendar time is
  allowed, and it says "since your last sitting," not dates.

### The three or four signals he watches (and the coordinator watches for him)
1. THE BUILD STAMP MOVED AND THE LINK PLAYS ON HIS PHONE. If the stamp did not change
   since the last sitting, nothing shipped, whatever the lanes say. He is the
   integration test; the build is the design document (Supergiant).
2. THE VOTE TAB'S KEEP RATE. Under about 40 percent for two sittings means the
   candidates are weak, not his taste. The coordinator tightens the filter and the
   lanes widen the search. The ballot never grows to compensate.
3. THE SIZE OF THE QUEUE WAITING ON HIM. More than about 20 items in the tab, or more
   than one bold question in a brief, means the coordinator is a fast-forward button
   instead of a shield. That is the Insomniac bottleneck, reversed: now the
   coordinator is making HIM the bottleneck.
4. FOURTH VERSIONS AND REWORK. Any lane on a fourth version of the same thing, or any
   feature rebuilt after his kill, is the Duke Nukem and Star Citizen pattern in
   miniature. The coordinator stops it and says so in the brief.

### The one sentence version
He is the audience and the studio head: he plays the cut once a week and taps thumbs
on finished things for a few minutes a day; the coordinator is the director and the
producer who sees every department every day, writes down the why, and lets nothing
through that is not a finished choice between two or three real options.
