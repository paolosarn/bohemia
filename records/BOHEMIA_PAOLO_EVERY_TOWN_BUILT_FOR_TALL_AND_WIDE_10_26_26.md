## THE RULING (rule 220)
(a) EVERY TOWN HAS TWO LAYOUTS FROM ONE PLAN: a town is a PLAN (its buildings, stalls, the hall, the bar, its gate or entrance, its
    open ground, its people's places) and two COMPOSITIONS of that same plan: TALL (9:16, a phone upright) and WIDE (16:9, a phone
    sideways, a computer). Turning the device swaps the composition; it is recognisably the same town (the same buildings, the
    same look), with the buildings and the entrance moved a little to fit the shape (his 'slightly alter the location of the
    entrance and buildings'), never a crop, never black bars.
(b) BUILT BY RULES, NOT ONLY BY DICE: the plan is generated from the town's own place on the map (its district, its faction, its
    tier, its trade, how deep in the city it sits, rule 186(b)) by MAP RULES that hold for every town (the entrance faces the road
    he arrives by; the hall, the bar and the stalls around one open centre; the gate on the street side; nothing important in the
    phone's corner, rule 213); the dice only vary inside the rules; no two towns alike (the likeness gate).
(c) THE GROUND IS MADE FROM THE LAYOUT: the walk trace (rule 169) and the doors are written by the composer for both compositions,
    never traced by hand, so he walks on the ground in both and never on a roof.
(d) ZOOM: each composition opens fitted to the screen (218(c)); a pinch zooms in as far as the town's size earns (a camp a little,
    a big town more) and pinching out past the fit leaves for the map (211(c)).
(e) THE SAME ON EVERY SCREEN: phone upright, phone sideways, iPad both ways, computer: one game, the right composition for the shape,
    switched live when the device turns, nothing reloaded, his place in the town kept.
(f) THE GATE: every town, both compositions: same buildings in both; nothing cropped or barred; every door reachable on the composed
