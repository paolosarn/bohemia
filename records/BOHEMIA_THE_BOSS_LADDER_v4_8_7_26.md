## THE THREE RESEARCH FINDINGS, AND ONE OF THEM IS THE BEST OF THE SESSION

### ★★★ SHOES: YOU WERE RIGHT THAT THERE ARE SHOES EVERYWHERE, AND IT IS WORSE THAN THAT

*"IM ASSUMING THERE WOULD BE ENOUGH SHOES AROUND. THIS DOESNT LOOK LIKE ITD BE AN ISSUE FR
HELLA SHOES EVERYWHERE IM CONFUSED."*

**HYDROLYSIS.** Polyurethane midsoles — which are most modern footwear — have a service life of
**five to ten years FROM THE DATE OF MANUFACTURE, NOT FROM WHEN YOU START WEARING THEM.** They
degrade sitting in a box. The polymer absorbs water, dries, hardens, goes porous, and the sole
turns chalky and crumbles off the upper. It is **accelerated by warmth**, and **it happens from
the inside, so nothing looks wrong until the sole comes apart under you.**

**BOHEMIA OPENS TEN YEARS AFTER THE CRASH. IN LAS VEGAS.** So: he is exactly right that the
valley is full of shoes, and **every single one of them is at or past end of life.** The
warehouse of pristine unworn sneakers is the *worst* find in the game, because it has been
sitting in desert heat for a decade. What is actually scarce is **a sole that has not
crumbled** — leather, real rubber, cut tyre tread — and somebody who can attach one.

**AND IT IS THE WHOLE GAME'S THEME AS PHYSICS:** everything looks intact and is quietly dead
inside. That is worth more than the boss.

### GUNS: YOUR INSTINCT WAS RIGHT, AND THE HARD PART IS NOT THE GUN

*"IF ITS PIPE WEAPONS MAYBE ACT 1. CAN PEOPLE REALLY JUST MAKE GUNS THAT EASILY IM PRETTY
IGNORANT DO ONLINE RESEARCH."*

**Yes, trivially.** A slam-fire pipe shotgun is documented as *"no more than two pieces of pipe
and an end cap with a nail affixed in the middle,"* manufacturable *"quickly with very limited
skill."* The only fiddly part is lining up the welds so it fires consistently. **So pipe weapons
are ACT 1**, exactly as you guessed.

**What is genuinely hard is AMMUNITION.** Cases can be reused and projectiles can be cast from
scrap lead, but **primers need real chemistry** — that is the bottleneck, and it is a chemistry
problem, not a metalwork one. So the firearm boss is not about guns at all: it is about
**making the round go bang**, and it moves to act 2 under industrial chemistry.

### ★★ HOW NIGHT CITY BECAME NIGHT CITY: THE REBUILD *WAS* THE TAKEOVER

Arasaka was blamed for a nuclear detonation that levelled its own tower and killed a quarter of
a million people. It spent **decades** clawing back. Then it sided with Night City in the
Unification War, staved off an invasion, and was **invited to rebuild its headquarters** — and
the new tower became **the tallest building in the city and the symbol of its skyline.**

**Nobody conquered Night City. It was rebuilt, by the people who supplied the rebuild, and
supplying the rebuild is how they came to own it.**

That is the Amalgamation, and it is already our canon. So act 3's futurism is not a reward, it
is a **bill**: the slightly-better-than-before tech arrives with an owner attached. A finished
Bohemia looking like early Night City is not a happy ending, it is the moment the trap closes —
and the player will have built it themselves, one useful upgrade at a time. **The life lesson is
underneath and the game never says it out loud**, which is the standing requirement.

His own instinct already found the counter: **THE PRESS, because print is not scrapable data.**

### AND THE STAGES OF REBUILDING, WHICH RESTRUCTURED THE ACTS

The literature is blunt: **it does not start with technology, it starts with FOOD.** Survivors
spend years just stabilising food production. Then the stages run food/pastoralism → **state
structures and hierarchies** → pre-industrial → industrial. And: **advanced metallurgy requires
mining, fuel, TRANSPORT NETWORKS, machine tools and industrial chemistry** — five prerequisites,
which is five bosses that were missing.

So the acts now mean something specific:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **ACT 1** | ANIMAL | **SURVIVE.** One body. Water, warmth, getting in, getting about, not dying. |
| **ACT 2** | HUMAN | **PRE-INDUSTRIAL.** Food at scale, then the five prerequisites of industry: mining, fuel, chemistry, transport, machine tools. Plus the thing that makes a state: writing a rule down. |
| **ACT 3** | ANGEL | **INDUSTRIAL, AND EARLY NIGHT CITY.** Many-of-one-thing, freight, power across the valley, building UP — and every one of them arrives with somebody's name on the invoice. |

**★ 36 ANSWERS HIS CHALLENGE.** *"SOUNDS LIKE A CIVILIZATION TEACHING CHILDREN AND PUTTING THEM
IN SCHOOLS BUT I DONT SEE HOW IT IMPACTS GAMEPLAY."* Fair, and the old version had no answer.
Now it has the biggest mechanical stake on the ladder: **three generations is canon, and without
THE SCHOOL your heir starts at zero every single time.** Beat it and the run's progress survives
the death of the person who made it. That is a roguelite's whole spine, not flavour.

**★ 37 IS HIS SUMMON, WITH THE HOLDER FIXED.** *"HOW DOES A BOOK DICTATE THIS"* — it does not,
and that was the flaw. A ledger is paper. What makes a debt real in a city with no banks is
**a person everybody owes**, so the holder is now THE CREDITOR, and beating them transfers those
obligations to you. Spending one is the summon. **Whether clout is the mana is still his** (he
said "idk").

**★ 38 IS HIS NOTE.** *"MAYBE EXPEDITED CLOUT CURRENCY. REALLY RAMPS UP THE CITY BUILDING SHIT
IN ACT 3."*

**★ 40 AND 41 ARE THE NIGHT CITY LESSON MADE PLAYABLE.** Both are genuine upgrades. Both hand

the trap closing — built by the player, one useful thing at a time. Whether the valley should
