HOW IT WORKS (two families, because they have to be)
  HEIGHT rides the SKELETON. seg()'s WIDTH LAW (Paolo 7/2/26) scales the
  along-bone axis with bone length and NEVER the perpendicular axis, so
  lengthening bones in the POSE gives a taller body at unchanged width for free.
  Anchored on the ground line, so the feet stay planted at every height and the
  occupancy contract is untouched. The head keeps its authored bone vector and
  exact rest length -- a taller adult is not a bigger head, and uniform scaling
  of the whole sprite is what reads as A CHILD.
  BELLY and ARMS ride the REST PIXELS, because a width dial cannot ride the
  skeleton at all (the perpendicular axis is law-bound never to scale). Rows are
  re-mapped by an INVERSE sample, per part, so a row can never empty and a hole
  can never open. Nothing is repainted: Paolo's pixels are re-placed, never
  redrawn (RIG LAW, and REUSE-FIRST: this whole feature cooks zero new art).

THREE THINGS THE RENDER TAUGHT ME THAT REASONING DID NOT
  (all three were found by looking at pixels, per VERIFY ON THE REAL SURFACE)
  1. THE FLANK CONTRACT. On head-on facings the hanging arms COVER the torso's
     flank. A belly that only widened part 4 was invisible behind the arm, and a
     belly that narrowed tore a 1px hole between the torso and an arm that
     stayed put. The arms hang ON the flank, so the whole arm unit (arm + hand)
     now translates with it. Translated, never reshaped.
  2. THE ARMPIT BRIDGE. An arm and the torso ride different bones, so once the
     pose deforms them a 1px slide in rest space can open a 1px seam on screen
     even though the data is exactly adjacent. An arm moving outward now keeps
     its original inner columns as an anchor: outer edge travels with the belly,
     armpit edge never leaves the torso.
  3. THE FINAL FLOATER. With EVERY dial at its minimum at once, five frames
     across dance/cough/startle facing NE ended up with a single floating
     clothing pixel. The body already has a de-speckle pass -- but it runs
     BEFORE garments composite, so a stranded garment pixel sailed straight
     through it. Fixed at the root with a FINAL FLOATER CULL on the composited
     frame (zero orthogonal neighbours = a floater by definition; thin art
     always keeps two, which is the trap the body pass was written to dodge).
     No-op on the canon body, and it now protects every garment ever made.
  4. THE ARM ANCHOR. In profile the arms sit ENTIRELY INSIDE the torso's own
     footprint (facing E: arm-L spans x 25-29, torso spans 24-31), so the only
     part of the arm on screen is its outer edge. Shrinking about the row centre
     did not thin the arm, it DELETED it -- at every amplitude tested down to
     0.15. Fixed properly instead of by shrinking the dial: growing always
     pushes the outer edge outward, and shrinking pulls the INNER edge when the
     arm is beside the torso and the OUTER edge when it is buried in it. Full
     amplitude now renders clean.

THE ONE HONEST LIMIT: "TALLER" IS CAPPED BY THE SPRITE FRAME
  Measured, not guessed, across 61 clips x 8 facings x 4 phases on the real
  surface. Paolo's painted body ALREADY paints on the top row of the 56px frame
  in nine clips (jump, cheer, hands-up, stretch, heave, jumping-jacks, hail,
  flee-sprint, sleep). His guy is a TALL guy and the frame is full.
    at +8% the head is SHAVED on idle / walk / run facing NW -- 6 pixels of skull
      land on row 0
    at +6% two stray pixels appear on crawl-dying
    at +5% zero strays, zero shaves anywhere in a 5,712-frame sweep
  So the shipped range is +-5% of standing height, about +-2.3px on a 46px body,
  a 4.6px spread end to end. Side by side that is a clearly different person;
  it is NOT "make me a giant". Going bigger is not a taste question, it needs a
  ruling: either a taller sprite frame (touches everything) or re-centring canon
  so Paolo's painted body is no longer sitting at the ceiling.

  "in the character menu I would like to see a shuffle Animation button in the
   bottom right or something of the actual box that shows the character"
  -> SHUFFLE ANIM shipped, pinned bottom-right INSIDE the preview box, with the
     clip name bottom-left (tap it to return to idle). Pool is the real CLIPS
     table minus the terminal clips. The box also stopped drawing the rig
     skeleton over the body -- pink bones and green joints are a RIG tool, and
     this box is now where he judges body shape. Gate: charpreview_gate.js 18/18.
     This is not a convenience: it puts the animations under his thumb on the
     SAME screen as the dials, which is the whole lesson-7 discipline.

  "it seems like it's already breaking how the animation looks, where shit
   looks chopped"
  -> He was right, and my sweeps had all been green, because I was measuring
     strays, holes, part loss and frame edges -- none of which is what "chopped"
     means. Dumping the SHADE MAP (which pixel is base tone, which is the dark
     anatomy outline) found three real defects in ten minutes:
     1. THIN ARMS COLLAPSED TO A STRIPE. The renderer outlines a limb's inner
        AND outer column, so a 5px arm shows 3px of skin. Shrunk to 3px it shows
        ONE, between two dark lines: not a thin arm, a stripe glued to the body.
        Minimum arm width is now 4 columns.
     2. THE MINIMUM-WIDTH FLOOR SLID THE WHOLE LIMB. When a row hit the floor it
        re-centred, ignoring the held edge -- facing N the arm walked 2px across
        the body and the "thinner" arm came out WIDER than canon. The floor now
        grows back out from the anchored side only.
     3. THE BELLY DIAL FATTENED THE ARMS. The armpit bridge was keeping the
        whole vacated band, so a gut also gave the man bigger arms. Bridging one
        column instead left a gap. The bridge is DELETED: the arm is translated
        whole, and a full-clip-set hole sweep proves the seam it was written for
        does not exist. Dead machinery is worse than no machinery.
     Plus a look fix: at +1 the arms jumped 3px -> 7px in the row under the
     shoulder cap and the body read as a sloped CAPE. The thickening now ramps
     in over the top third of the arm -- a deltoid running into a bicep.
     All four are machine-locked in bodyvar_gate.js as THE CHOPPED CHECKS.

  LESSON FOR THE NEXT SESSION, stated plainly: green gates did not predict what
  he saw, AGAIN. Strays/holes/parts/edges are absence-of-crash metrics. The
  thing a person actually looks at is the SHADE MAP -- where the outline falls
  and how much skin is left between outlines. Dump that first next time.
