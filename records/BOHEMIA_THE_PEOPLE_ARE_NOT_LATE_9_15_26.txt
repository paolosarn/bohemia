================================================================================

off the call stack so the answer knows WHOSE body it is:

  the city draws its first anything      4,804 ms

and he is the only one, and everything before that is a blank or frozen city.

SIX WHOLE SECONDS WITH NO DRAW OF ANY KIND. That is not a slow crowd, that is a
stopped game. The commonest draw sizes over thirty seconds are 44x44 (2,707
ground tiles) and 704x704 (1,225 chunk blits): the ground being baked is what the
eight seconds are spent on.

================================================================================
SO WHICH HALF IS WHOSE, SAID PLAINLY
================================================================================
NOT THIS LANE'S: the thread being held. EYES routed that half themselves to
PLUMBER [sixty fps] and measured it at 28.9 s frozen out of the first 300 across
42 freezes. This round's walks saw 30 to 34 blocks totalling 23 to 25 s in thirty
seconds. Nothing this lane writes puts a person on a canvas that is not drawing.

THIS LANE'S: the 366 ms, and the promise that it stays that small. When the
freeze is fixed, people have to already be the first thing painted rather than a
second problem found afterwards.

================================================================================
THE GATE HOLDS A RATIO AND A TRIPWIRE, AND THEY ARE DIFFERENT QUESTIONS
================================================================================
THE RATIO describes the wait: of the time before the first other person, 90% or
more must be the main thread being held. It means the same thing on this box and
on his phone, which a wall-clock ceiling never could, because PLUMBER proved this
container is several times faster than the thing in his hand.

THE RATIO CANNOT CATCH THIS LANE GETTING SLOWER, and that is written down rather
than hoped over: a delay added inside a window that is already mostly blocked
leaves it high. Measured with a delay injected on purpose: three seconds left it
at 100%, ten seconds at 93%.

SO THE TRIPWIRE IS A DIFFERENT NUMBER: the time he is alone on the glass WHILE
THE THREAD IS FREE. That is time the game could have spent drawing somebody and
did not, it belongs to nobody else, and it is the literal form of his sentence.
It stands at 366 ms against a beat of 500 ms, and the injected delay pushed it to
1,351 ms and turned the claim red.

================================================================================
MY INSTRUMENT WAS WRONG THREE TIMES AND THE THIRD ONE IS THE ROUND
================================================================================
CUT ONE wrapped peoplePass after the page had started. The wrap landed at 12.9 s
and reported "the pass has run 0 times" about a pass that had already painted
three bodies. The zero was the moment I started looking, not a fact about the
game. EYES published exactly this error in round 4 and again in round 6 and wrote
it up both times; I made it a third time with their warning in front of me.

CUT TWO polled PLAYER_CV and read false the whole way through, including after
people were plainly on the glass. PLAYER_CV lives in the CITY frame and my poller
was reading the SHELL's window. Two windows, one name, and a confident negative.

*** CUT THREE COUNTED THE PLAYER AS A HUMAN BEING. *** It knew a body by the
ladder's own rungs, which is correct for "is this a body" and useless for "did he
see a PERSON", because the player is drawn on the same ladder. It reported the
first human being at 12.5 s. THE STACK SAYS 12.5 s IS renderHuman DRAWING HIM,
and the first draw from peoplePass is four seconds later. A control aimed at
something else is what exposed it: peoplePass was made to draw nobody until 22 s
and the "first body" did not move at all.

That is the same mistake as his complaint, made by the tool sent to measure his
complaint. A body is not a human being until you know whose it is.

WHAT CHANGED SO IT CANNOT HAPPEN AGAIN: the hook reads the call stack and a draw
counts as somebody else ONLY when peoplePass is on it. The control plants the
same 112 px square twice, once from a function called peoplePass and once not,
and refuses to believe the classifier unless it separates them.

AND ONE CLAIM OF MINE PASSED TWICE BY LUCK BEFORE THAT. The first version asked
"how long after the thread came free did a body land", which has no answer when
the body lands INSIDE a block, and it does, because drawing is exactly the work
that starves the heartbeat. It read 38 ms, then 42 ms, then 9,492 ms on a run
where nothing about the game had changed.
