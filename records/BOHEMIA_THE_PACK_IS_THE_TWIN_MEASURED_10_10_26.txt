THE THREE DIFFERENCES, MEASURED, NOT GUESSED. This lane has written "his has more detail"
before and it is worth nothing: it cannot be checked and it does not say what to change.
His 24 street tiles against every street and sidewalk cell in our own approved 7/28
starter set:
  COLOURS PER TEN THOUSAND PIXELS      his  5131.4   ours    36.7   (140 times over)
  COLOURS TO COVER HALF THE TILE       his    1121   ours       2
  HOW FAR A DARK MARK RUNS (of width)  his    4.2%   ours    7.4%

ALL THREE ARE RATIOS ON PURPOSE. The first cut of this measurement used raw counts, so
a 96 px pack tile beside a whole contact sheet read "his 5,120 pixels, ours 2,405,520".
Both numbers true, both meaningless. A measurement that compares the wrong two things is
worse than no measurement, because it looks like evidence.
  OURS runs further, and that is not better: with so few colours a dark mark comes out as one long flat smear, while his is many fine marks at many values. Long and flat is the tell.

THE SECOND NUMBER IS THE ONE THAT EXPLAINS HIM. It takes a couple of colours to cover
half of one of our tiles and hundreds to cover half of one of his. That is what "looks
like AI slop" is actually describing: a surface made of a handful of flat values. It is
a fair reading of our own tiles and not an insult.

AND THE FINDING THE THREE NEW CHATS NEED BEFORE THEY START. The 87 packs were drawn by
MANY HANDS. Battle Brothers reads as one world because every tile in a biome came from
one hand and one palette, so a road tile and a forest tile belong to each other before
either is placed. Ours will NOT do that by itself: laying pack tiles straight down will
give a board that is sharper than what we cook and still incoherent. The coherence has
to come from a weather, wear and light pass of our own ON TOP, which is exactly what rule
82a already says ("placed, flipped, weathered, recoloured INSIDE the pack's palette").
COOK TWO and COOK FOUR: that pass is your first problem, not an afterthought.
