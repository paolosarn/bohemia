HIS WORDS

    "IT LOOKS EXACTLY THE SAME DUMBASS"

That is the ruling, and it is the right one. I had built a picture to prove a

WHAT HAD BEEN BLOCKING IT
-------------------------
Row 2X's headline -- Paolo 8/14, "the character models need twice as many pixels" --
had steps 1 through 4 built, proved and DORMANT since 8/16 for one reason I wrote
down myself: composing natively at 112 removes the corner-rounding Scale2x was
manufacturing, so his head renders as a box, and that walks into his own LOCKED law

never been shown -- which is the approvals queue coming back through the side door
that EVERYTHING IS A THUMB (8/9) killed.

He looked. He could not see it. So it shipped.

AND THE PICTURE WAS HONEST, WHICH I CHECKED BEFORE TREATING IT AS A VERDICT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Looks the same" has two readings and one of them is "your picture is broken". So
the PNG was decoded and the two columns compared pixel for pixel in the head row:

    976 of 30,752 displayed pixels differ -- 3.2%

A real difference, correctly called nothing. The measurement beside it says the same
thing from the other end: 5.90% of the lit body differs, and only 11% of that is on
the head. THIRTY PIXELS ON A FACE were holding the headline.

WHAT SHIPPED
------------
`python3 tools/bohemia_2x_flip.py --flip`. Every seam was already in place; the flip
wraps his rig in the load-time doubler and RIG_RS becomes 2.
  - the frame composes at 112 NATIVELY; Scale2x is off, which DELETES per-frame work
  - the black border is ONE TRUE PIXEL (median 1 on all eight facings, measured)
  - his painted art is UNTOUCHED: rig_no_drift still hashes the literal byte for byte

TWO THINGS THE FLIP TOOL ITSELF GOT WRONG, AND BOTH REFUSED TO WRITE
--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. IT WOULD HAVE UNDONE THIS MORNING'S WARDROBE WORK. The tool's garment seam edit
   inserts a block-doubler -- hand the generators a grid downsampled back to 56 and
   stamp their output as squares. That was the deferral Paolo named from outside the
   code the same morning, and it is now retired and replaced with an ASSERTION: if
   the native seam ever disappears the tool refuses to flip rather than silently
   reinstating the old one.
2. FOUR SEAMS REPORTED AS MISSING WERE ALREADY DONE. They had been re-written later
   with a `typeof BAKED` guard, so the tool's exact-text matchers found ZERO of
   either form and it refused. A tool that cannot recognise its own finished work
   reports a MISS for a job that is complete. Matchers updated to the current shape.

The refusal in both cases is the tool working. It writes nothing unless every edit
matches exactly once.

THE RE-BLESS: FOUR GATES, AND NOT ONE NUMBER LOOSENED
------------------------------------------------------
  ALPHA LOADS + RIG NO-DRIFT -- both looked for `const BAKED={`. After the flip the
    literal is WRAPPED (`const BAKED=RIG2X({...})`), so both reported HIS RIG AS
    MISSING on a build where it is present and doubled. Regex accepts either form;
    the 25,000-char floor and the sha256 pin on his painted pixels are unchanged.

  CANVAS SCALE -- the CLOTHES previews were a 168 and a 56 content box around what is
    now a 112 sprite: x1.5 and x0.5, and x4.5 and x1.5 on a dpr-3 phone. A dead half
    device pixel on the surfaces garments are JUDGED on. Boxes resized to 224 and 112
    content; cloBig's pin moved x3 -> x2 because x3 of a 56 sprite and x2 of a 112
    sprite are THE SAME SIZE ON SCREEN. The claim -- a whole number, never a fraction
    -- is untouched.

  BORDER -- and this one was the ruler, not the border. It walked RIGHT from the
    silhouette and called that run the thickness. On a horizontal edge that is the
    same thing; on a DIAGONAL it measures the width of a staircase tread. It never
    showed up before because Scale2x was rounding every diagonal corner off, so
    treads were 1-2px. Removing that rounding -- the entire point of the flip -- made
    the same one-pixel border read as 8 on a shoulder. Verified: the run sat wholly
    over BACKGROUND cells, the median stayed 1 on all eight facings, gaps stayed 0,
    and "only the border moved" stayed 0 mismatches. It measures BOTH AXES and takes
    the smaller now, which is STRICTER -- it also catches a fat border on a vertical
    edge, which a row-only scan never could. Mutation: draw the border 2px and both
    checks fire.

*** THE ONE I CALLED A FINDING -- AND IT WAS WRONG. CORRECTED SAME DAY. ***
------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT I WROTE HERE FIRST, AND IT IS NOT TRUE:

    "the chin went from rig 4 game 4 to rig 4 game 6 purely by removing the
     smoothing. THE SKINNER WAS ALWAYS DRAWING THE CHIN THAT WIDE."

I read HEAD FOLLOWS RIG's ratchet moving 13 -> 64 across the flip, converted it to
56-equivalent cells, saw the chin row go from exact to two cells wide, and published
a claim about the skinner. Then I went to fix the skinner and measured what the
ratchet was actually comparing.

IT COMPARES THE RIG'S PAINTED **FACE** (part 2) AGAINST EVERY SKIN-COLOURED PIXEL ON
THE ROW. Those are different regions. The drawn row also holds the **HEAD** (part 1),
which is wider than the face everywhere except the cheekbones, and on the chin rows
the **NECK** (part 3) as well. All three are skin. Measured row by row on the real
frame, every single "deviation" is exactly accounted for:

    y12..y29    drawn skin == posed HEAD+FACE          to the pixel
    y30..y31    drawn skin == posed HEAD+FACE+NECK     to the pixel
    UNEXPLAINED PIXELS: ZERO

THERE IS NO JAW DEBT. THERE NEVER WAS. It was the head being wider than the face,
which is anatomy. And what moved across the flip was not the skinner: it was how much
of the HEAD EDGE got counted once Scale2x stopped rounding corners off it.

A RULER COMPARING TWO DIFFERENT REGIONS CANNOT BE REPAIRED BY RESCALING IT, which is
exactly what I did in the first pass -- I turned a pixel count into a ratio, made it
resolution-free, mutation-tested it, and it was a beautifully calibrated instrument
pointed at the wrong thing. Scale-free and wrong is still wrong.

WHAT IT ASSERTS NOW IS LIKE-FOR-LIKE AND EXACT: the POSED head+face silhouette, row
for row, IS the rig's head+face silhouette -- same regions, same space, read off part
ids so no colour heuristic can confuse it, and on ALL EIGHT FACINGS instead of one.

    S:0  SE:0  E:0  NE:0  N:0  NW:0  W:0  SW:0      total deviation 0

The renderer reproduces his painted head exactly. So the ratchet is pinned at ZERO
and can never be loosened by anybody. Mutation: widen the posed head by one cell on
facing S and it fires, naming the facing and the amount.

SAMPLED AT THE REST PHASE ON PURPOSE. At a moving phase the skinner is SUPPOSED to
deform the head -- the bob, the turn -- so demanding it equal the rest rig mid-stride
would be asserting that animation does not happen. For the record, at ph=0.37 the
deviation is 12-44 per facing, and that is the animation working.

AND THE PIXELS ARE STILL CHECKED, by the jaw-edge tone assertion the gate already
had: on the rows his art puts the dark anatomy index on, is the EDGE pixel darker
than the pixel beside it? That is the check that caught the real 8/11 bug, it is
about what the renderer PAINTS, and it is untouched.

THE COST OF THE ORIGINAL MISTAKE, said plainly: a false claim about the most
dangerous code in the repo, published in a commit message, a record, the backlog and
the handoff, where the next session would have found it and gone hunting a bug that
does not exist. The commit message cannot be edited; this record supersedes it.

*** AND THE MEMORY, WHICH IS THE ONE THING STILL WRONG ***
-----------------------------------------------------------
Row 2X said to measure 4x the pixels against the 224 MB iOS ceiling. Done, and the
answer splits three ways.

WHAT A PLAYER HOLDS IS FINE: 25.4 MB loaded, and the RUN tab adds ZERO. The flip
does not move the number for anyone who opens the link and plays.

WHAT I BROKE AND FIXED: the CLOTHES tab builds a live preview plus an 8-pose strip
for every garment in the catalogue -- 258 of them -- and each canvas' backing store
went 56x56x4 to 112x112x4. Measured: 25 MB before the flip, 96.8 MB after. The tab
has always refused to RENDER what is off screen and never let go of the pixels.
Off-screen canvases now release their backing store and the render path re-allocates
on the way back in, which it already did.

    CLOTHES tab   96.8 MB  ->  2.4 MB
    whole build   1,188 MB ->  1,093 MB  (canvases 164 MB -> 64 MB)

So this turn IMPROVES the worst case by ~100 MB while doubling the character.

WHAT IS STILL BROKEN AND IS NOT MINE. The gate is RED, and it was red before I
touched anything -- it has been passing on a STALE MEASUREMENT FILE, and its own
NOTE says so on every run ("measured before the latest change to ... ALPHA"). A
fresh probe on the build that shipped this morning reads 1,188 MB, 530% of the iOS
floor. The cause, hunted down frame by frame:

    69 images in the VOTE tab    632.7 MB decoded
    27 images in the ART tab     209.2 MB decoded
     2 images in the LOOK tab      8.3 MB
     6 images in the LIFE tab      0.7 MB
    ---------------------------------------
    104 images                   850.8 MB

Ninety-six full-resolution art boards, ~8 MB each once decoded. That figure is
BYTE-IDENTICAL before and after the flip, so none of it is the rig or the wardrobe.
Quantizing the files does not help: decoded cost is width x height x 4 whatever the
PNG weighs. The fix is smaller DIMENSIONS or thumbnails, and that is ART/VOTE
content, not this lane's to resample.

I am shipping with that red rather than restoring the stale file to buy a green,
because a green bought by a measurement you know is wrong is worse than the red --
which is a rule this repo already wrote down.

AND ONE MORE RULER THE FLIP EXPOSED: THE CROWD BOARD
-----------------------------------------------------
CROWD's determinism check -- "redrawing the same crowd gives byte-identical pixels
(no dice in the render path)" -- started failing, intermittently, only after the
flip. Pre-flip: green twice. Post-flip: green, green, red, red, red.

IT IS NOT DICE. Measured directly: twelve citizens redrawn FOUR TIMES in a row are
byte-identical every time, once the board has actually finished its first paint. The
check read the canvases, called crowdRefresh() and compared -- and if the first paint
was still in flight, "before" was a HALF-DRAWN BOARD and the refresh that followed
finished it. A stopwatch started too early, not a random number.

It went from rare to frequent because a 112 frame is FOUR TIMES THE WORK, so twelve
of them take four times as long and the race that was always there now usually wins.
The gate settles on the BOARD (two consecutive identical reads, all twelve canvases
allocated) instead of on a clock. Four runs green. Mutation: put a real
Math.random() in the crowd's draw call and it fires exactly as it should.

THE HONEST COST BURIED IN THAT: the first crowd paint IS slower now. Twelve
characters at four times the pixels is four times the composition work, one time, on
a workbench tab. Nothing in the RUN path pays it, and Scale2x coming off gives back
per-frame work on every character the game draws.
