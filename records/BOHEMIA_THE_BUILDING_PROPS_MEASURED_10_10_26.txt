AND WHY ONE EACH, NOT SIX OF EVERYTHING. Battle Brothers keeps a SHORT prop vocabulary
per terrain on purpose (02_COMBAT_RULES.md, the TERRAIN line) so that a board reads
from across the screen. What this kit does that theirs does not: BB's props are
nature's and the same in every forest, while every piece here is what one particular
BUILDING left behind when the money stopped. The pumps belong to the gas station and
nothing else. A board made of them tells you what the place used to be.

COVER OR BLOCKER IS MEASURED AGAINST A MAN'S CHEST AT 1.30 m, NEVER FILED BY EYE.
This lane filed two pieces wrong by eye on 9/30 and the tape corrected it, so the tape
runs on every piece here and the build refuses if a height and a kind disagree:
  FUEL PUMPS ON THEIR ISLAND       1.90 m -> BLOCKER
  PEW ROW THROUGH THE BROKEN WALL  0.92 m -> COVER

THE PIECE THAT GIVES A BOARD BOTH HEIGHTS AT ONCE is the back lot: two machines still
standing at 1.55 m stop a shot, two on their faces at 0.62 m are something to crouch
behind, and they are one prop. One piece that gives a board two heights is worth two
that give one.

RULE 77, AND THE PART THAT IS NOT A LABEL. Every prop declares what runs out of its
four sides. TWO of the six claim to RUN end to end -- the pew row down the nave and the
school fence -- and both were LAID DOWN TWICE AND THE JOINT MEASURED against the
inside of the piece. The other four are single objects and their edges say so, which
is not dodging the test: this lane typed a sign gantry as a run earlier the same round,
the measurement said the joint was twelve times the inside, and the measurement was
right and the label was wrong.

REUSE-FIRST PAID AGAIN, AND ON THE PIECE IT MATTERED MOST. A school fence IS a
chain-link run with one way through, so the school prop is this lane's own 9/30
chain-link run, two lengths of it, with the gate cut in the middle and its leaf
standing open against the mesh. Nothing was redrawn. The back lot stands on his own
7/28 yard rubble. Every ramp, every cell and every material in the kit is his:

*** AND TWO WHOLE PIECES CAME OFF THE SHELF AND WENT BACK ON IT, WHICH IS ALSO WHAT
*** REUSE-FIRST LOOKS LIKE. I gave myself a rule this round that two of the six had to
be a whole piece out of the cupboard. To hit that number I put his yard rubble at the
foot of the slot machines and his oil drum on the fuel island, and both times the
picture said no:
  rubble_yard  tried in the casino back lot        painted for bright open ground at a finer grain than this kit; in a dark service yard it read as boulders and took the eye off the machines
  oil_drum     tried in the gas station island     it is a BURNING BARREL with a lit fire in it; a fire on a fuel island is wrong twice over, and it is painted hotter than anything else in the kit
A quota I invented is not the law. REUSE-FIRST says look first and say what you found,
and this is what I found. One whole piece earned its place: the school fence.

AND THE GATE IS THE WHOLE POINT OF THE SCHOOL PIECE. A wall is geometry. A wall with
ONE DOOR IN IT is a decision, and both sides know where you have to come through.
2.4 m wide, in the middle of 12 m of fence you can see straight through, which is the
only reason to put chain-link on a board: you know what is coming and you still
cannot get to it.

WHAT COMBAT TWO DOES WITH IT. Each prop carries its FOOTPRINT in metres (w x l)
SEPARATELY from its picture. Place the footprint on the pitched board; do not scale the
face, because squashing a finished picture resamples every pixel and that is how a
painted thing turns to mush.
