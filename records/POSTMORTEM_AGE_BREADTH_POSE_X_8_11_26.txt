===============================================================================
WHAT HE SAW, IN NUMBERS
===============================================================================
Measured on the real famBody canvas, shoulder span over figure height:

    FATHER  shoulder 44 / height 106 = 0.415
    SISTER  shoulder 37 / height  84 = 0.440

Close enough to identical that the child was, proportionally, her father. The
age axis (BOH_AGE) scaled the pose in Y and ONLY in Y:

    for (const j in P) n[j] = [ P[j][0] , ground + (P[j][1]-ground)*S.h ];
                               ^^^^^^^^
                               X passed straight through, every joint

So an 84px child stood with a grown man's shoulder span, a grown man's stance,
and arms hanging a grown man's distance off the ribs. That is not a child, that
is an adult who has been stepped on. HIS READ WAS CORRECT AND THE CAUSE WAS ONE
UNSCALED COORDINATE.

===============================================================================
THE FIX THAT WAS WORSE THAN THE BUG
===============================================================================
Added a `wide` factor per stage and scaled every joint's X about the pelvis.
This is the obvious fix and it is what the anthropometry asks for (biacromial
breadth falls to ~0.70 of adult while stature only falls to ~0.73, so a child is
genuinely narrower than a scaled-down adult).

Rendered it. Zoomed the child to 5x. LOOKED. She was destroyed:
  - her arms had FUSED into her torso, no outline between them
  - her jeans had collapsed to a blob with a hole in it
  - her sneakers had DETACHED into a tan brick wider than her legs

Worse art than the squashed adult it was replacing. Deleted, not softened.

*** THE RIG FACT WORTH KEEPING ***
HANDS (7/8) AND FEET (11/12) ARE RIGID STAMPS BOUND TO BONES, NOT SKINNED
GEOMETRY. Narrowing the SKELETON drags the limb bones inboard, but the
adult-sized stamps hanging off them do not narrow with the stance. So the arms
merge into the ribs while the shoes stay where they were and stick out past the
hips. A POSE-SPACE X SCALE CAN NEVER BE RIGHT ON THIS RIG, at any amplitude.
Softening the number would only have made the tearing subtler.

===============================================================================
WHAT REPLACED IT
===============================================================================
BODYVAR's `shoulders` / `arms` / `hips` dials. They narrow a body by remapping
REST PIXELS row by row and letting the skinner rebind -- so the stamps come
along and nothing detaches. Already built, already gated (bodyvar_gate.js). The
age stage now carries a dial BIAS merged under the character's own dials
(BOH_AGE.bias), which is the supported road to the same place.

===============================================================================
AND THE HONEST RESIDUAL -- READ THIS BEFORE PICKING THIS UP AGAIN
===============================================================================
The dial bias is a REAL but SMALL improvement. Measured, the child's shoulder
span moved 37 -> 36 (one pixel); by eye her waist, the gap between her legs, and
the separation between her two shoes all improved visibly. It is better. It is
not solved.

THE REMAINING CHUNK IS NOT THE FEET AND NOT THE STANCE, and I chased both before
measuring properly:

  - scaled the FOOT STAMPS (limb factor): rest-grid feet went 13px -> 11px wide
    on the child. Render moved ZERO pixels.
  - scaled the STANCE: broke the art (above).
  - measured the rendered sole line: 28px on ALL FOUR cast members, before and
    after everything.

BECAUSE THE BOTTOM BAND IS NOT THE FEET. It is the SHOE, and genShoes generates
over the UNION of parts 9/10/11/12 -- legs plus feet -- painting a sole on every
row where the silhouette ends. The legs sit 11 sprite px apart in the rest pose
at every age, so the union is ~14 sprite px wide on a child and on her father
alike. Nothing in the age axis touches leg SPACING, and leg spacing is what sets
that number.

SO THE NEXT PERSON'S QUESTION IS: should the rest pose's leg spacing vary with
age, and if so is that a BODYVAR dial (hips already exists) or a rig-level
change? THAT IS A PAOLO CALL, not a thing to keep guessing at. I stopped here
rather than write a fifth version -- STOP PRODUCING, 7/26: "writing a fourth
version of anything means you already failed."

The limb stamp scale also had a wrong first version worth recording: it scaled
the stamps in Y as well as X, which took a 4-row foot down to 3 and opened gaps
between shin, ankle and sole. genShoes paints a SOLE on any row where the
silhouette ends (`if(!inG(x,y+1))`), so row after row read as "the bottom row"
and the child's whole lower leg came out STRIPED sole/midsole/sole -- her jeans
and sneakers were a stack of pancakes. The banding was the generator correctly
describing a leg with holes in it. Fixed by scaling X only: in the 45 view a
foot's LENGTH is horizontal extent, its vertical extent is the thickness of the
shoe (a shoe fact, not an age fact), and Y is the axis welded at BOTH ends --
the ankle above, the ground line below.
