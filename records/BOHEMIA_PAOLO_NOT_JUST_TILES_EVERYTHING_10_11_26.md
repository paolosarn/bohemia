## WHAT I HEARD (rule 91c; correct me with one word)
The tool has to make every kind of asset the game shows: weapons, portraits and faces, characters in their eight directions, buildings from their angles, wrecked vehicles, street lights, props, icons. A tile-only tool is not the answer. The coordinator's pick of Aseprite plus Tilesetter was too narrow and is withdrawn as the pick (both stay as helpers).

## THE RE-CUT, HONESTLY
Of the six, the tools that make EVERY kind of pixel asset from his packs as reference are the two models: Retro Diffusion (tiles, props, vehicles, weapons, buildings, portraits, lamps; characters too) and PixelLab (people and beasts in eight directions with walk and attack; items). Aseprite cleans and sizes all of it; Tilesetter only extends tiles; an artist does everything but by hand and once.

Against his three rules (no graphics card, no monthly, no outside credits) no tool makes everything: the local model needs a graphics card, the online one is prepaid credits, PixelLab is a subscription, and code alone failed. So the honest pick needs ONE rule bent, and the smallest bend is a PREPAID BALANCE on Retro Diffusion online: no monthly, bought once like a gift card, about 3 to 18 cents an asset, nine of his tiles as the reference and the palette locked on every call, driven by the chats with no human in the loop, every kind of asset. Aseprite ($19.99 once) cleans what it makes. PixelLab would be the second bend, a subscription, only for the eight-direction people; if he will not bend twice, the people come from the artist once ($800 to $2,500) and the chats extend them.
