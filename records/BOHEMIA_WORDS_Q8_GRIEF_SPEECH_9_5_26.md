# WORDS Q8 -- GRIEF SPEECH
# VAMILY research round, 9/5/26, lane WORDS (words-8dqrnq). MODE: RESEARCH.
# The question, verbatim: "Grief speech. The cold open kills the sister. How real
# people talk in the first hour, the first day, the first month, and which games
# wrote it honestly. Test lines for the grief dinner."

## THE ANSWER IN ONE LINE
**The five stages of grief are not a finding about grieving people, and the most
common real pattern is not collapse but RESILIENCE, so a grief dinner where
everybody is sad for the whole scene is not merely dull, it is wrong.**

## 1. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG, AND IT WOULD HAVE CAUGHT ME
Kübler-Ross's five stages came from watching people who were DYING, not people who
were BEREAVED, and empirical testing has never supported them as stages of grief.
Bonanno's longitudinal work on thousands of bereaved people finds five patterns,
of which "common grief" is relatively INFREQUENT and **the most frequent is
resilience: stable low distress, about 46% in the landmark 2002 study.**

Left to my own instincts I would have written the dinner as a slow build of
sadness toward a break. That is the television version. **The evidence says the
most likely person at that table is functioning, eating, and handling it**, and
that writing them as visibly destroyed is both false and, worse, makes the ones
who ARE destroyed less legible by flattening everyone to the same note.

## 2. THE MECHANISM, AND IT IS THE MOST USEFUL THING IN THIS ROUND
**The Dual Process Model (Stroebe and Schut).** Grieving is not a direction, it is
an OSCILLATION. People swing, minute to minute, between LOSS-oriented attention
(yearning, remembering, looking at her things, imagining what she would say) and
RESTORATION-oriented attention (the mundane strains that follow a death: who does
the thing she used to do, the door, the money, the room). The model explicitly
includes **time off from grief**, where a person just relaxes, and treats that as
healthy rather than as avoidance.

**So the grief dinner oscillates. It does not build.** That is a structural
instruction, not a tonal one, and it is writable: the scene should cross the
subject and leave it, several times, and the leaving is not a failure to face it.

**And Bonanno on laughter:** more than half of bereaved subjects showed positive
emotion while recounting their loss, and those who genuinely laughed did BETTER
over the following years and drew warmth out of the people around them. "Context
sensitivity" -- being able to laugh at the funny thing and look sad at the sad
thing -- predicts recovery.

**A laugh at the grief dinner is not a relief valve for the player. It is the most
accurate thing in the scene.**

## 3. THE THREE TIMESCALES THE QUESTION ASKS FOR

    THE FIRST HOUR    Language goes PRACTICAL and small. Who has to be told. What
                      happens to the body. Where the keys are. This is not denial
                      and it should not be written as denial; it is a person doing
                      the only tasks that exist. The register is flat and the
                      sentences are short, which is the same shape Q2 measured for
                      any acute stress.
    THE FIRST DAY     Oscillation becomes visible. The loss surfaces, gets crossed,
                      and gets left, repeatedly. Tense slips: people speak about
                      the dead in the PRESENT and correct themselves, or do not.
    THE FIRST MONTH   The practical strains arrive and outlast the shock: her
                      things, her job, her half of everything. Most people are
                      functioning by now, which is exactly when others stop asking,
                      and that gap is the honest subject.

## 4. MEASURED AGAINST OUR OWN BUILD, AND THE HONEST ANSWER IS "IT IS NOT WRITTEN"

    marker                          lines   share of 1,834
    a stage-of-grief word               0    0.0%   (denial, closure, "the stages")
    logistics after a death             0    0.0%
    present tense about the dead        6    0.3%
    laughter or a joke                  4    0.2%
    the sister                          2    0.1%

**A death word matches 26 lines, but READING THEM shows most are dead batteries,
dead storefronts, a dead payphone and a dead band.** About five concern a person
dying, and roughly one is a grief line:

    "Couldn't carry it. She died with the words still in her mouth.
     I'll wonder about that for a long time."

And both "sister" lines are self-introductions in the belonging bank, not grief:
"I came back for my sister and I'll be gone", "a sister who isn't here anymore".

**So the grief dinner does not exist yet.** This round is a spec, not a critique,
and the one genuinely good thing to report is a negative: **we have imported ZERO
stage-of-grief vocabulary.** Nobody in this valley says closure, or denial, or
talks about processing anything. That is the single hardest habit to avoid and we
have never had it.

## 5. WHAT THIS SAYS TO DO, WHEN THE LANE RETURNS TO BUILD
1. **THE DINNER OSCILLATES, IT DOES NOT BUILD.** Cross the subject and leave it,
   three or four times. The leaving is the accurate part.
2. **SOMEBODY LAUGHS, AND IT IS NOT CALLOUSNESS.** Over half of real bereaved
   people do. Writing the table as uniformly devastated is the false version.
3. **THE FIRST HOUR IS LOGISTICS.** Who tells the block. What happens to her room.
   Short flat sentences, which is the same shape as any acute stress (Q2's rungs
   apply here unchanged).
4. **ONE PERSON AT THE TABLE IS FINE, AND THAT IS THE HARDEST LINE TO WRITE.**
   Resilience is the most common trajectory and the least written one. A character
   who is eating, functioning and not performing sorrow is the honest majority, and
   putting one at the table makes the person who is NOT fine visible.
5. **NEVER THE STAGE VOCABULARY.** No closure, no denial, no processing, no moving
   on. It is unsupported and it sounds like a pamphlet.
6. **PRESENT TENSE IS THE CHEAPEST TOOL WE HAVE.** "She keeps the good knife in the
   drawer." One slipped tense does more than a paragraph, and we already do this
   six times somewhere in the build.
