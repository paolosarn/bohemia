# THE BLIND SPOTS OF THE WHOLE ENCHILADA (coordinator 10/4/26, his question: 'what are my current blind spots?')
His words: "I'm trying to get this demo live... it still needs everything for an actual release, but as far as a demo is
concerned I just want it presentable and good looking; I want it to fit on an iPhone screen, and then fit differently when the
screen is flipped or on wide screen monitors, on console, or even Nintendo Switch... What are my current blind spots of this
whole enchilada? Let's keep working."

## THE BLIND SPOTS, HONEST, IN THE ORDER THEY WILL BITE A DEMO
1. NOBODY BUT YOU HAS PLAYED IT. No stranger has opened the demo on their own phone. The checker for 'a stranger opens it'
   exists; a stranger does not. The first stranger finds the dead end you did not. (RUN [release list] line 9; the demo in a
   friend's hands, 9/5, never happened.)
2. THE FIRST LOAD. The demo page is 6 MB, the city world 5.5 MB, the engine 5 MB: about 17 MB before the first frame. On a
   phone on cell data that is 10 to 30 seconds of a blank screen, and the loading screen you hate is what covers it. Nobody
   has measured the time to first tap on a real phone on cell data. (RUN [first load], new.)
3. WHAT THE DEMO ENDS WITH. There is no end. A demo needs a stop: a contract chain that closes, a day count, a last screen
   that says thanks and opens NOTES. Right now it runs until the stranger gets bored. (RUN [demo end], new.)
4. SAVE AND CONTINUE. The save survives a kill and a relaunch on paper; unmeasured on a phone since the loop was folded in;
   CONTINUE on the start screen is not built. A stranger who closes the app and loses the crew is gone. (release line 6.)
5. TWO SONGS AT ONCE, AND EVERY VOLUME. You heard it. One-song is on the run board and not shipped; the sounds you approved
   are still 'quieter please'. A demo with doubled music reads as broken in the first ten seconds. (RUN [one song].)
6. THE WORDS. Every line a stranger reads is a draft nobody has read back (42 draft tags in the demo page alone); the
   contracts, the posts, the front door's cards, the narrator. You voted the old lines 'boring asf'. The words chat is paused.
7. THE FRAME RATE THROUGH A FIGHT ON A REAL PHONE. The fight's real-pixel mode fell to 16 fps in the container and backs off
   to a lower ratio by a valve; nobody has run a whole fight on your phone with a counter. (release line 5.)
8. ORIENTATION, WIDESCREEN, CONSOLE, SWITCH. The screen classes are a claimed row, not built: today the game is laid out for
   a phone upright. Landscape, a monitor and a TV are three more layouts and a gamepad is a fourth input nobody has written.
   THE SWITCH TRUTH: the game is a web page; it runs anywhere with a browser (iPhone, iPad, computer); a Switch and the
   consoles have no browser a player can use, so console means a wrapper build later, not a web page. (62; RUN [screen fit].)
9. WHEN A STRANGER'S DEMO BREAKS, WE NEVER KNOW. You killed friend-watching on 9/5 (right). But without even a crash line
   sent on error, a broken demo on someone's phone is invisible to us; the NOTES button is the only channel and it is
   unmeasured. (your call; release line 10.)
10. THE FROZEN FIGHT IS STILL IN THE FILE. The alpha carries the old fight's code and tabs; it adds weight and a wrong door.
    Cutting it out is a size and a confusion win. (RUN [first load] does it.)
11. BALANCE BY A HUMAN. Twelve against the early band wins 3 of 4 by a bot. No person has played ten fights. The demo's
    difficulty is a guess until you or a stranger does.
12. THE PAPER. Every chat still spends most of a round on records, claims and items; that is credits. The one-file loop and
    the rebuilt fight shipped fastest because their paper was suspended (58). Doing that for the demo's whole cut is the
    biggest speed lever left and it is mine to pull.

## WHAT IS NOT A BLIND SPOT (you see it already)
The UI, the map's pixels, the valley edge, the settlement picture, the character art in the fight, the floor, the start
screen: all on the boards with owners.
