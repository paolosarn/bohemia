## HOW BATTLE BROTHERS DOES IT (rule 33; recall, marked)
The loot screen shows the stash and the found items side by side; 'loot all' fills what room there is; to take a better piece
with a full stash you drag your junk out and the better piece in, one at a time. No button does it for you. The screen's back is a
flat panel.

## THE RULINGS
244. OPTIMIZE LOOT
(a) A button, OPTIMIZE LOOT, on the end-of-fight screen beside LOOT ALL, shown when the stash is full and loot is left.
(b) One tap: every piece in the stash and on the ground is ranked by what it sells for at the nearest town (the same price the
    shop shows, rule 242); the cheapest stash pieces are left on the ground and the most valuable loot taken in their place, until
    nothing left on the ground is worth more than the cheapest piece kept.
(c) It never touches what his men wear or hold, their bag slots, a piece he marked KEEP (RUN TWO's KEEP), a job's item; counts
    (batteries, rounds, scrap) take no room anyway.
(d) It says what it did in icons: what it left and what it took, and how many batteries more the stash is worth (rule 224); an
    UNDO until he leaves the screen.
(e) THE GATE: a full stash and ten pieces of loot, phone and computer: one tap; the stash's total sell value rises to the most it
    can; nothing worn, held, bagged or KEPT moved; UNDO puts it back exactly.

245. THE AFTERMATH PLAYS BEHIND THE LOOT SCREEN
(a) The end-of-fight screen's black is gone: behind it, a live scene, zoomed in as close as it goes, at a lower opacity: his own men
    on the fight's own board where the dead fell, looting the bodies, walking between them, kneeling by a wounded friend, binding a
    cut, reloading, a smoke, two of them talking over what comes next.
(b) What men really do after a fight (research, cited: soldiers' memoirs and history on the minutes after a battle: the quiet, the
    shaking hands, checking on each other, stripping the dead, counting what is left), shown in the Crashbox HBO look.
(c) Dimmed to the contrast floor so every slot reads; drawn on open and kept; inside the phone's memory and frame line.
(d) THE GATE: after rolled fights, phone and computer: the screen's back is the fight's board with his surviving men animating
    aftermath acts; never black; text contrast at the floor; frame time inside the line.

246. EVERY FACE BREATHES
(a) Every portrait on screen moves a little, always: his crew and anyone with a portrait (keepers, speakers, faces in a card):
    breathing, a blink, the eyes moving, a weight shift; small enough never to pull the eye off the words.
(b) A man with a serious wound reacts to it: a wince, the breath catching, a hand to the hurt, more when it is fresh (rule 234's
    marks), easing as it heals.
(c) Thumbs too, as far as their size allows; it costs nothing he can feel (one shared clock, frames reused, 60 on a phone).
(d) THE GATE: on every screen with a portrait, phone and computer: the face changes over two seconds (pixels differ, breathing at
    a human rate, 12 to 20 a minute); a wounded man's face carries a reaction an unwounded one does not; frame time inside the line.
