# BOHEMIA COORDINATOR SCHOOL, ROUND 6 OF 10: THE DEMO LANDS (10/10/26)

## THE QUESTION
How do the best teams ship a demo that lands in a stranger's hands and holds, and how does one person hold a release line without shipping garbage or never shipping? Studied: Zukowski's Next Fest surveys, mobile and browser first-session data, the load budgets Poki and itch.io enforce for HTML5 games, how studios define "done" (zero bug bounce, exit criteria, go/no-go), the single release owner, and "a delayed game is eventually good" against "release early, release often." Our side: BEGIN took 75 to 86 seconds at phone speed before this round's cut and about 22 after it (records/BOHEMIA_THE_DEMO_BOOTS_LIGHT_RUN_10_10_26.md), DIRECTION counts 18 of 20 screens still wrong (records/BOHEMIA_THE_DEMO_VERDICT_TWO_10_10_26.md), and the release line has machine checks but no single owner's go/no-go. The search budget ran out mid-round and three primary pages (Poki, How To Market A Game, Think with Google) could not be fetched from this machine, so their numbers come from the search index and pages that quote them.

## WHAT I FOUND

1. **The median Steam demo is played for 14 minutes, not the 30 the developer designed.** Zukowski's survey of about 130 games: median 14 min, 30th percentile 10 min, 70th percentile 22 min, average near 20; developers most often said they built a 30 minute demo. The lowest revenue tier's demos had a median near 7 minutes; the top tier's 38 to 65. Source: https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/10/26/what-is-a-good-median-play-time-for-a-demo-benchmark/ Why it matters here: whatever we build, a stranger plays half of it; a median under 10 minutes is the warning light, and we do not measure ours at all.

2. **About 20% of people who play a demo wishlist it, and the rate is stable per game at 10 to 20%.** Zukowski's June 2024 survey of 40 developers. Source: https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/06/24/do-demos-help-earn-wishlists-steam-next-fest-june-2024/ Why it matters here: the demo's job is to convert the one in five who finish it; the other four are decided in the first minutes.

3. **Most wishlists do not come from demo players: 68 to 88% of a game's Next Fest wishlists came from people who never touched the demo**; the median fest take is roughly 800 to 1,100 wishlists. Source: https://presskit.gg/field-guides/analyzing-next-fest-results Why it matters here: the page, the title screen and the first picture do most of the selling; our title screen is a gold glitter logo on a grey plate (DIRECTION's screen 1).

4. **In 2021 the median demo got 2,834 downloads against 957 wishlists.** Source: https://howtomarketagame.com/2021/07/06/how-was-the-summer-2021-steam-next-festival-festival/ Why it matters here: a friend opening the link and closing it is the expected case, not the failure case.

5. **53% of mobile visits are abandoned if the page takes longer than 3 seconds to load**, from Google's 2016 study of more than 10,000 mobile domains, and bounce probability rises 113% as load goes from 1 to 7 seconds (Think with Google, neural net at 90% accuracy). Sources: https://www.marketingdive.com/news/google-53-of-mobile-users-abandon-sites-that-take-over-3-seconds-to-load/426070/ and https://thinkwithgoogle.com/intl/en-gb/marketing-strategies/app-and-mobile/mobile-page-speed-new-industry-benchmarks Why it matters here: our title lands at 1.3 seconds now, which clears this bar; BEGIN at 22 seconds does not.

6. **Akamai, Spring 2017, about 10 billion visits: a 100 millisecond delay hurt conversion 7%; a two second delay raised bounce 103%.** Source: https://www.akamai.com/fr/newsroom/press-release/akamai-releases-spring-2017-state-of-online-retail-performance-report Why it matters here: the cost of waiting is measured in tenths of a second by people with ten billion data points; we are arguing about tens of seconds.

7. **Poki's rule for HTML5 games: initial download not over 5 MB, 8 MB total, and players move to another game if loading takes more than 10 seconds; load progressively.** Sources: https://developers.poki.com/guide/web-engine and https://developers.poki.com/guide/requirements-quality Why it matters here: this is the one published browser game load law from a platform that lives on it; we are 6.7 MB and 22 seconds at phone speed, so the bytes pass and the processor fails.

8. **Poki's own case: Stickman Hook's 40 MB WebGL port had a median load of 29.5 seconds; Madbox rebuilt it in HTML5 at 6 MB and the median fell to 3.7 seconds, with a notable rise in plays.** Source: https://poki.com/blog/what-makes-high-quality-browser-game Why it matters here: a 30 second load is a known killer with a known cure, and the cure was a rebuild of the boot, not a smaller picture.

9. **itch.io's limits for HTML5: 200 MB per file, 500 MB total, about 1,000 files; staff say HTML5 projects should be treated like websites and big games should be downloads.** Sources: https://itch.io/t/1925786/what-is-the-maximum-file-size-you-can-put-in-itchio and https://itch.io/t/1257250/need-more-files-for-html5-upload Why it matters here: the platform that hosts the most indie browser demos tells you a slow browser game is a download wearing a costume.

10. **Mobile game median day 1 retention is about 22%; the top quarter on iOS reaches 31 to 33%; day 7 about 8%; day 30 under 3%** (GameAnalytics benchmarks as reported). Sources: https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/how-to-think-about-retention-in-games and https://maf.ad/en/blog/mobile-game-retention-benchmarks/ Why it matters here: even a good game loses three of four strangers after one session; the first session is the only one we are sure to get.

11. **Supercell's CEO named 1, 7 and 30 day retention as the key indicators, and the company kills games that miss its bar.** Source: https://www.pocketgamer.biz/1-7-and-30-day-retention-rates-are-key-says-tablet-first-supercell (The 40/20/10 rule of thumb at https://wnhub.io/news/other/item-48621 is a former marketing head's, not Supercell's.) Why it matters here: the best hold a numeric bar and ship or kill against it, not a feeling.

12. **Zero Bug Bounce (Microsoft): zero open bugs against pre-defined exit criteria; before it, a lockdown where check-ins need permission and bugs failing the criteria move to the next milestone; the "bounce" after is expected.** Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/archive/blogs/victor.pearson/zbb-zero-bug-bounce Halo's "zero bug release" still ships unimportant bugs: https://www.halopedia.org/Zero-bug_release Why it matters here: done is defined before the fixing starts and new work is locked out while the count falls; we add to the demo while 18 wrongs stand.

13. **One release owner.** Mozilla's release checklist gives Go or No Go to the project lead; a No Go is a formal stop notice and a Go is announced 24 to 48 hours ahead (https://wiki.mozilla.org/Mobile/Releases/Release_Checklist). Fogel's Producing Open Source Software calls it "dictatorship by release owner" (https://producingoss.com/en/stabilizing-a-release.html). A LeadDev talk's Release Captain owns go/no-go conditions, health checks and rollback and aggregates what feature owners report (https://leaddev.com/velocity/supporting-major-launches-staff-ic). Chromium freezes features two weeks before branch and moves dates on build quality (https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/da492f144feba8874ce6d25a9da170228763b21e/docs/process/release_cycle.md). Why it matters here: 26 lanes each mark their own row green; nobody says GO or NO GO in one line.

14. **"A delayed game is eventually good" is not a Miyamoto quote.** Earliest print: GamePro issue 111, December 1997, as an industry catchphrase; Origin's Siobhan Beeman believes she coined a version in 1996; the first Miyamoto attribution is a Usenet post years later. Sources: https://acriticalhit.com/solved-miyamoto-quote-late-game-bad-forever and https://videogamecanon.com/adventurelog/bite-sized-game-history-did-miyamoto-really-say-his-most-famous-quote The opposing law is sourced: Eric Raymond, 1997, "Release early. Release often. And listen to your customers," tested on fetchmail at a release every 10 days (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Release_early,_release_often). Why it matters here: "delay it until it is good" is folklore; the written practice is small releases to real users on a beat, with the bar held by numbers.

## WHAT THE BEST DO THAT WE DON'T

- **They ship under 10 seconds to play or they do not ship.** Poki's line is 10 seconds and 8 MB; Madbox rebuilt a whole game to get from 29.5 to 3.7 seconds. We went from 75 to 22 seconds at phone speed this round and called it progress. Poki would not list us.
- **They know their median minutes.** Steam hands every developer the demo's median playtime. We have no number for how long a friend plays before closing the tab; the NOTES button is the only thing that comes back.
- **They design for the half that gets played.** A 30 minute demo is played for 14. Our demo is nine screens and 18 of them are counted wrong; the best cut the demo to the three screens that hold and polish those.
- **They lock the door before they count down.** Zero bug bounce means exit criteria first, then lockdown, then the count falls to zero. We put new things on screen 3 while screens 1, 2, 4, 5, 8 and 9 stand wrong.
- **One named person says GO or NO GO in one line, and a NO GO stops every lane.** Mozilla, Chromium, Fogel and the Release Captain pattern all agree. Our release line is a list of rows each lane marks itself.
- **They know the page sells more than the demo.** 68 to 88% of wishlists never played. Our first picture is the one screen DIRECTION rates worst.

## THE CHANGES FOR THE COORDINATOR

1. **The release line adopts Poki's numbers as line 12's bar.** Title under 2 seconds, BEGIN ready under 10 seconds at 4x phone speed, under 8 MB downloaded before ready, measured by PLUMBER's FIRST LOAD on every push that touches the demo. Red on any one is a NO GO. Testable: the three numbers in the gate's output; today 1.3 s, 22 s, 6.7 MB, so one red.
2. **Lockdown on the demo.** From this round, a change enters the demo only if it closes a numbered row on DIRECTION's wrong list or a red release line. DIRECTION re-shoots the same nine screens each round; the "still wrong" count must fall every round or the round is a NO GO. Testable: 18 this round; next round's count is lower or the handoff says NO GO.
3. **The coordinator is the release owner and writes the GO line.** Every coordinator round ends with one line in the handoff: GO or NO GO, the one number or row that blocks, and the date it was written. A NO GO is a stop notice: no lane ships to the demo except against the blocker. Testable: the line exists in the handoff every round; its absence fails the handoff gate.
4. **Measure the median minutes.** The demo records time from BEGIN to close and puts it in the NOTES export and in a one-line beacon that lands in records. After the first five friends, the median is read. Under 10 minutes is the warning (Zukowski); under 3 means the demo ends where the friend quit, and that screen goes to the top of the wrong list. Testable: a median exists after five friends.
5. **Two lines, not one.** The FRIENDS line is Raymond's: three named friends get the link the first round the GO line reads GO, told up front that it loads in about 20 seconds, and a fresh cut follows every seven days with their minutes read. The STRANGERS line is Poki's: nobody outside the three gets the link until BEGIN is under 10 seconds and the wrong count is under 5. Testable: the names of the three, the date of each cut, the two counts.

## ONE RULE
**TEN SECONDS OR IT IS NOT A DEMO.** A browser demo that takes longer than ten seconds to reach play on a phone is a download wearing a costume, and the release owner says NO GO in one line until the number is under ten.

## FOR PAOLO, IN PLAIN WORDS
The sites that live on browser games say a game must be playable in ten seconds or people leave, and we are at about twenty on a phone after this round's cut, down from seventy five. From now on one person says GO or NO GO in one line each round, nothing new goes into the demo until the list of wrong screens gets shorter, and we will measure how many minutes each friend actually plays. Three friends can get the link soon with a warning about the wait; everyone else waits until it loads in ten.
