## WHERE IT SITS IN THE LAWS

second. Rule 213: one phone in one place whose screen changes (the combat-time animation). Rule 130: the UI the Crashbox way

## THE RULING (rule 229)
(a) NOTHING CUTS: every change between the map, a town, a fight, the camp, the crew screen and back is a TRANSFORMATION: the pieces
    that exist on both sides MORPH into each other (the top bar into the fight's bar, a job card on the board into the card on the
    phone, the shop's item flying onto the man who buys it, the phone's map apps folding into his abilities when a fight starts, the
    town shrinking back into its place on the map); what leaves goes out the way it came; what is new grows out of something already
    on screen, never from nowhere.
(b) SMART, NOT SLOW: a transition never blocks a tap (input is live through it and a tap lands at its end state), it is short (UI
    motion in the range modern design systems use, a few hundred milliseconds; a mode change a beat or two at 120 BPM), it lands on
    the beat, it can be skipped by tapping, and the settings' REDUCED MOTION turns every morph into a quick fade.
(c) OUR LOOK: the morph moves like the rest of the game: Crashbox stop-motion (held frames, the tin bending, a rivet popping into
    place), analog horror in the grain, never a glossy app slide.
(d) THE RESEARCH FIRST: how the best interface systems do it (shared-element and container transforms, motion that shows where a
    thing came from and where it went, timing guidance), cited; never a game he has not named as a reference (rule 6).
(e) THE GATE: on phone and computer, every listed change (map to town and back, map to fight and back, the shop's buy, the job's
    take, the camp, the crew screen) plays a morph with no frame where a shared piece vanishes and reappears; a tap during any morph
    is never lost; each fits its time budget; REDUCED MOTION gives fades.
(f) MOTION (lane 25, opened for it); UI, UI TWO, RUN, RUN TWO, RUN THREE, COMBAT and CAMP hand MOTION the pieces on their screens
    (NOTEs); line 151 on his list; EYES watches every change.
