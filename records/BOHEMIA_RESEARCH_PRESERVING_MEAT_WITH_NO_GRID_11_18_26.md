## THE CLOCK
- Raw meat in the heat is a race. Between about 40 and 140 F the germs on it can double roughly every 20 minutes. The plain
  rule: two hours out is the limit, ONE hour above 90 F. In a Las Vegas summer a fresh kill is turning within an hour or two and
  is rotten by the next day. So the question is never 'can it be kept' but 'did somebody start in the first hour'.
- GROUND meat spoils fastest of all: grinding spreads the germs from the outside all the way through. A grinder without salt and
  smoke makes food that must be cooked and eaten that day.

## THE WAYS, CHEAPEST FIRST
| Way | What you need | How long | How long it keeps | Who can do it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DRY IT (jerky, the oldest way) | a knife, string or wire, a window screen against flies, sun and moving air; salt if you have it | 1 to 2 days in desert heat, sliced a quarter inch thin | weeks to months kept dry; loses about two thirds of its weight | anybody; this valley is hot and dry, about the best place there is for it |
| SALT IT | salt: roughly a pound for every 10 to 20 pounds of meat for a hard cure | days to weeks | months | anybody with salt |
| SMOKE IT | a small smouldering fire under hanging meat; any closed space: a shed, a tent, an oil drum | 1 to 2 days | weeks to months; the smoke also keeps the flies off | anybody with wood and a drum |
| POUND IT INTO FAT (pemmican) | dried meat beaten to powder, mixed half and half with melted fat | a day once the meat is dry | a year and more; the most food per pound a man can carry | anybody who dried the meat first |
| SAUSAGE THAT KEEPS | ground scraps, heavy salt, a casing (the gut), then smoke or weeks of drying | days to weeks | months | somebody who knows how; done wrong it kills |
| CAN IT | jars, lids, a pressure cooker, 75 to 90 minutes of steady heat a batch | hours | years | few; needs gear and a lot of fuel |
| KEEP IT COLD | a freezer and power: about 1 kWh a day, which is about 400 AA batteries of charge EVERY DAY | no time | as long as the power lasts | only whoever owns sun and a working freezer |

## WHAT DECIDES IT IS SALT, NOT FIRE
- Fire helps (smoke dries, coats and keeps flies off) but in a desert the sun already does the drying. What a household runs
  out of is SALT and the first hour.
- WHERE THE SALT IS IN THIS VALLEY: every pool store and many garages hold 40-pound bags of pool salt and water-softener salt,
  which are almost pure table salt; one bag hard-cures 400 to 800 pounds of meat. Past that, the dry lake beds.
- So yes: everyday people can keep meat, cheaply and fast, with a knife, a screen, the sun and a bag of pool salt. Cold storage
  is the rich man's way and it burns money every day.
