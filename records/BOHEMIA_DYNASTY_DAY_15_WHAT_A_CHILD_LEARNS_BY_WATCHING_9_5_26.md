## 1. THE ROW'S OWN PREMISE, CORRECTED IN A USEFUL WAY
The row says "what a child learns by **WATCHING** a parent work". The learning
research says watching is not the mechanism, and the correction is the finding.
The standard account of how people actually pick up a trade is **legitimate
peripheral participation**: a newcomer is allowed to do **real but peripheral**
work inside a working community, and moves inward over time. The authors are
explicit that observation is the smaller half:
> *"newcomers' legitimate peripherality provides them with MORE THAN AN
> 'OBSERVATIONAL' LOOKOUT POST: it crucially involves PARTICIPATION as a way of
> learning -- of both absorbing and being absorbed in -- the 'culture of
> practice'."*
The load-bearing word is **LEGITIMATE**. The child of the tradesman is not
learning because they saw more. They are learning because they were **allowed in**
-- handed the small real job, present while the work was argued about, in the
room when the decisions were made.
> *** THE CHILD DOES NOT INHERIT WHAT THEY SAW. THEY INHERIT PERMISSION TO BE
> THERE. ***
A child watching the same work from outside the door gets a fraction of it. That
distinction is the entire answer to this row, and it is also why the answer is a
**DOOR AND NOT A STAT**.

## 3. THE GAMES AISLE -- AND IT AGREES, LOUDLY
"How games have made a next generation start with something the parent earned" is
meta-progression, and the design writing on it is unusually unanimous. The stated
core problem is that permanent upgrades must not trivialise the thing the game is
actually about. The player complaints are blunt: *"grinding for things is not
fun"*, *"there's nothing exciting or interesting about permanent stat boosts
between runs... it's inelegant"*, and incremental upgrades turn a challenge into
a grind. And the stated preference:
> **PLAYERS PREFER PROGRESSION THAT UNLOCKS NEW CONTENT AND OPTIONS RATHER THAN
> STAT BOOSTS.**
That is day 9's horizontal growth and day 10's access-not-power, reached
independently for the third time in this study. **Both aisles, on this row, say
the same thing: the inheritance is a door.**

## 5. *** THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG ***
### WE HAVE THE DOOR AND WE HAVE THE DEEDS AND NOTHING TURNS ONE INTO THE OTHER
Our machine already converts **what you did** into **what people say about you**:
that is the whole standing web, and rounds 8 and 14 measured it working. What it
has never done is convert **what you did** into **what your child is allowed to
learn**.
```
BUILT   deed -> witnessed -> retold -> reputation -> inherited as reputation
BUILT   trade -> will answer these subjects and refuse those
MISSING deed -> which trades count your family as one of theirs
```
> **THE HEIR INHERITS A REPUTATION AND NOT AN APPRENTICESHIP, AND THE
> APPRENTICESHIP IS THE ONE THE RESEARCH SAYS ACTUALLY TRANSFERS.**
### AND IT ANSWERS THE ROW'S EXACT WORDING
"What the heir should START with because of what the player DID, not what they
owned." The carry list (round 13) was the OWNED axis. This is the DID axis, and
the answer is one sentence: **the trades your parent worked with are the trades
that will answer you.** Not a skill, not a stat, not an item. A door that is open
because of a life somebody lived, and which the game already knows how to open.

## 6. WHAT WE TAKE AND WHAT WE REFUSE
**TAKE**
- **The door, not the stat.** Which trades will answer the heir, decided by what
  the parent actually did. Both aisles agree and the machine is built.
- **LEGITIMATE, not observational.** The child was let in, not just present. That
  is why the inheritance is permission rather than knowledge.
- **The refusal is content too.** The asking module already ships one refusal per
  trade. A trade that will NOT talk to the heir is as informative as one that
  will, and it costs nothing new.
- **Peripheral first.** The real pattern is small real jobs moving inward, which
  is a shape for act 2's opening rather than a stat block.
**REFUSE**
- **A starting skill, a starting stat, or a starting bonus.** Round 5, round 10,
  round 13 and the players quoted in section 3 all refuse it from four
  directions.
- **A knowledge screen or a tech tree.** The asking module's own rule is that
  nothing resolves and every answer asks a sharper question. A tree would resolve.
- **Inventing which trades exist or what they know.** Four trades and seven
  subjects are already his content, and the destinations ship empty on purpose.
- **Filling DEED_WEIGHT.** It ships empty because what counts as a deed is his.
- **A grind.** The row says "because of what the player DID". A door that opens
  because you repeated something is a grind; a door that opens because of a
  particular thing you did is an inheritance.
