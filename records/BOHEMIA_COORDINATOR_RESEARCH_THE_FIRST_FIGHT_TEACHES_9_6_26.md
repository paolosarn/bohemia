# THE FIRST FIGHT IS THE ONLY TUTORIAL WE GET (coordinator research swing, this round)

## THE QUESTION
Enemies landed this round, and the demo now contains a real fight. A stranger who
opens the link meets that fight with no manual, no onboarding, and a phone in one
hand. What has to be true about the FIRST one so they learn the game by surviving
it instead of reading about it?

## AISLE ONE: THE RESEARCH
- **The level IS the tutorial.** The strongest practice is to build the teaching
  into the first encounter itself rather than bolt a lesson on beside it.
- **Progressive disclosure.** One idea at a time, revealed only when it matters.
  The famous first screens teach movement, then a threat, then a reward, in that
  order, without a single word.
- **Active learning beats being told.** An obstacle the player can only pass by
  understanding the mechanic makes them keep it. Trial and error is the point,
  not the failure.
- **Diegetic or nothing.** A lesson that makes sense inside the story does not
  break the world; a text box does.
Sources: [Game Developer: teaching game mechanics, a hierarchy of learning](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/teaching-game-mechanics-a-hierarchy-of-learning),
[Game Developer: how to train players right](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-train-players-i-right-i-so-they-don-t-hate-learning-to-play),
[Rempton Games: convey information through design](https://remptongames.com/2018/09/22/no-more-tutorials-how-to-convey-information-through-design/),
[Pause Button: how video games introduce their mechanics](https://pausebutton.substack.com/p/how-video-games-introduce-their-mechanics),
[AtDelfi (arXiv): automatically designing legible instructions for games](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.04375).

## AISLE TWO: OURS, MEASURED
We have four rules a stranger must learn in their first minute of combat and NONE
of them is taught by anything:
1. NO DAMAGE BEFORE THE DIAL -- the beat is the timing, at 120 BPM.
2. It is always a GROUP, never a duel.
3. A tile is a HOUSE, so a pistol reaches one and a rifle two.
4. The companion takes her own turn.
The fight now starts where you stand (street or the room you walked into), the
beat exists at the front door (SOUNDS shipped it this round), and the fight
readout is one number (UI shipped it this round). The pieces are all here. The
ORDER nobody has authored.

## THE FINDING THAT CHALLENGES US
Our instinct has been to make the first fight FAIR. The research says the first
fight should be UNFAIR IN ONE SPECIFIC WAY: it must contain exactly one obstacle
that cannot be passed except by using the thing we want learned, and nothing else
new. Ours should teach THE BEAT and nothing else: one enemy who is untouchable
off the beat and trivial on it, in a street where there is nowhere to hide, so
the player finds the beat by failing twice. Group, range and companion come after,
one per encounter. A first fight that teaches four things teaches none.

## ROUTED
- COMBAT [first fight]: author the first encounter as a lesson in the beat alone.
- SOUNDS [beat teaches]: the beat is audible and unmissable during that fight.
- UI [no text box]: nothing in the first fight is explained in words.
