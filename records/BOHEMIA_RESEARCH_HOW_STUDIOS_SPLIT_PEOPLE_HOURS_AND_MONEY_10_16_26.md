# BOHEMIA RESEARCH: HOW STUDIOS SPLIT PEOPLE, HOURS AND MONEY (10/16/26)

Research only. Nothing here is a ruling. Written for the coordinator, to answer one question:
how do real studios divide time, headcount and money across departments, how does that differ
between a 3-to-20-person studio and AAA, and what does that say about a 26-lane fleet fronted
by one director.

Method note. Every number below was surfaced through web search on 10/16-10/17/26. Direct page
fetches were blocked from this container, so quotes come from search excerpts of the cited pages,
not from reading the full page. Where a number is a blog estimate rather than a studio's own
disclosure, it says so. Where I could not find a number, it says NOT FOUND rather than guessing.

---

## 1. THE FIVE NUMBERS THAT CARRY THE MOST WEIGHT

1. Art is the biggest department on a AAA team: 30 to 50 percent of heads, engineering 20 to 35,
   QA 10 to 20, design 5 to 15, production 5 to 10 (industry-blog estimate, Kevuru Games, 2024-25,
   https://kevurugames.com/blog/game-development-team-structure-average-team-sizes-and-production-statistics/).
   The one hard credits number that backs the QA share: Red Dead Redemption 2 credited 383 QA
   testers against roughly 1,600 direct developers, about 19 to 24 percent (Kotaku via Berfrois,
   2018, https://berfrois.com/?p=58129; team size per Wikipedia and Guinness,
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Red_Dead_Redemption_2).
2. Small 2D teams are NOT art-heavy by headcount. Owlboy: 1 art director out of 5 (20 percent).
   Dead Cells: 2 artists out of 7 developers (29 percent). Yacht Club's founding six: 2 artists,
   3 programmers, 1 director (33 percent). Celeste: 2 artists out of 6 (33 percent). Sources in
   section 3. Art runs 20 to 35 percent of heads at this scale, with code 33 to 50 percent.
3. Battle Brothers was made by a core of THREE plus a few freelancers (managing director Jan Taaks,
   Turn Based Lovers interview, https://turnbasedlovers.com/10-turns-interview/with-battle-brothers-developer/).
   Hades: about 20 people in 2020, 23 by April 2021, up from 7 on Bastion and 12 on the two games
   between (Film Stories 2020, https://filmstories.co.uk/gaming/hades-interview-were-holding-nothing-back/;
   AFP via TechXplore 2021, https://techxplore.com/news/2021-04-paradise-hades-maker-gaming-goliaths.html).
4. Producers run about 1 per 20 heads, and there is no fixed ratio. Certain Affinity: 120 people,
   6 producers (Kotaku, 2016, https://kotaku.com/what-a-video-game-producer-actually-does-1772519753).
   Roles consolidate by size: at 2 to 10 people one person is producer and director; at 10 to 30
   they separate; at 30 to 80 there is a producer layer (Nasty Rodent production guide,
   https://nastyrodent.com/game-production-roles/). Managers do best with 5 to 9 direct reports and
   average 9 (Quantum Workplace, Namely, Gallup; section 6).
5. Marketing is 30 to 45 percent on top of development at AAA. Shadow of the Tomb Raider:
   $75 to 100 million to make, about $35 million to promote (David Anfossi, Eidos Montreal, 2018,
   https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/news/67035/shadow-of-the-tomb-raider-estimated-to-have-cost-75-to-100-million-to-develop/).
   The Witcher 3: 306 million PLN, about $81 million, development AND marketing together, 240
   in-house, 1,500 people total, about 3.5 years (CD Projekt H1 2015 presentation,
   https://www.cdprojekt.com/en/wp-content/uploads-en/2016/03/H1_2015-English_-_final.pdf;
   PC Gamer 2015, https://www.pcgamer.com/the-witcher-3-wild-hunt-cost-81-million-to-make).

## 2. HEADCOUNT BY DISCIPLINE AT AAA

### 2a. What the surveys say (who answers, which is a proxy for who is employed)

- IGDA Developer Satisfaction Survey 2014, primary discipline across the sample: programmer 33
  percent, designer/scripter 27, visual artist 17, QA 9, audio 4 (2014,
  https://s3-us-east-2.amazonaws.com/igda-website/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/11144410/IGDA_DSS_2014-Employment_Report.pdf).
  Caveat: survey respondents skew toward people who answer surveys; artists are under-counted
  relative to credits.
- GDC State of the Game Industry 2025, roles respondents hold (multi-select, does not sum to 100):
  game design 35, programming 34, production and team management 31, visual arts 20, business 19,
  narrative 17, community/marketing/PR 14, QA 11, audio 10 (2025,
  https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry).
- IGDA 2023 summary report exists but the extracted text was garbled; not used
  (https://igda-website.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/01161842/2023-04-14_IGSA-DSS-2023_SummaryReport.pdf).

### 2b. What credits and studio statements say

- Red Dead Redemption 2: about 2,000 Rockstar employees touched it, 1,600 directly on development
  (producer Rob Nelson via Wikipedia and Guinness, 2018,
  https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/557449-largest-development-team-for-a-videogame).
  Credits listed 383 QA testers (Kotaku via Berfrois, https://berfrois.com/?p=58129). That is the
  only per-discipline credits count I could confirm for any of the named AAA titles. Discipline
  splits for Horizon Zero Dawn, God of War 2018 and The Witcher 3 credits: NOT FOUND in search.
- The Witcher 3: 240 core in-house, 18 nationalities; 1,500 people globally over about 3.5 years
  (MCV 2015, https://www.mcvuk.com/development/cd-projekt-spent-53m-developing-the-witcher-3).
  So in-house was 16 percent of total credited heads; 84 percent was outsourcing, localisation,
  QA vendors and publishing.
- Horizon Zero Dawn: Guerrilla had about 250 permanent staff at launch, with part-timers added
  during development, then planned growth to 400 (DualShockers 2017,
  https://www.dualshockers.com/following-success-horizon-zero-dawn-guerilla-games-greatly-increasing-staff-size/).
- FromSoftware: about 400 employees, about 300 in development, 200 to 230 at peak on Elden Ring,
  the same on Armored Core 6, staff shift between projects (producer Yasunori Ogura via PC Gamer,
  2023, https://mp1st.com/news/fromsoftware-created-elden-ring-and-armored-core-6-with-a-300-strong-team).
  Development is 75 percent of the company; the other 25 percent is publishing, admin, support.
- Larian: over 400 people on Baldur's Gate 3 across seven studios, ten times the Original Sin team;
  earlier "over 200 internal, over 300 with externals" (Gamereactor 2022,
  https://www.gamereactor.eu/baldurs-gate-is-being-developed-by-a-team-of-400-people/;
  Game World Observer 2022,
  https://gameworldobserver.com/2022/03/24/larian-grew-10-times-since-original-sin-although-swen-vincke-never-thought-studio-would-need-400-people).
- Breath of the Wild: started with fewer than 10 people, a prototype shown to seniors, then grew;
  roughly 300 at Nintendo at the very end, plus "over 100" from Monolith Soft (Zelda Dungeon,
  https://www.zeldadungeon.net/?p=21252; My Nintendo News 2017,
  https://mynintendonews.com/2017/05/14/aonuma-on-zelda-breath-of-the-wilds-300-dev-team-and-seeing-fan-videos-online/;
  Nintendojo, https://www.nintendojo.com/news/monolith-soft-assisting-on-breath-of-the-wild).
  Aonuma: the 300-person organisation "was really made at the very end"; keeping everyone on one
  vision was the hardest part.
- Nintendo, the "a third programmers" shape: Super Mario World core staff was 5 people plus 1
  character designer and 3 main programmers, about 10 in all (Miyamoto, 1990,
  https://shmuplations.com/supermarioworld/). Super Mario Bros. was 7 or 8 people (Miyamoto 1989,
  https://shmuplations.com/miyamoto1989/). Super Mario 64 was about 15, with artists and
  programmers in separate groups (Giles Goddard,
  https://pixelatron.com/blog/the-making-of-super-mario-64-full-giles-goddard-interview-ngc/).
  So 3 of 10 programmers on SMW is the real number; the "one third" line is a fair reading of it,
  not a quote.
- Star Citizen: of 464 employees in 2017, 397 were development (86 percent) (Variety 2018,
  https://variety.com/2018/gaming/news/star-citizen-budget-accounting-1203093928/).
- Hellblade: average team of 20 for three years inside a 100-person studio (Ninja Theory on
  PlayStation Blog 2017,
  https://blog.playstation.com/archive/2017/08/07/ninja-theorys-retrospective-as-hellblade-senuas-sacrifice-releases-tomorrow-on-ps4).
- Ask a Game Dev: about 20 people at vertical slice, growing to 100 to 200 in production for a
  typical $50 million AAA game (2014,
  https://askagamedev.tumblr.com/post/105543803526/what-is-the-average-team-size-for-developing-a-aaa).
- IndieDevGames approximate AAA headcounts: programming 20 to 40, art 30 to 50, design 15 to 25,
  QA 20 to 30 (unsourced blog,
  https://indiedevgames.com/decoding-game-development-team-roles-and-structures-a-deep-dive-into-aaa-teams-and-their-influence-on-the-gaming-industry/).

### 2c. Reading across them

The art-heavy AAA team is real but only at the content stage. The hard numbers that exist say:
in-house core 200 to 300; total credited heads 5 to 7 times that; QA about a fifth of direct
developers on the biggest open world ever shipped; development 75 to 86 percent of a company,
the rest publishing and admin.

## 4. MONEY

### 4a. Published totals and splits

- Star Citizen / Cloud Imperium, 2019: $70.4 million spent. Salaries $39.7 million (56 percent),
  publishing and marketing $11.4 million (16 percent), contracted development $5.6 million
  (8 percent), other development costs $8.9 million (13 percent) (Nosy Gamer summary of CIG's own
  statement, 2020, https://nosygamer.blogspot.com/2020/12/cloud-imperium-games-2019-financial.html;
  CIG's own page https://cloudimperiumgames.com/blog/corporate/cloud-imperium-financials-for-2019).
  2020: $74.8 million, headcount up 15 percent, payroll only $4.2 million of the $10.4 million
  increase (https://nosygamer.blogspot.com/2021/12/cloud-imperiums-2020-financial-statement.html).
  2017: over $4 million a month, 464 employees, 397 development (Variety 2018). A split by
  department (art versus engineering): NOT PUBLISHED by CIG; only the category split above.
- Shadow of the Tomb Raider, 2018: $75 to 100 million production, about $35 million promotion
  (Anfossi, GamesIndustry.biz via PCGamesInsider). Marketing is 35 to 47 percent of development.
- The Witcher 3, 2015: 306 million PLN, about $81 million, development and marketing combined,
  40 marketing campaigns; the company's slide does not separate the two. The widely quoted
  "$32 million dev, $35 million marketing" split is from an opinion piece and is unverified
  (PC Gamer, https://www.pcgamer.com/the-witcher-3-wild-hunt-cost-81-million-to-make).
- Hellblade, 2017: under $10 million (press estimate, USGamer), 20 people, 3 years, break-even at
  500,000 units in 3 months, over $13 million revenue in that window (Gematsu 2017,
  https://gematsu.com/2017/11/hellblade-senuas-sacrifice-sales-top-500000).
- Shovel Knight, 2022: the whole Treasure Trove series cost $9 million over 110 months and 16 team
  members; studio burn rate $5 million a year; about $40 million net revenue (Game Developer 2022,
  https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/in-depth-yacht-club-gets-transparent-with-sales-numbers).
  $9 million over 110 months is about $1 million a year for a 2D pixel-art studio of 16.
- Kingdom Come: Deliverance, 2014: Kickstarter raised £1,106,371 from 35,384 backers against a
  £300,000 goal, after $1.5 million seed from one investor; total cost about 750 million CZK,
  $36.6 million (Gamereactor, https://www.gamereactor.eu/kingdom-come-deliverance-reportedly-cost-366-million/;
  Kickstarter, https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/1294225970/kingdom-come-deliverance).
  The Kickstarter was about 4 percent of the final cost. The campaign's category pie chart:
  NOT FOUND in search excerpts.
- Stardew Valley: one person, no budget line, 4.5 years at 10 hours a day, about 16,000 hours
  (Wikipedia).

### 4b. Rules of thumb that circulate (not disclosures)

- QA: "the 10 percent rule", about 10 percent of a mid-sized project's budget to QA (Game
  Developer, https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/how-much-people-time-and-money-should-qa-take-part1).
  Software-wide starting point: 1 tester per 3 to 4 developers (Functionize,
  https://functionize.com/blog/how-many-developers-does-it-take-to-test-a-product).
- Audio: 5 to 15 percent of budget per vendor blogs (Twine, https://www.twine.net/blog/game-composer-pricing/).
  Not survey data. God of War Ragnarok is a rare case of 50 or more audio people
  (A Sound Effect, https://www.asoundeffect.com/gameaudioexplained/).
- Marketing for indies: 2 to 10 percent of dev budget for small campaigns up to 30 to 50 for high
  ambition, spent 70 percent during production and 30 at launch (Tavrox via Steam Page Analyzer,
  https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/indie-game-marketing-budget). Agencies say 30 percent
  and they sell marketing. Zukowski's 2025 data says genre and visibility events, not ad spend,
  made the top 3 percent of Steam releases.

## 5. HOW A PROJECT'S TIME SPLITS BY PHASE

No GDC talk with a published percentage came up. What exists:

- Blog and estimator consensus: pre-production 10 to 20 percent of the schedule, production
  40 to 60, polish and QA 15 to 20, launch prep 5 to 10 (Ocean View Games,
  https://oceanviewgames.co.uk/resources/game-development-timeline-estimator; Game Design Skills,
  https://gamedesignskills.com/game-development/stages-of-game-development-process/; Nasty Rodent,
  https://nastyrodent.com/pre-production-in-game-development/). QA and polish is the phase most
  often underestimated.
- Mark Cerny's Method (DICE 2002): pre-production is finished when you have a "publishable first
  playable", a polished slice that shows exactly the game; pre-production "is not just a bad idea
  to schedule, it's impossible"; all the risk belongs in pre-production so production is clean
  execution; "you will spend a million dollars before you know what your game is" (VGC,
  https://www.videogameschronicle.com/features/who-is-mark-cerny/; Mobile Free To Play,
  https://mobilefreetoplay.com/why-games-fail-pre-production/).
- Real shape: Breath of the Wild under 10 people to prototype, 300 at the end. Ask a Game Dev:
  20 at vertical slice, 100 to 200 in production. Both say the team is small until the slice is
  proven, then it grows 10 to 30 times.
- Certification: 3 to 6 weeks of cert prep, platform review 4 to 7 business days on a clean build,
  a failed submission resets 4 to 8 weeks (vendor blogs, https://snoopgame.com/blog/pass-certification-first-attempt/;
  https://www.juegostudio.com/blog/console-certification-failures-outsourced-games). For a web
  demo this phase is near zero; for a phone store it is real.

## 6. PRODUCTION RATIOS, SPAN OF CONTROL, AND WHO TALKS TO THE DIRECTOR

- "There really isn't a general ratio" of producers to developers; Certain Affinity had 6 producers
  for about 120 people, 1 per 20 (Kotaku 2016,
  https://kotaku.com/what-a-video-game-producer-actually-does-1772519753).
- Roles by studio size: 2 to 10 people, one person is producer and director; 10 to 30, they
  separate; 30 to 80, a producer layer (https://nastyrodent.com/game-production-roles/).
- Naughty Dog has no producers and no formalised sign-off process; developers organise themselves
  (Richard Lemarchand, DICE 2010,
  https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/dice-2010-naughty-dog-s-lemarchand-on-why-the-studio-has-no-producers).
  After Evan Wells retired the studio added a head of production and design and a head of
  operations (SI 2023, https://videogames.si.com/news/naughty-dog-leadership-changes).
- Span of control outside games: no magic number (CEBMa 2021); engagement peaks at 8 to 9 reports,
  studies range 5 to 9 up to 9 to 12 (Quantum Workplace,
  https://www.quantumworkplace.com/future-of-work/whats-the-optimal-span-of-control-for-people-managers);
  managers average 9 direct reports (Namely, https://namely.com/blog/best-number-of-direct-reports/);
  two thirds of managers oversee fewer than 10 (Gallup,
  https://www.gallup.com/workplace/700718/span-control-optimal-team-size-managers.aspx).
- Motion Twin's cooperative said growing past 8 to 10 people "would have to start having managers",
  so they spun off Evil Empire instead (Destructoid 2019).

## 7. HOW DIRECTORS WITH BIG TEAMS PROTECT THEIR TIME

- Miyamoto, 2020: he no longer "upends the tea table" mid-development; he raises ideas early and
  only reworks when he can see how the whole game pans out; "you can't see all the key components
  unless you're the director" (Famitsu via GoNintendo,
  https://gonintendo.com/stories/356342-miyamoto-has-moved-on-from-upending-the-teatable-when-a-game-is).
  Aonuma calls the late review "the Miyamoto Test" (https://zeldadungeon.net/wiki/Upending_the_Tea_Table).
- Nintendo's gate before scaling: BOTW's under-10-person prototype went to senior staff before
  the team grew (Zelda Dungeon). The director sees a slice, not every asset.
- Kojima is the counter-example: he checks models, backgrounds, lighting, camera, sound and effects
  daily and plays the PS4 build daily in the critical phase (Metal Gear Informer 2017,
  https://www.metalgearinformer.com/kojima-is-checking-death-stranding-and-sharing-images/;
  TechRadar 2019, https://www.techradar.com/news/death-stranding-is-in-a-critical-phase-according-to-director-hideo-kojima).
  That works with about 120 core developers at Kojima Productions (VGC,
  https://videogameschronicle.com/news/more-than-half-of-death-strandings-team-defected-from-konami),
  and it is the whole job: he directs nothing else.
- Santa Monica, 2026: Cory Barlog moved from game director to Head of Creative, guiding the series
  vision while game directors (Ariel Lawrence) run the games; on Ragnarok he was creative director
  while Eric Williams was game director (Push Square 2026,
  https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2026/06/cory-barlog-now-head-of-creative-at-god-of-war-dev-guiding-the-long-term-vision-of-the-series).
  The vision holder talks to directors; directors talk to leads; leads talk to the floor.
- Supergiant: Kasavin holds creative direction and writing, Rao holds production, design tuning
  and operations. Two people split "director" and "producer" even at 20 heads
  (https://www.supergiantgames.com/team/).
- Naughty Dog: two directors (Druckmann story, Straley tech) shared one game; "too much authority
  can leave a leader surrounded by yes men" (Wells, LA Times 2016,
  https://www.pressreader.com/usa/los-angeles-times/20160528/282084866051373).
- A weekly or milestone cadence for director reviews at a named AAA studio: NOT FOUND as a quote.
  The generic creative-director day that circulates (mornings for creative reviews, afternoons
  for stakeholders) is a consultancy template, not a studio account
  (https://www.icreatives.com/iblog/what-does-a-creative-director-actually-do-day-to-day/).

## 8. APPLIED TO A 26-LANE FLEET

The fleet: run, world, life+city, combat, combat two, character, direction, cook, cook two (ground),
cook three (people), cook four (places), sounds, people, factions, ui, words, economy, dynasty,
animation, eyes-and-ears, plumber, tuning (research), mods (research), portrait, quests (research),
run two. One human director, one coordinator chat, one VOTE tab.

### 8a. What the studio numbers say about this shape

- The product is Battle Brothers-shaped: that game shipped from 3 core people plus freelancers.
  Hades shipped from 20. Shovel Knight's whole series from 16 over nine years. Twenty-six
  departments is a 200-to-400-person AAA org chart (FromSoftware, Guerrilla, Larian) wrapped
  around an indie-shaped product. No studio in sections 2 or 3 ran 26 departments; FromSoftware's
  300 developers sit under one development division that shifts between projects.
- By studio headcount proportions the fleet is art-heavy. Art and animation lanes (direction, cook,
  cook two, cook three, cook four, portrait, animation, character) are 8 of 26, 31 percent, right
  at the AAA floor of 30 to 50 and at the top of the small-2D range of 20 to 35. Engineering and
  pipeline lanes (run, run two, plumber) are 3 of 26, 12 percent, under the 20 to 35 AAA band and
  far under the 33 to 50 percent of heads that Owlboy, Yacht Club and Dead Cells put on code.
  Design and systems lanes (world, life+city, combat, combat two, economy, dynasty, factions,
  people) are 8 of 26, 31 percent, double the 5 to 15 AAA design band. QA and measurement
  (eyes-and-ears) is 1 of 26, 4 percent, against the 10 percent rule and RDR2's 19 percent.
  Research-only lanes (tuning, mods, quests) are 3 of 26, 12 percent, and have no equivalent in
  any studio table; studios fold research into the lead's own time.
- Lane count is not hours. The split that matters is where the day's hours go, below.

### 8b. A proposed split of a day's hours, from the numbers

The industry rule when the build is unstable is Cerny's: you are still in pre-production until
there is a publishable first playable, and in pre-production the risk is in engineering and the
core loop, not in content volume. The current state per the handoff is a demo whose build has
been the standing complaint. So the honest split is engineering-leaning now, swinging art-heavy
once the build is stable, which is exactly the BOTW and Ask-a-Game-Dev curve (small team on the
slice, 10x team on content).

Proposed, while the build is unstable (the pre-production profile):

| Department (lanes) | Share of the day's hours | Why this number |
|---|---|---|
| Build, pipeline, stability (run, run two, plumber) | 35 percent | Code is 33 to 50 percent of heads in every small 2D team in section 3; Cerny puts all risk here before production |
| Art and animation (direction, cook x4, portrait, animation, character) | 25 percent | Under the AAA 30 to 50 band on purpose; content volume before the slice is proven is the Owlboy failure mode (rewrite after demo) |
| Design and systems (combat, combat two, world, life+city, economy, dynasty, factions, people) | 20 percent | Above the AAA 5 to 15 band because the game is systems-first (Battle Brothers is a systems game), below the fleet's 31 percent of lanes |
| QA and measurement (eyes-and-ears) | 10 percent | The 10 percent rule; RDR2 ran 19 percent |
| UI, words, sound (ui, words, sounds) | 7 percent | Audio 5 to 15 percent of budget per vendor blogs; UI and words are small lanes in every credits roll |
| Research only (tuning, mods, quests) | 3 percent | No studio budgets a research department; a lead reads on their own time |

Proposed, once the demo is stable and fun on a phone (the production profile):

| Department (lanes) | Share of the day's hours | Why this number |
|---|---|---|
| Art and animation | 40 percent | The AAA production band of 30 to 50; a 2D game's content is drawn |
| Build, pipeline, stability | 20 percent | The AAA engineering band of 20 to 35, low end, because the engine is done |
| Design and systems | 15 percent | Top of the AAA design band |
| QA and measurement | 12 percent | The 10 percent rule plus polish, the most underestimated phase |

Both tables are proposals built from section 2 to 5 numbers. They are not a ruling.

### 8c. What a studio would say about one director fronting 26 departments

- Nobody does it. Managers do best with 5 to 9 direct reports and average 9 (section 6). Twenty-six
  reports is about 3 times the span any studied organisation runs. The industry answer, in every
  studio in section 7, is LEADS plus a PRODUCER: the director talks to 4 to 8 leads, the leads
  talk to the floor, and one producer owns the schedule and the list so the director never does.
- At 26 lanes and the Certain Affinity ratio (1 producer per 20), the fleet should have 1 to 2
  producers. The coordinator chat is already that producer. It should stay the only chat the
  director reads, which matches the structure law already in force.
- At 26 lanes and a span of 5 to 9, the fleet needs 4 to 6 LEAD lanes between the coordinator
  and the rest: one art lead (direction, already named the art director, over cook x4, portrait,
  animation, character), one engineering lead (run over run two and plumber), one design lead
  (combat over combat two, world, life+city, economy, dynasty, factions, people), one
  presentation lead (ui over words and sounds), one QA lead (eyes-and-ears), with the three
  research lanes reporting to whichever lead they serve (tuning to combat, mods to run, quests to
  world). That is 5 leads plus a producer, the Santa Monica shape (head of creative, game
  directors, leads).
- The director's review is a slice at a milestone, not a department at a time. Miyamoto's 2020
  method (raise ideas early, judge when the whole is visible), Nintendo's gate (the prototype goes
  to seniors before the team grows) and Barlog's 2026 role (vision over directors) all say the
  same thing: the director sees the build, votes, and leaves. The VOTE tab is that room. Kojima's
  daily everything-check is the exception and it costs the whole of his day with 120 people; it
  does not scale to a director who also has a life outside the game.
- The thing a producer would say first: the fleet has more departments than a 3-person game needs
  and fewer hours on the build than any 2D studio in section 3 puts there. Cut the number of
  things the director hears about to 5 leads and 1 producer, and move the day's hours toward the
  build until the first playable is publishable.
