## 0. THE QUESTION, IN ONE BREATH
A price you hear about before you travel is what makes a day's travel worth a price (Day 68). The
phone feed in the city view is where he asked to 'learn more about the world' (Paolo 9/4), and the
overworld is Battle Brothers (9/24). So: how does Battle Brothers decide what a tavern tells you
about the towns around it and how far that reaches; what did price news do in the real record once
it started travelling faster than the goods; and what, on main, could carry it. Candidates for
WORDS, UI and RUN TWO; none picked.

## 1. HOW BATTLE BROTHERS DOES IT, FROM THE WIKI ITSELF
Sources: reference/library/grok/wiki/PAGES_ALL.tar.gz, bb_all/: 1931 Tavern Rumors (1718 Rumors
redirects there), 1763 Settlement buildings, 0616 Dev Blog 138, 2128 Update 1.4, 1415 News and Dev
Blogs (Progress Update, Taverns and Tracking, 12 February 2016), 1966 The Minstrel, 1940 The Agent,
0827 Game Guide, 1191 Loading Screen Tips.

**1a. ONE FREE, FOUR BOUGHT.** Page 1931, verbatim: 'Each time you visit the Tavern you get 1 free
rumor. You can buy drinks for the patrons to get up to 4 more rumors. Paying for 1 rumor costs 20
crowns x buying price multiplicator of the settlement and you gain +0,1 Relations with the
settlement.' At ten crowns to the battery that is TWO batteries a rumour in a calm town, and five
heard for eight batteries a visit. A round for YOUR OWN men is a different service on the same
counter: 'Buy drinks to your soldiers to boost their morale ... Prices scale proportionally depending
on the number of men' (page 1763), with a chance to get them drunk (the 2016 blog). Two buttons: the
room's round buys news, the crew's round buys spirits.

**1b. WHAT IT PICKS.** Page 1931: 20% a Contract Rumor 'if available', 20% a Location Rumor, 20% a
Named Item Rumor, 40% the B pool (General, Military, Civilian or Settlement); any of the first three
that has nothing to say falls to B. A contract rumour 'names a settlement that has 1 or more
Contracts available. The rumor wont create new Contracts.' A rumour never makes the thing it tells:
it reads what is already on the map.

**1c. THE NEIGHBOUR'S SITUATION IS A RUMOUR SINCE 1.4.** Update 1.4 (2128): 'Added tavern rumors
about situations of nearby settlements, including some hinting at the presence of particular
beasts, as well as rumors specific to each of the late game crises, and reduced chance of pure
flavor rumors slightly.' Dev Blog 138 (0616) gives the why: 'tavern rumors now also include hints
of settlement situations of nearby villages and towns, and less flavor responses, so that you get a
better idea of trading, recruiting and work opportunities nearby. For example, if you're set on
hunting Unholds to wear their furs as cloaks, tavern rumors can now point you to villages where the
giant creatures have been sighted.' The design moved flavour OUT to make room for news you can act on.

**1d. HOW FAR IT REACHES.** Named item rumours: 'The map has a size of 140x140. The rumor can reveal
named item locations with a range of up to 30 from the settlement' (21% of the map's width).
Location rumours: 'There is no range limitation for the reveal', and 'there is no guarantee of
treasure, even if the rumor said so'. Situation rumours: 'nearby villages and towns', no number on
any page. The 2016 blog on directions: 'The level of directions that individual people can give will
differ, so it's sometimes useful to get directions from several people in order to better pin down
where a location might be.' A second mouth is how you get surer.

**1e. FAR NEWS IS A LATE BUY.** The Agent follower (1940), 4,000 crowns and 90 relation with a noble
house or city state: 'Reveals available contracts and active situations in the tooltip of
settlements no matter where you are.' So by default a far town's situation is learned by going there
or by a tavern; knowing every town at once is an end-of-act purchase (400 batteries at ten to one).
The Minstrel (1966, 2,000 crowns, visit every settlement) 'makes Tavern Rumors more likely to contain
useful information'; the page's note says its rumours are then only contract, location and named
item ones (the note does not say whether it predates 1.4's situation rumours).

**1f. THE FLAVOUR FOLLOWS THE PLACE.** The 2016 blog: 'These types of responses depend heavily on
where you are, so you'll more likely be talking with soldiers at castles, and fishermen at fishing
villages.' And the wiki's own rumour texts carry their source and their doubt in the mouth: 'A
traveler told me the other day', 'Sounds like hogwash to me', 'Don't take my word for it', 'Maybe
someone else can tell you more'.

## 2. WHAT THE REAL RECORD SAYS: WHEN THE NEWS OUTRUNS THE GOODS, THE GAP CLOSES
- KERALA, 1997 TO 2001 (Jensen, 'The Digital Provide', Quarterly Journal of Economics 122(3),
  2007). Before the phones a boat chose one beach market blind and sold there, because fish does
  not wait; 5 to 8% of the catch was thrown away on days a beach had too much. As the phones came,
  the gap between beach prices fell to about a quarter of its size (the CORE textbook's summary),
  the waste went to nothing, fishermen's profit rose about 8% and the buyer's price fell about 4%
  (press summaries of the paper). The boat called ahead.
- NIGER, 2001 TO 2006 (Aker, American Economic Journal: Applied Economics 2(3), 2010). As the phone
  towers came, the gaps between grain markets narrowed 10 to 16%, and MORE between markets where
  the road was dearer: news is worth most where the walk costs most. (Aker and Fafchamps 2015 found
  a smaller effect on what farmers were paid.)
- THE 1866 CABLE (Steinwender, American Economic Review 108(3), 2018; the Richmond Fed's account).
  Before it, New York's cotton quote trailed Liverpool's by a week or more, the time a ship took.
  After it the average gap fell from 2.56 to 1.65 pence a pound, about a third, and how much it
  jumped around fell by more than 90% (her VoxEU column). Before the cable the news was exactly as
  old as the road it came down.
- LEBANON, 2020 (SMEX, the digital-rights group's reporting). The bank rate stayed at 1,507.5 lira to the dollar while
  the street rate hit about 4,000 in late April; on 30 April the prosecutor ordered 28 rate apps
  blocked for 'false information'. The caretaker prime minister said the apps did not reflect the
  real rate but had become 'a reference for cashiers and traders'. The phone in the hand had become
  the price board. It kept working through a VPN.
THE SHAPE, four times: news that travels faster than the goods shrinks the gap between places, and
whoever has it first collects what is left of the gap. Public news closes the gap for everybody; a
private tip keeps it open for one buyer.

## 3. OURS, MEASURED ON MAIN
**3a. THE BAR TELLS ELEVEN THINGS, THE SAME ELEVEN IN EVERY TOWN.** records/target/settlement_rumours.json
(RUN TWO [the bar]) holds 11 lines: 2 place, 3 faction, 1 gear, 5 keeper. Not one of the 11 names
any of the region's 22 settlements (exact match against engine/BOHEMIA_REGION_LAND.json's places and
inValley). Not one tells a town's situation. Two touch a price (copper 'paying in real batteries';
car doors 'real cheap') and neither is a place on the map. nextRumour() in
slices/BOHEMIA_SETTLEMENT_SCREEN.html picks a fresh line by the place's name and the night and reads
no other town, no trait and no distance; S.heardIds is cleared on every visit (open(), 'a new visit,
a new night at the bar'), so every bar in the valley draws from the same eleven every time.

**3b. THE NEWS IS ALREADY THERE, ROLLED BY THE GAME'S OWN DICE.** A town's rolled situation is a
pure function of its name and the week: traitsFor() in engine/bohemia_livingmap.js (lines 175 to
186; the screen's rollTraits rolls the same line for line), chance_any 0.7 and period_days 7 from
records/target/settlement_traits.json, and only two rolled cards in the pool now that raided,
conquered and well supplied come from FACTIONS' ledger (sickness, market day). Run over the 22
settlements with a screen (the 21 'base' places of WORLD's region land plus Henderson) for 52 weeks:
- 1,144 place-weeks; 791 carry a rolled situation (69.1%): sickness 540, market day 487.
- Battle Brothers' item-rumour reach scaled to ours: 30 of 140 tiles is 106 of our 496 cells, 40.7 km.
  Every settlement has at least one other in it (mean 4.36; Pahrump, Searchlight and Echo Bay one each,
  Sloan ten).
- In 1,080 of the 1,144 place-weeks (94.4%) at least one town in reach has a situation to tell; 4,433
  tellable situations in the year. The bar told 0. The ledger's caused traits (a real raid, a town
  taken, a convoy in) are on top of this and are not told either.

**3c. AT OUR DISTANCES THE NEWS IS STILL TRUE WHEN YOU GET THERE.** WORLD's region land: 384 m a
cell, 5.376 minutes a cell on a road and 10.752 on open ground on the 1,440-minute day, so 268 cells
a day by road and 134 on open ground. Every settlement's nearest neighbour is 6.3 to 31.6 km away:
0.06 to 0.31 days by road, 0.12 to 0.61 on open ground. A situation holds for its week. A teller who
walked here and a crew that walks back spend twice the trip: for the farthest nearest-neighbour on
open ground that is 1.22 days of a 7-day week, so the news is still true on arrival at least 83% of
the time; at the reach's edge (106 cells, 0.79 days each way) 77%; both before counting a re-roll
that lands the same card. Battle Brothers' rumour, which names what is on, is honest at our scale.
The 1866 problem (news as old as the road) is a far-region problem here, never a next-town one.

**3d. THE MARK NOBODY DRAWS.** When a rumour carries a mark, the bar prints 'Marked on your map:'
and posts {act:'rumour', mark} to its parent. Three of the 11 carry one: PUMP CAMP, THE TOWER,
COPPER SHEDS. The map's two listeners for the screen's messages (slices/BOHEMIA_CITY_WORLD.html:
the loop's handler reads the act's name, the battery count, a contract and 'leave'; the raid
handler reads only act 'take' and the place); no code on main (the city page, the demo, engine/) reads a rumour's mark. And PUMP CAMP and
COPPER SHEDS are not a name anywhere on the city page, the region land, the demo or the engine; only
THE TOWER is. The screen promises a mark on the map that nothing draws, two of three to a place that
does not exist.

**3e. THE PRICE OF HEARING IS SIX TIMES BATTLE BROTHERS' FOR A FULL COMPANY.** Battle Brothers: one
free a visit, then 20 crowns times the buy multiplier, two batteries in a calm town. Ours: the bar
has no free one; the rumour rides the crew's round, a battery a man (TUNING's one table,
bar.round_price, 'typed, not read from the table'): 3 for three men, 12 for twelve, against two. A
passer-by tells one a third of the time (openPerson: 0.35 a face). Rule 117(a) says start easier
than Battle Brothers; for the rumour we start dearer, and we have merged its two buttons into one.

**3f. THE PHONE CANNOT SAY A TOWN'S NAME.** engine/bohemia_feedstream.js, the one stream the phone
reads, takes its prices from the city page's own shelf (feedWorld: one price per good, from
mktShelf) and posts 'food is up to two batteries. it was one battery last week.' with no place in
it. The settlement screen's situations and the bar's rumours never reach it: the map's listener
logs the act and syncs the purse. So the feed cannot carry 'they are coughing up the road' because
nobody hands it a town, and it only streams in the city view (his 9/23 vote).

## 4. CANDIDATES (NONE PICKED; WORDS', UI'S AND RUN TWO'S TO CHOOSE)
- A. THE KEEPER'S FREE ONE. Every visit the keeper says one thing about one town in reach: its
  rolled or caused situation, in that town's terms, from his own mouth with his face (rule 32).
  Battle Brothers' free rumour, and the easier side of rule 117: no price to hear the first.
- B. TWO ROUNDS, TWO BUTTONS. The crew's round stays morale, priced by heads; a round for the room
  buys up to four more rumours at a flat price, the patrons' round of page 1931. The rumour stops
  costing twelve for a full company.
- C. THE PHONE SAYS WHAT HAPPENED, THE BAR SAYS WHAT IT COSTS YOU. The feed posts a town's
  situation as news everyone reads (public, so by the Kerala case it is not an edge); the keeper's
  rumour carries the edge (the dear medicine, the market to sell into). Realism and Battle Brothers
  agree here: the public half moves nothing, the private half pays.
- D. THE AGE IN THE MOUTH. The teller says where it came from and when ('a trucker, this morning').
  At our distances it is hours old, so this is dressing and trust, never a timer on screen.
- E. THE SECOND MOUTH. Heard in two places, it is surer (the 2016 blog's 'directions from several
  people'): a rumour a second keeper repeats is marked as sure, one mouth is 'maybe'.
- F. THE STREET RATE. Lebanon's apps as our swag: on the cracked iPhone in the city view, a post
  from a money-changer handle of what a battery buys in a town this week (rations, not percents);
  it is the one place a NUMBER about another town could live, and it is UI's and WORDS' to judge.
- G. THE MARK, OR NO MARK. Either the map draws a rumour's mark at a place WORLD's land names, or the
  bar stops promising one. A line that says 'marked on your map' and marks nothing is a lie told to
  the player by the interface, not by a person.

## 5. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG
Battle Brothers added the neighbour's situation to the tavern because the map got bigger and the
player needed to know where to go (1.4, Dev Blog 138). Ours got bigger the same way (rule 109): 22
settlements, a rolled situation in 69% of their weeks, a town in reach with something to tell in
94% of visits, 4,433 tellable situations in a year, and the bar has told none of them; three of its
lines promise a map mark nothing draws, two of them to places that are not on main; and the phone
that was meant to teach the world posts prices without a town's name. The news exists and is free
to read (one pure function of a name and a week); the mouths that should carry it read a fixed list.
