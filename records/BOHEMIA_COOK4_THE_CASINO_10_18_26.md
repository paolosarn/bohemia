## What it is
The valley's first casinos were ranch houses grown big: El Rancho Vegas (1941) and the Last Frontier (1942)
opened on Highway 91, the road that became the Strip, as low western buildings under shingle gables, with the
sign on the roof over the entrance (El Rancho's windmill, seen on top of the building in UNLV's 1950s photos).
So the straight-roof family reaches the casino without stretching. At 57 px a metre, 29 m:
- the gaming hall: 26 m of his flat tan render (Wall tiles 1 #7) to a 4.5 m plate and NOT ONE WINDOW, as no
  casino has one; two of his steel doors (4. #9) as fire exits along the blank wall, each under a strip of his
  steel plate to 2.1 m; one measured tan roof against one storey (as the warehouse, school and church);
- three of his condensers on the roof (an air-conditioned hall is what made a desert casino possible);
- the entry, 6.5 m from the road end: six half-glass doors, each his window glass whole (5. #0 and #3, his sill
  cut off, a door has none) over his steel kick plate (Wall tiles 1 #10) to 2.3 m, in a shaded reveal;
- the marquee: the family's flat deck in his grey-tan (slate #27 recoloured in round 6, its eave #15) seen from
  above, cantilevered 2.4 m off the wall at 3.6 m, its notches showing the deck's dark underside as on the gas
  station's canopy; its fascia is the reader board, his steel plate with the louvre of his vent (Wall tiles 1
  #8) laid along it, so the board's tracks run empty: the letters are gone;
- the sign: on the roof over the doors, a 6 by 3 m cabinet of the plain half of his framed steel panel (Wall
  tiles 1 #13), framed top and foot with that panel's rim, its top 4 m over the ridge, on two legs of the same
  panel rising from the marquee through the roof. Its face is blank: the packs hold no lettering, and a name in
  the valley is his.

## The one wrong thing (analog horror)
THE DOORS ARE OPEN. Every door the family has drawn is boarded, chained, barred or down; the casino's middle
pair stands open on a black room with no windows, and the man stands in it. It is grounded: the public doors of
the old casinos were built never to lock; when Nevada shut them in March 2020, downtown's El Cortez had no locks
built in and its staff went looking for padlocks and chains, and South Point's crews wrapped chains round the
handles (Las Vegas Advisor and the 2020 local reporting). Nothing is painted for it but the dark where two
leaves were, the jamb's shade and the sill.

moved from 20.5 m to 19 m, over the cell. Then 0 red. The marquee is 8.6 m long, never a walk the length of the

## WEAK
- The sign is a blank steel box: a Vegas sign reads by its shape and its letters, and the packs hold neither a
  lettered face nor a bulb; a name is his (identity), and WORDS' text if he gives one.
- The reader board's louvre reads close to a vent grille; a real board is a pale panel with dark tracks. No
  pale tracked panel exists in the packs.
- The glass doors are his windows on a kick plate: half-glass doors of the forties, not the full glass of a
  later casino.
- The legs are 0.5 m of steel panel; a real roof sign stands on a braced steel frame. No frame or truss in the
  packs.
- No porte-cochere: at the family's 45 degrees a 6 m deep drive-under canopy hides the doors (measured: its front
  edge sits 75 px above the wall foot), so the entry wears a shallow marquee instead.
