## What it is
The 1960-1980 Las Vegas light-industrial warehouse (Industrial Road, Western Avenue), at 57 px a metre, 23 m:
- the shed: 18 m of his light stone panels (Wall tiles 1 #5, the nearest pack surface to tilt-up concrete)
  to a 6 m plate, panel joints in shade between the bays, six small high windows under the eave (5. #0, #3,
  one boarded #6), under one measured tan roof;
- the dock: 1.2 m high (a truck bed), 2 m deep, his pale cracked slab (Floor and wall tiles #22) as the face
  in shade and the top seen from above, its edge catching the light;
- three dock doors of 3 x 3.6 m at 5.5 m centres, standing on the dock, each one roll-up cut from his
  shutter (11. #1, the family's one_roller_door);
- the office on the end: 5 m, 3.2 m, flat render (Wall tiles 1 #7), the door a man fits under its transom,
  the barred window (5. #19), its own measured tan roof; one bin (15. #21); the man at the door;
- the yard fence past the shed's end: three panels of his chain link with his KEEP OUT (the family's fence).

## The one wrong thing (analog horror)
Every door is down and the yard is dead, but the office's barred window is lit warm from inside: the dark
glass behind the bars repainted toward a lamp's amber, the bars left dark. Light is paint we are allowed.

## What it is not
No pallet, no drum, no truck: every pack prop is a 96 px box, so at 1:1 they are giants and at any other
scale they mix pixel sizes (rule 114). WEAK: no stair up to the dock (the packs hold none), no dock bumpers,
stone panels where real tilt-up is smooth, the roof pitched where many real ones are flat behind a parapet
(the family's roof is his ruling).
