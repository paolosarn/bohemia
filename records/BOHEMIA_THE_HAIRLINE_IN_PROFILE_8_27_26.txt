--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.  EVERY NUMBER I HAD SAID HE WAS WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Before touching anything I wrote a measurement pass over all fifteen canon styles. It
reported:

    bare forehead in profile                 0px on all 15
    typical row width                        11px
    style/facing pairs with a break          1 of 45

Green, green, green. All three were asking a question that was NEARLY the right one:

    THE FOREHEAD   it walked only the rows ABOVE the face part -- two rows, side-on --
                   and took the best of them. The bald patch is BESIDE the face.
    THE WIDTH      it took the BOUNDING BOX of a row. A row with hair at both ends and a
                   hole between measures eleven pixels wide and renders as two lines.
    THE BREAK      it called a row unbroken if ANY pixel on it was hair. The break is
                   HORIZONTAL -- the fall lets go of the head -- and every row involved
                   still has hair somewhere on it.

SECOND TIME IN THREE DAYS. On 8/25 an edge-parity audit read 50.9% "already native" over
nine styles that were solid blocks, because it was measuring the outline and the shape is
the inside. So this is a rule now, not a coincidence:

    WHEN A NUMBER DISAGREES WITH HIM ABOUT A PICTURE, GO AND LOOK AT THE PICTURE, AND
    THEN FIX THE RULER.

What found all four was rendering every canon style in every direction and LOOKING -- his
own PLAYTEST DISPATCH (8/25, item 3) said hair goes to reference first, all 8 directions,
before another cook, and I had not done it.
    slices/look/hair-reference.png   (LOOK tab)

2.  CAUSE 1 -- SIDE-ON, A FACE IS MOST OF A HEAD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The part grid in profile:

    row 10-11   ########                  the crown: all skull
    row 16-25   ####FFFFFFFFFFFF          FOUR columns of skull, TWELVE of face

The head is 16-18px across side-on and only the back 4-6 are the SKULL part. Forehead,
temple, ear, cheek and jaw are all painted as FACE, because side-on the face IS most of
the head. put() refuses hair on a face pixel on any facing that is not `back`, except
above the style's forehead line.

So hair got the top fifth of the head plus a four-pixel strip down the back. That is BOTH
of his first two complaints out of one rule -- a hairline that stops a fifth of the way
down is "balding back", a four-pixel vertical band is "a single line going down". They
arrived in one sentence because they are one bug.

AND THE 8/2 FIX ALREADY KNEW THIS AND DID HALF OF IT. That turn taught sideBot that
"profile joins back" -- how far DOWN hair may reach -- and left put(), which decides what
it may reach ACROSS, still believing a face part is a face.

FIX: hair may paint the face part behind a per-row front limit -- a real hairline. Down to
the style's forehead depth, receding over three cells to a temple line, then holding, then
pulling back over the ear toward the nape. The style's `front` dial drives the temple line
(a fringe keeps 43% of the width for the face, a slicked-back head 55%), so the dial that
meant "how far down the forehead" now also means "how far forward at the temple" -- the
same fact about the same haircut from two sides. 8/25 clause 1.

THE FIRST CUT OF THAT RECEDE KEYED OFF THE STYLE'S LENGTH DIAL AND LOOKING KILLED IT.
`side: 0.30` says a buzz cut does not HANG below a third of the head; it does not say the
scalp stops there. BUZZ CUT came back as a four-pixel strip -- the exact bug being fixed.
I had reached for the nearest dial instead of asking what the dial meant. The ear is a
fact about the head, so the recede is measured on the head.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.  CAUSE 2 -- A LOOP WITH TWO BOUNDS HAS TWO PLACES TO BE WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The front fall is two curtains. The loops run from `mn` to `fs[0]+cw`, and from
`fs[1]-cw` to `mx`.

An earlier fix -- correct in intent, and its comment says exactly the right thing, "HAIR
HANGS FROM THE WIDEST PART OF THE HEAD, NOT FROM THE CHIN" -- widened `fs` to the whole
head on the last rows of the skull, and left `mn`/`mx` where the mass loop had put them,
which on those rows IS the chin.

The left loop started to the right of where it was told to stop. The right loop stopped to
the left of where it started. BOTH RAN ZERO TIMES.

    LONG LOOSE facing S     rows 16-29  drew hair
                            rows 30-31  DREW NOTHING AT ALL
                            row  32     picked up again

Two blank rows right at the jaw. The hair let go of the head and started again at the
shoulder. That is his sentence as a pair of numbers.

FIX: both bounds move together, and they can only ever widen.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.  CAUSE 3 -- A NAPE IS WHERE HAIR ENDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

From behind the last two rows of the skull draw inward to a nape. His 8/1 ruling, and
right -- for hair that ENDS there. It was drawing on every back view however long the
style was.

    LONG LOOSE facing N     rows 28-29 drew 6 pixels
                            row  30    drew 11

The mass pinched to a point at the jaw and flared straight back out underneath it. A WAIST
IN THE MIDDLE OF THE HAIR, which is what "breaks in the middle" looks like when the break
is a narrowing rather than a hole. Same mistake as cause 2 on the other side of the head.

FIX: the taper only draws when the hair actually stops there. Crops, buzzes and bobs are
untouched.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.  AND ONE THING ONLY LOOKING CAUGHT, AFTER THE FIX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

With hair covering the side of the head, the strand-separation shadow turned into RULED
HORIZONTAL LINES across the crown. Its drift stepped once every four pixels, which was
invisible when the mass was four pixels wide and reads as a straight line with two kinks
across fourteen. A SEGMENT LENGTH HAS TO BE SMALL AGAINST THE THING IT IS BREAKING UP, and
this one stopped being small when the mass grew. Halved. No new travel.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The five that remain are short styles on the FRONT facing -- SLICK BACK, SALT CROWN, GREY
WISPS -- whose crown band and temple hair meet only along the outline. Nothing floats and
he did not name them. Recorded, not quietly capped.

NOT ONE HAIRCUT WAS DELETED, SHORTENED OR RENAMED. All three past-shoulder styles still
hang 6+ rows below the head, which the gate checks separately, because a haircut that is
whole BECAUSE IT STOPPED AT THE JAW would pass every other test.

THE 56 WARDROBE MOVED ON PURPOSE. 220 of 1744 pinned hashes, ALL genHair, no other
generator touched. gates/clothes_56_pin.txt now carries a REBASELINE LOG in its header
saying who ruled and what changed -- the pin exists so 4x work cannot disturb approved
art, and it was never meant to outrank him (A GATE MUST NEVER OUTRANK A RULING, 8/1).

7.  THE GATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  gates/hairline_gate.js     12 passed, 0 failed

  1  the ruler proves its own eyes first -- runs on a bald head and asserts a bowl cut
     moves 60+ pixels of the profile render. Three of four numbers in the first version
     of this measurement reported green on a build broken in four visible ways.
  2  no style is balding back in profile          (>= 33% of the browline is hair)
  3  no style in profile is a line going down     (>= 11px in a typical row below the brow)
  4  no piece of hair floats off the head         (flood fill; 6+px, 2+ rows = a piece)
  5  no fall chokes at the neck                   (narrowest / widest >= 0.45)
  6  the past-shoulder styles are whole from every angle, AND still hang past the jaw
  7  this law records the CAUSE, not just the symptom

  The measurement body is imported from tools/bohemia_hair_the_four_complaints.js, so the
  gate and the report cannot drift into disagreeing about what a break is.

  MUTATION-PROVED BY HAND, three separate mutations, each caught by a different check:
    A  put() forgets the profile hairline    -> brow collapses to 25%, 11 styles named
    B  the curtain's two bounds move apart   -> 6 loose pieces, 2 chokes, 11 split
    C  the nape taper draws on every back    -> 6 chokes at 0.36-0.42
  A gate that still passes with the feature deleted is not a gate.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.  THE LESSON UNDERNEATH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

TWO OF THESE THREE BUGS WERE HALF-FINISHED FIXES, AND BOTH LEFT A CORRECT COMMENT
STANDING OVER INCORRECT CODE.

  8/2   wrote "profile joins back" and taught it to ONE of the two places that needed it.
  the curtain fix  wrote "hair hangs from the widest part of the head, not from the chin"
                   and moved ONE of the two bounds that decide where it hangs.

Both would survive any review that reads the comment and believes it. Only the pixels
disagreed, and only somebody looking at them would know.
