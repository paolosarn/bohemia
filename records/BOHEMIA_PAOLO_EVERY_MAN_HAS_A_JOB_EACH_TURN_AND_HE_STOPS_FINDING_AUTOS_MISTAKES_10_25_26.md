## THE RULING (rule 188)
(a) EVERY MAN HAS A JOB EACH TURN, FROM WHAT HE HOLDS: a melee man's job is to get onto a tile beside an enemy by the
    shortest walkable path, around houses, walls and his own men, and to strike; a shooter's job is a tile with sight and
    reach (height when it pays, rule 176(f)); a shield man screens the shooters while closing in. A man is never idle while
    any move brings him closer to doing his job; if no tile this turn gets him there, he takes the step that gets there
    soonest, never stands behind a house.
(b) THE STUPID-MOVE WATCH, EVERY ROUND (he stops being the one who finds them): TUNING plays many AUTO fights a round on the
    live fight, every crew shape he uses (rifle plus two spear-and-shield men, the Lone Wolf, a full twelve), and counts every
    move a good player would call wrong, by class: idle while useful moves exist, stuck behind cover from the fight, the
    off-and-back dance, a melee man who never reaches, a shooter who never gets sight, the key man ignored (rule 182), a wasted
    shot. The counts are written each round, worst class first, as rows on COMBAT's board; COMBAT fixes them worst first; the
    counts only go down. EYES watches AUTO fights by eye in its playtest and files what it sees too.
(c) The fix is always the rule the brain reads (rule 182(d)), never a patch for the one fight he saw.
(d) THE GATE: in the watch's fights, zero turns where a melee man with a reachable enemy ends farther from every enemy than
    the best path allows, and the idle count near zero.
