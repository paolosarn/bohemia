# ANALOG HORROR — THE REGISTER (DIRECTION, 9/20/26)
# PAOLO 9/20, to the art direction chat, verbatim: "Analog horror"
# Two words, recorded the turn they arrived (NOTES ARE RULINGS). This card

# allowed to live, and what it must never touch, so no lane guesses and no

## 0. WHAT HE NAMED
Analog horror is the genre built from DEAD BROADCAST TECHNOLOGY: the
emergency-alert card, the corrupted transmission, the tape that has been
copied too many times, the CRT glowing in an empty room, the voice that
keeps announcing to nobody. Its dread is that the MACHINES ARE STILL
TALKING after the people stopped listening. That is not an imported mood
for this game - it is already Bohemia's premise. 2050 crashed; the
signage, the screens and the speakers of our own era are still up and
still running on whatever power is left. THE HORROR IS THAT THE OLD WORLD
KEEPS BROADCASTING.

## 1. THE ONE LAW OF THE REGISTER: DIEGETIC OR DEAD
Analog horror in Bohemia is IN THE WORLD, never ON THE LENS.
- The horror comes from OBJECTS: a CRT in a dead storefront, an
  emergency-broadcast card on a casino marquee, a PA speaker that still
  makes the announcement at the same hour, a tape someone copied until
  the faces smeared, the phone's own dying glass.
- NEVER A FILTER: no scanlines, static, chromatic fringe, vignette or
  tape-warp over the game frame itself. The act-1 rules already ban
  filters and fades (the phone card, the act cards, the cloud rule) and
  this register does not reopen them. A screen INSIDE the world may show
  static; the world itself never does. The player's eye is an eye, not a
  camcorder.
- This is the same discipline as the phone (the feed's crack never
  crosses the text) and the cloud (one mechanism, value only): the effect
  belongs to the thing, not the frame.

## 2. WHERE IT LIVES (the three homes, and no fourth)
1. DEAD MEDIA OBJECTS, act 1: CRTs, marquees, PA horns, tape decks -
   pixel-native by style card 2C, drawn by the 7/27 craft rulers, one
   material two-three readable parts (PROP-02). A lit screen in a dark
   room is the register's strongest single image and it obeys the phone
   card's glass rules: never true black, few inks, no moving glare.
2. THE STRANGE: the act cards already reserve "electronic texture for
   the strange" in the sound, and FFX's sound law stands untouched -
   analog horror is the strange's TEXTURE (a carrier hum, a broadcast
   voice too calm, a tape warble under a scene), never a second score.
   SOUNDS owns the cook; the anchor stays Besaid, his word.
3. THE AMALGAMATION: the faction that reads as manufactured owns the
   broadcast voice. Purple stays theirs alone (PURPLE RESERVATION), and
   a purple-lit screen is the one place both reservations meet - in act
   3, on their surfaces, as canon already rules.

## 3. WHAT IT NEVER TOUCHES
- The runway. Clothes are the living world; the register never dresses a
  body (no static-pattern garments, no test-card prints).
- The fight. Rule 17 owns the fight's picture and the register adds
  nothing there until the fight looks like the game at all.
- The chrome. The UI stays the no-slop object language; an emergency
  card drawn as UI decoration would be the vibe-coding tell wearing a
  costume. The NOTES control, the pad, the phone chrome: untouched.

## 4. HOW A COOK IS JUDGED (when rows exist)
Side by side against the real thing (the compare law): a real emergency
broadcast card, a real over-copied tape frame, a real CRT in a dark room
- structure from the reference, style from us, nothing of any named
genre work enters the vocabulary (8/28 holds; he named a GENRE, not a
game, and no game gets added by this card). The judge question is his
premise in one line: DOES THE OLD WORLD FEEL LIKE IT IS STILL
BROADCASTING, AND DOES NOTHING SIT ON THE LENS.

## 5. ROUTED (for the coordinator - only the coordinator adds rows)
- SOUNDS: the strange's analog texture (carrier hum, tape warble, the
  too-calm announcement) under the standing FFX sound law.
- WORDS: what the broadcasts SAY - emergency-card language, the
  announcement that keeps repeating, Spanglish where people wrote over it.
- COOK: the dead-media prop family (CRT, marquee, PA horn, tape deck),
  after the playable cut holds (rule 18 - nothing new ships to the alpha
  until then; this card is paper, not pixels).
- UI/PORTRAIT: nothing; the register does not touch chrome or faces.

Proof: his two words at the top; the laws this card cites by name (2C,
PROP-02, the phone card's glass, the act cards' strange, PURPLE
RESERVATION, rules 17-18, the 8/28 vocabulary ban).
