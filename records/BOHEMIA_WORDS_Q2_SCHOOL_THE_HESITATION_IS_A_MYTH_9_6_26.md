## THE SHORT VERSION, BECAUSE IT IS BLUNT
**The row's own brief names hesitation first, and hesitation is on the measured list
of things that do not indicate lying.** Pauses, gaze aversion and self-references are
among the 118 cues out of 158 that show NO association with deception at all. We were
about to write folklore, and it is not harmless folklore: the same belief, dressed as
forensic science, has been used in more than a hundred criminal cases.

## 1. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG, WITH THE NUMBERS
The largest meta-analysis of deception cues assessed **158 possible cues.**

    showed NO association with deception            118 of 158   (75%)
      and that list includes, by name:              gaze aversion, postural
                                                     shifts, PAUSES, self-references
    of the 50 cues studied five or more times,
      significantly related to deception             14   (28%)
      average effect size of those 14                d = 0.25

**A d of 0.25 is small.** And that is the AVERAGE of the cues that survived, not of
all of them.

Then, how well people actually do. Across **206 documents and 24,483 judges**, people
watching for lies in real time with no aids score:

    overall accuracy                 54%
    lies correctly called lies       47%
    truths correctly called true     61%

**People are worse than a coin at catching a lie and better than a coin at clearing a
truth.** The bias is toward believing. So in a scene, the realistic outcome is that a
liar is believed, and the tension is not whether the player spots a tell.

## 2. WHAT ACTUALLY DIFFERS, AND IT IS THE OPPOSITE SHAPE
The cues that hold up are not manner. They are CONTENT:

    fewer details          truth-tellers give more
    less coherence
    feigned ignorance      "I wouldn't know", "I never noticed"

**A LIAR IS NOT HESITANT. A LIAR IS THIN.** That is a completely different line on the
page. The 9/4 round would have written a liar who stumbles. The evidence says write a
liar who is smooth and gives you nothing, and who reaches for not-knowing when pressed.

And the modern approach follows from it: since lying is the harder mental job, the
tells appear when you INCREASE the load, with **questions the liar did not anticipate.**
**So catching a liar is a property of the QUESTION, not of the answer.** That is a
scene mechanic, not a speech mannerism, and it is the same conclusion Q17 reached from
the other direction: a lie is caught by checking it, never by reading the person.

## 3. FILLED PAUSES ARE WORDS, AND THE SPEAKER CHOSE THEM
The foundational work on "uh" and "um" found they are **conventional English words
that speakers plan, formulate and produce.** Speakers monitor their own upcoming
speech for a delay worth commenting on, then decide where to suspend, which item to
use, whether to attach it to the previous word, and whether to prolong it.

    **uh**  announces a SHORT delay
    **um**  announces a LONGER one

**A FILLED PAUSE IS NOT A LEAK. IT IS AN ANNOUNCEMENT.** A character who says "um" is
being considerate: holding the floor and warning the listener that the next bit is
coming slowly. Writing it as the sound of somebody caught is backwards.

## 4. STRESS DOES NOT DO WHAT THE ROW ASSUMES, AND MOST OF IT DIES ON THE PAGE
The row's brief says "shortened sentences." Under a standard laboratory social
stressor the measured result is the reverse: **participants showed HIGHER word
productivity under stress**, while also **pausing more**, with the pausing strongest in
those with the biggest cortisol and heart-rate response.

**STRESSED PEOPLE TALK MORE AND BREAK MORE AT THE SAME TIME.** Not clipped. Voluble
and broken, which is a different and better thing to write.

**AND HERE IS THE HARD PART FOR THIS QUESTION SPECIFICALLY.** Almost everything else
that is reliably measured under stress is acoustic: a pitch shift of over an octave in
panic, faster tempo, changed voice quality. The row's own constraint is "in TEXT with
no voice actor." **So the majority of the real science of speech under stress is
unavailable to us by construction.** What survives onto the page is: where the breaks
fall, and the fact that the word count goes UP.

That is a small toolkit and school's job is to say so honestly rather than pretend the
acoustic findings transfer.

## 5. EXHAUSTION IS THE ONE THAT WRITES ITSELF, AND NOBODY USES IT
Sleep deprivation research reports slurring, monotone, slowness and reduced intensity,
which are all acoustic and all lost in text. But one finding is pure page:

    after sleep deprivation there is significant deterioration in word generation
    and **a tendency to become FIXATED WITHIN A SEMANTIC CATEGORY**, a
    perseveration-like difficulty shifting between categories

**AN EXHAUSTED PERSON GETS STUCK IN ONE FIELD OF WORDS AND CANNOT MOVE OFF IT.** That
is visible in written dialogue with no performance at all, it is specific, it is
measured, and it is not in any craft advice I found. **It is the single most useful
thing in this school round.**

## 6. THE REAL TESTIMONY, AND WHY THIS MATTERS BEYOND OUR GAME
The testimony for this question is emergency calls, and it comes with a warning that
belongs in this record permanently.

In 2006 a police lieutenant wrote a master's thesis on **100 homicide-related 911
calls** and, with an FBI analyst, published "911 call analysis" in the FBI's own
bulletin: a scheme for deciding whether a caller is lying from how they speak.
Researchers who have tried to reproduce the findings **have failed.** It has
nonetheless featured in **more than one hundred cases across twenty-six states**, some
prosecutors used it while knowing it would not survive as scientific evidence, and
prosecutors and judges are now pushing for a ban and for conviction reviews.

**PEOPLE WENT TO PRISON BECAUSE SOMEBODY BELIEVED THAT A FRIGHTENED PERSON'S SPEECH
REVEALS GUILT.**

**THE DESIGN CONSEQUENCE IS NOT OPTIONAL.** If we write scenes where the player is
rewarded for catching a liar by their manner, we are teaching a false skill, and it is
the specific false skill that has ruined real lives. **The player must catch a lie by
going and checking it.** Q17 already put an address on every rumour for a different
reason. This is the second reason and it is a better one.

## 7. THE STANDARD CRAFT ADVICE, AND ONE COLLISION WITH OUR OWN LAW
The craft advice for putting these states on a page is punctuation and stage
direction: a dash for an abrupt cut off, an ellipsis for trailing off under one's own
steam, italic or parenthetical direction for what the face is doing.

**Two problems, and the second is ours.**

First, it is all surface. It tells you how to typeset a hesitation, not whether the
hesitation belongs there, and section 1 says it usually does not.

Second, **the em dash is banned in this project, everywhere, permanently.** So the
standard tool for the abrupt cut off is not available to this lane and round two has
to solve that with structure instead of punctuation: a line that stops on an
incomplete clause, or the next speaker's line beginning mid-thought. **That is a
constraint worth naming now rather than discovering while writing.**

And the third channel the craft advice reaches for, stage direction for the face, this
lane already measured (Q11): 94.5% of our lines are delivered by a flat face because
the only emotional channel reads punctuation we do not write. **We cannot lean on the
face for this until that one argument is passed.**

## 8. WHAT SCHOOL LEAVES ME HOLDING FOR ROUND TWO
Not lines. The shape they will have to take.

1. **A LIAR IS THIN, NOT SHAKY.** Fewer details, less coherence, and reaching for not
   knowing. Smooth is more accurate than stumbling.
2. **THE LIE IS CAUGHT BY THE QUESTION, NOT BY THE MANNER.** The unanticipated
   question is the mechanic. Nothing in the liar's mouth should be a tell the player
   is meant to read.
3. **A FRIGHTENED PERSON SAYS MORE, NOT LESS, AND BREAKS MORE WHILE DOING IT.**
4. **AN EXHAUSTED PERSON GETS STUCK IN ONE SEMANTIC FIELD.** This is the one state
   whose real signature transfers to text intact.
5. **"UM" IS A COURTESY, NOT A CRACK.** If it appears at all it is somebody keeping
   the floor, not somebody caught.
6. **AND WE CANNOT USE THE DASH**, so the cut off has to be built out of sentence
   structure.
