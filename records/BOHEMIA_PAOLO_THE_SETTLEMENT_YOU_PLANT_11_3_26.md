## WHAT IS LOCKED (his words, read plainly)
1. THE MAP WORK: every map (the fight boards and the overworld) must make visual sense and look good; no straight lines where the
   ground ends, no ground colour that stops abruptly (rule 346, said again).
2. YOUR SETTLEMENT IS A PILLAR, NOT A SIDE THING: "really important to give people that itch... a settlement that saves civilization".
   Completely optional, but it helps Las Vegas Bohemia.
3. ONE ZONE, NOT THE WHOLE WORLD: you plant your part of the city where you choose; the rest of the world stays as it is.
4. EVERYTHING IN IT CAN BE CUSTOMIZED ("literally everything"), unlike every other settlement, and it is EASY: you feel like the mayor
   ("that pseudo mayor vibe"), the game makes it easy to follow along. Not Valheim's tile-by-tile building; Stardew Valley's place you
   shape (rule 343(d)).
5. YOU RUN IT, SO YOU PAY FOR IT: the daily battery use and the rest is on you.
6. THE BOSSES FEED IT: what a boss held helps your settlement (rule 343(b)). MOST BOSSES DIE because they are not negotiable; SOME
   could be spared.
7. UNIQUE PEOPLE POST UP THERE: the special people you meet on the journey can come and live in your settlement.
8. THE FIRST PLANTING IS A BIG DEAL AND PERMANENT: the game asks "are you sure you want to plant down here? This is permanent."
9. YOU SEE ITS PROGRESS THROUGH THE ACTS: act two and act three show what your settlement became.
10. IT HAS ITS OWN LANE: HOME (started 11/3 for rule 343) now owns the whole settlement system.
PARKED: "maybe it gets involved with the main quest" waits with the main quest, which is on hold.

## THE COORDINATOR'S DEFAULTS (draft; his to change; realism first)
- WHERE YOU CAN PLANT (his "smart system"): any block that no faction holds, that is not a boss's place and not a town, and that a road
  reaches. The map grades every spot you hover on the four things a real settlement lives or dies by: WATER (a well, the main, the
  wash), POWER (sun is everywhere in the valley; a dead substation or a battery yard nearby is better), A ROAD (trade comes in, you get
  out) and DANGER (how near the raiders roam). The real valley gives you good candidates: Las Vegas suburbs are walled subdivisions
  behind block walls with one or two gates, ready-made walls; a strip mall has a roof and a car park; a school has a fence and a field.
  You can plant anywhere that qualifies; the grade tells you what it will cost you.
- WHEN: never in the first minute (nothing forced, 9/23). The game offers it once the company could hold a place: as one of the
  company's own goals (the 9/30 YES, 'a goal the company sets itself', Battle Brothers' ambitions). The first planting asks "are you
  sure? This is permanent." Once per game.
- HOW IT GROWS: one zone that grows block by block next to itself (the 9/29 YES, 'what you hold grows block by block').
- WHAT YOU CHANGE (everything, and easy): which building stands on each lot and where; what each building is made of and painted (in the
  valley's tans and the faction colour law's room); the signs; the settlement's NAME (the player's); who lives there and what job
  each one does. Every change is a tap on the settlement screen, Battle Brothers' way (tap a building to use it), never a building mode
  you have to learn.
- WHAT IT COSTS: a daily battery bill for the settlement beside the company's (records/target/bb/nightly_bill.json), food and water for
  the people living there; what you build pays some of it back (the solar panel, the garden bed, the water tank, his 10/1 pick); a
  boss's prize pays more (the kitchen, the main, the purifier).
- WHO LIVES THERE: unique people from the journey (a spared boss, a special person met on the road, a follower you hired once, Battle
  Brothers' retinue), each with a face that talks, each giving the settlement one thing.
- THE BOSSES: killed by default. A boss whose hold over the valley is a SERVICE (a midwife, a barber, a smith) may be talked down
  instead, and then he lives and works in your settlement; a boss whose hold is cruelty is not negotiable and dies. Each boss's record
  says which and why.
- THE ACTS: act two and act three show your settlement grown, or fallen if you let it go (the future goes both ways, 9/27).
- [bb] Battle Brothers has towns you visit and a company you run, never a home: its closest pieces are the retinue (followers bought
  once) and ambitions (goals the company sets itself). The home is our twist on its map, which is exactly his point.
