BUILT, PROVED, AND DORMANT — THE 112 PIPELINE
===============================================================================
The whole renderer can compose the character natively at 112. It is in the file
and it is off (RIG_RS = 1).

  RIG_RS            one scalar, derived from BAKED itself, so nothing can disagree
                    about the rig's resolution
  RIG2X()           the load-time rig doubler. PROVED LOSSLESS AND EXACTLY
                    INVERTIBLE on his real rig by tools/bohemia_rig_double.js:
                    halve the output and you get his original pixels back, byte for
                    byte, every part, every facing. That round trip is what makes it
                    legal under RIG LAW rather than merely promised.
                    56 -> 112, 96 part lists, 5,248 painted cells -> 20,992,
                    240 joints doubled with them.
  23 seams          every place the renderer knew "56" now asks. PD layers stay on
                    their 24 grid and stamp as blocks at the placement seam; all 258
                    CLO garments stay on their own 56 grid and stamp as blocks at
                    the gen() seam. Nothing was rewritten. No garment was touched.

*** EVERY SEAM IS THE IDENTITY AT RIG_RS = 1, AND THAT WAS PROVED, NOT ASSUMED. ***
96 rendered frames (8 facings x 3 clips x 4 phases) captured before the seams and
after them: 96/96 BYTE-IDENTICAL. That is why the two halves could be separated at
all — a half-applied resolution migration does not look worse, it looks like
garbage, and this one can be flipped and unflipped at will.

  python3 tools/bohemia_2x_flip.py --flip     (and --unflip)


===============================================================================
WHY THE FLIP IS NOT ON — AND THIS IS THE WHOLE FINDING
===============================================================================
It works. It renders. The gates pass. It looks WRONG.

At 112 native, HIS HEAD RENDERS AS A BOX. Flat-sided hair, a jaw that drops
straight down with no taper, square corners everywhere.

WHY: his art holds 56x56 of information. Doubling cannot invent detail — a
block-double is four cells where there was one, and not one new decision. What was
ALSO happening at 56, invisibly, is that Scale2x does not merely enlarge: it ROUNDS
DIAGONAL CORNERS. A large part of the roundness of that head was never painted. It
was manufactured by the upscaler, every frame, and it has been in every build he
has ever approved.

Compose natively and the upscaler is gone, so the rounding is gone with it.

And it lands directly on a LOCKED craft law — HOW HAIR AND SHAPE WORK, 8/1:
"no straight lines (hair is little off shapes)". The 112-native head is straight
lines. Shipping it would have traded a border he asked for against a silhouette he
already ruled on.

THE PICTURE OF IT: records/BOHEMIA_2X_REJECTED_112_NATIVE_BOXY_HEAD_8_16_26.png.
That image is THE REJECTED PATH, not a shipped result -- its own heading still says
"twice the pixels, half the border" because it was made before the head was looked
at properly. Look at the hair and the jaw in the bottom row: straight lines.
(The picture of what DID ship is slices/look/border-one-pixel.png, in the LOOK tab.)

THE TEMPTING WRONG FIX: double the rig with Scale2x instead of blocks, so the
rounding bakes in. That is RESHAPING HIS PAINTED ART — the exact thing RIG LAW
forbids — and it would have destroyed the round-trip proof that makes the doubler
legal in the first place. Not mine to do.

SO THE REAL ANSWER, and it is not a code answer:
    THE WAY TO GET TWICE THE PIXELS IS TO PAINT AT 112, NOT TO UPSCALE TO IT.
The pipeline is now ready for that the day the art exists. One call flips it.

===============================================================================
FOUR GATES WERE READING 56 INTO A 112 WORLD — ALL FOUR WERE RULERS, NONE A DEFECT
===============================================================================
Every one of these looked like a catastrophic character regression and every one was
the instrument. Recorded because the same four shapes will recur on any future
resolution work.

 1. RIG SPACE -> RENDER SPACE HARD-CODED AS `* 2`
    chin_law_gate + head_follows_rig_gate: `at(x,y)` doubled the coordinate because
    the rig was 56 and the render 112. On a 112 rig that reads at 224 — off the
    canvas — so every lookup returned null and the gate reported "the head has no
    edge, 0/0 rows". Fixed: the factor is PL / BAKED.W, derived.

 2. A TAPE MEASURE THAT STOPS AT 56
    head_follows_rig scanned `for (x = 0; x < 56; x++)`. His head sits at x=40..75
    on a 112 frame, so the ruler walked off its own measurement halfway across his
    face and reported the skin as HALF as wide as the rig paints it — 20 vs 10,
    every row. Fixed: scan BAKED.W.

 3. COMPARING A PIXEL WITH ITSELF
    Both edge rules asked "is the outermost skin pixel darker than the one beside
    it". Block-doubled art makes two adjacent CELLS the two halves of ONE painted
    pixel — necessarily the same colour — so half of all comparisons were false by
    construction. The tell was the number: EXACTLY 20/40. Every real comparison
    passed. Fixed: step by RIG_RS to reach the actual neighbour.

 4. A RULING RE-EXPRESSED AS A ROW COUNT
    CHIN LAW B caps the throat at "ONE row of his face". That was said about a
    56-tall face; the same physical band is two rig rows at 112. A literal 1 would
    have halved the throat tone he approved on 7/27 and 8/11 — a gate quietly
    overruling a ruling. Fixed: the cap is RIG_RS rows, which is the same
    conversion the renderer's own throat code makes.

*** THE DISCIPLINE THAT MADE THIS SAFE: every re-blessed gate was re-run at 56
FIRST and had to return the IDENTICAL verdict and the identical numbers. It did —
CHIN LAW 10/0, HEAD FOLLOWS RIG 5/0, RIG IS LAW 12/0, unchanged. A ruler fix that
changes a verdict at the old resolution is not a ruler fix, it is a relaxation. ***

STILL PINNED AND STILL HONEST: head_follows_rig's PINNED_TOTAL/PINNED_WORST are raw
rig-pixel counts and are meaningful only at 56. At 112 the same head measures 64/4
against pins of 14/3 — but normalized, the WORST ROW (the shelf under the mouth he
circled) IMPROVES 3 -> 2, while the total rises 13 -> 16 because the deviation
becomes uniform. Nothing was re-pinned, because nothing shipped at 112. When the art
is painted at 112, re-pin from measurement and say so out loud.
