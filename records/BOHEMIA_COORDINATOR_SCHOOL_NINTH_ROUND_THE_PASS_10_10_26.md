# BOHEMIA COORDINATOR SCHOOL, ROUND NINE OF TEN: THE PASS (10/10/26)

## THE ANGLE
A kitchen has this fleet's problem: eight stations, each cooking at its own speed, and a table that must get every dish at the same second. Kitchens solved it with one job. The expediter stands at the pass, cooks nothing, calls when each station starts so slow and fast finish together, holds what is early, checks every plate, then calls for hands. Hospitals, pit lanes and Broadway dressers run the same way. Bohemia has 26 stations, a ticket rail (the backlog), runners (RUN), and no pass. The steak reaches Paolo with no sauce because nobody's job is the plate.

## WHAT THAT WORLD KNOWS
1. The pass is "the main timer," and every plate for a table "lands at the same moment." https://www.foodrepublic.com/1539644/what-is-expo-restaurant-kitchens/ . Bites here: our timer is per lane, never per screen.
2. The expo is "the only position that sees the whole board at once," so it, not the cooks, decides when each table's items get called. https://climbtheladder.com/what-does-expo-mean-in-a-restaurant-the-expeditor/ . Bites here: the coordinator alone sees all 26 lanes and uses that view to sweep, not to call.
3. Fire times are staggered from the slowest item back: a strip steak takes 12 minutes, a stuffed lobster tail 20, so the steak "should not be fired until 8 minutes after the lobster." https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminFireByPrepTime.html . Bites here: art is the lobster, layout is the steak, and we fire them together.
4. "All day" is the running count across every open ticket: "eight chicken all day" instead of eight separate orders. https://pos.toasttab.com/th-us/blog/on-the-line/what-does-all-day-mean-in-a-kitchen and https://catalog.webstaurantstore.com/article/511/kitchen-slang-phrases.html . Bites here: nobody can say how many screens are in flight, so nobody sees we have too many.
5. A dish that waits too long at the window "dies on the pass": it has lost temperature or quality and goes back or in the bin. https://www.foodrepublic.com/1588209/restaurant-dying-on-the-pass-meaning . Bites here: a finished part live without its partners is dying under the lamp, and Paolo eats it anyway.
6. The line's words are short and fixed: "fire" (start now), "5-out / 3-out" (minutes to plate), "heard," "behind," and "in the weeds," a call for help, not a confession. https://www.hcareers.com/article/career-advice/food-service-slang-20-insider-kitchen-terms . Bites here: our lanes have no word for "I am behind," so the pass learns it when the plate is late.
7. The line check comes before the first ticket: the chef walks every station, tastes the sauces, works from a written sheet, and what is short becomes the 86 list read at pre-shift; "a check that never reaches the dining room has only done half its job." https://climbtheladder.com/what-is-a-line-check-and-why-it-matters/ and https://www.eatloyalist.com/glossary/line-check/ . Bites here: Paolo is the first ticket of every round and nobody tastes before he orders.
8. Thomas Keller put "Sense of Urgency" under every clock in The French Laundry and Per Se; he says it means being prepared, not rushing. https://www.timesensitive.fm/episode/thomas-keller-on-cooking-as-a-pathway-to-happiness . Bites here: mise en place, everything in place before service, is the opposite of shipping each part the hour it is done.
9. An operating room is a plate too. NYU's "PIT Crew" cut 10% of turnover steps and made 25% of sequential steps parallel; median turnover fell from 37 minutes to 14 over 35 pilot turnovers. https://www.ctsnet.org/node/47831 . Kaiser's "parallel processing for patient and room readiness" cut turnovers of 30 minutes or longer from 72.5% to 11.7%. https://divisionofresearch.kaiserpermanente.org/publications/improving-operating-room-turnover-time-a-systems-based-approach . Bites here: the win was not faster hands; it was starting the slow part while the fast part was being set.
10. An F1 crew is 20 to 23 people with one job each, in position 15 to 20 seconds before the car; teams did roughly 100,000 practice stops before 2019; Red Bull's best stop took 1.82 seconds. https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/anatomy-of-a-pit-stop-how-do-f1-teams-service-their-cars-in-less-than-two.5p9LNdd8XJdvP4mRsXoGsB . A Broadway quick change is pre-set, not fast: costumes stacked in scene order, layered so a change is a removal. https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/quick-change-artist-advice-14580/ . Bites here: one voice releases the car when every gun is up; our demo is a quick change with the costumes still at the tailor.
## WHAT THE COORDINATOR DOES NOT DO
- It does not call fire. Every lane starts when its row lands, so slow art and fast layout start together and the layout waits dead for weeks. This alone would have kept the roster screen off his phone until its materials existed.
- It does not hold. A finished part goes live the hour it is done, with or without its partners. The roster layout shipped bare because nothing said "hold."
- It does not check the plate. It sweeps lane by lane; every part passes its own gate and no one looks at the whole screen before it leaves the window.
- It does not keep an all-day count, so there is no moment when the pass says "no new tickets."
- It does not run a line check before he plays. Paolo tastes the sauce that broke overnight.

## THE ONE CHANGE
THE PLATE TICKET, AND THE COORDINATOR STANDS AT THE PASS.

The ticket. Not a lane's job but a plate: one screen or one moment with everything on it. One ticket is one file, tickets/PLATE_<name>.md, one line per station: STATION, PART (what they put on the plate, in Paolo's words), FIRE (the round it starts), OUT (its "3-out" call, updated each round), STATE (fired / held / dead / on the plate). The last line is HANDS: the tab, and the one sentence Paolo should see. The backlog stays the ticket rail; a row on a plate carries the ticket's name in brackets.

Who stands at the pass. The coordinator; it already cooks nothing, and the expo never takes a station. DIRECTION tastes the art at the line check. RUN is the runner: it carries into the demo only plates the pass has called "hands," never a part.

The pass does four things each round. FIRE: the slowest station first, the fast ones later (20-minute art fires 8 rounds before 12-minute layout). HOLD: a part that lands before the plate is whole merges behind a flag or off the demo, never on his screen. CHECK: when the last part calls "0-out," the pass opens the one tab on one phone, reads the HANDS sentence against what is there, wipes the rim or sends the part back with "refire" and the reason. HANDS: RUN carries it, the build stamp carries the plate's name.

All day. The START handoff's first line is the count, every round: "3 plates all day: FIGHT BLOCK (fired, 2-out), ROSTER (held, 5-out), SETTLEMENT (dead, refire COOK 4)." Over three in flight, no new ticket fires.

The line check. Before any round Paolo may open the game, the pass walks the fired plates on one phone from a written sheet: one line per station, tasted in its tab, 60 on a phone, analog horror, the law it must not break. What fails is 86'd and told to him in one plain sentence, never found by him.

In the weeds. A lane writes "in the weeds, 6-out was 2-out" the round it knows. The pass answers within the round: send help (one named lane takes one named sub-part, written on the ticket) or kill the ticket (the plate goes dead, every part held, nothing reaches him half). A third round in the weeds on one part is a kill.

The first plate: PLATE_FIGHT_BLOCK, one Rogue Fable 4 fight on one Vegas block with its HUD. Fire order, slowest first: COOK 2 and COOK 4, house-sized floor tiles, roofs and blockers of one district, fire round 0, 6-out. COMBAT, the board cut from that map cell, seeded, round 1, 5-out. ANIMATION with CHARACTER, two fighters and a companion at 112 px on the beat, round 2, 4-out. UI, the one HUD, same in map and fight, round 4, 2-out. SOUNDS, the 120 BPM bed and the hit, round 5, 1-out. All five say 0-out in the same round. HANDS: COMBAT tab; "I tap a fight on the map and I am on a Vegas block that looks like Vegas, with the same buttons I had on the map, and the hits land on the beat."

The words, and nothing else: FIRE, HOLD, N-OUT, HEARD, IN THE WEEDS, REFIRE, 86, DEAD, HANDS, ALL DAY.

## ONE RULE
NOTHING LEAVES THE WINDOW UNTIL THE PLATE IS WHOLE, AND ONE VOICE CALLS IT
A screen reaches Paolo only as a plate the pass has fired slowest first, held early, checked on one phone, and called hands on; a part on its own is held, never served.

## FOR PAOLO, IN PLAIN WORDS
In a good kitchen one person stands at the counter, cooks nothing, and makes sure your steak and your sauce hit the table at the same second. Our chats have been sending you the steak when the steak is done and the sauce three weeks later. From now the manager chat stands at that counter: it starts the slow part first, holds the fast parts, checks the whole screen on a phone, and only then lets it reach you.
