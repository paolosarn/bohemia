BOHEMIA -- THE SMALL THINGS: FOUR MORE GARMENT TYPES GET A MARK THEY NEVER HAD
CHARACTER lane, 8/23/26.  Row 2X step 5, third pass.

WHERE THIS STARTED
------------------
The rig composes at 112. A mark drawn one CELL wide is TWO pixels there, so a garment
that only scales comes out chunkier relative to the body. Measured 8/22 across every
canon garment (tools/bohemia_seam_width_audit.js): 102 OF 200 HAD NO ONE-PIXEL DETAIL
ANYWHERE. Boots got a welt stitch the same day, 0/18 -> 18/18, and the total fell to 84.

THIS PASS, FOUR MORE
--------------------
    gloves    a knuckle seam        hands  0/4 -> 4/4
    belts     edge stitching        waist  0/5 -> 2/5   (tool belts are genGear's)
    scarves   a woven weft line     neck   0/7 -> 7/7
    masks     a hem stitch          face   0/8 -> 5/8

    84 of 200 -> 66 of 200.

EVERY ONE IS A MARK THAT DID NOT EXIST. At 56 a glove is three rows and a seam inside it
would have eaten the garment; a belt is two rows and both are the edge; a scarf ring is
two rows and a weave line would be half of it. At 112 each has a row to spare.

*** THE RULE THAT MATTERS MORE THAN THE MARKS: ADD, NEVER SHRINK. ***
Nothing he ruled on got thinner. genShoes' heel seam is S wide with HIS OWN reason
written above it. The belt's buckle, the glove's edge, the scarf ring's dark/light split
and the mask's top band all keep exactly the widths they had. The temptation on every one
of these was to thin the existing line, and that would have quietly overwritten a ruling.

MEASURE THE BODY, DO NOT GUESS IT
----------------------------------
The knuckle seam went in at row mn+2*S and DREW NOTHING. The hand is six rows tall at
112, so mn+2*S is the fifth of six and the generator's own edge test -- which reaches two
pixels in every direction -- correctly called it an edge. Probed the real part grid
(6 rows, 10 wide), moved the seam to mn+S, and it landed. A row index guessed from the
shape of the code rather than the shape of the body is a coin flip.

AND THE PICTURE LIED ONCE BEFORE IT TOLD THE TRUTH
---------------------------------------------------
The first before/after came out with the two columns IDENTICAL. CLO_NOSTITCH gated the
boot stitch and genAcc had never been taught to read it, so "before" was drawing the new
marks too. The flag is read in genAcc now, typeof-guarded like the others, and all four
marks honour it. A before/after where nothing differs is not a null result, IT IS A
BROKEN TOOL, and the only reason to catch it is to look at the picture instead of
trusting that the tool did its job.
