## THE RULING (rule 217)
(a) NO PORTRAITS ON THE POSTS FOR NOW: a post on the phone's FEED is its USERNAME and its words; 215(b)'s 'a named account with a
    face' is amended to 'a named account'. Rule 19(c) stands (it covers text OUTSIDE the phone; the feed is the phone). A post's
    account keeps an empty portrait field so faces can come later without new code.
(b) THE USERNAMES ARE THE STORY: every account's handle tells who they are and where the world is in a few characters: the trade,
    the faction, the block, the grudge, the joke, the year they made it. A reader learns the world from the names alone.
(c) THE INTERNET OF THAT ERA: the handles and the posts read like the internet of Bohemia's time, an economic crash in Las Vegas
    with three generations ahead (rules 1, 33 of the three acts), grounded in how real handles and memes have evolved: the old
    accounts that kept their teenage names, the numbers and years, the brand and crypto-era leftovers from before the dollar died,
    the faction tags, the burner accounts, the bots that kept posting after their owners left, the meme formats that lived and
    mutated. Research first (how handles and memes changed era to era, cited), then the world's own; never a meme from a game he
    has not named (rule 6).
(d) THEY EVOLVE WITH THE ACTS (the three acts at once, 9/23): an account's name and voice in act one is not its name in the future;
    the feed shows the internet growing up (or dying) as the world does.
(e) THE GATE: every account in FEED's file has a handle; no two handles alike; a stranger reading twenty handles can name at least
    three factions and two trades from them alone (EYES reads them cold); no post shows a portrait until faces exist for it.
(f) WORDS [the usernames tell the story] (the handles, the research, the era's internet; WORDS is already open for the feed);
    FEED NOTE (accounts are usernames first, the portrait field empty); UI TWO NOTE (the FEED app shows the handle, no face for
    now); line 137 on his list; EYES checks.
