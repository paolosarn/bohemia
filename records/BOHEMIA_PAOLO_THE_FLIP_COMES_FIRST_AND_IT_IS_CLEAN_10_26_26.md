## HOW GAMES DO IT (coordinator's research, the reading a COORDINATOR'S DEFAULT, rule 205)
Three ways a scene survives a rotation: (1) one big scene and a camera that crops it differently: cheap, but the upright view loses
buildings off its sides, which 220 forbids ('never a crop'); (2) two layouts and a cross-fade: clean but reads as a cut, which 229
forbids; (3) a SHARED-ELEMENT MOVE (Apple's matched-geometry and Android's shared-element transitions, the way a phone's home screen
re-flows its icons on a turn): the same pieces exist in both layouts, each slides from its old spot to its new one, and only what has
no partner fades. Our towns are one plan in two compositions, so every building and every person HAS a partner: (3) fits exactly.

## RULE 289
(a) ORDER: every settlement type and every camp tier looks good TALL and WIDE on its own first, judged with NO phone on screen; only
    then is the phone's corner cleared in each (279, 286). A composition that only looks good once the phone hides part of it fails.

    spot to its wide spot (or back) in one beat at 120 BPM, eased (MOTION's curve, 229); every person rides with the ground under
    them and keeps doing what they were doing (a walker keeps walking, the smith keeps hammering); the roads and ground blend under
    them; the man he is talking to and the door he is at stay where his eye is (220(e)). No black frame, no reload, no pop, nothing
    lost, nothing overlapping at any frame.
