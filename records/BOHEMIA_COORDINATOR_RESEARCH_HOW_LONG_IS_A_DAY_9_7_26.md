# HOW LONG IS A DAY: NOBODY HAS DONE THE CLOCK MATH (coordinator swing, 9/7)

## THE QUESTION
A hundred hours, three generations, sixty bosses, a valley that runs out in ten
days, and a phone session of five minutes. Those are all clocks. Nobody has checked
that they agree. Measured in our own files this round:
```
DAY_MIN = 1440         a day is 1,440 in-game minutes (real length)
a step  = 0.084 min    so one in-game day of walking is about 17,000 steps
the clock reads zero over 160 presses (RUN, 9/5): time passes by WALKING and by
the bed; standing still costs nothing; nightfall "IS the clock" for the day turn
```
Nobody has measured how many in-game minutes pass per REAL minute of play, so
nobody can say how many in-game days a hundred hours holds, and therefore whether
three generations fit inside it at all, or whether the fold has to be a time skip.
That is the spine of the game and it is an unmeasured number.

## WHAT THE CRAFT DOES
- The best-known farm-and-life game runs a full day in about 14 real minutes, and
  its designer copied a brisker ancestor on purpose (5 seconds real = 10 minutes
  game) because the pressure is what made a day feel like a decision.
- The other famous answer is real time: a day is a day, so a player can dip in for
  five minutes without missing a deadline, and nothing rushes them.
- The trade is explicit in the craft: compression creates strategy and a fear of
  missing out; real time creates ease and long return. Ours is a hundred-hour
  phone game with five-minute sessions, so it needs BOTH: a day short enough to be
  a decision, and a session that never punishes leaving.
Sources: [Stardew's day is about 14 real minutes, and why](https://steamcommunity.com/app/413150/discussions/0/1697168437867105189/),
[games that run in real time](https://gamerant.com/x-games-which-run-in-real-time/),
[the real-time mod that makes one game feel like the other](https://www.gamingbible.com/news/stardew-valley-real-time-valley-mod-581210-20251221).

## THE ARITHMETIC THAT HAS TO BE TRUE
If a day takes about 15 real minutes (the craft's answer), a hundred hours is 400
days: three generations at 130 days each, which is a season each, not a life. If
a day takes 90 real minutes (17,000 steps), a hundred hours is 66 days and the
three generations do not exist. Either way the FOLD is a time skip and the years
between are told, not walked; the question is how many days a player actually
lives in each life, and it has to be chosen, not discovered.

## ROUTED
- PLUMBER [clock math]: measure in-game minutes per real minute, walking and idle,
  on the real surface; publish the number; a gate holds it.
- RUN [a day is]: rule the day length as a dial with the craft's 15 minutes as the
  default, so a day is a decision and a shift (five minutes) is a third of one.
- DYNASTY [days per life]: research how many lived days a generation needs to
  feel like a life, and what the skip between them is made of.
