# BOHEMIA COORDINATOR SCHOOL, ROUND EIGHT OF TEN: I INTEND TO (10/10/26)

## THE ANGLE
A submarine is the worst place to run on orders: the facts live in the hands on the valves, not in the captain's head. In 1999 L. David Marquet took the USS Santa Fe, the worst boat in the fleet, gave one order that was mechanically impossible, and the officer obeyed anyway ("because you told me to"). He stopped giving orders. The crew said "I intend to," he said "very well," and the boat went from worst to first. The Prussians called the same thing Auftragstaktik; Nelson did it at Trafalgar; the US Army wrote it down as mission command. For a coordinator who writes rows for 26 lanes it cannot see and is asleep when they run: the information is in the lane. Move the authority there, not the information here.

## WHAT THAT WORLD KNOWS
1. "Request permission to" became "I intend to"; the captain's answer became "very well." Within two years the Santa Fe had the highest retention and operational evaluations in the force, and it later produced ten submarine captains from one boat. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Marquet . Bites here: a lane that writes "waiting on a row" is at the bottom of this ladder because the row format puts it there.
2. "Don't move information to authority, move authority to the information." A leave request had needed 14 approval steps; Marquet gave it to the chief who knew the sailor. https://danmall.com/learn/design-leadership/move-authority-to-the-information . Bites here: the lane that measured the blocker was wrong IS the information, and the row told it to send that upward and wait.
3. The Ladder of Leadership: "tell me what to do," "I think," "I would like to," "request permission to," "I intend to" at rung 5, "I just did" at 6, "I've been doing" at 7. Marquet says "strive for five." https://davidmarquet.com/ . Bites here: "the named blocker was not the blocker, sixth row running" is rung 7, and the shop treated it as a problem.
4. Deliberate action: after a sailor shut the wrong breaker, every operator had to pause, point, say aloud what he was about to do, then act. An inspector later reported mistakes stopped because someone caught them in the pause. https://www.shortform.com/blog/turn-the-ship-around-david-marquet/ . Bites here: a push to main has no pause, so a wrong row ships at the speed of a reflex.
5. The three-name rule: whoever greets a visitor says the visitor's name, their own, and the ship's. Act like an owner before you think like one. https://charles.io/turn-the-ship-around-l-david-marquet/ . Bites here: READBACK is already our three-name rule; it lacks the verb that says who decides.
6. Auftragstaktik: the superior says what and why, never how. Moltke, 1871: "no plan of operations extends with any certainty beyond the first contact with the main hostile force." https://wist.info/von-moltke-helmuth/9434/ . Bites here: the lane's measurement an hour later is first contact, and the row's how rarely survives it.
7. ADP 6-0 (2019) names seven principles: competence, mutual trust, shared understanding, commander's intent, mission orders, disciplined initiative, risk acceptance; the definition is "disciplined initiative within the commander's intent." https://www.army.mil/article/106872/understanding_mission_command . Bites here: "disciplined" is the half the lanes miss and "initiative" is the half the rows miss.
8. Commander's intent is purpose, key tasks, end state, and the end state matters most; key-task lists bloat into "laundry lists" nobody can prioritize. Intent must be understood two echelons below its author: know your boss's intent and your boss's boss's. https://www.mca-marines.org/?p=87512 and https://armchairgeneral.com/tactics-101-027-commanders-intent.htm/4 . Bites here: a Bohemia row is nearly all key tasks and nearly no end state.
9. The back-brief: the subordinate first restates the intent, then after planning briefs the commander on HOW he will do it, and the commander checks it nests. https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/report/call/call_93-3_ch8.htm . Bites here: READBACK is a confirmation brief with no back-brief; the lane repeats the row and never states its own plan.
10. Nelson's Trafalgar memorandum, 1805: "In case signals can neither be seen or perfectly understood, no captain can do very wrong if he places his ship alongside that of the enemy." The only order that day was "England expects that every man will do his duty." https://www.antiquestradegazette.com/news/2001/no-captain-can-do-very-wrong-if-he-places-his-ship-alongside-that-of-an-enemy/ . Bites here: the coordinator's signals cannot be seen while it sleeps; every row needs its "alongside the enemy" line.
11. Horst Schulze let every Ritz-Carlton employee spend up to $2,000 per guest per incident without asking, because a guest is worth about $200,000 over a lifetime; nobody ever maxed it. https://chiefexecutive.net/part-ii-ritz-carltons-schulze-on-empowering-employees-to-think-like-owners/ . Netflix calls it "context, not control." https://theproductperson.substack.com/p/reed-hastings-on-building-a-streaming . Bites here: a lane with no stated limit assumes zero and asks.

Accountemps 2014: 59% of US workers have had a micromanager; of those, 68% said it cut morale and 55% said it cut productivity. https://press.roberthalf.com/2014-07-01-Survey-More-Than-Half-of-Employees-Have-Worked-for-a-Micromanager . The opposite failure is intent too vague to act on, "restatements of the mission or collections of adjectives." https://www.canada.ca/en/army/services/canadian-army-journal/articles/2025/short-bursts-rolls-commanders-intent-redux.html . Bites here: "make it feel right" is one failure; "draw it at 112 px with the 9/28 patch" is the other.

## WHAT THE COORDINATOR DOES NOT DO
- It does not write the HOW. Not the tool, the file, the measuring method or the patch name. A lane obeying a wrong method cannot fix it without a new row, and the coordinator is asleep.
- It does not answer "request permission to" with permission. It asks "what do you intend to do?" once, then "very well" by default when the intent fits the end state.
- It does not override a lane's measured finding with the row's wording. "The named blocker was not the blocker" means the row was wrong. The record is corrected; the lane is not stopped.
- It does not write key-task laundry lists. One purpose, one end state on his phone, one constraint.
- It does not blur orders and intent. The laws, the one link, the reserved names, the ship flow, STOP PRODUCING: orders. Everything else: intent.

## THE ONE CHANGE
A row is written as INTENT, three lines: PURPOSE (the why, tied to Paolo's words). END STATE (what Paolo sees or hears on his phone, in which tab, when it is done). CONSTRAINT (the one law or limit that must not break). The HOW is deleted and belongs to the lane.

Before (a real-sounding row): "COMBAT [board cut]: make the board builder read the map cell's district id and pick blockers from the 9/29 asset table; measure with the board gate at 60 fps on a phone; use the roof set from cook 4; report unique boards over 50 seeds."

After: "COMBAT [board cut]. PURPOSE: Paolo said no two combat boards are the same, and the board is cut from the city. END STATE: in the COMBAT tab he taps three fights in three districts and sees three boards that look like those districts, no repeat, at 60 on his phone. CONSTRAINT: a combat tile is a house; the mound is the first terrain effect, not the only."

The lane's claim merges the READBACK and the intent into one line, in this order: "I read this as [the end state in the lane's words]. I intend to [the how, one sentence]. Two up: Paolo wants [his words]; the coordinator wants [the purpose line]." That is the confirmation brief and the back-brief in one breath, rung 5. If measurement later shows the how was wrong, the lane adds one handoff line, "I intended X, measured Y, now doing Z," and keeps going. That is rung 6 and it is correct.

The coordinator MAY write: purpose, end state, constraint, and a pointer to the record holding Paolo's words. It MAY NOT write: a tool, a file, a patch, a measuring method, a number the lane can measure itself, or a sequence of steps. If it catches itself typing "use," "via," "with the," or "then," it is writing a how.

Deliberate action for a push to main: the commit message carries the row's end state line and the one thing that would prove it is not met. Pause, point, say it, push.

The two-levels-up line is mandatory. A lane that cannot name both Paolo's intent and the coordinator's purpose has a task, not intent, and says so instead of building.

THE TEST FOR INTENT: cover the how with your thumb. If what is left still tells the lane when it is done and what Paolo will see, the row was intent. If nothing is left to aim at, it was an order in a row's clothes.

## ONE RULE
THE ROW SAYS WHY AND WHAT HE SEES; THE LANE SAYS HOW, OUT LOUD, THEN GOES
A row is purpose, end state and one constraint; the lane answers "I read this as, I intend to, two up," and a measured finding that the row was wrong is the lane's to act on, never to wait on.

## FOR PAOLO, IN PLAIN WORDS
A submarine captain stopped giving orders, his crew said "I intend to" instead of asking, and the worst boat in the fleet became the best and made ten captains. Our manager chat has been telling the other chats how to do the work, and they wait when the how turns out wrong and the manager is asleep. From now the manager writes why and what you will see on your phone, and each chat says how it plans to get there and then just goes.
