## 2. THE GOOD NEWS RULE 31 NEEDS FIRST, AND IT IS BIG

Rule 31 says the future is **derived on every flip, never authored**. That only works if
folding is a pure function you can run a thousand times. **Measured:**

    same input folded twice -> byte-identical output        TRUE
    does folding MUTATE the ledger it was handed?           NO
    does it say which half it is missing?                   YES: missing:'memory'

**`fold(ledger, memory)` is pure, deterministic and non-destructive, and when it is handed
only half it says which half by name instead of pretending.** So the flip can re-derive the
future as often as he taps, and nothing degrades from being looked at.

**That is the single most important fact this round has for DYNASTY, and it was already true
before the law existed.** The expensive half of rule 31 is already paid for.

## 3. THE REAL RECORD: WHAT ACTUALLY CROSSES A GENERATION

The row names four things. They transmit at wildly different rates, and **two of our own cited
numbers are off.**

### MONEY AND STATUS, AND OUR TWO NUMBERS ARE BOTH SLIGHTLY WRONG

    UK, standard parent-child estimates
      earnings elasticity            0.22 - 0.69
      WEALTH elasticity              0.48 - 0.59
      education                      0.43 - 0.71
      occupation                     0.08 - 0.30

    Gregory Clark, rare surnames, 1858-2012
      underlying status elasticity   0.70 - 0.75, strikingly constant across 150 years

**OUR FOLD SAYS WEALTH PERSISTS "ABOUT 0.3 TO 0.4". THE MEASURED WEALTH RANGE IS 0.48 TO
0.59.** Our number is low, by a lot at the top end.

**OUR FOLD SAYS STATUS PERSISTS "ABOUT 0.79". CLARK SAYS 0.70 TO 0.75** — and, more
importantly, **Clark's claim is contested.** His method assumes observed status is a noisy
proxy for a latent one; other work does not find the bias he predicts, and the "simple law of
mobility" is disputed rather than settled. **We cited the high end of a contested finding as
though it were a fact.** Both corrections are small and both matter, because these two numbers
are the only rates in the whole fold.

### WHAT TRANSMITS BEST IS NOT MONEY. IT IS STATUS, AND THEN TRUST.

Clark's whole point, whatever one thinks of the size, is that **the thing that persists is not
the money, it is the position** — and our fold agrees with that shape already, because
standings is the stickiest field it carries.

**And trust is inherited.** The descendants of US immigrants carry a measurable trust level
predicted by **their forebears' country of origin and the year they arrived**, decades and
generations later, and that inherited component has a causal effect on growth. It is
persistent **and not invariant** — it moves, slowly.

### THE GRID DOES NOT INHERIT. IT AGES.

Much of the US grid is **40 to 70 years old, well past its intended life**; **nearly 70% of
power transformers are over 25 years old**; the maintenance backlog is on the order of **a
trillion dollars**, and a water main breaks somewhere **every two minutes**. Water systems
depend on electricity, so a short outage takes pressure and sanitation with it (round 47's
finding, from the other end).

**Infrastructure is the opposite of status: it transmits nothing by itself.** A grid is not
inherited, it is *maintained or lost*, and **the default across a generation of nobody
maintaining it is decay.** *That is exactly the field our fold carries at one hundred percent
forever.*

### DEBT DOES NOT CROSS AT ALL

Already ruled and already right: a child is not personally liable for a parent's unsecured
debts; the claim is against the estate and an insolvent estate means the creditor loses. **The
lender survives, the bill does not** — and round 49 measured the mechanism that makes this
true in our game, where a loan cannot compound and what is recorded is a night count.

## 4. THE ONE THAT TRANSMITS BEST IS THE ONE WE HAVE NO FIELD FOR

**There is no trust field in the fold.** The thirteen are standings, deeds, territory, builds,
economyCapacity, invest, karma, virtues, family, wounds, blindSpot, recordedKnown, debt.

`standings` is the nearest and it is not the same thing: **standings is what one named outfit
thinks of your family. Trust is whether you expect a stranger to deal straight with you** —
and the real record says that second thing is inherited, measurable generations later, and
economically load-bearing.

**In a game whose first morning is a stranger being handed a battery by somebody who wants him
to owe them** (round 49), *whether his grandfather's valley was one where people dealt
straight is the single most interesting thing a fold could carry*, and it is the one thing it
does not.

## 5. THE ONE FIELD THAT DIES IS SILENT IN THREE PLACES

`debt` is the only field ruled to die, and:

1. **Nothing executes it.** Round 48 measured this: twelve favour debts went into a fold on
   the walked surface and twelve came out.
2. **Nothing reports it.** `whatDied()` can say exactly two sentences — deeds that died with
   their witness, and unsettled wounds. **A ledger carrying a debt of 4 folds and the word
   debt never appears.**
3. **And the heir is therefore told only what he KEPT.** `whatYouKept` fires; `whatDied`
   returns the two it knows; the one ruled loss in the whole system is invisible at every
   layer.

**A generational handoff that can only report gains is the ratchet again, wearing a different
hat.** The player flips forward and every screen says he still has things.

## 10. THE PROBES THAT WERE WRONG, AND THE ONE THAT WAS RIGHT FOR THE WRONG REASON

1. **`fold(ledger, memory)` takes TWO halves.** I called it with one and got
   `whole:false, missing:'memory'` — **and the module telling me what was missing is the only
   reason I did not read that as a broken fold.** A function that names its own missing input
   is the best-behaved thing this study has probed.
2. **`fold(fold(x))` nests instead of advancing**, because I fed the result back as a ledger.
   My misuse, not a flaw, and worth writing down before somebody else tries to model two flips
   that way.
3. **AND THE ONE THAT WAS RIGHT FOR THE WRONG REASON.** `whatDied()` returned `[]` on a ledger
   carrying a debt of 4, and I nearly published *"the fold reports nothing as dying."* Then I
   read the function: it only ever reads `memory.died` and `ledger.family.wounds`, and my test
   ledger had neither. **The empty list was my shape.** Given the shapes it does read it says
   two true sentences — **and the real finding survived anyway and got sharper: the debt is
   never mentioned, because the field is not in the function at all.** *The right answer and
   the wrong reason are not the same thing, and one more read was the difference.*
