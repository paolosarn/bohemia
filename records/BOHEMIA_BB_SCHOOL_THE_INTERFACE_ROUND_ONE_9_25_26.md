## 1. HOW BATTLE BROTHERS' INTERFACE ACTUALLY WORKS
- THERE IS ONE THIN BAR AND IT NEVER LEAVES. Across the top of the campaign: the company's
  banner and name, CROWNS, food, and the supplies that get spent while you walk (tools,
  medicine, ammunition), the DAY number, and the time of day. Beside it, the speed and
  pause control. That bar is on screen every second you are on the map.
- THE MAP IS THE WHOLE SCREEN. No minimap, no window inside a window. The world is the
  interface: you read it by looking at the ground, the roads and who is moving on them.
- A PLACE IS A PANEL OVER THE MAP, NOT A NEW SCREEN. Tap a town and its services open as a
  list on top of the map you were just looking at: market, armourer, tavern, temple,
  training ground, recruits. You never travel to a "town screen"; the map stays behind it.
- THE CLOCK IS NOT A NUMBER YOU READ, IT IS THE LIGHT. Night falls on the map, your sight
  shrinks, and the day counter ticks. Time is felt before it is read.
- THE PURSE IS ALWAYS VISIBLE AND EVERY PRICE IS SHOWN AGAINST IT. You never open a wallet.
  When you hire, the daily wage is shown next to what you have, so the cost of a decision
  is in the same glance as the decision.
- THE NUMBERS LIVE IN TOOLTIPS. Hover anything and a long, honest explanation appears:
  every modifier, every chance, why the price is the price.
- THE COMPANY IS THE REAL SCREEN. The roster is a paper-doll per man, drag and drop, stats,
  traits, injuries. It is where the hours go.

## 2. WHAT PLAYERS PRAISE, AND WHAT THEY COMPLAIN ABOUT
Praised: nothing is hidden from you if you look; the bar tells you the truth about how
close to broke you are; the map is legible at a glance; a place never costs you a loading
screen. Complained about: the numbers are behind a HOVER, which does not exist on a phone;
the roster is a wall of tiny icons; and the tutorial explains almost none of it.

## 3. *** THE ONE PLACE BATTLE BROTHERS IS THE OPPOSITE OF HIS RULING, NAMED ***
BATTLE BROTHERS TELEPORTS YOU TO THE FIGHT. A contact on the map cuts to a separate
tactical screen with its own bar: a turn order along the top, the selected brother's
abilities along the bottom, different buttons in different places. It is a second mode.
Paolo killed exactly that on 9/21 (rule 24): "teleporting shit not part of the universe,
the buttons changing, no consistency... one game mode, not teleporting to different game
modes." So "look how Battle Brothers does this and then do it" cannot be obeyed wholesale
in this department. What we take is the THIN ALWAYS-ON BAR, the PANEL OVER THE MAP, and the
PRICE BESIDE THE PURSE. What we refuse, by his own newer ruling, is the battle screen.
A school round that only copied would have shipped the one thing he already banned.

## 5. THE SHAPE FOR US (defaults, his to knock down in VOTE)
1. ONE HUD, THREE SURFACES, and it is nine things: the bar, notes, the canvas, the pad.
   The map adds the phone. The fight adds NOTHING THAT IS NOT ALREADY AN OBJECT.
2. THE FIGHT DRAWS ON WHAT IS THERE: the ground he stands on lights for reach (rule 23c,
   pistol 1, rifle 2, scope 3) and the enemy's next beat rides the ring of the walk pad he
   already has his thumb on. That is option A in the tab. Option B adds one thin strip to
   the bar saying whose beat it is. Two options, no more (rule 25).
3. THE BAR EARNS ITS KEEP, AND THIS IS A REAL FORK, NOT A FIX. Battle Brothers' answer is
   four or five readings always on; his 9/20 answer was LESS ON SCREEN and the cut hid
   them. Both are his. The default I would build: THREE readings and no more -- batteries
   (the money, 9/4), the hour, and where you are -- because a purse you cannot see is the
   one thing every Battle Brothers player says the bar is for, and three is not twenty
   seven. NOT BUILT THIS ROUND: it touches the demo's first screen, which is RUN's cut and
   rule 18's hold. It is a question for him, phrased in the tab, not a change I make behind
   a ruling he gave four rounds ago.
4. A PLACE IS A PANEL OVER THE MAP, NEVER A SCREEN. We already obey this without meaning
   to: the phone opens over the city and the city is still behind it.
5. NO HOVER, EVER. Battle Brothers' numbers live in tooltips and he plays on a phone.
   Whatever we take from their depth has to survive having no hover at all: the number goes
   ON the object or it goes on the phone.

## 7. THE ANALOG HORROR LINE (rule 9, every chat, every round)
Battle Brothers' bar tells you how many days of food you have left. Ours would tell him how
many batteries. The horror is not in the type or the frame: it is that the most useful thing
a bar can say in this world is how long you can keep the lights on, and the honest number is
small. A bar that reads THREE and keeps reading three while he walks is the dread. Nothing
has to flicker.
