# BOHEMIA COORDINATOR SCHOOL, ROUND 2 OF 10: LOOKS FIRST, THE ART PASS (10/10/26)

## THE QUESTION
Paolo ruled LOOKS FIRST: the art reads like a seventh grader made it, the HD tile packs we bought were never used, and features are on hold until the look holds, with three new production-artist chats under DIRECTION. This round asks how real studios run an art revamp mid-project and reach "looks right" before piling on features: Cerny's Method and the publishable first playable, the visual target and the style bible, the beautiful corner, art review and lock, how small teams make bought packs read as one game, and what Darkest Dungeon, Dead Cells, Hollow Knight, Factorio, Stardew Valley, Slay the Spire, Hades and Owlboy did when they redid or locked a look while already building. Note: page fetches were blocked from this container, so each finding comes from search-indexed summaries of the named pages; URLs are real, wording is paraphrase unless quoted.

## WHAT I FOUND

1. Cerny's Method puts all the risk in pre-production and ends it with a "publishable first playable": two levels at shippable quality, judged on a checklist naming player behaviour, enemy behaviour, ART DIRECTION and scope. His slides list "Alpha = First Playable" as a myth; the common gloss is that a demo, an alpha and a whitebox are not a first playable. Pre-production is allowed to be chaotic; production starts only after the look and the play are proven. Sources: https://www.slideshare.net/holtt/cerny-method , https://www.videogameschronicle.com/features/who-is-mark-cerny/ . Why it matters here: with 60 bosses and 26 lanes we still have no screen finished to the standard the rest must match. By Cerny's rule we are in pre-production and should act like it.

2. The visual target: pick one small but important scene, character or object, develop it to near-final quality before asset production, and it becomes the guideline for every asset after, while also exposing the hardware's real limits. Source: https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/art-preproduction/ . Why it matters here: a target screen is what a cook can be measured against on a phone. We measure against a frozen baseline of our own old art.

3. The beautiful corner (or mock screenshot) is one screen's worth of a level with art and audio near final, sitting in the build next to the grayboxed rest. Its jobs: set mood and style, fix the production-value target, reveal how long assets really take. Highguard's team called theirs the moment the visual identity "truly came together". Sources: https://mycours.es/interactivity/?p=130 , https://itch.io/post/14117823 . Why it matters here: one finished fight board on house tiles, with its sound, is our beautiful corner, and it does not exist.

4. Unwritten art direction fails quietly. One guide: a style not defined in shape language, proportion and material rules "doesn't fail loudly. It fails asset by asset", and consistency stops holding past about 30 to 40 assets and 2 to 3 artists unless a named owner runs review checkpoints against the bible, which holds shape language, proportion, palette and material logic, lighting intent, do and don't examples. Another source: "Rules and reference assets are the brief." Sources: https://www.juegostudio.com/blog/art-direction-in-games , https://nastyrodent.com/game-art-bible/ . Why it matters here: thousands of assets, five drawing lanes, and a genre word instead of a rules sheet.

5. The live review loop: the Free Realms art deck says all completed assets are reviewed and approved or returned to the artist with noted changes, under the theme "consistency through re-use". Source: https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/the-free-realms-filter/10662621 . Why it matters here: our gates check that a comparison happened; nobody with taste approves or returns each cook before it enters the city.

6. Darkest Dungeon locked its look with rules one artist could keep: Bourassa was the lone artist for most of development, pulled from illuminated manuscripts and woodcuts plus Mignola, avoided black and dead grey outside the inks, kept a warm parchment cast even on cool screens so one warmth ties the palette, and let strong line work carry isolated colour splashes. The workload drove the side-on view, which became the game's personality. Sources: https://www.gamespot.com/articles/the-gothic-sensibilities-of-darkest-dungeon/1100-6424880/ , https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/road-to-the-igf-red-hook-studios-i-darkest-dungeon-i- . Why it matters here: a tiny set of hard colour and line rules made a one-artist game read as one game. We have no such list.

7. Dead Cells built the pipeline before the volume: Vasseur, alone for a year, drew a basic pixel model sheet, built the character in 3DS Max, and rendered it tiny with no antialiasing through an in-house tool, each frame a sprite plus a normal map under one toon shader; the model stayed rough because the character is about 50 px tall; keyframes first, interpolation after; a change never meant redrawing every frame. Source: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i- . Why it matters here: the fix for "looks amateur" was one repeatable machine with one light, not more hand drawing. Our 84 patch tools draw by hand with no shared light.

8. Hollow Knight's look was there in the 2013 jam game Hungry Knight: Game Informer notes Gibson's striking art style was already present when little else was, and the team deliberately left pixel art for a hand-drawn look. Sources: https://gameinformer.com/2018/10/16/the-making-of-hollow-knight , https://www.acmi.net.au/stories-and-ideas/from-ludum-dare-to-pharloom/ . Why it matters here: the look came first and the game grew into it. We did it backwards, which is the harder order.

9. Factorio ran a true mid-project revamp, the high-resolution remaster: first batch in 0.15, over 4,000 sprites counted (double with HR), weekly public reports, and the warning that re-rendering is "almost never 'just make it have more pixels'"; high res exposed old drawing bugs the small sprites hid, and resources were rebuilt from the 3D source semi-automatically instead of recoloured. Sources: https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-218 , https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-172 , https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-179 . Why it matters here: a revamp is a counted list with a per-round report, and HD packs will expose what the 112 px art hid.

10. Stardew Valley's solo developer ran "a month and a half of graphics": a redrawn, taller player and NPCs, nearly everything in the mines, nearly every interior, because his skill had passed his old sprites. Source: https://www.stardewvalley.net/mini-dev-update-4/ . Why it matters here: he stopped features and redrew in one concentrated pass. He did not redraw in the background while adding features.

11. The bought-pack problem has known causes and fixes. The "asset flip" look comes from how packs are used: palette clashes, mixed detail and resolution, zero modification. Working artists' fixes: judge every asset in a neutral test scene under the game's light before blaming it; fix the lighting, never hide it in colour grading; colours must look OK with no post-processing; one directional light and one palette or atlas; one light direction across characters, backgrounds and props. Sources: https://www.wayline.io/blog/asset-pack-visual-identity , https://haukethiessen.artstation.com/blog , https://pixnote.net/en/learn/shading . Why it matters here: our packs were never unified to one palette or one light, which is why they sat unused.

12. Placeholders work when they are honest. Slay the Spire 1 launched with about half its cards marked BETA; for the sequel Casey Yano kept deliberately ugly stand-ins so nobody would "judge the game as if it's a 1.0" and so each card stayed readable. Hades revised portraits in small steps across early access and marked every community-inspired change in the notes, while Supergiant took feedback on everything but not high-level decisions. Sources: https://www.gamesradar.com/games/roguelike/slay-the-spire-2-lead-says-the-garbage-placeholder-art-is-important-and-gen-ai-would-ruin-it-people-would-judge-the-game-as-if-its-a-1-0-fully-developed-video-game/ , https://www.supergiantgames.com/blog/hades-welcome-to-hell-update-patch-notes/ . Why it matters here: art that half-imitates finished art reads as seventh grade; art that is clearly a stand-in does not. We ship the first kind.

13. Owlboy held one look for nine years by banning cheats: no gradients, no blur, no tracing, no 3D captures. Source: https://www.siliconera.com/owlboy-result-nearly-decade-spent-chasing-pixel-art-perfection/ . Why it matters here: a look is partly a list of refusals, and a machine can check a list.

## WHAT THE BEST DO THAT WE DON'T
- They finish ONE screen before they draw a thousand assets. Cerny, the visual target and the beautiful corner all say it: one piece at final quality is the contract and production waits for it. We have 26 lanes cooking volume against no finished screen, and the coordinator never named one.
- They write the look as rules a machine can check, not a genre word. "No black outside the inks, warm cast everywhere", a banned list, one light direction, one palette. "Analog horror" and "runway" are directions; our reference gate checks that a comparison happened, not that hex values, light side and outline weight match a bible.
- They have one named approver with a return button. Free Realms approved or returned every asset. Our cooks mark themselves SHIPPED and the city absorbs whatever lands; DIRECTION owns the revamp with no returns and no count of them.
- They unify bought packs before using them: neutral test scene, one light, palette remap, matched detail density. We left the HD packs on disk and hand-drew around them.
- They stop features for a counted pass with a public tally. Our hold is a sentence with no gate on feature commits and no "redrawn N of M".
- They let placeholders look like placeholders.

## THE CHANGES FOR THE COORDINATOR
1. Name the target screen and make it the only art job until Paolo says yes. DIRECTION produces two mock screenshots at the game's camera and phone pixel count: one fight board on house tiles and one map view, with HUD and sound. Test: both are in the VOTE tab with his yes, and no cook batch is marked SHIPPED before that date.
2. Turn the analog horror bible into a numbers sheet: palette as hex values, one light direction, outline weight and colour, pixel size per surface, a banned list, three do and three don't images. Test: the reference gate reads the sheet and fails any asset with off-palette pixels over a tolerance or a wrong light side; the sheet replaces the frozen baseline.
3. Run the pack pass before any new drawing: cooks 2, 3 and 4 each take one bought pack, put it in a neutral test screen under the one light, remap it to the palette, and report sprites fixed out of total. Test: the count sits in each handoff every round, and a remapped pack appears in the target screen within two rounds.
4. Give DIRECTION the return button and count it: every cook batch is APPROVED or RETURNED with one noted change before it enters the city or demo. Test: zero assets in the demo without an APPROVED line; three returns in a row stops a lane until it re-reads the sheet.
5. Make the hold a machine fact: a gate lists art folders and feature folders and refuses feature commits (outside PLUMBER fixes) while LOOKS FIRST is on; every reply to Paolo carries one line, redrawn N of M, features held. Test: the gate exists and the line appears every round.

## ONE RULE
FINISH ONE SCREEN BEFORE YOU DRAW THE THOUSANDTH ASSET. One screen at final quality, with its rules written as numbers, is the contract every other piece of art is measured against, and nothing else ships until it exists.

## FOR PAOLO, IN PLAIN WORDS
The best studios make one screen look perfect before anyone draws the rest, and they write the look down as hard rules like "these exact colors, light always from the top left, no gradients". Then one person checks every new piece against that screen and sends it back if it misses. We skipped that step, so the first job is one finished fight screen and one finished map screen for you to say yes to, and the tile packs we bought get fixed to match before anybody draws anything new.
