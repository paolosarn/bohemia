2. THE PAD SAYS WHAT IT IS ABOUT TO DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Yesterday's patch fixed the CONSEQUENCE of his report and left the CONFUSION.
LOOKING AT THE MAP IS NOT TRAVELLING stopped a glance from moving his body 194
tiles. But he did not say "it moved me". He said IT IS CONFUSING, and that is a
different defect.

    THE SAME CONTROL, IN THE SAME CORNER, UNDER THE SAME THUMB, LOOKING EXACTLY
    THE SAME, MEANT TWO COMPLETELY DIFFERENT THINGS.

        zoomed in     one tile. About a metre. Free.
        zoomed out    one overmap cell. Ninety-six metres. TEN MINUTES of his
                      day. And as of this turn, a road encounter that can eat
                      twenty more.

Norman's split is the one that applies. An AFFORDANCE is what a control can do; a
SIGNIFIER is what it TELLS YOU it will do. The pad's affordance changed at the
seam and its signifier never did.

THE WRONG FIX IS A CAPTION. A sentence explaining the mismatch is words on his
screen, and TALK TO HIM LIKE A PERSON prices words on his screen as a cost. So
the CONTROL changes instead.

THREE SIGNALS AT ONCE, because one can be missed:

    SHAPE   round 50% -> square 7px. A circle is a thumbstick, which is a body
            control. A square is a tile, which is a map control. Silhouette reads
            before colour, which is the same argument the clothing law makes.
    GLYPH   the single arrow becomes a double arrow. The arrow says "a long way",
            which is the difference between one metre and ninety-six.
    WEIGHT  rgb(184,154,106) warm walking accent -> rgb(159,180,196) map line.

Driven from updHud, which all nineteen mode changes in the file already call,
including both zoom seams and the chip. Hooking the seams by hand is how the next
seam gets forgotten, and driving it from render() would be per-frame DOM work for
something that changes twice a minute.
