================================================================================
WHY NOTHING CAUGHT IT
================================================================================

GRAVEYARD IS FINAL has been law since July. gates/bohemia_graveyard.txt is 1,300
lines. NOTHING HAS EVER READ IT. It was not among the six broken laws the 7/16
sweep found, because it had no gate to be broken.

And a name check would not have helped. RIDGE CREST shares no word with MOHAWK.
A NAME IS THE ONE THING A FRESH COOK ALWAYS CHANGES, so a name-matching gate
would have passed all seven and taught the next session the registry had been
consulted.

IN THIS GENERATOR A SHAPE IS A DIAL:

    strip   ->  the crest        MOHAWK / LIBERTY SPIKES / HIGH TOP
    tie     ->  the tied mass    PONYTAIL / BRAIDED TAIL / TOP KNOT / LOW BUN

Nothing canon uses either dial. Every shape either has ever produced is a
standing tombstone. That makes them DEAD MECHANISMS, and cooking with one is a
remake however it is named. The gate reads the dials.

MUTATION TESTED, because a gate that reads what you handed it is not a gate:
adding `tie:'pony'` to BUZZ CUT turns it red and names the style, the dial and
the graveyard entry.

================================================================================
THE SENTENCE THAT INVITED IT
================================================================================

laws/BOHEMIA_LAW_A_HAIRCUT_READS_FROM_EVERY_ANGLE_8_28_26.md, written two days
ago, by me, ended:

    "The two mechanisms that are not finished -- the crest and the tie -- are
     named rows, not silent omissions."

That is wrong in the most expensive direction available. It calls two REFUSED
things UNFINISHED. A row on the backlog is an invitation, and the next session
accepted it.

WHEN YOU WRITE DOWN THAT SOMETHING IS UNFINISHED, CHECK FIRST WHETHER IT IS DEAD.
From inside the code the two are identical. In the record they are opposites.

Corrected in that file and in CLAUDE.md today, struck through rather than
deleted, so the trail survives.
