## THE SHORT VERSION
**A thank you is a way of counting, and counting turns help into debt. Where people
have to keep needing each other, thanks is refused, deflected or insulted away, and
our row asks for three of them. Asking across distrust is not persuasion at all: you
hide how badly you need it, because showing need hands over the pricing, and instead
of asking to be trusted you propose a SHAPE that makes trust unnecessary. And the
refusal is the part with hard numbers on it: in real recorded speech a rejection is
prefaced twice as often as an acceptance and runs half again as long.**

## 1. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG, AND IT OVERTURNS A THIRD OF MY OWN ROW
The row asks for three thanks. The record says a plain thank you is the move to be
careful with, and in some of the best-documented cases it is an insult.

Peter Freuchen, in *Book of the Eskimo* (1961), records what happened when he
thanked a Greenlandic hunter who had left him several hundred pounds of walrus meat
after a failed hunt. **The hunter objected to being thanked**, and gave the reason:
*"Up here we say that by gifts one makes slaves and by whips one makes dogs."*

That is not politeness folklore, it is a theory of obligation, and the
anthropological literature on egalitarian hunting societies is consistent with it:
what is refused is the CALCULATION. To thank somebody is to mark the help as a
discrete completed item, which starts an account, and an account is the beginning of
one person being able to say the other owes them. The hunter's position is that being
human means refusing to measure who gave what to whom, precisely because measuring
ends in some people owning others.

The same thing shows up from the other side in Richard Lee's *Eating Christmas in the
Kalahari*. Lee bought the largest ox he could find as a Christmas gift for the Ju
/'hoansi he had lived among, and **they mocked it relentlessly**: too thin, too old,
a bag of bones. He eventually learned this was the practice, applied to any big
kill, and its job is to stop a provider turning generosity into standing. **The
culturally correct response to a large gift there is not gratitude, it is an
insult.**

**WHAT THIS DOES TO THE ROW.** Three thanks is still the deliverable, but a thank you
in Bohemia's act one is a LOADED move, not a warm one, and writing three warm ones
would be the error this round exists to prevent. The interesting versions are: thanks
REFUSED by the giver, because accepting it would convert a standing relationship into
a settled one; thanks DEFLECTED into the future ("you will get yours") which keeps the
account open on purpose; and thanks given deliberately and coldly BECAUSE the speaker
wants the account closed and the relationship ended. Our own text already reaches for
this twice without knowing it (section 6).

## 2. ASKING SOMEBODY YOU NEED AND DO NOT TRUST
**2.1 YOU HIDE HOW MUCH YOU NEED IT, WHICH IS THE OPPOSITE OF THE DRAMATIC
INSTINCT.** The writing instinct is to raise the stakes inside the line so the
audience feels the need. In a transaction with somebody you do not trust, revealed
need is pricing information, and handing it over is expensive. Day-labour ethnography
describes the shape: employers pull up at a corner and call out how many they need,
workers have a few minutes to settle work, hours and pay, and workers manage their
self-presentation to match what the employer wants to see. **Nearly half of day
labourers surveyed had experienced wage theft within the previous two months, and
enforcement is effectively closed to them.** So the pressure is maximal, the trust is
zero, and the correct move is still to understate.

**2.2 YOU DO NOT ASK TO BE TRUSTED. YOU PROPOSE A SHAPE THAT MAKES TRUST
UNNECESSARY.** This is the finding with the most direct writing consequence. Research
on how migrants choose a smuggler describes exactly this: information gathered by
word of mouth through family and friends, reputation checked across physical and
virtual communities, and **"substitutes for trust" in the form of guarantees and
escrow arrangements.** One migrant's account of the moment is the whole speech act in
one sentence: *"It was a stranger we just trusted, there wasn't another way for us."*

So a real ask across distrust contains a STRUCTURE: half now and half on delivery,
a third party holding the rest, one side going first on something small, a named
person who will vouch. "Trust me" is the move of somebody who has no structure to
offer, which is why it reads as a warning.

**2.3 WHAT THEY LEAVE OUT.** Three things, each for a different reason.
- **The need**, per 2.1, because it is pricing information.
- **The questions.** The day-labour material describes workers performing the "good
  worker" the employer expects. Asking what exactly the job is, who else has done it,
  what happened to them, is itself a signal of suspicion and it loses you the job. So
  the ask is thinner than the asker's actual uncertainty, on purpose.
- **The why.** Criminal communication is cryptic by design because words become
  evidence and because stating a purpose gives the other side leverage. The ask names
  the SHAPE of the thing and not its reason.

## 3. HOW THEY NAME THE PRICE, WHICH IS NOT A FINANCIAL QUESTION
Viviana Zelizer's work is the anchor and it reframes the whole thing. People do
constant **relational work**: setting boundaries between kinds of relationship and
adopting the kind of transaction appropriate to each. Her sharp version is that
**adopting a particular payment system with somebody is (re)defining the relationship
with them**, and that money does not only get earmarked for uses, it earmarks
relations. People experience money relationally rather than mathematically.

Against this sits the "hostile worlds" view, that money and intimacy contaminate each
other and must be kept apart. Zelizer's alternative, "connected lives", is that people
are perfectly capable of mixing them and do it by carefully choosing the FORM.

**The writing consequence: naming a price is a statement about what you two are to
each other.** Offering to pay a neighbour can be an insult. Refusing payment from a
stranger is a claim on them. Paying in work rather than in batteries says something
different again. So in a scene of asking across distrust, the price is where the
relationship gets declared, and it is the most loaded sentence in the exchange, not
the most boring.

## 4. THE REFUSAL, AND THIS PART HAS HARD NUMBERS
Conversation analysis calls this preference organisation. A response that goes along
with what was just proposed is PREFERRED and arrives fast and bare. A response that
blocks it is DISPREFERRED and is systematically marked: delay or silence, a preface
like "well" or "uh", hedging, mitigation, and an ACCOUNT saying why. A study of 329
texting interactions found that **a reply delayed by more than a minute was much more
likely to be a no than a yes**, which is the same structure surviving into a medium
with no audible pause.

**I TESTED IT MYSELF ON REAL RECORDED SPEECH** rather than taking the textbook's word,
using the 199,740 dialogue-act-tagged turns of recorded telephone conversation in the
cached corpus:

| act | n | starts with a preface (well, uh, oh, actually, I mean...) | mean words |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACCEPT / agree | 11,123 | **12.7%** | 1.75 |
| REJECT / disagree | 344 | **26.7%** | **2.73** |
| saying no WITHOUT the word no | 302 | **40.7%** | |
| YES answer to a question | 3,030 | 25.3% | 1.17 |
| NO answer to a question | 1,374 | 13.3% | 1.20 |

**Rejections are prefaced at twice the rate of acceptances and run 56% longer.**
Confirmed, on real speech, with the size of the effect.

**AND THE SAME TABLE KILLS THE NAIVE VERSION OF THE CLAIM.** "People never just say
no" is false: answering a yes/no QUESTION with a bare "No." is common, short, and
barely prefaced at all. The marking belongs to the ACT of refusing a request, not to
the word no. Anybody writing from the slogan rather than the table would strip a
perfectly natural bare "No." out of an answer and put a long hedge where it does not
belong.

## 5. WHERE THE STANDARD ADVICE IS WRONG, AND IT IS THE FAMOUS TRICK
The persuasion literature's best-known move for exactly this situation is the "but
you are free" technique: end the request by telling the person they are free to
refuse. The original result is spectacular. Guéguen and Pascual asked passers-by for
coins for the bus and got **10% compliance**; adding "but you are free to accept or to
refuse" got **47.5%**. Carpenter's 2013 meta-analysis over 42 studies found it
effective across contexts.

**AND IT HAS A REPLICATION PROBLEM THAT THIS RECORD IS NOT GOING TO HIDE.** A later
re-examination reports that recent studies are too low-powered to detect the effect
Carpenter found, with an R-index around 9.8% and an expected discovery rate around 6%.
Those numbers describe a literature whose headline effect probably is not the size it
claims. (I could not fetch the paper directly, the host is blocked from here, so this
is cited at the level of its published summary and should be treated as such.)

**SO THE LANE TAKES THE MOVE AND LEAVES THE JUSTIFICATION.** Do not write an ask that
hands somebody an out because a persuasion result says it doubles your yes. Write it
because section 4 says a refusal needs room: a request that leaves no room forces a
bare no, and a bare no to a request is so unmarked that it reads as hostility. Giving
an out is never a trick for winning a yes. It is what lets the other person say no
without it becoming a fight, which in a place with no law (Q20) matters more than the
yes did.

**"TRUST ME" IS ALREADY ABSENT, AND THAT IS A STRENGTH.** Three lines in 3,093 touch

**Reputation checked before an ask: one line in 3,093.**

**AND THE REFUSAL MEASUREMENT, WHICH MY OWN RULER GOT WRONG.** The regex found 36
lines and reported 0.0% prefaced and 2.8% carrying an account, against real speech's
26.7% and its near-universal account. A damning result, and wrong.

**I READ ALL 36 BY HAND AND ALMOST NONE OF THEM IS A REFUSAL.** They are inability
statements about the world ("I cannot tell you why it does not add up", "I can't hear
anything past it rattling in my coat"), moral declarations ("I won't choose", "I won't
pretend those are the same thing"), and narration ("a favor I won't enjoy spending").
Two of the 36 are ASKS, not refusals. The genuine article, somebody turning down a
request to their face, is about three lines in 3,093.

**THE REAL FINDING IS THEREFORE BIGGER THAN THE FAKE ONE: we have essentially never
written the scene.** And the 0% preface rate is explained: you do not preface an
inability statement, you preface a refusal.

**THE RULER LESSON, AND IT IS NOW THREE FOR THREE.** A regex over WORDS cannot measure
a SPEECH ACT. This round it counted "can't" and called it refusal. The same trap sits
inside the corpus table in section 4, where a bare "No." is a no ANSWER and not a
refusal, and anybody counting the word would conclude the opposite of the truth. Q20
hit the same wall from the other side, where a deference ruler built from "sir" and
"ma'am" could not see courtesy done through behaviour. **Print the hits, read them,
and ask what ACT each one performs before believing any rate.**

## 7. WHAT SCHOOL LEAVES ME HOLDING FOR ROUND TWO
1. **A THANK YOU IS A WAY OF COUNTING, AND COUNTING MAKES DEBT.** The three thanks
   should be a refusal of thanks, a deflection that keeps the account open, and one
   given coldly BECAUSE the speaker wants it closed.
2. **YOU HIDE THE NEED.** Revealed need is pricing information.
3. **YOU PROPOSE A SHAPE INSTEAD OF ASKING FOR TRUST.** Half now, a third party, one
   side going first, a name that vouches. "Trust me" is what somebody says when they
   have no shape to offer.
4. **THE ASK IS THINNER THAN THE ASKER'S UNCERTAINTY**, on purpose: the questions and
   the reason are left out.
5. **THE PRICE IS WHERE THE RELATIONSHIP GETS DECLARED**, so it is the loaded line of
   the scene and not the dull one.
6. **A REFUSAL IS PREFACED AND CARRIES AN ACCOUNT**, at twice the rate and half again
   the length of an acceptance, and we have never actually written one.
7. **AND EVERY ASK GIVES THEM AN OUT**, not to win the yes but so the no can happen
   without becoming a fight. We have zero.
