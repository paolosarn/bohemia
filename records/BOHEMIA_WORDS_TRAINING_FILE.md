## 1. THE BIGGEST THING I LEARNED THIS ROUND
### A REFUSAL IS NEVER JUST "NO", AND IT IS MEASURED IN MILLISECONDS
Conversation analysts have timed real talk. A **yes** lands at a median of about
**269 ms**. A **no** lands at about **561 ms**. More than twice the gap. The
delay is so reliable that a listener starts reading a pause as a refusal before
any word arrives.

And the refusal does not arrive naked. It comes in a package, in this order:
1. a **delay** — a breath, a click of the tongue, a beat of nothing
2. a **preface** — "well", "look", "it's just", "the thing is"
3. an **appreciation** — naming what was asked, before turning it down
4. an **account** — the reason, usually BEFORE the refusal itself
5. very often a **trail-off** that means no without ever saying no

**THE RULE FOR US:** somebody who agrees answers instantly. Somebody who refuses
buys a second first. That is free characterisation and it costs one word.
Our people hedged **4.8%** of the time.

### AND FEAR MAKES PEOPLE SAY LESS, NOT MORE
Measured: higher anxiety correlates with **shorter sentences, fewer words, and
shorter runs of speech between pauses.** A frightened person does not deliver a
paragraph about their fear. They go clipped, and they stop.
**Our scared characters were giving composed speeches about being scared.** That
is backwards, and it is the single easiest thing to fix in a rewrite: when a
character is afraid, take their words away.

## 2. THE CHARACTER SHEET — "DIFFERENT CHARACTERS AND THE WHOLE ENCHILADA"
The craft answer to "how do I make them sound different" is not accents. It is
five dials, and every person in the valley gets a setting on each.

### DIAL 1 — WHAT THEY NAME
The strongest and cheapest. Every trade names the world differently, and a
person reaches for the noun their hands know.
- an electrician says **warm cable**, **the feed**, **it tests clean**, and calls
  himself a **sparky** before he calls himself an electrician
- a plumber says **elbow**, **reducer**, **snake**, **rough-in**, **sweating a
  pipe**; a bricklayer calls mortar **muck**
- the trades name each other in insults that became affection ("drips", "leaks")
**THE TEST: does this speech contain one noun only this person would reach for?**

### DIAL 2 — SENTENCE LENGTH, AND WHAT MOVES IT
- pragmatic people: short, declarative, no subordinate clauses
- frightened people: SHORTER, and they stop early (measured, above)
- nervous people: run-ons, because they are still deciding while talking
- people explaining their own trade: LONGER, because they finally get to be the
  expert, and this is the one place a long sentence is earned
- a person who is lying takes longer to get there than a person who is not

### DIAL 3 — WHAT THEY WILL NOT SAY
The topics a character talks AROUND say more than what they say. This is subtext
made practical: pick the polite word for the ugly thing and let the player
supply the ugly one. "I don't steal it. I move it." "Indentured citizen", not
"slave". A whole character can be built out of one euphemism they will not drop.

### DIAL 4 — THE TIC, AND IT IS ONE
Repeated phrase, filler, or the way they OPEN. One per person, not three.
"Easy. Easy, easy." "Yeah. Fair." "Huh." A tic used twice is character; used
five times it is a catchphrase and it is dead.

### DIAL 5 — AGE, AND IT IS NOT VOCABULARY
The research kills the lazy version. Older adults have **the same vocabulary
size**; what changes is **lexical access speed** and **how a story is
organised** — more setup, more digression, the point arrives late and by a
longer road. So an elder in Bohemia is NOT a small vocabulary. An elder takes
the long way and gets there.
A child is not a small adult either: simpler and **less varied** structure, and
they say the true thing at the wrong moment.

### THE TEST FOR ALL FIVE, AND IT IS THE ONLY ONE THAT MATTERS
**COVER THE NAMES.** Read the scene with every speaker label hidden. If you
cannot tell who is talking, the dials are all set the same and the scene is one
person arguing with themselves.

## 3. WHAT A COLLAPSED ECONOMY ACTUALLY SOUNDS LIKE
This is Bohemia's exact subject and it had never been researched for this game.

**ARGENTINA, DECEMBER 2001 — THE CORRALITO.** Accounts frozen, withdrawals
capped at 250 pesos a week. Shop sales fell **50 to 70 percent in four days**.
Barter clubs went from nothing to **over 1.5 million people**, then more than 2
million by 2002. People traded homemade food, clothes, plumbing, language
lessons, massages, computer repair.
**WHAT THAT TEACHES US ABOUT SPEECH:** the vocabulary of a collapse is not
abstract. It is a LIST OF ACTUAL THINGS. A real testimony from that year:
*"We depended on our relatives to feed our daughters, until I came to a barter
club to offer my professional services."* Relatives. Daughters. Services. Nobody
says "the economy". They say what was in their hands and who was in the room.
**THIS IS WHY THERE IS NO MONEY IN BOHEMIA, AND THE LAW IS RIGHT.** People in a
collapse do not talk in currency. They talk in medicine on the barrel, a case of
batteries, half a tank, a roll of tape.

**THE WPA LIFE HISTORIES, 1936-1940.** The US government paid unemployed writers
to record **more than 10,000** ordinary Americans' life stories, 2,000-15,000
words each, in the middle of the Depression. Richard Wright, Ralph Ellison,
Nelson Algren, Zora Neale Hurston and Saul Bellow all worked on it.
**WHY IT MATTERS TO ME:** it is the largest body of recorded American
working-poor speech that exists, from exactly the kind of moment Bohemia is set
after. **NEXT ROUND: read actual transcripts, not summaries.** I have only read
about this collection. I have not read it.

## 4. LAS VEGAS, SPECIFICALLY
Small haul so far and worth much more digging, but one find is worth the round:
- **"Today's my Friday."** A Vegas local says this on a Tuesday, because they
  work when visitors play. **THE WEEK IS NOT THE WEEK IN THIS CITY.** That single
  idiom carries the whole shape of a service town, and it is the kind of line
  that could not come from any other game.
- **"Graveyard"** is not only the shift. A **graveyard special** is the cheap
  breakfast served from midnight to six.
- The valley's real rhythm is 24-hour and inverted. In a dead Vegas that
  inversion would SURVIVE, because it was never about tourists, it was about who
  is awake when the light is cheap.

## 5. HOW TO WRITE AN ACCENT WITHOUT WRITING AN ACCENT
Never respell a word. Eye dialect ("joo" for "you") makes the reader work for
nothing and carries a long history of making the speaker look stupid. Already
banned for Spanish (THEY SPEAK SPANGLISH, 8/25); the research says make it total.
Carry region and class in:
- **grammar**: "I seen him", "I been waiting", "I says to him", double negatives
  — non-standard, not wrong
- **dropped helping verbs and articles**
- **regional idiom and proverb** — the phrasing only somebody from here uses
- **rhythm and syntax**, which is where a voice actually lives
Not one respelled word. Ever.

## 6. THE DIALS A MACHINE CAN SEE, AND THE ONES IT CANNOT
Honest ledger, because pretending otherwise is how this lane fails.
**A MACHINE CAN COUNT:** contraction rate, sentence-length spread inside a scene,
repeated openers, banned phrases, question marks, stumbles, hedges, em dashes,
whether a scene's structure moved.
**A MACHINE CANNOT TELL:** whether a line is good. Whether the euphemism lands.
Whether the tic is charming or annoying. Whether cutting the last sentence made
the speech better or just shorter. Whether two characters sound different, as
opposed to merely having different word counts.
**THE ONLY TEST FOR THE SECOND LIST IS READING IT OUT LOUD, AND COVERING THE
NAMES.**

## 7. THREE TIMES A NUMBER LIED TO ME IN ONE DAY, ALL IN MY FAVOUR
Keeping this in the training file because it is the most transferable lesson.
1. The stumble counter said **zero**. It demanded two identical ADJACENT words,
   so "Easy. Easy, easy." (comma) and "No. No, no." (case) read as clean. The
   real number was **twelve**.
2. My own gate had `if (!ok(...)) return;` and `ok()` returns whatever `++`
   evaluated to, so it was **silently skipping fifteen of its own checks**.
3. After a mechanical contraction pass the sermon count "improved" from 33.5% to
   18.1% — because contracting "That is" to "That's" **hid the word from my own
   detector**. Nothing had cut a single sermon.
**ALL THREE WERE FOUND THE SAME WAY: a number moved when nothing had been done
to move it. WHEN A METRIC AGREES WITH YOU TOO HARD, SUSPECT THE METRIC.**

# ROUND 2 — 8/27/26, same day, going after primary sources

## 9. THE BIGGEST THING I HAVE LEARNED SO FAR, FULL STOP
### STUDS TERKEL CUT A MILLION WORDS DOWN TO A HUNDRED THOUSAND
*Working* began with **about a million words** of interview transcript and the
published book is **about a tenth of that**. He also deleted his own questions,
so the speech reads as one person talking. Oral historians criticise him for it:
he edits, he cuts, he does not fully describe his subjects. He described his own
method as **"a prospector for gold"** sifting for each person's **"essence"**.

**WHY THIS IS THE WHOLE ANSWER TO MY PROBLEM.** The reason Terkel's people sound
so alive is NOT fidelity. It is SELECTION. He kept the one line in ten that could
only have come from that person and threw away the nine that could have come from
anybody.

A machine's failure mode is the exact opposite: it GENERATES THE AVERAGE, because
the most probable next word is by definition the least surprising one. So the fix
is not to write better on the first pass. **THE FIX IS TO WRITE LONG AND THEN CUT
NINE TENTHS, KEEPING ONLY THE STRANGE SPECIFIC PART.**
That is a process I can actually follow. It is also why "cut the last sentence"
worked on our scenes: it was a tiny version of this.

## 10. ACTUAL RECORDED SPEECH, AND WHAT ITS SHAPE TEACHES
Specimens, not summaries. This is what I came for.

### SPECIMEN A — a doctor, on hunger
> "every day … someone would faint on a streetcar. They'd bring him in, and they
> wouldn't ask any questions.… they knew what it was. **Hunger.**"

Three plain clauses, then a **ONE-WORD SENTENCE**, held to the end and delivered
alone. That is "nine words then two" occurring in the wild. And notice the last
word is a NAMED THING, not a lesson. He does not say "that is what poverty does
to a city." He says the noun and stops.

### SPECIMEN B — Mary Willingham, practical nurse, Athens Georgia, 1939
> "The most I ever got in one week was $14 and that was on a nursin' job. I'll
> never forgit what the man said that hired me after my $14-a-week patient got to
> where she didn't need me no mo'. He didn't offer me but $10 a week, and I
> didn't want to take $4 lose than I had been gittin' and **I told him so**."

- **ANCHORED TO A NUMBER.** $14, $14, $10, $4. A working person's grievance is
  denominated. It is never "they underpaid me."
- **IT IS A RUN-ON.** Three clauses stacked with "and". Not neat, not balanced.
- **IT ENDS ON WHAT SHE DID**, not on what it means. "I told him so." No moral.
- The whole speech is one grievance told as a story with a specific man in it.

### SPECIMEN C — a man interviewed by Ralph Ellison, New York, 1939
> "Ahm in New York, but New York ain't in me." … "I'm from Jacksonville Florida.
> Been in New York twenty-five years. **I'm a New Yorker!**"

He **CONTRADICTS HIMSELF INSIDE THREE SENTENCES** and does not notice. New York
is not in him; he is a New Yorker. That is not sloppy writing, that is a person.
**OUR CHARACTERS NEVER CONTRADICT THEMSELVES.** Every one of them holds a single
coherent position for the length of a scene, which is the tell of a written
argument rather than a talking human.

### SPECIMEN D — an ironworker
> "You ain't an Iron worker unless you get killed."

Trade pride delivered as fatalism. A joke that is not a joke. The whole identity
of a trade in ten words, and it is a boast and a complaint at once.

### SPECIMEN E — Virginia Durr, on the logic of the crash
> "**Have you ever seen a child with rickets?** No proteins, no milk. And the
> companies pouring milk into gutters. People with nothing to wear, and they were
> plowing up cotton. People with nothing to eat, and they killed the pigs. If
> that wasn't the craziest system in the world, **could you imagine anything more
> idiotic?**"

- **OPENS ON A QUESTION TO THE LISTENER AND CLOSES ON ANOTHER ONE.** Two question
  marks in ONE speech. Our whole corpus of 504 NPC speeches had two, total.
- **VERBLESS FRAGMENTS**: "No proteins, no milk."
- **PARALLELISM, AND IT IS EARNED**: three "people with nothing / and they" beats
  stacked. She is furious, and fury is what produces parallel structure. When our
  characters do parallelism calmly it reads as an essay; when a furious person
  does it, it reads as a person losing it.

## 11. A CORRECTION TO MY OWN CARD, FROM THE PRIMARY SOURCES
The voice card says CUT THE LAST SENTENCE and treats every general truth as a
tell. **The transcripts prove that is too absolute.** A real jobless man in the
Depression said:
> "A man is not a man without work."
That IS a maxim, and it is real, and it is devastating.

**THE REFINEMENT.** A maxim is legitimate when THREE things are true at once:
1. the person is at the end of their rope about **the specific thing destroying
   them** — not about life in general
2. it is **blunt and short**, the shape of something said through the teeth, not
   balanced and writerly
3. it is **RARE**
Compare ours: *"That is worth more out here than you will understand for a few
years."* Balanced, patient, generous, teacherly. Nobody at the end of their rope
talks like that.
**THE TEST IS NOT "IS IT A GENERAL TRUTH". THE TEST IS "IS THIS PERSON IN ENOUGH
PAIN TO EARN IT, AND DID THEY SAY IT SHORT."** A third of our speeches ending on
one was the problem. Two or three in a whole game, earned, is the target.

## 12. THE EYE-DIALECT TRAP, AND HOW TO TAKE FROM THESE SOURCES SAFELY
The 1930s transcribers wrote "nursin'", "forgit", "no mo'", "gittin'", "ain't",
"Ahm". Modern research is clear that respelling makes the reader work for nothing
and has a long history of making the speaker look stupid, and Bohemia bans it.

**SO: TAKE THE SYNTAX, LEAVE THE SPELLING.** From Mary Willingham I take the
number-anchoring, the stacked "and", the ending on what she did. I do not take
"gittin'". From the ironworker I take "You ain't an Iron worker unless you get
killed" — and "ain't" stays, because **AIN'T IS A REAL WORD, NOT A RESPELLING**.
That is the line: a non-standard WORD or GRAMMAR is fair; a standard word spelled
wrong to imitate a mouth is not.

## 13. THE HUNDRED-HOUR PROBLEM, AND SOMEBODY HAS ALREADY SOLVED THE MECHANISM
Bohemia is one character, about 100 hours, and a generated valley. Nobody
hand-writes that much at Terkel quality. The mechanism half is solved and
documented:
**Elan Ruskin, GDC 2012, "AI-driven Dynamic Dialog through Fuzzy Pattern
Matching"** — the system behind Left 4 Dead 2, Team Fortress 2 and DOTA 2.
Instead of branching code, it matches **thousands of facts about the world state
and the speaker** against a rule database and the most specific matching rule
wins. A line is chosen by WHO THIS PERSON IS AND WHAT IS TRUE RIGHT NOW.
That is the machine version of Emily Short's point in the lane brief: characters
should speak from their own **knowledge and memory**, not from a bag of lines.
Valve's own writing practice alongside it: **write three to ten variants per
trigger**, and let the criteria pick.

**WHAT THIS MEANS FOR ME.** The hundred hours are not written. They are
**CONDITIONED**. The craft question stops being "write 50,000 lines" and becomes
"what does this person KNOW that nobody else on this street knows, and what fact
about tonight makes them say it." That is a much smaller and much better question.
[NOT BUILT, NOT PROPOSED. Recording the mechanism only; the QUESTS/PEOPLE lanes
own systems, and I am in training and touching nothing.]

## 14. THE RUNNING LIST OF WHAT MAKES A BOHEMIA LINE SOUND HUMAN
Consolidated from both rounds. This is the thing I am actually training toward.
1. **Contract it.** Spelling a phrase out is a choice made once, for weight.
2. **Anchor it to a number or a thing.** $14. Nine o'clock. Half a tank. Two
   cases of water. Never "resources", never "the economy".
3. **End on what happened or what they did**, not on what it means.
4. **Let them contradict themselves** inside one speech and not notice.
5. **Let them ask.** Real anger and real fear both produce questions.
6. **When they are scared, take their words away.** Measured: fear shortens.
7. **When they refuse, make them buy a second first.** Measured: 269ms vs 561ms.
8. **One tic per person, used twice.**
9. **One noun only they would reach for.**
10. **Write long, then cut nine tenths.** The Terkel method is the whole job.
11. **Cover the names and read it.** If you cannot tell who is speaking, the
    scene is one person arguing with themselves.

# ROUND 3 — 8/27/26, the two findings that change the whole cast

## 16. "BORN INTO RUIN" — BOHEMIA HAS TWO GENERATIONS AND I HAD BEEN WRITING ONE
From work on Rust Belt cities: younger residents were **"born into ruin"**, so
they **lack the nostalgia of older generations**, who carry what the research
calls **heavy loss narratives**. The young are not burdened by having known the
before.

**THIS IS THE SINGLE MOST USEFUL STRUCTURAL FACT I HAVE FOUND FOR THIS GAME.**
Bohemia's crash is about ten years back — the cold open is *"the family, the
table, ten years ago."* So the valley splits clean:

**ANYONE OVER ABOUT TWENTY-FIVE** remembers the before. They compare. They date
things to it ("since the lights went"). They carry a loss narrative and they will
tell you about it whether you asked or not. Their speech reaches backward.

**ANYONE UNDER ABOUT TWENTY** was born into this. **THEY HAVE NO NOSTALGIA AT
ALL.** This is not a ruined world to them, it is the world. They do not know what
a bank was and they are not sad about it. They find the old people's comparisons
BORING. Their speech reaches sideways and forward, at what is actually in front
of them.

**AND THAT IS A FREE ENGINE FOR FRICTION.** An old man saying "this used to be a
buffet" to a kid who does not care is an entire relationship in two lines, and
neither of them is wrong. It costs nothing and it makes every scene with an age
gap in it play.

**THE TEST I WILL USE:** does this character date anything to the crash? If they
are young and they do, I have written an old person in a young body.

## 17. NOBODY WHO LIVES IN A RUIN CALLS IT A RUIN
Same research, and it corrects something I suspect we do badly. Actual residents
of "Rust Belt" cities **push back on the label**, preferring their own names for
where they live. People who live in a place that outsiders call dead do not talk
about it as dead. **They defend it.** They say it is fine, you just have to know
where to go, and they get short with you if you say otherwise.

**SO OUR PEOPLE PROBABLY TALK ABOUT THE APOCALYPSE TOO MUCH.** A character who
keeps describing the state of the world is a narrator, not a resident. A resident
talks about tonight, this block, that cable, their kid. The collapse is the
WEATHER, and nobody explains the weather to somebody standing in it.
[TO MEASURE WHEN I AM UNFROZEN: how many of our lines describe the state of the
world rather than the state of tonight. I would bet it is high. Not measuring
now; I am touching nothing.]

## 18. VEGAS, 2008-2009 — THE CLOSEST REAL THING TO BOHEMIA'S PREMISE
The valley genuinely did this once. Unemployment hit **13.4%**; about **113,000**
Nevadans were claiming unemployment, many at risk of running out of payments
before finding work. Las Vegas was called the deepest slide since the 1940s.
What survives in the reporting is better than the statistics:

- **"The unemployed ate less and drank more, mostly during happy hour."**
  That is the texture. Not a graph. A specific hour of the day.
- The out-of-work described as **"an unorganized confederacy of people yearning
  to connect"** — they kept running into each other.
- From a later Vegas job-loss documentary, a woman, verbatim:
  **"I cry and pray a whole lot."**
  Six words. No metaphor. "A whole lot" is the intensifier a real person reaches
  for. Compare anything our characters say about grief.

**WHAT I TAKE:** the specific hour, the specific number, the plain intensifier.
And the fact that people in a collapse **keep bumping into each other** — the
valley is not lonely, it is a small town that used to be a city.

## 22. THE ONE THAT EMBARRASSES ME: THESE LINES LIVE ON A PHONE
I have written and rewritten hundreds of lines this week without once asking how
wide the screen is.

The standards: **38 characters per line maximum on mobile**, and fewer on a phone.
**Never more than two lines at once.** Reading speed 17 CPS for a general
audience, 20 CPS for experienced readers, and above about 20 CPS text becomes
hard to read at all.

Bohemia is iPhone portrait. Two lines at 38 characters is about **76 characters
on screen**.

Now measure a line I was pleased with:
> "I don't steal it. I move it. The grid doesn't belong to anybody so there's
> nothing there to steal, I'm just faster than the meter. That's it."

**About 145 characters. Roughly four phone lines. Nearly double the ceiling.**
I was congratulating myself on the rhythm of a run-on that may be a wall of text
on the only screen this game runs on.

**THE HONEST CAVEAT, because overcorrecting would be its own error:** those are
SUBTITLE standards, for text that disappears on a timer. If a Bohemia line waits
for a tap, length is far less punishing, and a long line can be a deliberate
effect. **I DO NOT CURRENTLY KNOW WHICH ONE BOHEMIA DOES.** That is a fact about
our own build I have never checked, and it decides whether this finding is a
crisis or a footnote. FIRST THING TO CHECK WHEN UNFROZEN.

## 23. TERKEL AND KURVITZ ARE TWO HALVES OF ONE METHOD
Round 2 got Terkel: **cut a million words to a hundred thousand.**
Round 4 got the other half. Robert Kurvitz on Disco Elysium: they
**"write every scene as hard as we can"**, including **every kind of weird or
stupid thought that comes to them.**

Put together, that is the whole thing:
**WRITE EVERYTHING, INCLUDING THE STUPID THOUGHTS. THEN CUT NINE TENTHS.**

And that is the exact diagnosis of machine prose, better than anything in the
lane brief. A machine does NEITHER half. It writes the ONE most probable thought
and keeps it. No overproduction, so nothing strange ever gets generated. No
selection, so nothing average ever gets removed. Flat output is not a style
failure, it is the absence of both halves of the process.

## 24. OUR PLAYER IS NEVER ALLOWED TO BE A FOOL
Disco Elysium will let a failed check make you say **"I want to have f— with
you"**, and have the woman answer **"that's literally not how language works."**
The game lets the player character humiliate himself.

**All 558 of our player choices are competent.** Every one is composed, capable,
and says the thing a person would say if they had thought about it first. Nobody
in this game has ever misjudged a room, said the wrong thing, or been laughed at.
That is not a player character, that is a spokesman.

## 25. GALLOWS HUMOR — THE ENTIRE MISSING REGISTER, AND IT BELONGS TO OUR CAST
Our corpus has **zero jokes** that I can find and zero exclamation marks in 504
speeches. I had filed that under "the setting is bleak". That is wrong.

Gallows humor is documented as a coping mechanism in exactly the jobs Bohemia is
made of: **medical, police, military, disaster response** — the people who meet
death as a routine. It is a pressure valve, and researchers describe it as
psychological survival, not callousness.

The ironworker's line from Round 2 is gallows humor:
> "You ain't an Iron worker unless you get killed."
A boast and a complaint and a joke, at once, in ten words.

**SO OUR MEDIC, OUR WATCH, OUR LINEMEN AND OUR SCAVENGERS SHOULD BE MAKING JOKES
ABOUT DYING.** Not comic relief characters: the specific, dry, unbothered humor
of people who handle bad things for a living. Its absence is a large part of why
everyone in our valley sounds like a mourner. **THE PEOPLE CLOSEST TO DEATH JOKE
THE MOST. WE HAVE IT BACKWARDS.**

## 26. RECIPIENT DESIGN — AND IT IS A SYSTEM BOHEMIA ALREADY HAS THE PARTS FOR
Conversation analysis calls it **recipient design**: talk is built for the
particular person hearing it, tuned to what the speaker assumes they know.
> "If a speaker says 'Jane came for dinner', they are treating their recipient as
> knowing who Jane is."
Speakers also slow down and stretch their delivery when they know the listener
does **not** share their knowledge.

**OUR PLAYER IS A STRANGER TO EVERYONE, FOREVER.** So every NPC explains
everything, which is realistic for a first meeting and is also why every NPC
sounds like an exposition machine. Nobody ever treats you as an insider.

**THE LEVER:** Bohemia already tracks bond and clout. A line for a stranger names
the thing in full. A line for somebody known uses the shorthand and skips the
explanation. **BEING TALKED TO IN SHORTHAND IS HOW A GAME MAKES YOU FEEL LIKE A
LOCAL**, and it costs one condition on a line rather than any new system.
[RECORDING THE IDEA ONLY. Systems belong to other lanes and I am touching nothing.]

## 27. CHILDREN, FINALLY USEFUL
Two rounds of clinical papers gave me pitch and comprehension speed. The craft
sources gave me the actual thing:
- **The two failure modes are TOO ELOQUENT and TOO CUTESY**, and writers reach for
  one or the other. Both are the same mistake: writing an idea of a child.
- **Kids do recipient design too** — simpler with younger kids, more complicated
  questions aimed at adults.
- **Topic jumping.** They change the subject.
- **Age-appropriate concerns**, and this is the Bohemia one: a child in a
  catastrophe worries about **their dog, their shoes, whether somebody is angry
  with them.** Not about the collapse.
- Context changes them: chatty at home, silent on the street.

Put that beside Round 3's "born into ruin" and the valley's children write
themselves: **no nostalgia, kid-sized worries, changes the subject, and says the
true thing at the wrong moment.** Never a small philosopher.

## 28. THE PLAYER'S LINES ARE A DIFFERENT CRAFT, AND WE ARE ACCIDENTALLY RIGHT
Mass Effect's dialogue wheel showed **paraphrases**, not the line. Players picked
"Don't try to study me" and Shepard said "I'm not some artifact you can take back
to your lab, doctor." The complaint that followed was not about quality; it was
that **the character said something the player did not choose.**

**Our @OPT lines are the spoken line, verbatim.** That is the right side of this,
and it was not a decision anybody wrote down, which means it can be lost by
accident. Recording it so it is not: **in Bohemia, what you pick is what you say.**
Our parentheticals — "(say nothing, just go)" — are the honest exception, because
they promise an ACTION rather than a line, and they keep that promise.

## 29. YOU CANNOT MEASURE "GOOD". YOU CAN MEASURE "UNDERSTOOD".
The voice gate says out loud that it cannot tell whether a line is good, and that
is true and it has bothered me. There is a real bridge and it has a name.

**NARRATIVE USABILITY** (developed at Microsoft Studios): test the story early,
find the twists that do not work and the conclusions that confuse, and remove
**understanding blockers** so the characters can be seen. Studios ask narrative
comprehension questions, score them (-2 to +2), and track the same questions
across playtests to see the number move.

**THE REFRAME I NEEDED:** beauty is not measurable, but COMPREHENSION IS. "Did
you know what he wanted from you?" "Whose side did you think she was on?" "What
did you think would happen if you cut it?" Those are answerable, scoreable and
trackable, and they catch the failure that actually kills a scene, which is not
an ugly line, it is a player who did not understand what they were being asked.
[The demo gap list already wants a feedback card. Not proposing, not building.]

# ROUND 5 — 8/27/26. Still hunting the unknown unknowns.

## 33. THE BIGGEST ONE THIS ROUND, AND IT IS ABOUT BOHEMIA'S CORE SHAPE
Named plainly in the craft literature as the worst habit in the medium:
> **"One of the least person-like habits of typical NPC dialogue is that they
> always answer the same questions in the exact same words, regardless of how
> many times the player has asked."**

**NOW PUT THAT NEXT TO WHAT BOHEMIA IS.** There are no runs. One character. About
a hundred hours. You live on a block, with the same neighbours, for the length of
the whole game. Our @TALK nodes are static: the lineman says the same three lines
tonight, tomorrow, and in eighty hours.

**IN A GAME BUILT ON PERSISTENCE, A PERSON WHO REPEATS HERSELF VERBATIM IS THE
SINGLE MOST IMMERSION-BREAKING THING THERE IS**, and it gets worse the longer
somebody plays, which means it is worst for exactly the player we want. A game
with runs hides this. Ours cannot.

I have never checked whether our build does this. I believe it does. That belief
is the most important unverified claim in this file.

## 34. AND THE ARCHITECTURE THAT FIXES IT IS ALREADY DESCRIBED
Two memories, not one:
- **CONVERSATIONAL MEMORY** — what has passed between this person and you
- **WORLD KNOWLEDGE MEMORY** — what this person knows about the world

And the part that made me sit up: **dialogue checks each participant's memories
INDIVIDUALLY**, which buys **one-sided recollections, misunderstandings, and
hidden information.** Somebody remembers a thing you did that you have forgotten.
Somebody remembers it WRONG. Somebody knows a thing about you and never says so.

The relationship arc most systems use is: polite and distant at first, warmer and
more familiar as it goes.

## 35. TWO SEPARATE RESEARCH PATHS ARRIVED AT THE SAME LEVER
Round 4 got **recipient design** out of conversation analysis: talk is built for
what the speaker assumes you already know, and a speaker who thinks you do not
share their knowledge slows down and explains.
Round 5 got **favorability arcs** out of game systems work: distant and formal
first, shorthand and warm later.

**THOSE ARE THE SAME MECHANISM DESCRIBED BY A LINGUIST AND BY AN ENGINEER.**
Independent paths, one lever, and it is a lever Bohemia already has the parts
for — bond, clout, factions, and a deeds layer that already publishes what you
did and can already compute WHY somebody feels about you the way they do.
**The machinery may exist and the WORDS may be the missing half.**
[Recording the convergence, not proposing anything. Systems are other lanes'.]

## 36. THE GREETING IS THE HIGHEST-FREQUENCY LINE IN A GAME AND I NEVER THOUGHT
## ABOUT IT ONCE
A greeting can be about: the player (how they look, what is suspected of them),
the surroundings (recent events, who is fighting whom), something personal to the
speaker, a complaint about the neighbours, or a belief. The failure is the
introduction: *"hi there, sorry to bother you, I'm the town blacksmith."*
Also technical and easily missed: a greeting should be ONE line, because the
player can pick an option or walk before a second one appears.
**For Bohemia the greeting is enormous** — a hundred hours on one block means the
same hello, hundreds of times. It is the line most worth varying and the one I
have spent the least thought on.

## 37. SWEARING IS AN INDIVIDUALIZER, AND OUR VALLEY IS UNIFORM
The craft finding is not "swear more". It is:
> **"Having a character who habitually uses profanity in contrast to others who
> don't is, in itself, a good individualizer."**
And: **when a character's language shifts, the shift should be doing work** —
escalation, a change in power, a loss of composure that means something.

So profanity is a DIAL PER PERSON, and the value is in the CONTRAST. If nobody
swears, we lose a free way to tell people apart AND we lose the moment where the
one who never swears finally does. If everybody swears, the same loss.
**Our corpus is uniform, at zero.** Given who Paolo is and how he talks, I would
guess the valley is under-salted rather than over. But this is HIS call about HIS
world, it is not a research question, and I am recording the mechanism only.

## 38. WITH NO PROPER NAMES, WHAT PEOPLE CALL YOU IS THE WHOLE RELATIONSHIP
Our quests forbid proper names by design (W8), so a quest can cast against
whoever actually exists. I had been treating that as a limitation to work around.
It is not. It forces the **term of address** to carry everything.

Sociolinguistics gives the two axes: **POWER and SOLIDARITY** (Brown and Gilman).
Address terms "enact intimacy, foster collective belonging, and enhance
solidarity", and the choice signals how the speaker imagines their relationship
with you.
So in Bohemia: *nothing at all* / "hey" / "man" / "kid" / "friend" / "brother" /
"boss" / "sir" is not decoration. **IT IS THE RELATIONSHIP, STATED.** And it can
move: the man who called you nothing in week one calling you "kid" in week six is
a whole arc with no new system behind it.
**W8 IS NOT A CONSTRAINT ON THE WRITING. IT IS THE WRITING.**

## 39. THE ANSWER TO MY OWN BIGGEST OPEN QUESTION, AND I DO NOT LIKE IT
Round 4 ended asking whether a distinctive voice can survive being scaled with
tools. The industry answer is clear and it is not a tool.

Studios scale voice with **A HUMAN EDITORIAL PASS.** A game editor exists whose
job is refining narrative and content quality and **ensuring consistency in
character arcs, tone and pacing**. Leads mentor, leads revise. The written
artifacts — **world bibles, character bios, style documents** — are INPUTS TO
HUMANS, not transformers of text.

**SO MY CONTRACTION TOOL WAS NOT A VOICE MECHANISM. IT WAS A SPELLING
NORMALIZER**, and I was right to hold "contraction-passed" apart from
"voice-passed" but I did not fully understand why until now. Voice does not
scale through substitution. **It scales through a person reading every scene and
cutting.** Which is Terkel again, from the other direction.
**WHAT THAT MEANS FOR MY WORK WHEN UNFROZEN:** the job is not more tools. It is
reading scenes as an editor, one at a time. And the cast sheet in this file is a
proto **character bible**, which is exactly the right artifact to be building
while frozen.
