## 0. THE QUESTION, IN ONE BREATH
BATTERIES ARE THE MONEY (Paolo 9/4: 'for one aa battery a bag of rice'). Ten wiki crowns are one
battery and one battery is the floor (his sixth votes 10/2, GROK_31 and GROK_33). The settlement
screen's shelves price every weapon and plate off the wiki at that rate, and the stall sells four
goods at one battery each until ECONOMY rules the shelf (RUN TWO's own comment in
slices/BOHEMIA_SETTLEMENT_SCREEN.html: 'What the shelf holds is ECONOMY's to rule'). But nobody
has written down THE EXCHANGE A PLAYER FEELS ON DAY ONE: he walks up to the first counter with
whatever the door handed him, and one battery buys WHAT. This page answers that for the four
things the row names (bread, a round of drinks, a bandage, a bullet) and the four piles the bar
shows, each with the wiki price it maps from, the data file that already carries it, and the real
crash case it echoes. WORDS and RUN TWO read the same number here; TUNING holds it.

## 1. HOW BATTLE BROTHERS DOES IT, FROM THE WIKI ITSELF
Sources: the wiki's Provisions, Medical Supplies, Ammunition, Tools and Supplies, Bandages, Market
prices for various items and Game Guide pages (reference/library/grok/wiki/PAGES_ALL.tar.gz;
bb_all/1608_Provisions.txt, 1253_Medical Supplies.txt, 0055_Ammunition.txt, 2014_Tools and
Supplies.txt, 0173_Bandages.txt, 1244_Market prices for various items.txt, 0827_Game Guide.txt).

**1a. DAY ONE IN BATTLE BROTHERS IS A FULL PURSE AND FOUR FULL PILES.** The Game Guide's starting
funds table: High 2,500 crowns, 50 provisions, 40 tools, 80 ammo, 30 medicine; Medium 2,000 /
50 / 20 / 40 / 20; Low 1,500 / 50 / 10 / 20 / 10. Every origin then moves the crowns (the wiki's
own origin pages; our records/target/bb/origins.json carries all fifteen at ten to one: A New
Company 290 / 240 / 190 batteries, the Poachers 250 / 200 / 150, the Lone Wolf 125 / 100 / 75).
So a Battle Brothers player NEVER meets the counter with one coin. He meets it with 150 to 320
batteries and fifty meals already in the cart. His first purchase is a question of what to add,
not whether he can eat.

**1b. THE FOUR PILES HAVE A WIKI WORTH EACH, AND THE COUNTER SELLS THEM BY THE BUNDLE.**
| pile | wiki unit sold | base worth, crowns | what one unit does | page |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| provisions | a stack of 25 | ground grains 50, bread 65, dried fish 70, smoked ham 95, wine 110, master rations 150 | a man eats 2 a day (more on hard ground) | Provisions |
| medical supplies | a bundle (points) | 200 | 1 point a day per injury, or it does not heal | Medical Supplies |
| ammunition | a bundle of 50 points | 100 | 1 point an arrow or bolt, 2 a handgonne shot, 3 a thrown weapon | Ammunition |
| tools and supplies | a bundle (points) | 200 | 1 point repairs 15 durability | Tools and Supplies |
| bandages (a combat item) | one | 25 | stops bleeding, 6 AP, 5 fatigue | Bandages |
The base worth is multiplied by the settlement's buying modifier first (a village 0.95 to 1.04,
a town 1.08 to 1.17, a city 1.22 to 1.34 at neutral relations, the Market prices page), then by
the situation (siege doubles food; well supplied cuts 10%).

**1c. THE SMALLEST THING ON THE WIKI'S COUNTER IS THE BANDAGE, AND IT IS 25 CROWNS.** Nothing a
player buys in Battle Brothers costs less than that; a single loaf is never sold, only the stack.
So the wiki's own smallest purchase is already two and a half of our batteries, and its cheapest
STACK of food (grains, 50) is five. That is the whole reason this page exists: at ten to one the
floor of one battery sits UNDER everything on the wiki's shelf, and a stranger with one battery in
his hand is a situation Battle Brothers never has to price.

**1d. WHAT A BATTLE BROTHERS CROWN BUYS, FOR SCALE.** A per-head contract bonus is 10 crowns (one
battery, GROK_31). A cheap man's daily wage is 10 to 20 crowns (backgrounds.json; the tier-one
list in price_table.json wageAndHire). A stack of 25 grains at 50 crowns feeds one man 12 days.
So on the wiki a day of the cheapest labour buys about five days of the cheapest food. Keep that
ratio; it is the one this page tests ours against.

## 2. THE REAL RECORD: WHAT THE SMALLEST COIN BOUGHT ON THE FIRST DAY THE MONEY DIED
This lane's Day 1, Day 17 and Day 67 pages already hold the cases; this section pulls the DAY
ONE line from each, the one a player standing at a counter would feel.

**2a. ZIMBABWE 2008: THE SMALLEST DENOMINATION WAS CANDY.** When the shops repriced in dollars and
rand, nobody had imported coins, so change was given in sweets (Day 1, 2b). The bottom of the
money supply was whatever was in the box by the till. OURS: the one-battery floor IS that box. A
thing worth less than a battery does not get a smaller price; it gets bundled up to one, or it
is thrown in. That is not a design shortcut, it is what a till does when the small coin is gone.

**2b. VENEZUELA 2017 TO 2019: NO PRICE TAG, THE NUMBER IS SAID AT THE TILL.** Corn flour 220 one
day, 240 the next, 260 the day after; stores with no tags on the goods (Day 1, 2c). The price
is a property of the conversation, not the object. OURS: the stall keeper says the number with a
face (the law, A QUEST IS PEOPLE, PLACES AND THINGS; text comes from a mouth with a portrait);
the shelf's tiles carry no price until he taps, and the tap opens the keeper's line with the
number in it. RUN TWO already built it that way (openB: 'Hardtack. 6 batteries.'). This page
does not change it.

**2c. ARGENTINA 2001: THE FIRST WEEK'S CASH CAP WAS 250 PESOS, AND THE BARTER CLUB TICKET WAS
GOOD AT ONE CLUB'S TABLES ONLY.** The corralito capped withdrawals at 250 a week; the clubs ran
their own scrip, local to the node (Day 1, 2a). OURS: a battery is good everywhere in the valley
(one money, his 9/4), which is the one place we are MORE orderly than the record on purpose; the
localness lives in the SHELF (Day 62: what moves per settlement is what is on it) and in who will
sell to a stranger at all (the belonging rung), never in the coin.

**2d. LEBANON 2019 TO 2023: A DAY'S PAY BOUGHT A DAY'S FOOD AND NOTHING MORE.** The minimum wage
fell from about $450 a month to about $7 at the street rate, then was raised in lumps and fell
again; a teacher's official wage was worth under two dollars and 98 percent of her real pay moved
out of the wage line into side work and remittances (Day 17). The survival food basket rose 56
percent in seven months (Day 1, 2d). OURS: Day 49 found the game is already exactly here: work
pays one, food costs one, the loan asks one a night, so he is one short every day. This page's
table keeps that shape and shows what the one he DOES have buys.

**2e. WEIMAR 1923 (Day 67): PRICES LAGGED FOR THE FIRST WEEK, AND RENT WAS THE SLOWEST PRICE OF
ALL.** Week one's first sign is scarcity, not a sticker. OURS: the opening counter should have a
short shelf before it has a high price; Day 62's shelf-by-settlement already does the short
shelf, and the one-battery floor keeps the first price low. Both halves of the record are met by
things already built.

## 3. OURS, MEASURED ON MAIN: EVERY PRICE AT THE COUNTER, AND WHERE EACH ONE LIVES
**3a. The exchange rate and the floor:** records/target/bb/price_table.json conversion:
crownsPerBattery 10, floorBatteries 1, 'town markup first, then divide by 10, then floor at 1'.
The settlement screen's priceOf(v) is Math.max(1, Math.round(v/10)) (round to nearest; GROK_31
says round down; Day 57 routed that one-battery disagreement to RUN TWO, who owns the file; it is
still open and it bites exactly once on this page, at the bandage).

**3b. What the stall sells today, and at what:** four goods at ONE battery each, by RUN TWO's own
placeholder (STALL in slices/BOHEMIA_SETTLEMENT_SCREEN.html: a ration of food, water four litres,
a dose of meds, six rounds), 'until ECONOMY rules the shelf'. The purse prices every GOODS entry at
one (engine/bohemia_purse.js PRICES, RULED_ONE: '8/15 EVERYTHING COSTS ONE + 9/4 BATTERIES ARE
THE MONEY'). The barber is one battery. A build on a lot is one battery and a day. A round of
drinks is a battery a head (roundPrice: Math.max(1, crewSize)). The clinic is the one price over
one that a day-one player can meet: 20 crowns a laid-up day, two batteries, times a level factor
(TUNING's one table, clinic.base_price; typed in the roster screen, not yet read from the table).

**3c. What the door hands him:** THREE DIFFERENT ANSWERS ON MAIN, AND THAT IS THE FINDING OF THE
ROUND (section 4). The demo: RUN 2490fc8 measured (9/22) that he wakes with ZERO batteries, and
engine/bohemia_origins.js says in its own header 'NO STARTING MONEY, NO WAGE, NO GEAR'. The
settlement screen standalone: 3 batteries ('so it can be played'). The data file:
records/target/bb/origins.json carries Battle Brothers' own starting funds at ten to one, 75 to
320 batteries by origin and funds level, with nothing reading the batteries block (the fifteen
origins at the door build the company and the cap, RUN 89f993e; the purse is not in that commit).

**3d. The four piles' daily draw (so a battery can be read as DAYS):** food 2 units a man a day
(price_table.json dailyRates.foodPerManPerDay), scaled by terrain 1.0 to 2.0 (stash_rates.json);
medicine 1 a day per open injury; tools 1 point per 15 durability; ammo 1 to 3 points a shot off a
50-point bundle (stash_rates.json, from the wiki).

**4a. THE SHAPE, READ ACROSS THE TABLE.** Everything a stranger needs on day one is ONE, except
the two things that are about not dying: the bandage (two) and the clinic (two a day). That is
the wiki's own ladder at ten to one, floored, not a design pick: food, meds, tape and bullets all
divide into units under a battery, so the floor catches them at one; the bandage is the one item
the wiki sells singly above the floor, so it is the one thing that costs more than a battery at
the first counter. A player feels: a battery is a day of anything, and two batteries is the price
of a wound.

**4b. THE RATIO TEST FROM 1d.** On the wiki the cheapest day of labour (10 to 20 crowns) buys
about five days of the cheapest food (a 50-crown stack feeds twelve days; 20 crowns buys about
five days). Ours at the floor: a day's work pays one battery (PAYOUT.COMPLETE, 'a day's work pays
a battery'), and one battery buys ONE ration, one day. So OUR DAY ONE IS FIVE TIMES HARDER THAN
BATTLE BROTHERS' DAY ONE, by the wiki's own numbers. That is not a bug; it is Day 49's 'one short
every day' and the pillar (the game starts in the ruin, the floor is the start). But it is a
number TUNING should hold with its eyes open: the floor of one, applied to a ration instead of a
stack, is the single biggest difficulty lever in the opening, bigger than any enemy stat.

**4c. THE RATION, NOT THE STACK, IS THE RIGHT COUNTER UNIT, AND HERE IS WHY IT IS NOT INVENTED.**
The wiki sells by the stack because the player has 2,000 crowns. A stranger with one battery
cannot buy a stack, so the stall must sell the unit the floor can reach; the real record does the
same thing (Zimbabwe's candy, Venezuela's single tablets, every corner shop in every collapse
selling the cigarette instead of the pack). The stack price stays on the data file (6 for bread,
5 for grains) for a crew that can afford it; the ration at one is the floor doing its job.

## 5. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG: THE DOOR HANDS HIM THREE DIFFERENT PURSES
Three things on main disagree about what a player holds when he first reaches a counter:
- the demo: ZERO batteries (RUN 2490fc8, 9/22; the origins engine's own 'NO STARTING MONEY');
- the settlement screen standalone: THREE;
- the data file: Battle Brothers' own 75 to 320 at ten to one, with nothing reading it.
Battle Brothers never asks this question because every origin answers it on its page. Ours has
fifteen origins at the door (RUN 89f993e) and a batteries block per origin in origins.json, and the
purse is never seeded from it. THE DAY-ONE EXCHANGE THIS PAGE PRICES IS ONLY FELT IF THE DOOR
HANDS HIM SOMETHING TO EXCHANGE. With zero he feels nothing at the counter; he feels Day 49's loan.
With Battle Brothers' 190 to 290 he feels a stack, not a ration, and the floor never bites. Which
of the three is right is a felt number and a pillar (the floor is the start; 'economic apocalypse
Battle Brothers'), so it is not mine to pick: routed to TUNING (the one table takes a row
'origin.start_batteries' with the three candidates and their sources) and to RUN (the door seeds
the purse from whichever row TUNING holds). What this page CAN say from the record: every real
case starts a person with a little, not nothing and not a month's pay; Argentina's 250 a week was
about a week of food. A week of rations for a crew of three is about twenty-one batteries, which
sits nowhere near either of our two live numbers and well under the wiki's. Named, not picked.
