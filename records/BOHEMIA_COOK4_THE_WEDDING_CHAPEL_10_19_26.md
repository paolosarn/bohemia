## The building
The Strip's own kind of chapel (the Little Church of the West, 1942; the Little White Wedding Chapel, 1951): one room,
not a parish church, and a sign on a pole bigger than the chapel, because on the Strip a chapel sells weddings to people
driving by.
- THE FRONT: 9 m of his cracked plaster (Wall tiles 1 #0) to a 3.6 m plate; one of his round-topped windows (5. #15) each
  side; his plank doors (4. #7, one mirrored) under a transom of his glass to 2.3 m: the church's tower doors, the same
  pieces (the arched window the church set over them would hide behind the canopy, so it is not laid).
- THE CANOPY: the family's grey-tan deck from above, 3.2 by 1.6 m, on two of his posts (7. #2's upright), its shade on the
  wall as light.
- THE BELL COTE: on the ridge, a 1.6 m box of his stone panels (#5), his louvred window (5. #17) as the bell opening, the
  church's cross (cut from Wall tiles 1 #4) under it, its own measured cap.
- THE ROOF: the measured straight roof against one storey, his tan field, caps once, his dirt (the second pass).
- THE SIGN: at the left on one pole of his framed steel (#13), a 3.4 by 1.6 m cabinet 4.6 m up, its face blank.
- THE BOXES: two of his flower boxes (17. Benches and seating #2) by the doors.
- 12 m with the eaves and the sign; the man at the doors at 1:1.

nobody waters anything; the doors are shut. His tile as bought, laid where a chapel keeps its flowers.
