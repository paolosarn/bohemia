NEW VOICES BORN (ledgered to owner song, with the topology and why it is NOT
the thing it resembles -- the 7/8 failure was "different clothes, same body",
so each of these was chosen by first reading the 577 that already exist):

- atriumvox   -> THE VOICE THAT STILL ANNOUNCES FLOORS
                 FOF / GRAIN-PER-PERIOD FORMANT SYNTHESIS (the IRCAM CHANT
                 technique), computed into a finite buffer. ONE damped-sinusoid
                 grain per pitch period, each ringing at a FORMANT, not at the
                 note: the PITCH is the grain RATE, the VOWEL is the grain's own
                 frequency. What sings is a rate, not an oscillator.
                 NOT brokenrosary, which is a BEAD TRAIN of five separate plucks
                 at sd*0.16 -- a rhythmic echo, not grains fusing into a tone.
- signalrot   -> THE LAST BROADCAST CORRODES
                 RING MODULATION BY BAND-LIMITED NOISE. Bandpassed noise drives
                 the VCA gain on top of a DC floor, so the noise MULTIPLIES the
                 tone. The melody survives and corrodes at once.
                 NOT aeolianharp / windpsaltery, which FILTER noise through
                 resonators. Filtering shapes; multiplying eats.
- twintoll    -> THE BELLS DISAGREE
                 DUAL-CARRIER FM ON ONE MODULATOR AT AN IRRATIONAL RATIO (root
                 2), WITH OPPOSED INDEX ENVELOPES: one carrier starts clangorous
                 and purifies while the other starts pure and sours, crossing in
                 the middle, the two beating four cents apart throughout.
                 NOT emberharp, which is ONE carrier with a decaying index.
- fissionhymn -> THE NOTE THAT WOULD NOT STAY ONE
                 ADDITIVE WITH STAGGERED ONSETS AND DIVERGENT DETUNE. Partials
                 ARRIVE one at a time during the decay and drift further out of
                 tune the longer they hold. Starts single, ends a crowd.
                 NOT glassrequiem, which strikes every partial at once and gives
                 each a random decay.
