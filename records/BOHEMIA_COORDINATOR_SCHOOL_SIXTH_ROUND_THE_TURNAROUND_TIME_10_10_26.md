# BOHEMIA COORDINATOR SCHOOL, ROUND SIX OF TEN: THE TURNAROUND TIME (10/10/26)

## THE ANGLE
On the mountain, in the cockpit, in the cave and on the ice, the people who live set the hard stop BEFORE they start and keep it when every feeling says go on. The limit is set at the bottom, by a cold head, in writing, because the head at the top is oxygen-starved, invested and ninety-seven miles from what it came for. Our coordinator has the same disease. The three-week claim, the fourth version, the day-long hold, the 26-note sweep, the burst called "ten rounds": each is a party that passed its turnaround because nobody wrote down, at the start, the hour it would turn.

## WHAT THAT WORLD KNOWS
1. Everest, May 1996: Rob Hall set a 2:00 p.m. turnaround, everyone down at that hour wherever they stood. Hall broke his own rule for his client Doug Hansen, who had spent his savings to be there; Scott Fischer set no turnaround at all. Eight died on May 10 and 11. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Mount_Everest_disaster Why it bites: the rule-maker broke the rule for the one who wanted it most. Our fourth version is always built for a feature somebody asked for by name.
2. Ed Viesturs: "Getting to the top is optional. Getting down is mandatory." https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/174009-getting-to-the-top-is-optional-getting-down-is-mandatory Why it bites: the feature is the summit; the lane back on the backlog is the mandatory part.
3. Shackleton, 9 January 1909, 97 miles from the Pole, turned back because the food would not cover the return; to his wife, a live donkey beats a dead lion. https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/topics/south-pole-exploration-sir-ernest-shackleton Why it bites: he judged the walk back, not the distance left. "So close" measures the wrong leg.
4. Aviation: the Flight Safety Foundation found unstable approaches on 3.5 to 4 percent of flights, and 95 to 97 percent of crews land anyway, past a stabilized-approach gate at 1,000 ft. https://flightsafety.org/asw-article/stable-approach-criteria-and%20go-arounds Why it bites: a gate 97 percent of trained pros fly through is our "fourth version is failure" law. The FSF fix is a no-fault go-around (https://www.atsb.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-06/alar_bn6-1-goaroundprep.pdf).
5. Cave diving's rule of thirds, from Sheck Exley's accident analysis: a third in, a third out, a third reserve; on a 3,000 psi fill the dive is over at 2,000, and the FIRST team member to hit thirds turns the whole team. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_thirds_(diving) Why it bites: the weakest tank sets the turn, and one person's limit turns everyone.
6. Why the plan is made on the surface: BSAC says narcosis "can slow down your reactions and impair your decision-making ability," and a stressed diver breathes 50 litres a minute. https://www.bsac.com/safety/safe-diving-guide/gas/ Why it bites: the lane at version three is at depth and cannot set its own limit there.
7. Firefighting: OSHA 1910.134(g)(4) is two in, two out; NFPA 1981 raised the SCBA low-air alarm from 25 to 33 percent and added a flash at 50 percent; when the alarm sounds "you are supposed to be out of the building or on your way out." https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-01-14/html/2013-00371.htm and https://www.fireengineering.com/?p=548348 Why it bites: the half-tank flash is the turn, not the alarm. Version two is the half-tank; the fourth version is the alarm inside the building.
8. Mann Gulch, 1949: 13 smokejumpers died and the Ten Standard Firefighting Orders followed in 1957. South Canyon, 1994: 14 died, most of the ten orders compromised, crews hesitating to drop their tools. https://www.nwcg.gov/wfldp/toolbox/staff-ride/library/mann-gulch-fire and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Canyon_Fire Why it bites: forty-five years after the rules were written, trained crews still died carrying their tools. A rule without a reflex is a plaque.
9. Sunk cost and escalation: Arkes and Blumer 1985 found theatre patrons who paid full price ($15) attended more plays than those randomly given $2 or $7 off; Staw 1976 found that of 240 students, those PERSONALLY RESPONSIBLE for a bad first outcome poured the most into the same failing choice. https://cognition.aau.at/bg/BA/Arkes%20%26%20Blumer%201985.pdf and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escalation_of_commitment Why it bites: the lane that built version one is the worst judge of version three.
10. Trading and trials: Odean 1998, 10,000 accounts, investors realised 14.8 percent of gains but only 9.8 percent of losses; automatic selling in experiments greatly reduced the effect (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disposition_effect). O'Brien-Fleming boundaries fix the number, timing and thresholds of interim looks in the protocol before the first patient, team blinded (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O%27Brien%E2%80%93Fleming_boundary). The WHO surgical time-out, 8 hospitals, 3,733 patients before and 3,955 after, cut deaths from 1.5 to 0.8 percent (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2810011). Why it bites: a stop you must decide to honour in the moment is not a stop; it is set before the data because the data makes you want to keep going.

## WHAT THE COORDINATOR DOES NOT DO
- It does not let a claim exist without a turn round written on it. A row with no "turns at round N" is Fischer's team: no rule, everyone summits at four. Would this have stopped the three-week claim: yes, at week one.
- It does not let the lane that built version one decide whether version three gets built. The turn comes from the coordinator or any other lane, no fault, no discussion. Would this have stopped the fourth version: yes, at the second.
- It does not write a hold without an expiry in the hold note. A hold with no end is a lane left on the mountain. Would this have stopped the day-long hold: yes.
- It does not read past its own sweep limit. Twenty-six notes is reading at depth. Would this have stopped the 26-note sweep: yes, at note five.
- It does not count a burst as rounds. Ten agents in parallel is one dive with ten divers: one round.

## THE ONE CHANGE
THE TURNAROUND TABLE. One table in the coordinator's handoff block, written BEFORE the round's work starts and read first every round. The turn fires when the limit is hit, not when someone feels like calling it; any lane may call it on any other lane, no fault, and the called lane complies the way a pilot complies with a go-around call.

| THING | LIMIT | WHO SET IT | WRITTEN WHERE, WHEN | THE WORDS THAT TURN IT |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A claim (a row a lane holds) | 3 rounds from claim to shipped-or-back | The coordinator, this hour | On the row the round it is claimed: "CLAIMED R12, TURNS R15" | "TURN. Ship what is in the tab or hand the row back." The row returns unclaimed with a two-line post-mortem. |
| An attempt (versions of one thing) | 2 versions; the law's fourth is the alarm, the second is the half-tank | The coordinator, this hour | On the VOTE item at version one: "V2 IS THE LAST" | "TWO WAS THE LAST. Graveyard it or ask him one question." No third is drawn. If the director named the feature, he sees both versions and one bold question: the summit is optional, the handoff is mandatory. |
| A hold (a lane frozen) | 1 round, then it lifts by itself | The coordinator, in the hold note | In the hold note as it is written: "LIFTS R13 UNLESS RE-WRITTEN WITH A REASON" | "THE HOLD EXPIRED. RUN." A hold with no expiry line is void on sight. |
| A sweep (the coordinator reading before it writes) | 10 files or 20 minutes, whichever first; at most 5 notes | The coordinator, before the first file opens | First line of the sweep: "SWEEP: 10 FILES, 5 NOTES, THEN WRITE" | "TEN. WRITE." |
| A research round | One agent, one file, 8 to 12 cited findings; a burst of N agents is ONE round | Paolo, when he ordered ten rounds | In the file name and first heading: "ROUND SIX OF TEN" | "ONE FILE, ONE ROUND. A burst is one round." |
| His attention | 1 bold question per reply, 3 things to look at, each in a named tab | Paolo (one question max, 8/4) | In the reply contract, and now in this table | "ONE QUESTION. The rest I decide." Past three items the coordinator stops asking and builds, correct-after. |

Under the table: the limit is set where the head is cold, by someone not climbing. Nobody raises their own limit mid-dive. A wrong limit is changed at the start of the next round, in the table, with a date, never in the moment it bites.

## ONE RULE
THE TURN IS WRITTEN BEFORE THE CLIMB, AND THE FIRST TANK TO HIT THIRDS TURNS EVERYONE.
Every claim, attempt, hold, sweep and round gets its stop hour written on it the moment it starts, and when any lane hits it the whole party turns with no argument, because the feeling that says "we are so close" is the exact feeling the rule exists to overrule.

## FOR PAOLO, IN PLAIN WORDS
Climbers who live set the hour they will turn back before they leave camp, and they keep it even with the top in sight, because eight people died on Everest in 1996 when the guide broke his own two o'clock rule. We do the same thing to your game: a chat keeps a job for three weeks, or builds a thing four times after you said it was bad, because nobody wrote the stop on it at the start. From this round every job, every attempt and every freeze gets its stop written on it the hour it starts, and any chat can call the turn on any other one.
