## THE ANSWER IN ONE BREATH
The people alive in the valley lived through the famine years on the stew, and most of them still eat it. Nobody asks what is in the
pot and the cook does not tell. It is creepy, not shameful: they say the dead fed them. The line everyone still feels is HOW the meat
was got: the dead are food, a man killed for it is murder. And the survivors keep one rule from the people who learned it the hard
way: never the head.

## 1. THE REAL FLOOR
- LENINGRAD, 1941 TO 1944. About 800,000 civilians died in the siege, most of hunger (the most cited estimate; the range runs from
  about 630,000 to over a million). By December 1942 the Soviet secret police (NKVD) had arrested 2,105 people for cannibalism and
  sorted them into TWO legal kinds: corpse-eating and person-eating. Eating the dead was punished; killing for food was treated as
  plain murder. Of 300 arrested in April 1942, only 44 had killed anyone. The NKVD's own profile of those arrested is the most
  important number on this page: about two thirds were women, and only 2 percent had any criminal record. They were ordinary people.
  (The figures come from NKVD files published long after the war, through Anna Reid's Leningrad and the historians she drew on; other
  counts of arrests and convictions differ, so the shape is the fact, not the last digit.)
  WHY IT MATTERS HERE: the state itself drew Paolo's line in law. The dead are food; the killed for it is murder.
- UKRAINE, 1932 TO 1933. The famine killed millions (estimates run about 3.5 to 5 million). At least 2,505 people were sentenced for
  cannibalism in those two years, and the real number of cases was certainly higher. (Research only, never in the game: the regime
  printed posters telling starving villages that eating your own children was a barbarian act.)
- THE ANDES, 1972. Uruguayan Air Force Flight 571 came down in the mountains; 16 of the 45 aboard lived 72 days by eating the dead,
  who were their friends and teammates. All were Catholic and some feared damnation; a priest told them they had not sinned because
  they had no choice, and the Church cleared them (accounts differ on who said it). Some of them compared it to communion. That is
  the voice we want: "they fed us."
- KURU, THE SHAKES. Among the Fore people of Papua New Guinea's Eastern Highlands, families honoured their dead by eating them at the
  funeral, and the brain, the most infectious part, went mostly to women and children. It carried a prion, the family of mad cow
  disease. "Kuru" means to shake. Over 2,700 cases after 1957; on average about 12 years from the meal to the first tremble, and in a
  few cases more than 50 (Collinge and others, The Lancet, 2006). It ends in trembling, falling, fits of laughing, and death.
  Cooking does not kill a prion. Two facts the game can use: the Fore themselves believed kuru was SORCERY, a curse put on the sick;
  and some people carry a gene that slows it or stops it.
- MAD COW, THE WIDER RULE. When Britain fought mad cow disease it banned the brain and the spinal cord of cattle from food. The true
  rule of people who know prions is "never the head, never the backbone".
- THE DONNER PARTY, 1846 TO 1847. Trapped in the Sierra snow, the families ate hides, glue and bark first; where they ate the dead,
  they tried their best never to eat their own family (historian Michael Wallis).

## 2. OURS: WHAT IS LOCKED AND WHAT IS DRAFT
LOCKED (his words, rule 388 and both amendments):
- Common and never said. The Strip's stew is the Mob's clone meat and whatever came in; when a batch dies with the power, the pot
  still has meat in it. Everybody eats it; nobody asks.
- More accepted than taboo: most of the world ate it in the famine years and most still do. Creepy, not shameful. "They fed us."
- The line is HOW the meat was got. The dead are food; a man killed for it is murder. The factions differ: the Church may bless the
  dead's gift, the Mob sells it, some will not touch it.
- It is how people survived: no farming, everyone dying.
- The shakes (the coordinator's hole, draft:true, his to keep): never the head; whoever broke it shakes years later, and in act two and
  act three the shaking shows in whoever ate wrong.
DRAFT (this page, draft:true, his to change):
- THE FAMINE YEARS. The first two or three winters after the dollar died (about 2050 to 2052): the vats spoiled in a week (the cheap
  clone law, laws/BOHEMIA_LAW_THE_CHEAP_CLONE_11_3_26.md section 3), the cattle were eaten in the first winter, and two to three
  billion died worldwide in the first decade. That is when the stew began. Ten years on, in act one, the valley's 65,000 eat mostly the
  Mob's clones, the beasts that walk to them and what little the gardens grow; the human part of the pot is smaller now, and it comes
  from the dead nobody claims: the road's dead and the fights' dead. So THE MORE HE FIGHTS, THE FULLER THE POT (rule 389's loop: the
  dead feed the beasts, the beasts feed the people, the people feed the stew).
- A SEAM IN CANON, CLOSED WITHOUT A NEW RULE. The 7/18 STORY MASTER says food is the weak leg, answered by hydroponics; the 11/4 record
  says there is no farming in act one. Newest wins, and they agree under the lit-12-percent canon: hydroponics needs power, power is
  owned, so the gardens feed the people who own the power. Everyone else eats the stew.
- THE STEW'S FIVE RULES (the survivors' code; nobody writes it down; every one is on a real floor):
    1. Never the head. The careful ones say never the back either. (kuru; the mad cow rules)
    2. Never your own. (the Donner families)
    3. Never the killed. Found is food; taken is murder. (Leningrad's two kinds)
    4. You don't ask, and the cook doesn't tell. (his words)
    5. The head goes back to the ground. (draft:true, the strongest image here and his to cut: the cook carries a covered bucket to
       the chapel's yard at dusk; the Church buries what the pot cannot take, in short rows nobody visits.)
- THE FACTIONS (rule 374: only the factions we have):
  * THE MOB sells it (locked). Its pot on the Strip, its clone meat, its buyer for the dead. Its honour code is what keeps rule 3:
    the Mob makes an example of a man who took instead of found, in public, because a pot people trust is worth more than one man.
  * THE CHURCH may bless the dead's gift (locked as 'may'). It keeps the funerals and the only register of who is alive (its canon
    dossier), so it knows whose dead went to the pot and whose went to the ground. A family's word decides. The warm half: the blessing
    over the pot. The dark half: the register remembers who said yes.
  * THE CARTEL is where the killed-for-it rumours point (its canon: organised human predation, its main product other people). A rumour,
    never shown as fact in act one.
  * THE VOLUNTEERS (the clinics) are the ones who will work out the shakes (section 3).
  * SOME WILL NOT TOUCH IT: not a faction, a family here and a man there (seed c).
- THE PLAYER'S OWN CAMP (draft default, realism first, his own words: 'if I have to feed my family... I'm cooking that'): when the
  camp's food runs out after a fight where one of his men died, the choice exists, rare and never in the first hours: bury him, give
  him to the pot, or sell him to the yard. The crew eats and heals; one man may refuse and may leave. It is a quest moment, never a
  system with a price tag (rule 360(b): the dead are scenery, no new system).

## 3. THE SHAKES ACROSS THE THREE ACTS (the math the rule did not do, draft:true)
- Kuru's average is about 12 years from the meal. The famine years were about 2050 to 2052 and act one is about 2060 to 2061. SO THE
  FIRST SHAKERS APPEAR IN ACT ONE, not only in the later acts: a few people who ate the head in the first winter begin to tremble
  in the year he plays. Nobody in the valley knows why yet.
- AND IN ACT ONE IT LOOKS LIKE A HAUNTING. The Fore called kuru sorcery; our valley will call it a curse, a demon, the thing in the
  wash. That fits the 7/24 lock exactly (laws/BOHEMIA_ADDENDUM_ACT1_AMALGAMATION_IS_A_GHOST_7_24_26.md, THE REVEAL ENGINE: every act one
  haunting has a real mechanism under it). The shakes are the one haunting whose mechanism the player CAN learn in act one, from a
  clinic: it was something they ate. It teaches the whole game's method, a scare with a real cause, without touching the secret.
  GUARD: the shakers are never the possessed. A shaker trembles, falls and laughs; he never knows things he should not know and
  never speaks in another person's words. Those are the possessed's marks and they stay theirs.
- ACT TWO (35 years on, the future computed from the past): most who ate the head in the famine years are dead or shaking; the long
  ones are still turning up. If the player gave his own dead to the pot without the rule, a man of his can shake in act two.
- ACT THREE (70 years on): almost gone; a few old ones with the long gene. A story the old tell.
- NOT IN THE BLOOD. A prion from a meal is not passed to children. An heir never inherits the shakes, only the story of them (for
  DYNASTY's heirs and PORTRAIT's faces: the mark is on the man who ate, never on his line).

## 5. QUEST SEEDS (draft:true; people, places and things, never a card; every line from a mouth with a face)
a. DON'T OPEN IT (what is in the stew). The stew counter's cook needs a crate fetched from the Mob's yard before the dinner rush. The
   crate is heavy and it is not sealed. Choices: carry it shut, look, or ask the yard what is in it. Line, draft:true:
   "Don't open it. Not because of what's in it. Because then you'll know, and you eat here."
b. HER POT WAS FULL IN MARCH (who goes missing near the vendor). People have gone missing near a widow's stew stall on Boulder
   Highway; the Church's register has three names with no funeral. The question is only rule 3: found or taken. Choices: hand her to
   the Mob's rule, bring it to the Church, or say nothing. Line, draft:true:
   "Her pot was full in March. Nobody's pot is full in March."
c. THE FAMILY THAT SAID GRACE ONCE (the ones who will not touch it). A family will not eat the stew; the kids are thin. Choices: bring
   them a beast (a carcass, rule 392), talk them into the pot, or leave them to it. Line, draft:true:
   "We said grace over it once. That was enough for us."
d. ANYBODY CLAIMING THESE (a buyer for the dead). After a fight a man from the Mob's yard arrives with a cart and asks about the
   bodies. Choices: give them to the Church, sell them for batteries, or leave them to the birds (the human side of rule 392, as a
   quest only). Line, draft:true:
   "Anybody claiming these? No? Then they're nobody's, and nobody's is mine."
e. WHO ELSE WAS AT THE TABLE (the shakes). A clinic nurse has three men who tremble and laugh; the tunnels say a curse, the chapel
   says a demon. She says it was a meal in the first winter and wants to find everyone who ate from the same pot before the fear
   does something worse. Line, draft:true:
   "It's not a ghost. It's something he ate the first winter. I need to know who else was at that table."
f. HE'D HAVE SAID YES (his own camp, the draft default in section 2). The camp is out of food the night after one of his men fell.
   The company's cook asks him, not the men. Line, draft:true:
   "He'd have said yes. He said yes to everything."
