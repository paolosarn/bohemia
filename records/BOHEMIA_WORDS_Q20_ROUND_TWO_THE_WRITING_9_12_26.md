## THE TEST GOT A RULE, AND SCHOOL SUPPLIED IT
The row asks for lines that would sound WRONG in act two, which as written is a
matter of taste. School turned it into a check anybody can run:

**A LINE IS ACT-ONE IF ACT TWO COULD ANSWER IT WITH AN INSTITUTION.**

Every one of the fifteen carries its own act-two kill in the bank, on the line
underneath it, naming the institution that deletes it. That is the deliverable the
row actually wanted, and it came out of finding 2 rather than out of preference.

## WHICH FINDINGS CHANGED THE LINES, AND HOW
Six, and the first one decides the register of all fifteen.

**1. ACT ONE IS THE MOST FORMAL ERA, SO NOT ONE LINE IS CLIPPED OR FERAL.** School's
keystone is the corridor measurement: honour-culture subjects yielded to an
oncoming man at 2.7 m by default and at 0.94 m once insulted, against a flat 1.4 m
for law-culture subjects either way. Courtesy is the resting state and the switch
sits behind it. **So lines 1 to 3 are one man across that whole range**: elaborate
stair courtesy, then a warning that names politeness as a thing being spent down,
then the switch, which goes QUIETER and CLOSER instead of louder. Before school I
would have written act one clipped, because that is what the genre advice produces
and what "instinct before rules" sounds like.

**2. THE RECOURSE WORDS ARE GONE, SO THE CONSEQUENCE SITS IN THE SENTENCE.** Lines
4 and 5 do nothing else. "I am not going to go and get anybody. There is nobody to
go and get. There is me." A man saying out loud that he is the mechanism is a
sentence act two cannot use, because act two has bailiffs. **Measured: zero
recourse words across the fifteen**, which is the one ruler that had to stay at
zero.

**3. THE NAME AND THE HOUSE ARE THE PASSWORD, AND OUR BUILD NEVER ASKS.** School
found ZERO lines in 2,442 containing "who's asking", "where are you from" or "whose
block". Lebanon's checkpoints are the anchor: a name and a home town reported as
the words that made armed men stand down. **Lines 6, 7 and 8 are the whole
encounter**: the ask ("Name, and whose you are. In that order"), the answer, which
is three people who will vouch rather than a document, and the refusal, which
treats a name as cargo you can decline to hand over. This is the biggest hole
school found and it got the most lines.

**4. A RULE LIVES INSIDE A FIXED PHRASE.** Xeer is memorised in poetry and
proverbs; the Kanun was carried verbally for centuries. With no paper, a repeatable
sentence is the only storage a rule has. **Lines 9, 10 and 11 are that mechanism
shown three ways**: a saying quoted AS the law, the same saying completed word for
word by a second person, which is what proves it is shared, and a child being
drilled on the exact wording because oral storage has to be taught. Line 11 says
why out loud: "your own words will be gone by winter and the saying will not."

**5. THE WORST THINGS ARE SAID SIDEWAYS, AND WE WERE ALREADY GOOD AT IT.** School
measured this as the corpus's strongest suit, so lines 12 and 13 extend our
existing register rather than inventing one. 13 adds the reason school gave: face
has to be protected because escalation cannot be undone, so the number gets said at
the gate and not in front of the man's crew.

**6. A PROMISE IS A BESA, AND IT OUTLIVES THE MAN.** In the Kanun the besa is the
highest authority and the code has no enforcement at all without it; breaking it
stains the family name rather than drawing a penalty; and it is generational, so
your father's pledge becomes yours. **Line 14 is one being given** (sworn on the
house, in front of everybody, because the witnesses are the enforcement) **and line
15 is one being collected from a son who never made it.** Line 15 is the only one
of the fifteen that survives into act two, and that is the point of it.

Every ruler moved the way school said it should. **But the deference row is the
honest problem, and it matters more than the five good rows.** One line in fifteen
carries a deference marker, and the section's headline finding is that courtesy is
armour. Reading it rather than taking the rate: lines 2, 3 and 13 are all doing
courtesy work and none of them trips the ruler, because they do it through
behaviour rather than vocabulary.

**THE RULER IS PARTLY A LAW-CULTURE RULER AND THAT IS A REAL LIMIT ON SCHOOL'S OWN
DIAGNOSIS.** "Sir", "ma'am", "excuse me" and "beg your pardon" are institutional
forms. Film's 2.00 per 1,000 is substantially made of them, and our valley has no
institution to have grown them. An honour culture carries the same load through
other means: yielding, kin address, face protection and indirectness. So the -6.2 sd
number school reported is real but part of it measures a vocabulary our world would
never have had. The fix shows up in the ADDRESS row (13x) rather than the deference
row, and I am recording that rather than quietly reporting a rate that looks better
than the reading does.
