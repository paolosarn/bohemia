PART ONE: THE HAT BUG WAS FOUR TIMES BIGGER THAN "SEVENTEEN GARMENTS"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Yesterday's fix restored 17 canon garments that rendered nothing. Counting them as
menu items undersells it, because THE CITY DRESSES ITSELF OUT OF THE SAME
CATALOGUE.

MEASURED, 5,000 people generated exactly the way the game generates them
(BOH_PERSONLOOK.lookFor with the canon pool, the same call bohemia's crowd makes):

    29.6%   of everybody in the city is wearing headwear
    88.2%   of the hats they were wearing DREW NOTHING
    28.4%   of EVERY PERSON IN THE CITY put something on their head and stayed bare

Only TWO of the seventeen head garments in the whole game worked -- the shemagh and
the scrap helm. A crowd that should read as a working town read as a town where
almost nobody owns a hat.

That number is the honest measure of a bug that was, in code, one literal `56`.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART TWO: THE IDENTITY AUDIT ON CLOTHES, AND EVERY ROUND OF IT WAS ME MEASURING
THE CAMERA INSTEAD OF THE CLOTHES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Clause 1 of the 8/25 law ported from hair to the 204 canon garments. First run:

    52 GARMENTS CHANGE BY A TENTH OF THE BODY OR MORE IN ONE NOTCH OF TURN

Led by ROAD CAPE at 0.94 on sleeve coverage. Every single one was geometry.

  SLEEVE ON A BACKPACK. A pack hangs BEHIND the arms: from behind it is between you
    and them and paints them, side-on the near arm is in front of it. Asked only of
    layers that HAVE sleeves now -- and never off the profile, because side-on the
    near arm sits in front of the torso and a SLEEVELESS apron scored 0.68. There is
    no way to tell a sleeve from an occlusion in a flat grid seen edge-on.

  REACH, AND THIS WAS THE HARDEST TO ADMIT BECAUSE I WROTE IT AS AN IDENTITY
    MEASURE. How far a garment stands off the body SIDEWAYS is not a property of the
    garment at all. A cap brim projects FORWARD: head-on it points at the camera and
    adds nothing to the silhouette, side-on the whole of it lies across the frame. A
    gas mask's filter snout does the same. THAT IS FORESHORTENING -- the entire
    reason a three-quarter view reads as depth. Printed by the tool now, never
    judged.

  OCCLUSION, AND THIS ONE COST ME A CHANGE I HAD TO THROW AWAY. After the two fixes
    above the loudest survivor was SMITH'S APRON: hem 0.188 body-heights facing
    SOUTH, EXACTLY 0.000 from behind. I read that as an apron that stops existing
    when he turns round -- the same shape as the hair bug -- and wrote the fix: draw
    the skirt's edges wherever the body does not cover them, measured per row off
    the part grid so it could never paint over his legs.
        IT PAINTED ZERO PIXELS.
    The apron panel is 17 px wide and his hips are 22. IT IS GENUINELY HIDDEN FROM
    BEHIND and the code was right the whole time. Reverted rather than shipped,
    because a change that draws nothing is exactly the class of lie I have spent the
    week catching. A garment can be OCCLUDED rather than ABSENT, and a hem read off
    the pixels you can SEE cannot tell those apart. A facing showing under a quarter
    of a garment's biggest view is now judged as occluded and skipped.

WHAT SURVIVES IS VERTICAL: hem, rise, and sleeve off the profile. A hem does not
foreshorten when he turns, which is precisely why hem was the measure that caught
the hair bug that started all of this.
