## 0. THE QUESTION
Thirteen days have taken things from the game he named. **Day 14 asks
what it gets WRONG**, because there is one thing it is famously bad at and
it is the exact thing our demo lives or dies on: **the first five
minutes.**

## 1. THE GAME HE NAMED IS A BAD TEACHER, AND ITS OWN PLAYERS SAY SO
- It has *"a rather steep learning curve, and many players end up getting
  lost or stuck halfway through."*
- The standard community advice to a new player is, in order: **play on
  Beginner. Do not play ironman. Save often. Expect your men to die, and
  die a lot.** *"The most difficult part of the game is the start."*
  *"The first 10 days are the hardest."*
- The intended shape is that your first campaign is a lesson you lose.
**AND THAT IS A COMPLETELY REASONABLE DESIGN — FOR THE GAME IT IS.** A
deep strategy game somebody deliberately bought, on a desktop, expecting
to invest. The difficulty IS the product.
**IT IS FATAL FOR US, AND THE REASON IS ARITHMETIC, NOT TASTE.**

## 2. THE OTHER AISLE — WHAT A FIRST SESSION ACTUALLY COSTS
- **Median day-one retention is about 22-26%.** Roughly **three of every
  four** people who start decide against you inside 24 hours, **and most
  of that decision happens in the FIRST SESSION.**
- **Strategy is the worst-hit genre on this axis** — around 25% day one —
  *"because their depth takes longer to hook new players."* That is us,
  precisely.
- Games with strong first-time-user completion see **around twice** the
  day-one retention of games where people drop out during the tutorial.
- And the operational rule the practitioners state flatly: ***"Get to
  playable inside 60 seconds. No account creation, no settings, no
  extended tutorial before the first gameplay moment."***
**HE IS GOING TO SEND THIS LINK TO FRIENDS.** Nobody who taps it bought
anything, nobody is invested, and nobody will read. "Your first run is a
lesson you lose" spends a budget we do not have.

## 3. THE SHELF — AND WE ALREADY RAN THE RIGHT TEST, ONCE
### (a) THE GOOD NEWS FIRST, AND IT IS BETTER THAN I EXPECTED
- **THE COLD OPEN IS ALREADY DESIGNED AS A TEACHER, AND NOT A TUTORIAL
  BOX.** His own 8/8 order: *"build a tutorial-tier family-defense
  encounter for the cold open"*, and the code carries it: `hostiles: 2,
  /* tutorial tier [DIAL] */`, with the comment **"IT TEACHES BY
  STRUCTURE, NOT BY A TUTORIAL BOX: the threat walks toward you."**
  That is the correct answer and it is already the design.
- **TIME TO FIRST PLAY IS 24.2 SECONDS on throttled 4G**, measured by the
  RUN lane. Against the practitioners' 60-second rule that is INSIDE
  budget. **THE LOADING IS NOT OUR PROBLEM.** (It is still on the board
  as a performance row, and it should be — but it is not the thing that
  loses the friend.)
### (b) *** AND SOMEBODY ALREADY BUILT THE ONE TEST THAT MATTERS, RAN IT
### ONCE, AND FOUND THE PLAYER STUCK ***
From the walked surface, written into the CSS of all places, on 8/25:
> *"MEASURED with a cold hand that presses the loudest control on screen
> and never reads: from a cold boot it went WATCH, GET UP, then DROP IN /
> CITY / DROP IN / CITY ten times and stopped. **Phone opened 0. Job taken
> 0. Clock 06:00 at the first tap and 06:00 at the twelfth.**"*
**A SIMULATED STRANGER PRESSED THE BIGGEST BUTTON TWELVE TIMES AND THE
GAME'S CLOCK NEVER MOVED.** The cause was found and fixed properly — the
phone had rung, but as *"a dark chip with a hairline and a 14px dot, and
the dot is a sign on a door (Norman: when you have to put a sign on a
door, the design already failed)"*, so the ring now wears the same fill as
the WATCH button the player has already been taught to press.
**THE FIX IS NOT THE FINDING. THE TEST IS.**

## 4. *** THE FINDING THAT CHALLENGES WHAT WE BELIEVE ***
**I HAVE CLOSED THIRTEEN DAYS BY SAYING NOTHING IN THIS STUDY IS
DEMO-CRITICAL. THAT WAS TRUE OF EVERY FEATURE AND IT MADE ME MISS A
METHOD.**
The COLD HAND is the most valuable thing this study has found that
already exists, and **it has been run exactly once, against exactly one
control.** It is not a gate, it is not repeatable, nothing runs it on the
cold open, the first morning, the job offer, the fight, or the reckoning.
One run of it found a total dead end in the first minute of the game.
**Nobody has asked what a second run would find.**
And the second half of the finding is a REFUSAL, which after thirteen days
of taking is worth stating loudly:
**WE DO NOT COPY BATTLE BROTHERS' FIRST HOUR.** Not the "expect to lose
your men" opening, not "the first ten days are the hardest", not a
difficulty that is itself the tutorial. Every other day of this study
took something. **This is the day the study says no**, and it says no to
the single most characteristic thing about the game he named.
**AND HIS OWN RULING ALREADY COVERS IT:** 8/25, *"WE NEED TO MAKE THIS
GAME FUCKING FUN"*, and FUN CARRIES THE TIE. A brutal opening is the most
realistic thing we could build and it is the wrong trade, by the person
who is allowed to make that trade.
### THE TEST THAT FALLS OUT, AND IT IS ONE SENTENCE
**A COLD HAND PRESSES THE LOUDEST THING ON SCREEN AND NEVER READS.** If
doing that repeatedly does not advance the game, the screen is broken,
however good the systems behind it are.

## 5. AND A WARNING THIS STUDY OWES ITSELF
Three rows already routed by this study make the first fight HARDER, and
none of them was written with a stranger in mind:
- **BB-NERVE-ON** (day 12) turns on morale — which makes fights SHORTER,
  so it helps. Keep.
- **BB-GUNS-CLOSE** (day 3) makes guns bad up close, which pushes bodies
  toward you. In the cold open, with two hostiles walking at you, that is
  fine and probably good. **But it must be measured there, not assumed.**
- **BB-THE-ROUT** (day 12) adds a decision after the fight. In the FIRST
  fight that is one more thing to understand at the worst moment.
**THE RULE: NOTHING FROM THIS STUDY LANDS IN THE COLD OPEN WITHOUT A COLD
HAND RUN.** The cold open is his 8/8 design and it is already correct;
this study's job is not to complicate it.

## 6. WHAT WE TAKE, AND WHAT WE REFUSE
**TAKE:** teaching by structure rather than by a box, which is already
ours; the 60-second rule, which we already meet; and the cold-hand test,
promoted from a one-off measurement to standing practice.
**REFUSE:** a difficulty curve as onboarding; "your first campaign is a
write-off"; any settings screen, options wall or tutorial sequence before
the first real moment; and any new system landing in the first five
minutes without a cold-hand run.

## 7. ROUTED
- **SHARED / RUN — BB-COLD-HAND.** *** THE FIRST DEMO-CRITICAL ROW THIS
  STUDY HAS PRODUCED. *** Promote the cold hand from a one-off to a
  repeatable harness: press the loudest control on screen, never read,
  and assert THE GAME ADVANCES — clock moves, a job gets taken, the day
  ends. Run it over the cold open, the first morning, the phone, the
  first fight and the reckoning. It found a complete dead end the one time
  it ran. Nobody has run it since.
- **RUN — BB-NOT-LOSE-TO-LEARN.** Written as a refusal so no lane
  quietly imports it: the first hour does not teach by killing you.
  Median day-one retention is ~22-26% and strategy is the worst-hit
  genre; a friend with a link is not a player who bought a hard game.
  The cold open's "teaches by structure, not by a tutorial box" is the
  standing answer and it stays.
- **UI — BB-SIXTY-SECONDS.** Hold the line we already have: 24.2 seconds
  to first play, and nothing added before the first real moment. This is
  a guard, not a build: the row exists so that the next thing somebody
  wants to put in front of the game has to argue against a number.
**RUNNING ORDER: BB-COLD-HAND GOES ON THE DEMO PATH.** Everything else in
this study still queues behind the demo; this one is part of it.
