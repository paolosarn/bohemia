THEY ARE TOLD APART BY TIMING, NOT BY SHAPE
-------------------------------------------
All three put both hands in the same small patch in front of the body. They have to --
it is the same wound. So WHERE the hands are cannot be the channel, and that is not a
guess: a wide-brim hat is worth 1.9% of a body's pixels at this rig size (measured the
same day, records/BOHEMIA_THIRTEEN_OUTFITS_AND_WHAT_HEADWEAR_IS_WORTH_8_18_26.txt).
Small geometry does not read. Motion does.

    pour     6 of 12 keyframes with the hand STOPPED, 0 reversals, peak 3.2px
             -- the stillness is the tell, and nothing else in the set stops
    inject   peak 6.0px in ONE keyframe, the fastest single move of the three
             -- slow approach, jab, press, slow withdraw
    tweeze   7 direction reversals, only 3 still keys
             -- the only thing in the game that trembles

FOUR DEFECTS, EVERY ONE OF WHICH READ CORRECTLY AND MEASURED WRONG
------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE WOUND WAS OUT OF ARM REACH. The first cut treated a wound mid-thigh, which is
   where you would treat a gunshot. Measured on the real rig: the thigh sits 19.1px
   from the shoulder and the whole arm is 16px. A STANDING PERSON CANNOT REACH THEIR
   OWN MID-THIGH -- which is true of people too -- so solveIK clamped and the hand
   hovered at hip height doing nothing recognisable, for three clips at once.
   THE SITE IS THE FOREARM NOW, and that is the better answer for a second measured
   reason: the long coats that dominate this wardrobe SWALLOW THE THIGH. The forearm
   is held out in front, outside the coat's silhouette. A wound you cannot see being
   treated is not an animation.

2. THE JAB WAS SHORTER THAN A KEYFRAME. POSEHOLD.keys is 12, so 1/12 of a bar is the
   shortest move this engine can express. The jab spanned 0.06 of a bar -- less than
   one key -- and it did not exist: the spike fell between keyframes and never
   rendered. Measured, the "fastest move in the clip set" was the SLOWEST. It now
   spans exactly one key, 5/12 to 6/12, on the boundaries.

3. THE TREMOR WAS SAMPLED AT ITS OWN ZERO CROSSINGS. sin(2*pi*6*t) evaluated at
   t = i/12 is sin(pi*i), which is ZERO at every single keyframe. The clip whose whole
   identity is that it shakes measured 9 still keys out of 12. cos alternates +1/-1
   key by key instead, which is the tremor the grid can actually carry.

4. TWO OF THE THREE NEVER RETURNED HOME. A cyclic clip whose pose at phase 1 differs
   from its pose at phase 0 SNAPS once per bar. Pour ended 5px from where it started
   and inject 7px, so the loop seam was the biggest hand move in both -- which is also
   how the first version of the gate got fooled into calling it "the fastest move".

*** THE GENERAL LESSON, AND IT IS FOR ANY LANE THAT AUTHORS A CLIP ***
----------------------------------------------------------------------
THE POSE GRID IS 12 KEYS PER BAR. Design to it, not against it:
  - the shortest expressible move is 1/12 of a bar; anything briefer may not exist
  - land ramps ON key boundaries (multiples of 1/12) or they finish between frames
  - an oscillation must be a frequency the grid can sample -- 6/bar with a cosine
    alternates cleanly; 6/bar with a sine is invisible; 7/bar is noise
  - a looping clip must return to its start pose, or it snaps once a bar
