## 0. THE ANSWER IN ONE LINE

**A Battle Brothers event is a narrator's paragraph with buttons under it, and
Battle Brothers' own players say they stop reading the paragraph.** What survives
is the choices and the consequence. So the situation is the part we must not
write at all: it is the part we SHOW.

## 1. HOW ONE IS WRITTEN, MEASURED

One event situation, verbatim, the only full sample this round could source:

> *"You pry the weapon out of the man's vice-like grip, when from out of the
> crowd appear a band of weathered men. They are adorned with rusted armor
> bearing the sigil of a House which has faded into history. The apparant leader
> of the group slowly unsheaths his weapon."*

```
characters                          266
sentences                             3
words                                49
person / tense     second person, present
who is speaking                A NARRATOR
at our own 14 cps                  19.0 seconds of reading
our bubble hold                     7.0 seconds (98 characters, barkHold's clamp)
SO ONE BB SITUATION IS              2.7x OUR WHOLE CEILING
```

The system around it: roughly **400 events** now, up from the **47** of the first
pass that BB STUDY DAY 13 measured. They key off where you are, what you carry,
and **who is in your party** -- a Thief steals the merchant's map, a Historian
deciphers it. Day 13 already took that lesson and it is not re-taken here: *they
did not solve a thin event bench by writing more events, they gave events more
things to key off.*

## 2. THE FINDING THAT PROVES THE REFERENCE WRONG

**BATTLE BROTHERS' OWN PLAYERS SKIM THE EVENTS, AND THEY SAY SO IN WORDS THAT
DESCRIBE PAOLO EXACTLY.**

> *"after my third run, I realized I wasn't reading the prompts anymore, I was
> skimming through them, looking only at the highlighted text of my character's
> answers and the last paragraph."*

> *"there's so much text that i skip almost all of it."*

> *"just read some wall of text every now and then without any real impact to
> anything."*

And **the developers agree.** About the ~17 read-only events (mostly "a traveller
asks to camp with you"), Casey: *"Events that require player input and carry
rewards/pitfalls are obviously better, but at the time I just wanted something
'on the page' if that makes any sense."*

**THE FIRST QUOTE IS THE WHOLE ROUND, BECAUSE IT NAMES WHAT SURVIVES SKIMMING:
THE CHOICES AND THE LAST PARAGRAPH.** Not the situation. The situation is the
part that dies, in the reference itself, reported by the people who love it.

That is not an opinion about prose, it is the reading research. Skim readers
**satisfice**: they read until the rate of information gain drops below a
threshold and then jump. A situation paragraph has the lowest information gain on
the screen, because the choices below it already imply the situation. So it is
the first thing a reader drops, every time, by design of the human and not of the
writer.

**AND PAOLO IS THAT READER, ON THE RECORD, SEVEN TIMES.** His verdicts on this
lane: *"Boring asf"*, *"Boring asf"*, *"Idk what i was reading wtf"*, *"What
makes you think writing a storytime about this would be a good idea rather than
actually making pixel art of footsteps"*. Seven of this lane's nine items are
down and every one of them was a thing to read. He has been telling us the Steam
quote in his own voice.

## 3. MEASURED AGAINST OUR REPO, AND WE ALREADY WROTE TWELVE OF THEM

`ROAD_WORDS` holds twelve road moments. Before a word was changed:

```

contain a spoken line at all       3 of 12

```

**WE WROTE BATTLE BROTHERS EVENTS WITHOUT MEANING TO.** Second person, present
tense, a narrator, at half BB's length. *"You've got about two seconds to pick
where your foot goes."* That is a camera, not a mouth, and rule 19 has banned it
since 9/20.

**AND THE GAME ALREADY REFUSES TO SHOW THEM, WHICH IS THE BEST NEWS IN THIS
RECORD.** `roadCard` is defined and has **zero callers**. Another lane killed the
card under rule 19e and deliberately kept the table, writing in the file: *"they
are content waiting for bodies to say them."* So the twelve are parked, not
shipped. Nobody has to un-ship anything; the words simply have to change shape
before they come back.

**A CORRECTION TO MY OWN FIRST NUMBER, MADE IN THE SAME ROUND.** A crude first
count said 9 of 12 narrate him, by matching the word "you". Printing the five
lines that actually use second person outside quotes shows two of them have him
as the OBJECT of somebody else's verb -- *"a coyote picks you up at the corner"*,
*"he sees you and he doesn't slow down"* -- which is a thing a person really says.
The honest number is **3**, and the ruler is grammatical, not a word list: **a
narrator makes the player the SUBJECT of a verb, because only a narrator is
inside your body.** That is the tenth entry in this lane's ruler-failure series
and the control caught it before it shipped.

## 4. THE SHAPE FOR US

1. **THE SITUATION IS NOT TEXT.** It is what he sees. BB has to type "a band of
   weathered men appear" because BB has a still picture. We have a street with
   bodies on it that walk. **We can show the six dogs coming out of the wash.**
   Writing that sentence is doing BB's job with BB's handicap and none of BB's
   reason.
2. **THE WORDS GO WHERE THEY SURVIVE: THE CHOICE AND THE CONSEQUENCE.** That is
   the skimming quote read as a spec rather than as a complaint.
3. **A MOUTH SAYS IT** (rule 19). Not a narrator, and not a card.
4. **THE CHOICE IS A VERB** and it is what HE does, so it fits a button and needs
   no sentence around it.
5. **98 CHARACTERS IS THE CEILING THE MOMENT IT HAS A MOUTH**, because a bubble
   past that is held no longer than one at it. Today the twelve average 134.
6. **NOT EVERY MOMENT DESERVES WORDS.** BB's own read-only events are the ones
   its players name as skippable, and its own writer calls them the easy thing
   rather than the good thing. A moment with nothing to decide should be a thing
   you see and walk past, in silence.

## 5. WHAT MOVES THAT BATTLE BROTHERS' PICTURE DOES NOT (rule 33g)

Paolo: *"Battle Brothers is just a bunch of pictures... we can do more and put
more life into it with this analog horror pixel direction."*

- **BB's event: one still illustration.** Ours: the person **walks up**, on the
  same street, at one step per house, and you can walk away before they finish.
- **BB's event: a paragraph telling you they look desperate.** Ours: his hands
  are not steady **and you can see that**, because the body is drawn large.
- **BB's event: silent.** Ours has a mouth, so it has a voice and a sound.
- **BB's event: a modal you must answer.** Ours can be **overheard and ignored**,
  which is rule 32(a) in the same breath.
- **THE SHARPEST ONE:** BB must write the situation because its picture cannot
  change. **Ours changes every frame.** The sentence we save is a sentence the
  screen is already saying.
