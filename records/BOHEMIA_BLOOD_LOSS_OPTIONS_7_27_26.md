## WHAT HE IS ACTUALLY WORRIED ABOUT, NAMED PLAINLY

He is not asking whether bleeding is realistic. He is asking whether it becomes a
**CHORE**: clear a place, sit down, do first aid, stand up, walk, repeat. That is a
tax you pay for playing well, and it is the exact failure that killed the Zomboid
loot page — a real mechanic that is correct on paper and boring in the hand.

The test that matters: **would a player ever CHOOSE not to treat it?** If the
answer is no, it is not a decision, it is a button you have to press, and it should
not exist as a decision at all.

## THE THREE OPTIONS, ALL THREE PLAYABLE ON THE DIAL PAGE

### 0 — ALWAYS
Every wound that bleeds keeps costing you health until you treat it.
- **For:** every fight leaves a mark; the camp always has a job; hardcore.
- **Against:** it is exactly the chore he is describing. You will treat it every
  single time, so it is not a choice — and it makes clearing three rooms in a row
  feel like paperwork.

### 1 — SELF-LIMITING (bleeding stops on its own)
A bleed costs you health for a while and then clots by itself. Treating it early
saves you the health it would otherwise have taken.
- **For:** the decision comes back. Do I spend supply and time now, or eat the
  damage and push on while I still have daylight? That is a real trade and it is
  the same trade the whole survival system is built on.
- **Against:** slightly more to explain; a player who never treats anything still
  survives, so first aid needs to be worth it on the maths.

### 2 — ONLY SERIOUS WOUNDS (my recommendation)
Ordinary fights do not leave a bleed at all. A REAL wound — the bullet still in
you, the deep one — is what needs the camp, and it hurts enough that you want it
handled.
- **For:** it makes first aid a **rare event instead of a routine**, which is what
  makes "a companion digs the bullet out of you" a scene rather than a chore. It
  answers his question with a no: you do NOT need to do this after every cleared
  area, only after the fights that actually got you.
- **Against:** most fights leave no mechanical trace, so the medical system is
  quiet most of the time. That is a feature if the rare case lands hard.

## MY ANSWER, SINCE HE ASKED

**Option 2, with option 1's behaviour on the rare wound.** Concretely: most fights
leave nothing to treat; a serious wound does, and even that one is self-limiting so
you are never STUCK — you are just paying for ignoring it.

The reason is his own design. He has already ruled that ignoring the camp entirely
must stay playable ("if people dont want to give a fuck about that its okay too").
A mandatory bleed contradicts that clause directly: it makes the camp compulsory
through the back door. Option 2 keeps the camp powerful and optional, and it saves
the bandage for the moment it means something.

**HIS CALL, NOT MINE.** All three are on the BLEED_POLICY dial. Walk into the
WOUNDS tab, give yourself something, and try each one.

## WHAT THIS DOES NOT DECIDE

Nothing about combat. NO DAMAGE BEFORE THE DIAL still stands: nothing on the dial
page can hurt you, and the wound buttons exist only so the bandage, the gauze and
the companion have something to treat. When and how a fight actually wounds you is
the COMBAT lane's business and it is downstream of the dial he has not built yet.
