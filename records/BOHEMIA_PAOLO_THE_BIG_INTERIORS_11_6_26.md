## THE RULE THAT DECIDES WHICH INTERIORS: THE TILE STAYS A HOUSE
A fight tile is a house (9/4, 9/28), about 18 m across on the map's own ruler (slices/BOHEMIA_CITY_WORLD.html LOT_M, 'the length of a
house'), so about 324 square metres or 3,500 square feet a tile. Inside, a tile is still that size: a house-sized piece of floor (a
pit of tables, a row of slot banks, a bay of racks, a stretch of tunnel). So only the BIG roofs of the valley make a board, and a
fight inside is still a turf war across a block-sized floor, never a man in a hallway. The numbers that make it work:
- a casino floor of 171,500 sq ft (MGM Grand, the Las Vegas Advisor's figure) is about 49 house tiles, 7 by 7; Wynn and Encore's
  191,424 sq ft of gaming (the Gaming Control Board's figure, PlayUSA) is about 55;
- a big store or a warehouse floor of 100,000 to 200,000 sq ft is 30 to 60 tiles; a convention hall is hundreds.
Battle Brothers fights only outdoors (its department is the campaign and the map, never interiors), so this is our own swag; ROGUE
FABLE 4 (the fight's lead) fights in rooms, and what we take from it is only that a fight can be inside and still be on the beat.

## HOW AN INTERIOR BOARD WORKS (the coordinator's design, built unless he corrects it)
- THE SAME CAMERA, THE ROOF CUT AWAY: the map's camera, the building's roof and upper floors cut away the way the map's houses
  already show up close; no new screen, no loading picture (rules 407, the first votes' ONE GAME MODE).
- THE BOARD IS THE BUILDING'S REAL PLAN (rule 407's way: a board is the place itself): its walls, its doors, its pillars at the
  real spots, on house tiles.
- WHAT THE FLOOR GIVES: COVER (slot banks, cage counters, store shelves, racks, parked cars), BLOCKERS (load-bearing walls,
  pillars, escalator wells), HIGH GROUND (mezzanines, catwalks, the next level of a garage, the stands; rule 9/27 'high ground is a
  roof', and AUTO HOLDS THE ROOF already reads levels), CHOKEPOINTS (a door or a corridor between two tiles costs more to cross).
- THE DARK: the power is out unless somebody pays for it (batteries are the money): an unlit interior is dark past a few tiles, lit
  only by fires, flashlights and what burns; a LIT interior is a rich faction's and is worth taking. Analog horror at the source.
- HOW YOU GET IN: a fight happens at a building with an inside (a job inside it, a raid on a home base inside it, a boss's lair, a
  crew that finds you camped inside), and the board is that building's floor; a place can have an inside board AND a street board.

## THE CANDIDATES (each a real place in the valley, the first three first)
1. A CASINO FLOOR (the Strip; the Mob's): slot banks in rows for cover, the cashier's cage as the strong room, the pit, the bar, the
   sportsbook's wall of dead screens; 49 to 55 tiles on the real sizes above. The most Vegas fight there is.
2. THE FLOOD TUNNELS: the valley has 600 to 700 miles of flood channels (Fox5 2023 counted 684; 8 News Now more than 700), about 200
   miles of them underground drains (the Washington Times, 2010), and somewhere between 300 (HELP of Southern Nevada, 2019) and 1,200
   to 1,500 (outreach workers, recent) people living in them. A long, narrow board (a chain of tiles, each a stretch of box culvert),
   camps along the walls, the street's light through the grates, and a flash flood as the event (31 flood deaths counted since 1960).
   The underground of the dead city, analog horror for real.
3. A PARKING GARAGE (every casino and the mall have one): five or six levels as high ground, the ramps as the slopes, concrete
   pillars, dead cars as cover; the turf war goes UP.
4. THE CONVENTION CENTER'S HALLS: the biggest roof in the valley; in the collapse a shelter for thousands (the real floor: New
   Orleans' Superdome and Convention Center after Katrina, 2005). The people's fight, the crowd as the board.
5. THE MALL (Fashion Show; the Forum Shops' painted sky): stores as tiles, the atrium, the escalators.
6. A DISTRIBUTION WAREHOUSE (North Las Vegas' industrial parks): racks as walls, lanes, the loading docks, forklifts.
7. THE DATA FORT (the valley's real data centres; our DATAFORT kind; the Network's): server rows, the cold, the one place with power.
8. THE AIRPORT TERMINAL: the long concourse, the gates, the baggage hall.
9. THE STADIUM (Allegiant): the field, the concourse ring, the stands as high ground.
10. THE SPHERE: the dead bowl under the biggest screen in the world.
11. THE DAM'S POWERHOUSE (Hoover): the generator hall, the galleries in the concrete.
12. THE HOME CENTER (WORLD TEN's new place), THE HOSPITAL, THE PRISON's cell blocks: smaller, still house-tiled.
