## 0. THE QUESTION, ASKED PROPERLY
Ten years after a collapse that killed most of a 2.3-million-person
metro, in the Mojave, at 2,000 feet, 110°F summers, no power, no water
treatment: **what is alive, what is fat, and what is dangerous?**
The answer is not "mutants." It is that a mass-mortality event is a
FEEDING EVENT, and ecology tells you exactly who shows up and in what
order. That is more frightening than mutants and it is free realism.

## 1. THE RULE THAT ORGANISES EVERYTHING: THE SCAVENGER GUILD
Carrion ecology is a studied field and it gives us a ready-made enemy
roster with built-in tiers. Decomposition runs through three groups:
**invertebrates, vertebrate scavengers, and microbes.** The vertebrates
commonly recorded on carrion are, almost word for word, a Bohemia enemy
list: **dogs, cats, rodents (mice and rats), coyotes, opossums, raccoons,
skunks, and birds — crows, ravens and vultures.**
AND THERE IS AN ORDER OF ARRIVAL, WHICH IS A DIFFICULTY CURVE FOR FREE:
- **FLIES FIRST.** Blow flies and flesh flies are "the first, and
  arguably most dominant, organisms that colonize and decompose
  vertebrate remains," worldwide, with "rapid development times and
  voracious appetite... the capacity to rapidly colonize and consume
  excess carrion biomass."
- **BIRDS ARE USUALLY FIRST OF THE BIG ONES.** Ravens are repeatedly
  documented as first to arrive at a carcass.
- **VULTURES AND TOP PREDATORS DOMINATE** where present: "functionally
  dominant species that are able to rapidly consume large amounts of
  carrion."
- **AND WHEN THE DOMINANT SCAVENGERS ARE GONE, CARCASSES PERSIST.**
  Removal of dominant scavengers produces "dysfunctional scavenger
  guilds" and "longer carcass persistence in the landscape."
**THAT LAST ONE IS A WHOLE DISTRICT DESIGN.** A part of the valley where
the birds do not go is a part where the bodies are still there. Nobody
has to explain why it is horrible.

## 2. WHAT IS ACTUALLY OUT THERE — THE REAL CLARK COUNTY ROSTER
Sourced to Nevada wildlife and Clark County material, not invented:
### THE WINNERS (thrive, multiply, become the common enemy)
- **COYOTE.** The headline animal. They have "thrived in Las Vegas
  despite rapid urban development," using **the valley's washes, golf
  courses and desert patches as corridors** to move through the metro,
  and they are "found in greater numbers in Clark County, drawn by water
  and prey." They eat **rodents, rabbits, animal carcasses, and domestic
  dogs and cats given the opportunity.** IN OUR VALLEY THEY ARE THE
  DEFAULT PREDATOR AND THE WASH IS THEIR HIGHWAY — which we have already
  built as a district type.
- **COMMON RAVEN.** Smart, social, first to a body, and a documented
  beneficiary of human landscapes. Ravens as a swarm over a block is a
  READ: something died there.
- **RATS.** The urban constant. Unlimited food, no sanitation.
- **FERAL DOGS.** The one the ecology literature lists first among
  vertebrate scavengers and the one every collapse account features. Not
  wolves. **Somebody's pets, in a pack, ten years on.** That is the most
  Bohemia enemy imaginable and it costs nothing in fiction.
- **GREAT-TAILED GRACKLES AND PIGEONS.** Already documented "sifting
  through trash for scraps" in urban Las Vegas. Ambience, not enemies.
### THE DESERT NATIVES STILL PRESENT
Mule deer, gray fox, **burros**, bobcat, kangaroo rat, desert cottontail,
desert bighorn sheep. Burros are big, unbothered by people, and iconic
Mojave — a hazard and a resource rather than a monster.
### THE DANGEROUS ONES THAT DO NOT CHASE YOU
Rattlesnakes (the Mojave green's reputation is its own quest hook),
sidewinders, Gila monster, bark scorpions, black widows, tarantulas,
centipedes. **These are TERRAIN, not encounters** — the thing under the
rubble, in the dark room, in the pipe. That fits the existing terrain-
kills mechanic from the RF4 lift far better than making them fights.
### THE ONE NOBODY THINKS OF
**AFRICANIZED HONEY BEES** are established in southern Nevada. A hive in
a wall is a no-go zone with a sound cue and a very fast punishment.

## 3. THE THREE TIERS THAT FALL OUT OF THIS, UNPROMPTED
This is the Valheim structure he asked for, and the ecology hands it to
us without a single invented creature:
- **TIER 1 — THE FED.** Rats, ravens, flies, single coyotes. Everywhere,
  low threat, they are the AMBIENCE that proves the world is alive. He
  said the city feels dead; this tier is the cure and it is cheap.
- **TIER 2 — THE ORGANISED.** Dog packs, coyote packs. Pack AI, flanking,
  they break off and come back. THIS IS WHERE THE RF4 MOVEMENT WORK
  LANDS — a pack that circles is the "fight has to move you" law with
  teeth instead of a rule.
- **TIER 3 — THE OWNERS.** Whatever holds the worst blocks. Reserved: the
  Amalgamation and the factions are HIS, and I am not filling that in.
**AND THE LOOT FALLS OUT TOO**, which he said we have zero of: a carcass
is a resource, a raven flock marks a body, a bee hive is a hazard AND
food, a dog pack has a den and a den has what the dogs dragged home.

## 4. THE FINDING THAT CHALLENGES WHAT WE BELIEVE
**WE HAVE BEEN TREATING "REALISTIC" AND "FUN" AS A TRADE. HERE THEY ARE
THE SAME MOVE.** The most realistic answer to "what lives here" is also
the scariest and the most mechanically rich: not monsters, but **the
animals that already live in Las Vegas, made numerous and unafraid by ten
years of easy food.** A pack of somebody's golden retrievers is worse
than a mutant, because it is true.
AND THE SECOND HALF OF THAT: **the reason the city feels dead is not that
we lack enemies. It is that we lack ANIMALS.** Ravens on a roofline, rats
at a bin, a coyote crossing the wash three blocks away and not caring
about you — none of those are combat and all of them are life. Tier 1 is
mostly not an enemy system at all. It is set dressing that moves, and it
is the cheapest fix on this list for the loudest complaint on his list.
