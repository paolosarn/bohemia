## HOW A CEILING IS DRAWN FROM A CAMERA THAT LOOKS DOWN
From the fight's camera a ceiling is its top, which is nothing. So it is built the way a builder draws one, the REFLECTED
ceiling: the slab is the page and everything that hangs under it stands off the page toward the camera (a girder 0.9 m deep
stands 0.9 m proud), lit and shadowed by the rig like every other thing.

## WHAT WAS TRIED AND THROWN OUT (each measured on the real page)
1. ONLY THE MEMBERS, THE SLAB LEFT OUT (a tee's stem every 1.5 m, pipes, lights, real transparency between): on a phone a
   tile is 30 points wide at the opening view, and at 10% and at 20% nothing could be seen at all. So the members are few
   and thick, and the slab is in: the pictures are whole.
2. A BAY ONE TILE WIDE: the girders fall on every tile line and read as his own grid a little darker, not as a thing over
   his head (COMBAT THREE's note said the same: 'a rhythm that is NOT the tile grid reads best'). Looked at three ways (a bay
   one tile, two, two and a half). A BAY IS TWO TILES: the bays read as panels of a deck and no girder cuts a tile's middle.
3. A PALE CASINO CEILING: at 10% it lifted the dark carpet 6.5 steps of tone and only 74 in 100 of the pixels it touched
   moved 13 or less: a haze over his men. The tiles are dim now (a hung ceiling nobody has lit in years): 5.0 steps, 92 in 100.
4. THE TUNNELS' LIME as flat strips: two faces at one height rendered black, and the lime on a bay's own line was missing
   from the picture's last column and row (a bay's east edge is another's west): 17 steps over the allowance, found by the
   gate. Many small crusts each at its own height, drawn on every line: 0.9.
