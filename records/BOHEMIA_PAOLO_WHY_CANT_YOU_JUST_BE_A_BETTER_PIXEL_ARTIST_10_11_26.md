## Three rulings inside it

1. THE PACKS ARE NOT SACRED. He approved them as a floor, not as the look of three arcs of apocalypse. Where he does not like a pack piece, it is a placeholder like everything else (10/10). ROUND ELEVEN's 'the pack pieces are the art' is read as 'the pack pieces are the material we have', not 'the pack pieces are the game'.
2. BUYING IS NOT THE ROAD. 'Keep buying' is not an answer he will accept. ROUND ELEVEN (b) is amended: buying is one road, taken rarely, for a hole that a pack happens to fill well; the main road for what the packs lack is the chats getting better.
3. THE REAL QUESTION: why can't the chat be a better pixel artist itself, and what can he do. Answered below, honestly, and turned into ROUND TWELVE.

## What a chat is really like as an artist (the honest self-description)

- It draws blind. It writes, and sees the picture only after it is rendered. It CAN look at a picture and say in words what is wrong with it; it cannot see while its hand moves. Every bad tile so far was drawn with eyes closed and shipped without opening them.
- It has no hands and no muscle memory. No personal style accumulates on its own; each chat starts fresh. Its only memory is what is written in the repo.
- It is good at: structure, rules, shapes, consistency once a recipe exists, iterating when it gets feedback, writing code that builds forms (3D shapes, vector parts), and describing a picture's faults in words.
- It is bad at: placing pixels by hand, organic forms, knowing a picture is wrong without something to hold it against, and 'is this beautiful'.

## ROUND TWELVE: THE CHAT LEARNS TO SEE WHILE IT DRAWS

The way a chat becomes a better artist is the way a person does: look, fix, look again, many times, with a process, and keep what it learns.

1. EYES OPEN WHILE DRAWING. Every asset is built in a loop: render, LOOK at the PNG (at 1x and at 8x, and shrunk to 16x16), write down what is wrong in words, fix, render again. Twenty to forty cycles per asset, not one. The chats' time, never his. A sheet that was rendered once and shipped is a failed sheet; the loop log rides with the asset.
2. STAGES, LIKE A PERSON. Silhouette first (does the thumbnail read as the thing), then big shapes and light, then colour ramps, then detail, then outline. Each stage is looked at before the next begins. No detail before the silhouette reads.
3. STUDY, NOT COPY. The chat takes real pixel art apart to learn CRAFT (how many colours a ramp uses, where the outline breaks, how light sits on a roof): his packs, public-domain and CC0 art, the free tutorials (Saint11, Lospec). What it learns becomes a written rule in the skill file, with a before/after picture it made itself. The skill file is the chat's memory of getting better; it only grows.
4. BUILD THE FORM, THEN HAND-FINISH WITH EYES OPEN. The 3D rig and the vector parts give true shapes and sizes; the Aseprite scripts give clean pixels; the loop in (1) is where the chat's judgement actually gets exercised.
5. HIS HELP, ONCE, IN THE ONLY PLACE HE IS IRREPLACEABLE: HIS EYE. Not per asset. Two things, each one time:
   a. A MOODBOARD: ten to twenty pictures he loves for the world's look across the three arcs (photos, film stills, paintings, anything, not game packs), dropped into this chat once. Directors give artists this on day one; nobody gave the chats one. DIRECTION turns it into the analog horror bible's picture page and every look decision is held against it.
   b. A TASTE SESSION in the VOTE tab, once: twenty pairs, 'which of these two'. The pairs are built to separate the choices that matter (outline weight, colour count, light hardness, grit level, how broken things are). His twenty answers become rules in the skill file. After that, his taste is in the system and the chats stop guessing at it.
