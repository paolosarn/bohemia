HE IS RIGHT, AND THE DIAGNOSIS IS EXACT

I measured everything and shaped nothing. Every dimension on that house was real
and checkable, and I built a 15.0 x 9.0 m BAR with a flat unbroken front, one
continuous roof plane, and no massing breaks anywhere. That is a trailer
silhouette. Bolting a two-car garage onto it made the thing incoherent as well as
ugly, which is exactly what he caught: a trailer home does not have a garage.

His "75% done" is not a joke, it is a measurement of how close I got to the wrong
building. Drop the garage, uglify the roof, and I would have shipped a competent
single-wide.

WHAT THE RESEARCH SAYS, and every line of it convicts the house:
  - a mobile home reads as one at 2:12 to 3:12 pitch with ~6 in eaves; site-built
    is 4:12 or steeper with 12 to 16 in eaves. "Without eaves, most homes look
    like a cheap box."
  - the suburban house types that read as HOUSES all have MASSING BREAKS: the
    L-ranch (main bar plus a projecting wing, cross gable at the junction), the
    snout house (garage pushed forward to the street with its own gable), the
    cross-gable ranch (a gable END facing the street over the entry), the
    two-story, the split-level.
  - the ONE type with no break is the hip ranch with the garage absorbed into the
    main volume. That is precisely what I drew. Of every shape available I picked
    the flattest one and then drew its roof as shallow as it goes.

THE REAL FAILURE IS AN ORDER-OF-OPERATIONS FAILURE. I went and got the door right
to the millimetre before deciding what the building WAS. Human sizing is a
constraint, not a design. Shape is the design, and it comes first.

WHAT SURVIVES, and it is not the art:
  - the scale work stands. 1 px = 1.705 cm, the sprite is 1.74 m, and the tile
    grid's 2-cell door is 1.50 m, which is 24 cm shorter than the character. That
    finding is real and independent of this house.
  - gates/human_scale_gate.py stays. Its invariant (a person fits through the
    door) is true of every house that will ever be built here, and it never had an
    opinion about shape, which is the hole this verdict exposes.

WHAT CHANGES: a house is judged on SHAPE FIRST, before a single detail is drawn.
The next thing he sees is massing, not a finished elevation.
