# corrects what he hates in play. None of these is a name or an identity. Realism first.
# The ECONOMY handoff listed them "for the coordinator, one at a time". Here they are, all nine.

1. THE VALLEY EATS ITS LAST SHELVES IN TEN IN-GAME DAYS. Already a ruled premise (9/5);
   the far end is round 19's day 67 when the shelf line stops moving. Stands. Nothing to
   decide.
2. NOTHING CHARGES A BATTERY. Already ruled by Paolo 9/5 (BATTERIES law section 4):
   buildings make them, nobody sells currency, the player does not charge them by hand.
   Stands. The remaining question "a faction SELLS it?" is answered NO by his words.
3. THE CENTURY RULE COUNTS BUILDINGS AND SOME PEOPLE. Already ruled by Paolo 9/7
   ("buildings and some people depending on how many years passed"). Stands.
4. THE GOOD FIRST BUILDING IS THE BLOCK'S PUMP. The disaster record puts water and
   wastewater first for generators and fuel (the lesson written down after Sandy by the
   water utilities' own association); ECONOMY round 7 measured the valley floor is 2,028
   feet up and the pumps ARE the city (WORLD [water lifted]). So the first thing a player
   should build is the pump that keeps a block's water on: it costs one, it is what makes
   the block livable and therefore rentable, and a lit block with dry taps is not a home.
   Power comes second. Realism first; he re-orders it if he hates it.
5. HOW MANY BATTERIES EXIST AT THE START (the money supply). Not typed. It is what the
   buildings have mined since the crash: every power site times the days since the crash
   times one a day, minus what the shelves ate. Measured off the map, never a constant.
6. A BROKEN PROMISE COSTS EXCLUSION, NOT SEIZURE. ECONOMY round 9 found the real record
   (informal lenders and outfits cut you off; they do not come and take). The `refuse`
   flag on the against organ already exists and is the mechanism. Nobody seizes.
7. THE MARKET WEEK IS SEVEN DAYS. A fortress market is open every day; a town's opens on
   one day of the week (its own, off the seed); a camp's opens when a caravan arrives
   (the 28 parties already move). Realism: weekly markets are the oldest thing in
   economics and the game already has a day clock.
8. A TRADER WILL REFUSE MONEY AND ASK FOR GOODS: YES, AT A CAMP, TO A STRANGER. Sixth
   vote; BARTER_ONLY already exists and is unreachable (round 18). Wire it: a camp's shelf
   to a stranger (rung 0) trades goods for goods; a counted person pays one. In a real
   shortage the seller wants the thing, not the paper, from people he does not know.
9. A REPAIRED RUIN HOUSES PEOPLE (his 8/1 ruling stands), AND A BUILDING HOLDS A SECOND
   HOUSEHOLD WHEN THERE IS NOWHERE ELSE. Doubling up is the documented crisis response
   (the post-2008 doubled-up household studies; Caracas and Beirut apartments holding two
   and three families). Cap at two households; the rent is split, the room is not.
