## WHICH FINDINGS CHANGED THE LINES, AND HOW
Seven, and the first one rewrites a third of the row.

**1. A THANK YOU IS A WAY OF COUNTING, SO NOT ONE OF THE THREE THANKS IS WARM.** The
Greenlandic hunter objected to being thanked for several hundred pounds of meat,
because "by gifts one makes slaves and by whips one makes dogs": thanking marks help
as a finished item, and a finished item is an account, and an account is how one
person comes to own another. **So THANKS 1 is the giver REFUSING the thanks** in
almost exactly the hunter's terms ("Say it and it is a thing that happened and it is
finished. It is not finished."), **THANKS 2 is a deflection that keeps the account
open on purpose** by aiming it at the future and at nobody, and **THANKS 3 is the only
plain thank you in the section and it is the coldest line in it**, because the speaker
is using it to close the account and end the relationship. Same words as a warm
thanks, opposite job. Before school all three would have been warm.

**2. YOU HIDE THE NEED, so no asker names his trouble while negotiating.** Revealed
need is pricing information. ASK 1 never says why the cart has to go up today; the
closest it comes is a sideways admission that his exposure is larger than the
listener's, which is a bargaining move rather than a confession.

**3. YOU PROPOSE A SHAPE INSTEAD OF ASKING FOR TRUST, and each ask carries a different
one.** From the research on how migrants pick a smuggler: reputation, guarantees,
escrow, the "substitutes for trust". **ASK 1 is half on delivery. ASK 2 is a third
party who already holds things for strangers. ASK 3 is going first on something small,
priced so that being robbed is survivable** ("If you walk off with both I will have
learned something about this corner and it will have been cheap"). **Nobody says trust
me**, and the section bans it, because it is what somebody says who has no shape to
offer.

**4. THE ASK IS THINNER THAN THE ASKER'S UNCERTAINTY.** None of the three answers
questions nobody asked, and none states its reason. ASK 2 does not explain what is
being left with the woman at the stall.

**5. THE PRICE IS WHERE THE RELATIONSHIP GETS DECLARED**, from Zelizer: adopting a
payment system with somebody is redefining the relationship with them. **REFUSAL 2
refuses the PRICE rather than the ask** and becomes a counter-offer wearing a no, and
the line that does it ("I would not get out of bed for that") is a statement about
standing, not arithmetic.

**6. A REFUSAL IS PREFACED AND CARRIES AN ACCOUNT, and we had never written one.**
Measured in round one on 199,740 dialogue-act-tagged turns of real recorded speech:
rejections are prefaced 26.7% against 12.7% for acceptances. **All three refusals open
with a run-up ("Ah. No, look.", "Well, hold on.", "Mm. I cannot, no.") and all three
carry an account**, and in REFUSAL 1 the account arrives before the refusal has
finished landing, which is the shape the corpus shows.

**7. AND THE SAME CORPUS KILLED THE SLOGAN, SO THE SECTION PROTECTS THE BARE NO.** A
bare "No." answering a QUESTION is common, short and unmarked (1,374 turns, 13.3%
prefaced, 1.20 words); the marking belongs to refusing a REQUEST. **So the section
carries a two-line exchange whose whole job is to show a bare "No." that would be
WRONG to hedge.** A writer working from "people never just say no" would have ruined
that line, and this is the finding that stops them.

Plus the eighth, which is ours rather than the literature's: **EVERY ASK GIVES THEM AN
OUT**, because our build had ZERO in 3,093 lines. Not to win the yes. So the no can
happen without becoming a fight, which in a place with no law (Q20) matters more than
the yes did.

## ROUTED
- **WORDS** Q21 is complete under the 9/6 mode: school, then the writing, findings
  named. The row can be marked SHIPPED with both records. Q22 [debt words] is next
  and starts with school. Q4 to Q17 are still owed their school rounds.
- **QUESTS** This row exists for [first ask], and finding 3 is the handshake:
  **the first ask a player meets should hand them a STRUCTURE, not a plea.** Half on
  delivery, a third party, or the asker going first. A quest that opens by asking the
  player to be trusted is asking for the one thing a stranger cannot give. And
  finding 7 is a warning about the opposite failure: do not strip the bare "No."
  out of answers while making refusals sound realistic.
- **PEOPLE** Finding 8 is a systems note, not a line: **an ask that offers no way to
  decline forces a bare no, and a bare no to a request reads as hostility.** If a
  refusal ever costs the player standing, the ask that produced it should have
  carried an out, or the system is punishing people for a sentence we wrote badly.
- **ECONOMY** Finding 5 in one line, free: **how somebody offers to pay is a
  statement about what the two of them are to each other.** Paying in work, paying in
  power, and paying in batteries are three different relationships, not three prices.
