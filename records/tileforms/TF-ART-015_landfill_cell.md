# TILE FORM TF-ART-015 - LANDFILL CELL (REFUSE GROUND, COVER CAP, POND EDGE, HAUL ROAD, LITTER DRIFT)

## A. IDENTITY
- NAME: The landfill floor. The five surfaces you actually stand on inside a dead dump: compacted refuse where the last lift was never capped, the pale daily-cover soil that caps the rest of it, the dry leachate pond and its edge, the haul road the trucks wore in, and the drift of blown trash packed against the fence.
- FAMILY/SET: LANDFILL GROUND family, one drawing job, five members plus the two transition sets that bind them: (1) compacted refuse ground, (2) daily-cover soil cap, (3) leachate pond edge and pan, (4) haul-road surface, (5) blown-trash drift against a fence, (6) the WANG-16 cap-over-fill edge set, (7) the WANG-16 pond edge set.
- THE JOB, ONE SENTENCE: this family exists so the landfill reads as an ENGINEERED WASTE SITE that ran out of money mid-lift instead of a brown square with speckles on it, because 67 percent of every landfill cell in the game is one of these five surfaces and every one of them currently draws as the frozen starter tile `dirt`.

## C. WHERE
- SURFACE + TAB: RUN (the walk - you cross the haul road, you walk out onto the uncapped fill, you go down into the dry pond) and CITY (human mode ground plane). At MAP zoom the landfill is one pale mass with a dark hole in it; the cap is the lightest ground in the valley and the pond pan is the darkest, so the district reads at every zoom off VALUE alone, which is the whole M14 argument.
- DISTRICT FAMILIES: landfill (all five members). Member by member beyond that: the daily-cover cap also serves the boneyard and quarry graded-soil pads; the haul-road surface also serves quarry, gypsum, farm service roads and the wash maintenance road; the litter drift serves EVERY fenced district downwind of anything, which is most of the industrial family; the refuse ground and the lined pond edge are landfill-only and should stay landfill-only, because a surface that appears everywhere stops meaning anything.
- LAYER: ground
- SOLID? no - all five are walkable and the pond is dry, so you go down into it - ENTERABLE? no
- HEIGHT NOTE, and it is a SCOPE BOUNDARY rather than a member: the CELL BERM (engine code 7, 1,071 tiles a district, declared `kind:'structure'`, `solid:true`) and the TERRACE RISER between lifts are 2 to 3 m of relief that BLOCKS, which makes them structure-layer objects, not ground. I am not silently absorbing them into a ground form to make my member count look better. They are the district's signature silhouette and the theme sheet's own hook word, and they need their own form. NOT IN THIS FORM, and NOT IN A TAB YET. What this form does carry is every place the ground MEETS them: the cap running up to the berm toe, and the fill spilling over an uncapped crest.
- MUST SIT BESIDE: itself, endlessly, in all four directions (all five members are large-area grounds); the cap against the fill through the WANG-16 cap edge set; the cap and the fill against the pond through the WANG-16 pond edge set; the haul road against cap and fill on both shoulders; the litter drift against chain-link (TF-ART-004) and against the berm toe; the desert margin outside the fence (TF-RUN-001); the concrete pad and truck-scale marking at the gate; the dead dozer and compactor standing on the fill; the gas wellhead risers standing on the cap.
- NEVER BESIDE: never against a sidewalk, kerb, lawn, plaza, driveway apron or any residential tile - a landfill has no pedestrian language anywhere in it and a kerb at the edge of a waste cell is the loudest possible tell that nobody knew what they were drawing; never refuse ground touching the perimeter fence directly with no cover, no berm and no drift between them (the fill is set back inside the berm by law and by physics); never lot asphalt meeting the haul road with no transition, because the haul road is unpaved and a paved-to-unpaved junction is a real edge; never the pond pan touching the fill directly with no edge band, which would read as a puddle instead of a lined impoundment; never anything green, ever.
- EDGE CONTRACT: two contracts, declared per member, and the family is bound by the transitions. The four field surfaces - refuse ground, cover cap, pond pan, haul road - are SELF-SEAMLESS, 2 to 3 variants each per craft law 12, with the haul road additionally carrying a DECLARED WASHBOARD PHASE (see E: 3 ripples per 2 cells, an 88px period, so the corrugation rhythm cannot break at a cell boundary). The two boundaries that carry the district's meaning are WANG-16 edge sets: cap-over-fill, and pond-edge. The crack runs, settlement dishes, erosion rills, cap blowouts and litter drift pieces are SINGLE PLACEMENT overlays laid ON the seamless field and NEVER baked into it - a crack drawn inside a repeating tile is a 44px grid tell, which is our own black-grid history and the exact failure M10 names. WANG-16 rather than BLOB-47 for both boundaries, and the reason is stated so it can be argued with: the engine rasterises its pond as a disc and its cap as rectangles, the boundary band is 10 to 20px of readable content at this cell size, and 31 extra tiles buys a corner nuance nobody will see - if the assembled proof shows diagonal artefacts at the inner corners (M12 names the inner corner as the trap), it escalates to BLOB-47 and that decision is made ON THE PROOF SHEET, not now. Every touching edge will be MEASURED per M10's offset test (wrap step over the tile's own internal step, target near 1.0) against the roughly 9-value normal neighbour step from the 7/28 seam finding.

## D. WHEN
- ACT: 1
- BEST TIME: both, and the landfill is one of the few districts where night is not just "darker". Nothing here is self-lit: the four site pole lights (engine code 9) are dark heads, LIGHT=TERRITORY, nobody owns this place. What changes at night is that the VALUE STRUCTURE is all that survives - the pale cap goes to a cold high-mid, the pond pan goes to true black, and the refuse fill collapses into the cap unless the 18-point separation (M14) was actually built. If the family reads at night it was built right; if the fill vanishes at night the value skeleton was skipped.
- WEATHER STATES: sunny baseline. Cloudy needs nothing but the loss of the shadow inside every settlement dish, which flattens the cap dramatically and is worth checking, because a cap that only reads because of its shadows is a cap that does not read. RAIN is the state that matters here more than on any other ground in the game and it is FOUR separate effects, not one: (1) caliche cover soil darkens hard and unevenly, because the calcium-carbonate-cemented clasts shed water while the fines between them soak it, so a wet cap gets MORE contrast, not less - the opposite of what wet does to asphalt; (2) compacted refuse barely darkens at all, because thirty years of UV has left a surface of shattered polymer film, glass, ceramic and rusted metal that is mostly non-absorbent, which makes wet weather the moment the fill and the cap separate MOST strongly and is a gift worth taking; (3) the haul road is the one surface that turns to slick mud, and its washboard troughs hold water in transverse lines, which is the single most legible wet-weather detail in the district; (4) the pond pan is the one place in act-1 Bohemia that would actually hold standing water again, and it should - a monsoon cell puts a shallow sheet in the bottom of the pan for a day and it is the only water in the game that is REFILLING rather than draining. Per M9 all four are palette states on indexed tiles, not new geometry. [PENDING, Paolo's call: whether the pond ever holds water again in act 1, because that is a story beat about the place waking up, not an art decision.]
- LIT/UNLIT: no separate lit tile is drawn. Nothing in this family emits. The pole lights and the gas flare are structure/prop objects owned by other forms; the ground is a receiver only.
- ANIMATION: static, and deliberately so - the theme sheet's landfill is a place that STOPPED mid-lift. The two honest candidates are dust lifting off the haul road crown and heat shimmer over the black pond pan in July, and per M16 both are palette cycles on an indexed tile rather than frame sets, so neither is built until indexing (M9) lands. A third candidate is explicitly REJECTED here rather than left to be discovered later: fluttering plastic bags. The research in G says the film is embrittled and gone, and animating something the material science says is not there would be a beautiful, expensive lie.

## E. HOW
- EXACT SIZE: THE CORPUS CELL, 44px, CELL_M 0.75, so 1px = 1.70cm. Every dimension below is a real measurement converted at that rate, which is the difference between drawing wear and drawing 22-centimetre blotches and calling it wear.
  - DAILY COVER DEPTH: 40 CFR 258.21(a) requires six inches of earthen material over the working face at the end of every operating day. Six inches is 15.2cm is **9px**. That is the thickness of the pale band you see at any cut, riser or blowout edge, and it is small - a 9px stripe, not a geological stratum. Draw it once at the blowout and never again.
  - LIFT HEIGHT: waste is spread in roughly 2ft layers and rolled, and a day's cell plus its cover comes out around 3m, which is 4 CELLS of vertical rise. That number belongs to the berm/terrace form, but the ground must agree with it: a cap blowout exposes ONE lift face, 4 cells tall, with ONE 9px cover seam in it. Two seams would be two lifts and a different story.
  - THE REFUSE MATRIX: after compaction at 1,200 to 1,500 lb per cubic yard nothing in the fill is intact at the surface except the hard stuff. What is visible is a matrix of torn film, paper, fabric and crushed board at 5 to 15cm, which is **3 to 9px**, so the fill is a **3-to-6px CLUSTER texture** and NEVER single pixels. This is the number that decides whether this family is art or noise.
  - THE NAMEABLE JUNK, and the hard floor under it: a tyre is 0.65m = 38px; a 55-gallon drum is 0.58m across = 34px; a refrigerator carcass is about 0.70 x 0.85m = 41 x 50px, so almost exactly one cell; a 5-gallon bucket is 0.30m = 18px; a mattress is 1.35 x 1.90m = 79 x 112px, so 2 x 2.5 cells. Below about **12px an object cannot be named**, and NAME-IT-OR-DON'T-DRAW-IT says a thing you cannot name does not get drawn. So the fill has exactly TWO scales and nothing in between: a sparse population of nameable 18-to-50px objects placed as single-placement overlays, and the 3-to-6px matrix everywhere else. Anything drawn at 6 to 12px is the mush that gets this family rejected.
  - CALICHE CLASTS IN THE CAP: Clark County cover soil is ripped caliche-cemented alluvium and it breaks into angular chunks of roughly 5 to 30cm, which is **3 to 18px**. So the cap is a pale field with a coarse, sparse, ANGULAR clast population in it - that clast size is the entire visual difference between the cap and the frozen `dirt` tile, and it is why the cap must not be drawn as smooth soil.
  - TENSION AND SETTLEMENT CRACKS: settlement cracks in a landfill cap run 2 to 15cm wide, which is **1 to 9px**, and tens of metres long, which is TENS OF CELLS. Draw them as 2-to-3px dark lines running WITH the contour as single-placement runs. A crack that starts and ends inside one 44px tile is not a crack, it is a grid.
  - SETTLEMENT DISHES: where a big void collapses the cap dishes rather than cracking. Real dishes are metres across with centimetres of relief, so at 44px a dish is a 3-to-6-cell soft dark ellipse with almost no edge - value only, no line. This is the most 45-degree-legal shape in the family and the easiest to overdraw.
  - EROSION RILLS: 10 to 30cm wide and deep, which is **6 to 18px**, running down the slope perpendicular to contour and branching upslope. Single placement, on slopes only. A rill on a flat cap answers "what did this" with nothing (M1) and gets deleted.
  - HAUL-ROAD WASHBOARD: corrugations on unpaved haul roads run roughly 0.3 to 1.0m in wavelength, which is **18 to 59px**. THE CALL: 29.33px, three ripples per two cells, an 88px period, phase declared - the identical trick the rail bed uses for tie pitch, for the identical reason, and it lands mid-range on the real number. The ripples are TRANSVERSE to travel; longitudinal ripples are a different failure (rutting) and mixing them reads as fabric.
  - TRUCK GEOMETRY ON THAT ROAD: a loaded transfer trailer is 2.6m wide (3.5 cells) and its dual-tyre pairs are about 0.6m wide (35px) at a 2.5m track (3.3 cells). So the road carries TWO rut bands 3.3 cells apart, each 35px wide, with a crowned centre between them. The engine's haul road is 7 cells (5.25m) wide, which is one truck plus shoulders and NOT two-way - a real landfill haul road is 24 to 30ft (10 to 12 cells) for two-way traffic. WIRING NOTE for the WORLD lane, not an art job: either the road widens or the art commits to a single-lane road with pullouts, and the art should not guess.
  - THE LINED POND, and this is the detail that makes it a pond and not a mud hole: 60-mil HDPE geomembrane is 1.5mm, which is 0.09px - the liner has NO drawable thickness. What IS drawable is its PANEL SEAMS, because geomembrane rolls are around 7m wide, so the black slope carries parallel seam lines at a **9.3-cell pitch**, and its THERMAL WRINKLES, which stand 5 to 20cm high at 10 to 40cm spacing, so **3 to 12px high, 6 to 24px apart**, always running parallel to the seams. A 9-cell seam pitch with wrinkle bands between it is unmistakable and it costs almost nothing.
  - THE SALT RING: leachate concentrates as it evaporates and its metals precipitate as salts at every stand of the falling water level. On a 3:1 side slope, 12cm of vertical drawdown is 36cm of horizontal travel = **21px**, so the rings read as concentric pale bands roughly 10 to 20px apart across the edge band, each band 3 to 12px wide. That is the WANG-16 pond edge set's entire content and it is why the pond needs an edge set instead of a colour change.
  - THE LITTER DRIFT: real drifts against a site fence stand 0.6 to 1.2m deep, which is **1 to 1.6 cells** against a 1.8m (2.4-cell) chain-link fabric, so the drift covers the bottom 40 to 65 percent of the fence and no more. Profile is a wedge: steep on the fence face, long ramp on the windward side over 2 to 3 cells. ONE GLOBAL WIND, declared here so every drift in every district in the game agrees (uniformity beats realism, craft law 11): the valley's prevailing summer wind is out of the SOUTH-SOUTHWEST, so drifts pile on the north-east face of a fence and the ramp runs back to the south-west. That constant also governs which side of the berm the fill has crept over.
- VIEW: 45-degree world view, always. Four of the five members are flat plates and get sky-lit tops with their wear bowed toward the viewer; the two that have real geometry are the POND EDGE, which is a genuine slope and must be built as a bowed 3:1 face with a lit upper band and a shaded lower one, and the DRIFT, which is a wedge with a lit windward ramp and a shaded fence face. NO flat side-on scroller face anywhere, ever (45 DEGREE ART LAW).
- PALETTE: the constitution ceiling and the GROUND value band, drawn as a SUBSET of the one Bohemia master palette (M17), never its own invented ramp - and this family is the strongest argument yet for the master palette existing first, because it needs both the lightest and the darkest ground in the game and it must not get there by inventing colours at either end. Budget: 7 steps shared across all five members, with ONE saturated accent (rust-orange) spent only where E says. Zero purple, zero green: PURPLE RESERVATION is absolute, and the engine's current placeholder swatch for the pond (`#3a4436` in engine/bohemia_landfill.js, a green-grey) is a PLACEHOLDER and not canon - the cooked pond is neutral-to-warm black-brown, and if it reads green it is wrong.
- LIGHT: the one global direction, upper LEFT. NO keyline. NO dither (act-1 law). The family gets one free gift from physics: the cap is genuinely the brightest ground and the pond liner genuinely the blackest, so the key and the material agree without being argued into it.
- SHADOWS: none baked (separate-layer law). Expected runtime footprint: the berm throws a long shadow onto the cap in the morning; the dozer and compactor throw full body shadows onto the fill; the fence throws a picket shadow across the drift, which is the one shadow that matters aesthetically here and is exactly why the drift and the fence must be judged in one screenshot. Inside a settlement dish the shading is BAKED VALUE, not a shadow - a dish is a shape in the surface, not an object casting.
- SCALE ANCHORS: a human is 1.75m, so a standing body clears the litter drift by half and is dwarfed by a 4-cell lift face; a car is 2x3 tiles and a landfill haul truck is bigger than the road the engine currently gives it; a door opening is 2 cells and the scale house door at the gate is the fixed reference at the district entrance; the six-inch daily cover is 9px and is the smallest deliberate measurement in the family.
- WEAR LEVEL: thirty summers, nobody watering, nobody grading - and the honest research answer is that a Mojave landfill does not ROT, it MUMMIFIES, SETTLES and BLEACHES. Every mark below answers M1's "what did this" in one word.
  - SUN did the colour. Thirty years of Mojave UV strips dye and pigment out of everything at the surface. The correct fill is a BLEACHED, DESATURATED grey-tan field, and colour is the first thing to go, not the last. A rainbow dump is a dump that was buried yesterday.
  - SUN also removed the plastic film, which is the finding that changes the whole design. Polyethylene sheet embrittles in weeks of real desert exposure and disintegrates within about two years unstabilised; after thirty there is no intact bag anywhere on the surface. What is left is a mat of pale opaque flakes and a few thick HDPE items that survived. So there is no bag ballet - the drift is a COMPACTED, SUN-WELDED MAT, closer to felt than to litter.
  - TIME did NOT do what everyone expects. The Garbage Project excavated three dozen landfills and found fifty-year-old newspapers still legible and two-decade-old hot dogs intact, because biodegradation needs moisture and there is none; roughly a quarter of organics go in the first fifteen years and then it stabilises at half a percent or less. So the buried fill is MUMMIFIED and the surface objects keep their SHAPE. No compost, no slime, no sag, no sinking soup.
  - SETTLEMENT did the cap. Waste consolidates differentially, so the cap dishes and cracks rather than eroding flat. Long tension cracks follow the contour and dish-shaped depressions sit over collapsed voids.
  - MONSOON did the slopes and the blowouts. One September thunderstorm is enough - the real local case is in G - so side slopes carry erosion rills and gullies, and where a crack caught concentrated runoff the cap is TORN OPEN and the fill shows through in a raw patch with a 9px pale cover seam at its lip. That blowout is the single most story-carrying tile in the family: it is the theme sheet's "the last lift was never capped" made local and specific.
  - WIND did the haul road and the drift. With no dust suppressant the road's binding fines blow off the crown and drift into the shoulders, so the road INVERTS - the crown coarsens to a stony lag and the soft powder ends up at the edges, which is the opposite of a fresh dirt road and is the cheapest way to say "nobody has watered this in decades". The washboard survives because there is nothing to erase it.
  - EVAPORATION did the pond. It never drained; it dried. That leaves concentric salt rings up the side slope, a cracked black pan at the bottom, and the upper liner exposed and still intact, because carbon-black HDPE is the one man-made material on this site that was engineered to survive UV and it did.
  - WHAT DID NOT HAPPEN, stated because the absence is the point: no vegetation of any kind, alive or otherwise, beyond dead brush caught at the fence; no green; no rust rivers; no smoke. Landfill gas generation is essentially over after thirty dry years, so the flare is out and there is no shimmer, no plume and no burn scar.
- VARIANTS: 44 tiles in the drawing job plus 6 single-placement overlays. Field surfaces: refuse ground x3, cover cap x3, pond pan x2, haul road x2 plus the two-phase washboard pair, all self-seamless, all inside craft law 12's 2-to-5 range. - REAL-WORLD GROUNDING: two named real sites in Clark County, per M15, because grounding is a looking job not a writing job. (1) **APEX REGIONAL LANDFILL, 13550 US Highway 93, north of Las Vegas** - opened 1993, about 2,200 acres, the largest landfill in the United States by capacity, taking roughly 9,000 tons a day at present and up to 15,000 at peak off 500 to 600 truck loads, with a landfill-gas plant powering around 11,000 homes. That is the operating template for the district: a fenced site the size of a town, scale house at the gate, haul roads to an active face, gas wells over the capped cells. (2) **SUNRISE LANDFILL, on Sunrise Mountain east of Las Vegas**, closed 1993, and this is the one that tells us what the ACT-1 SURFACE ACTUALLY LOOKS LIKE. Its cap was built of clay and soil, a model unsuited to the desert; it cracked, with one fissure running 100 feet across the top of the cover, and on 11 September 1998 a single thunderstorm tore a 30 by 75 foot hole in it and washed roughly 100,000 cubic yards of waste into the Las Vegas Wash, which drains to Lake Mead. EPA ordered Republic Dumpco and Clark County Public Works to stabilise it; the 2008 consent decree put about $36 million into an ARMORED engineered cover with stormwater controls, gas collection and groundwater monitoring, precisely because a soft desert cap does not hold. So a thirty-year-unmaintained Vegas landfill is not a heap and not a swamp: it is an engineered cap that has CRACKED ALONG THE CONTOUR, DISHED OVER ITS VOIDS and BLOWN OUT where a monsoon found a crack, with the fill showing raw through the holes. MATERIALS, all Clark County specific: the cover soil is not brown dirt, it is ripped CALICHE - calcium-carbonate-cemented alluvium that occurs across the whole valley as shelves hard enough to need ripping, hammering or blasting, and which comes up in angular chunks that are white to bone-tan, which makes the landfill cap the PALEST large ground surface in the game; six inches of it goes on the working face every day by federal rule (40 CFR 258.21). The leachate goes to lined evaporation ponds rather than treatment because arid-region evaporation reaches around 45 inches a year, and those ponds are lined with black carbon-loaded HDPE geomembrane chosen specifically for UV and chemical survival - so after thirty years the liner is the one intact engineered thing on the site while everything it was protecting has bleached apart. The haul roads are unpaved native soil under a Clark County Division of Air Quality dust-control regime in a PM10 area, which means they were only ever passable because somebody watered them and ran a 15 mph limit; thirty years without that is the road inverting to a coarse blown lag with frozen washboard. And the fill itself does not rot: the Garbage Project's excavations of three dozen landfills found completely legible fifty-year-old newspapers and intact two-decade-old food, because decomposition needs moisture, so a Mojave dump is a DRY TOMB whose contents keep their shapes and lose their colours.

## H. DON'T WANT
- NOT the seagulls-and-flying-bags dump. This is the single biggest miss available and the research kills it outright: unstabilised polyethylene film embrittles in weeks of desert sun and is gone in about two years, so after thirty there is no intact bag on the surface to flutter. The drift against the fence is a sun-welded mat of pale flakes and dead thistle, not a bag ballet - and there are no living birds in act 1 anyway.
- NOT a compost heap. No slime, no ooze, no black rot, no sagging organic mush, no methane shimmer, no flies. The Garbage Project's whole finding is that landfills MUMMIFY. Drawing a wet rotting dump is drawing a different climate.
- NOT brown. The cap is pale caliche, the fill is bleached grey-tan, the pond is black-brown. Mud brown is the default every artist reaches for here and it is wrong on all three.
- NOT green, ever - no algae, no scum bloom, no weeds. The engine's current pond swatch `#3a4436` reads green and it is a PLACEHOLDER, not canon.
- NOT purple. PURPLE RESERVATION belongs to the Amalgamation alone, and a "chemical pond" is exactly the excuse that would smuggle violet into a district gate that sweeps for it.
- NOT a heap. The theme sheet's hook word is THE TERRACES: engineered steps, not a pile. A landfill is a civil-engineering structure with berms, benches, lifts and a designed slope, and drawing it as a mound of garbage throws away the only thing that makes it interesting.
- NOT a random junk scatter. TF-WORLD-012 already says it for the neighbouring family and it is the same law here: the boneyard's hook is SORTING and the landfill's is ENGINEERING. Both are about human system, and mess without system is the mall-icon mistake in ground form.
- NOT single-pixel confetti. Refuse matrix is 3-to-6px CLUSTERS. A field of lone specks is the definition of noise (craft law 1) and it is precisely the 99.6%-orphan `concrete_0` failure that got the whole starter set re-cooked.
- NOT every object drawn. Slynyrd's rule against depicting every brick applies exactly: nameable junk is 18 to 50px and SPARSE, everything else is matrix, and nothing at all gets drawn in the 6-to-12px band where it can be neither named nor read as texture.
- NOT high-contrast rainbow trash. Contrast is a budget (M3) and this family is background (M13). The dozer, the scale house, the terraces and the flare stack are the heroes; the ground must measure QUIETER than the structures standing on it (M2), which both existing banks currently fail by 1.7x in the wrong direction.
- NOT a rust-river ruin. Steel corrodes at well under a micron a year in Mojave humidity, so the metal in the fill is a solid shape under a matte film. Rust is the family's ONE saturated accent and it is spent at the tie-plate scale, not poured over the district.
- NOT a crack drawn inside a repeating tile. Real cap cracks run tens of cells; a crack that starts and ends inside 44px is the heavy-tile-border failure M10 names and our own black-grid history twice over. Cracks, dishes, rills and blowouts are single-placement overlays or they are a grid.
- NOT the desert-pool failure at any seam. No edge darkening of any kind - a visible grid is the kill condition (records/BOHEMIA_DESERT_POOL_SEAM_FINDING_7_28_26.md), and the wall re-cook proved a family can get three times worse at the seam while every other number improves.
- NOT a flat side-on scroller face, NOT a black keyline, NOT dither (act-1 law).
- NOT a puddle with a rim. If the pond has no panel seams at a 9-cell pitch, no thermal wrinkles and no concentric salt rings, it is not a lined leachate impoundment - it is a mud hole, and TF-WORLD-004 already owns mud holes.
- NOT indistinguishable from TF-RUN-002's gravel or TF-WORLD-012's yard floor. If the assembled proof cannot tell haul road from gravel yard at a glance, member 4 should be KILLED and the existing form wired instead. Shipping two grounds nobody can tell apart is worse than shipping one.
