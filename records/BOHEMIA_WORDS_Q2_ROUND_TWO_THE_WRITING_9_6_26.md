## WHICH FINDINGS CHANGED THE LINES, AND HOW
Six, and the first one inverts the entire section.

**1. A LIAR IS THIN, NOT SHAKY, SO THE LIAR IS THE MOST FLUENT PERSON IN HIS
SCENE.** Of 158 measured deception cues, 118 show no association with deception at
all, and pauses are on that list by name. What holds up is content: fewer details,
less coherence, feigned ignorance. **So the liar in the bank never hesitates, never
trails off, and never says "um", and the frightened man three sections later is the
one who stumbles.** The 9/4 round had it the other way round. That single inversion
is the largest change school produced.

**2. THE LIE IS CAUGHT BY THE QUESTION, NOT THE MANNER.** People score 54%,
catching 47% of lies and clearing 61% of truths, so the bias is toward believing.
**The liar in the bank is believed.** He is broken by one question he did not
prepare for, and what exposes him is a fence the player can walk to. **Nothing in
his mouth is a tell.** That is the same rule Q17 reached from the other direction,
and it is now reached twice, independently.

**3. A FRIGHTENED PERSON SAYS MORE, NOT LESS.** The row's own brief says "shortened
sentences" and the measurement says the reverse: under a standard stressor word
productivity goes UP while pausing also goes up. **So the frightened lines are long
and broken rather than clipped**, and a second, quieter kind of fear is written
beside them to show that shortness is about how OLD the fear is, not how big.

**4. AN EXHAUSTED PERSON FIXATES IN ONE SEMANTIC FIELD.** The one stress signature
that survives into text with no voice actor. **The exhausted woman in the bank
cannot talk about her son without landing back in the water barrel**, and the word
"tired" never appears. Then the same woman is written again after sleep, saying the
same event, moving freely, to prove the fixation was the state and not the person.
**This is the showpiece of the round because we have zero of it in the build.**

**5. "UM" IS A COURTESY, NOT A CRACK.** Filled pauses are conventional words a
speaker plans and produces, "uh" for a short delay and "um" for a longer one.
**There is exactly one "um" in the section and it belongs to the calmest person in
it**, holding the floor.

**6. WE CANNOT USE THE DASH.** The standard craft tool for an abrupt cut off is the
em dash, banned in this project everywhere. **Every cut off in the bank is built
from an incomplete clause or the next speaker starting somewhere else**, never from
punctuation.

## THE MEASUREMENT, WITH A BROKEN RULER CAUGHT BEFORE PUBLICATION
Three tests, run because school named them.

**FOLKLORE CUES: we do not write them, and that is luck rather than design.**

    filled pauses (uh, um) in 1,669 lines      1     0.1%
    ellipsis lines                            18     1.1%

One filled pause in the entire game. We never fell into the hesitant-liar trap,
because we barely write hesitation at all.

**THE REAL CUE IS ALSO ABSENT, AND FOR A BETTER REASON.**

    claimed ignorance                         14     0.8%

Reading all fourteen: **not one is a liar feigning ignorance.** Every one is an
honest person admitting a real limit: "I don't know how far it goes", "I don't know
what happened to them", "I don't know you well enough to have an opinion".
**NOBODY IN THIS GAME LIES.** That is the hole this round is written into.

**AND THE EXHAUSTION TEST, WHICH IS WHERE THE RULER BROKE.** School says an
exhausted person fixates in one semantic field, so I measured how much our 40
`#tired` lines repeat their own content words against the other mood-tagged lines.

    first pass, unmatched
        #tired          389 content tokens   repeat share 0.290
        other           1,620 tokens         repeat share 0.553

**That comparison is worthless and I nearly used it.** Repeat share rises
mechanically with sample size, and one set is four times the other. It is the same
class of error as the type-token trap this lane already fell into once. So I matched
the lengths and resampled:

    other, sampled down to 389 tokens, 400 draws
        mean 0.276, sd 0.018, middle 95% from 0.242 to 0.314
    #tired sits at 0.290, which is +0.79 sd, inside the normal range

**NO DIFFERENCE ONCE LENGTH IS MATCHED. THE FIXATION IS ABSENT.** And the unmatched
number would have reported it backwards and loudly. **Length-matching a repetition
measure is now a standing habit in this lane, not a thing I remember to do.**
