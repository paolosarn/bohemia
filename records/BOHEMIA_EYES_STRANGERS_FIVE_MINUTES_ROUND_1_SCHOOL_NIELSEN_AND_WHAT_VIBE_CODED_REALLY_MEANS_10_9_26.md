## THE THIRD LENS: NIELSEN'S TEN USABILITY HEURISTICS, THE REAL 30-YEAR STANDARD

For judging ten different screens against each other fairly (not just "does it look horror," but
"does it function"), the real, standing industry method is Jakob Nielsen's heuristic evaluation:
ten general principles (visibility of system status, match with the real world, user control and
freedom, consistency and standards, error prevention, recognition over recall, flexibility,
minimalist design, helping users recover from errors, help and documentation), derived from
Nielsen's own study of 249 real usability problems across eleven projects. This gives round two a
second, non-aesthetic axis: a screen can pass every horror-bible rule and still be confusing or
inconsistent, and a heuristic pass catches that kind of failure the bible was never built to catch.
[Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics](https://www.uxtigers.com/post/10-heuristics-reimagined)

## TWO TERMS SHARPENED, NOT JUST USED

"Vibe coded," checked at its real origin: Andrej Karpathy coined it in February 2025 to describe
accepting AI-generated CODE without reading it closely -- a workflow description, not originally a
visual style at all. Rule 71's "VIBE CODED" label, applied to pixels and layout, is this lane's
and the house's own extended, colloquial use of the phrase, not its original meaning -- worth
naming precisely rather than implying the term always meant "looks generic."
[Vibe coding, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibe_coding)

The phenomenon rule 71's tells list actually targets has its own, more exact, currently-circulating
name: "AI design slop" -- interfaces that are often clean and competent but interchangeable,
catalogued by current design critics with a specific tell-list that lines up closely with this
game's own: gradients, a uniform grid of identical cards, an icon-on-top with a colour-coded
border, default/safe font choices, "averaged" design that commits to no real direction. The root
cause critics give is useful for round two's own judgement, not just its vocabulary: slop happens
when nobody COMMITS to one direction and the output defaults to the statistically safe average --
which is the same failure shape as "a flat rounded rectangle" or "a centred label," components
that are correct and inoffensive and belong to no particular game. Rule 71's own tells list is
independently confirmed accurate against this real, separate source, not just internally
consistent.
[AI Design Slop](https://mohitphogat.medium.com/ai-design-slop-why-every-ai-built-interface-looks-the-same-and-how-to-fix-it-bf874e0b470c)
