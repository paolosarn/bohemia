# WORDS Q16 -- HOW THE COLLECTOR TALKS AND HOW A CUT-OFF IS SAID
# VAMILY research round, 9/5/26, lane WORDS (words-8dqrnq). MODE: RESEARCH.
# The question, verbatim: "How the collector talks and how a cut-off is said. Real
# testimony from Lebanese and Iraqi households about the generator owner, his
# collector, and the day the line was cut; the best games' debt collectors and
# landlords; what makes a threat land in one line without a villain voice. Test
# lines: ten collector lines, five cut-off lines, five refusals from a family that
# cannot pay, draft:true."

## THE ANSWER IN ONE LINE
**A cut-off is not said. It is a breaker tripping, and the fridge stops, and nobody
announces anything. Every version of this scene I would have written was a man at a
door making a speech, and the real record says the drama is entirely in what people
say the next morning. We have ONE collector line in 1,669, it is four words long,
and we have ZERO cut-off lines.**

## 1. THE MEASUREMENT
Searched all 1,669 NPC lines for money owed, paying, rent, debt, arrears, being
behind. **42 hits, 2.5%, and I read all 42.**

**There is no collector in this game.** Not one line is spoken by a person
collecting from a household. The entire collector vocabulary of Bohemia is one
ambient bark, in three registers:

    when:owed   "You know what you owe me."

Four words, no speaker identity, no place, no amount, no consequence.

And the household side has exactly one line, which is the best thing in the set and
was written by accident:

    "Did you pay it?"
    "I paid it. Tomorrow it will be twice again and I will pay that too."

**Then I searched for the cut-off itself.** Ten candidate hits, all read: three are
about cutting the ends off vegetables, one is a generic "I shut it", and the rest
are an owner in a backroom threatening a public blackout. **Not one line in this
game is somebody's power being cut.**

The closest thing we own is good and is a different scene. S15's owner, in a
backroom, negotiating with the player:

    "Then it goes dark, and four blocks go dark behind it, and everybody sees
     whose hand was on it."
    "I'd rather be the man who posted a rate than the man whose corner went
     dark. Barely."

That is an OWNER talking to an equal. **It is not a collector at a door, and it is
not a family that cannot pay.**

## 2. THE REAL RECORD, IN THEIR OWN WORDS
Lebanon and Iraq have run this exact economy for thirty years, and the testimony is
specific.

**THE COLLECTOR'S VISIT IS THE DREADED EVENT.** In Beirut neighbourhoods, people
**"dread the monthly visit of the private generator bill collector."** Not the bill.
The visit. It is a recurring appointment with a person, and that is the scene.

**THE OWNER'S POWER IS PUBLIC AND EVERYBODY SAYS SO OUT LOUD:**

    "The generator owner is a thug. Everyone knows it, but there is nothing we
     can do."                          Yasmine, Saydeh Street, Achrafieh
    "He makes his own law in full view of everyone."
    He "set up a large station in one of the underground warehouses, and the
     residents of the neighbourhood know that he steals electricity through
     large cables" from the state utility's own rooms.       Majed, Barbour Street

**THAT LAST ONE IS THE WHOLE CHARACTER.** He steals the state's electricity and
sells it back to the people the state was supposed to give it to, in a warehouse
everybody can point at, and nothing happens. **"In full view of everyone" is the
phrase to build on.**

**AND THE WEAPON OF THE WEAK IS A METER.** One resident in Mar Mikhael, after two
years of complaining, got a meter installed and said: **"My electricity bill has
been cut in half."** Half. That is what unmetered billing was costing him, and the
whole fight was over a small box. Our own first quest is called THE METER READER.

**THE NUMBERS, so the scene has weight.** One 500 kVA generator feeds roughly 300
homes. You buy AMPERES, not electricity, monthly, and for years without a meter, so
the owner alone says how many hours he ran. The bill ran about **44% of an average
household's monthly income in 2023, and about 88% for the poorest.** Recent
published Baghdad rates are 12,000 dinars (about $9) per ampere for round-the-clock
supply, 8,000 (about $6) for nights only, against a minimum wage of 350,000 dinars
a month, about $269, itself well under the poverty line unions cite.

## 3. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG: THE CUT-OFF IS SILENT
I assumed the cut-off was a confrontation. It is not. In Iraq the connection runs
through **a circuit breaker set to the number of amperes you bought, and it trips
whenever you draw more than that, disconnecting you completely.** Those who cannot
pay the monthly bill are simply cut off.

**SO THERE ARE TWO KINDS OF CUT-OFF AND NEITHER HAS A SPEECH IN IT.**

    **THE PUNISHMENT.** Somebody at the generator throws a switch. The line dies.
    Nobody comes to tell you. You find out because the fan stops.

    **AND THE ONE I DID NOT EXPECT, WHICH IS BETTER: YOU CUT YOURSELF OFF.** A
    family living at its ampere limit trips its own breaker by turning on one
    thing too many. **The most common way the power goes out is your own kettle.**

That second one is a whole design in one sentence and it is real. A household that
buys five amperes has to decide, every evening, which two things run at once. The
argument about the kettle is not a small domestic scene, **it is the economy
arriving in a kitchen**, and it needs no antagonist in the room at all.

**AND IT ANSWERS THE ROW'S QUESTION DIRECTLY: how is a cut-off said? It is not.
Write the silence and write the next morning.**

## 4. THE COLLECTOR IS NOT THE OWNER, AND THAT IS THE CHARACTER
The man at your door is an employee. He does not set the price, he cannot give you
another week, and he is on the same street you are. He carries a book and he knows
what is in your kitchen because he was here last month.

**SO HE HAS EXACTLY THE SAME LINE THE FAMILY HAS: there is nothing he can do.** He
says it about himself and it is true, and it is also the oldest excuse there is,
and the family cannot tell which. That ambiguity is free and it is the best thing
available in this scene.

## 5. WHAT MAKES IT LAND WITHOUT A VILLAIN VOICE
Three things, and the first two come straight out of the testimony.

**ONE: THE POWER IS PUBLIC, SO THE THREAT NEVER HAS TO BE SPOKEN.** "Everyone knows
it, but there is nothing we can do." When both people in the room already know what
he can do, saying it out loud would be WEAKER, not stronger. The collector talks
about dates and amounts. The consequence sits in the room without being invited.

**TWO: HE IS RIGHT, AND HE KNOWS IT.** The craft answer to a menacing antagonist is
payoff plus conviction: he has to believe he is entirely justified. **The generator
owner genuinely provides the electricity.** Without him the block is dark. He is not
pretending. A collector who thinks he is the reason the lights work is far worse to
face than one who thinks he is a villain.

**THREE: AND THIS IS WHERE Q10 CASHES IN.** That round measured zero first-person
threats in the whole build, and the negotiation literature says the goal is to turn
your threats into warnings, because a threat costs the speaker his own freedom of
choice and a warning costs nothing. **The collector is the purest warning-speaker in
the game.** He never says "I will cut you off." He says what happens on Friday. Our
build's habit, which Q10 flagged as a five-times-repeated tell, is finally correct
in exactly one mouth, and the disclaimer must still never be spoken: he does not get
to say "that isn't a threat."

## 6. THE SPEC
1. **THE COLLECTOR TALKS LOGISTICS, NEVER CONSEQUENCES.** Dates, amounts, the book,
   what he can and cannot do. The consequence is the one thing he must not mention.
2. **HE HAS BEEN HERE BEFORE AND HE REMEMBERS.** He names something in the room.
   That is the whole of the menace and it costs one clause.
3. **THE CUT-OFF HAS NO LINE.** Write the second before and the morning after.
4. **THE FAMILY'S REFUSAL IS NEVER DEFIANCE, IT IS ARITHMETIC.** People who cannot
   pay do not make speeches, they show you the sum. Q2 measured what speech does
   under stress and Q14 measured that a hard question gets a short answer; both
   apply here and both say the same thing: shorter, flatter, more concrete.
5. **AND THE BILL IS A CHARACTER.** 44% of income, 88% for the poorest. When a
   number is that size it should be spoken as a fraction of a life, not a price.
