# HIS BLIND SPOTS: THE PERSON, NOT THE BOARD (coordinator, 9/6/26, asked for)
# "Before you continue, what do you think about any blind spots that I might be having?"

The 9/4 blind-spots answer was about the board (what nobody owned). This one is
about him, said plainly because he asked, in the order I think they cost him.

## 1. HE PLAYS THE GAME LESS THAN HE TYPES THE WORD
In this whole stretch he has played once. That one play found the biggest hole in
the game in five words ("where the enemies at") after nineteen chats and ~500
checkers had missed it for weeks. His eyes on the game are worth more than the
whole machine, and he uses them least. Every VAMILY is a round; a play is worth
ten rounds. He should play before he types, not after.

## 2. HE RULES ON WHAT HE SAW LAST, AND NEVER ON WHAT HE HAS NOT SEEN
Every ruling this stretch was about the last thing on his screen: the UI at 50%,
the zoom, the tutorial pop-up, the cloud. All correct. But the fold, the animal
opening, the perk tree, the reckoning card, the room fight, the room's own song --
none of those has had a word from him, because he has not opened them. Things he
does not look at drift. The lanes are building parts of his game he has never
seen, under my defaults, and he corrects only what crosses his screen.

## 3. HE KILLS THE METHOD AND THE QUESTION DIES WITH IT
Friend testing: dead, and rightly, it was not what he asked for. But the question
"can a stranger get through the first minute" is still open and now nobody is
allowed to find out. Hurt and death: killed in July, and the game had no stakes
for two months until I decided a default. When he kills something, the thing it
was for goes unanswered unless somebody notices. Somebody has to say "the method
is dead; what answers the question now?" That is my job and I did it late.

## 4. HE HAS NEVER SAID WHAT THE GAME SOUNDS LIKE
He has named the look twice (the runway; Fallout 1 for the interface) and the
city once (Las Vegas). He owns 200-plus songs and has one he likes. He has never
once said what Bohemia should SOUND like, the way he says what it should look
like. Sound is running on the chats' taste, which is the one thing he said the
chats do not get to have. That is the cheapest blind spot to close: one sentence.

## 5. EVERYTHING IS A THUMB, AND HE DOES NOT THUMB
He built the whole verdict system (judge pages, thumbs, comment boxes, sun mode),
then ruled that unjudged is dead. In this stretch the art director has judged 127
garments FOR him and he has thumbed zero. Art he would love may be dying unseen
under a rule he wrote. Either the judge pages are a thing he opens sometimes, or
they are not, and if not, the rule that kills the unjudged is killing blind.

## 6. HE POLISHES A GAME THAT IS FIFTEEN PERCENT
Half-size UI, three acts of interface, a cloud in the zoom, all real and all his
to call. But a player still cannot work, cannot be forgiven, and cannot find the
enemy in the crowd. He is ruling on how it looks before there is a loop to look
at. Not wrong, just early; the risk is that the look gets ruled three times as the
loop underneath it changes.

## 7. HE SEES EIGHTEEN CHATS THROUGH ONE
The one-word system works, and the price is that he knows what the fleet did only
through my summary. If I bury something, it is buried. He should assume I am
wrong sometimes and open a lane's own handoff once in a while, not to manage it,
just to catch me.

## WHAT I AM DOING ABOUT THE ONES I CAN
- A standing rule on this seat: every round names ONE thing in the game he has
  not opened, in plain words, with the tab, so it crosses his screen.
- EYES E16: a machine that lists what has shipped that he has never touched.
- The sound question goes to him as this round's one question.
