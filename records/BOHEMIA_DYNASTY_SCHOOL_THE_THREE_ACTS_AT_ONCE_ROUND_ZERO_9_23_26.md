## 1. THE GAMES THAT DID IT, SORTED BY HOW CLOSE THEY ARE TO HIS SENTENCE

### Closest: all eras open at once, flip at will, the past rewrites the future
- DAY OF THE TENTACLE (1993). Three people in one mansion, 200 years apart, in the past, the
  present and the future. After the opening you switch between them freely. A change in the
  past changes the present; a change in the present changes the future; small things travel
  between eras through a toilet, big things are just left in the past and picked up in the
  future. The famous chain: a medical chart sent to the past becomes the design of the flag,
  and the flag is picked up in the future as a disguise. THE LESSON FOR US: the flip is a
  button (the character's face), never a place; the world re-derives, the hands do not; every
  era has its own to-do list so no era is a waiting room.
- DARK CHRONICLE / DARK CLOUD 2 (2002). You rebuild ruined towns in the past with a city
  builder (Georama: buildings, people, plants), and each town has a list of requirements;
  meet them and the SAME place in the future is restored. You travel forward to see the
  result. THE LESSON: this is his sentence exactly, "the city is built like shit because you
  did not do enough in the past", as a building game with a future you can go look at. The
  future is a report card you can walk in. The requirement list is what makes it readable:
  the player knows what the future is waiting on.

### Two eras, instant flip, the same ground
- THE LEGEND OF ZELDA: OCARINA OF TIME (1998), HIS REFERENCE. Child and adult Link, seven
  years apart, the same map. The flip is a place (the sword's pedestal). Things done as the
  child pay off as the adult: a bean planted in soft soil is a platform seven years on; a
  song learned in one age opens a door in the other; a tree's skull tokens count across both.
  THE LESSON: the pay-off is PHYSICAL and IN PLACE (the bean is a plant standing where you
  planted it), never a number. The flip being a place is the part players remember as a
  chore; the pay-offs are the part they remember as magic.
- THE LEGEND OF ZELDA: ORACLE OF AGES (2001). A harp flips past and present anywhere. Plant a
  seed in the past, a tree stands in the present; dry a lake in the past, a road in the
  present. Same lesson, with the flip as an item, no walking.
- TITANFALL 2, "EFFECT AND CAUSE" (2016). A wrist device flips a ruined present and a working
  past instantly, mid-run, on one button. The level is two maps stacked; the flip nudges the
  player between them. Fights use it: flip to get behind an enemy. THE LESSON: an instant flip
  is a VERB in the fight, not just a menu.
- DISHONORED 2, "A CRACK IN THE SLAB" (2016). A handheld timepiece; a window on it shows the
  other era live before you flip. Three maps stacked; what you do to one man in the past
  changes which present you come back to. The studio built only one such level because every
  era is a whole extra map to build and fill. THE LESSON, THE COST: every era he can flip to
  is a whole world to keep alive on a phone. That is PLUMBER's row before it is anyone's.

### The past changes the future, but you travel, you do not flip
- CHRONO TRIGGER (1995). Gates between eras; a sapling planted in 600 AD is a forest in 1000
  AD; a ruined future you visit early is the reason to fix the past at all. THE LESSON, the
  one he said himself: SHOW THE BAD FUTURE FIRST. Chrono Trigger's ruined 2300 AD is the
  strongest motive in the game and it is shown in the first hours.
- RADIANT HISTORIA (2010). Two timelines, a book that flips you to any node you have reached,
  a visible tree of where you have been. THE LESSON: a visible timeline of nodes is how a
  player keeps three lives straight; the phone's feed (Paolo 9/4) is our version.
- SHADOW OF MEMORIES / SHADOW OF DESTINY (2001). Flip between 1580, 1902, 1980 and 2001 to
  change the present; the town changes each time. THE LESSON: the town is the display.
- TIME HOLLOW (2008). Dig a hole into the past, change one thing, the present rewrites around
  you while you watch. THE LESSON: let him SEE the rewrite happen, on screen, when he flips.

### Generations that inherit (the shape of our three acts, without the flip)
- DRAGON QUEST V (1992): child, adult, parent; the wife you pick decides your children. PHANTASY
  STAR III (1990): three generations, the bride decides the next hero. FIRE EMBLEM: GENEALOGY
  OF THE HOLY WAR (1996): who you pair in generation one decides who exists in generation
  two, with what skills. THE LESSON: the descendants' faces and gifts should come FROM the
  earlier act's choices; naming them at the start is fine, but what they look like and what
  they can do is earned. Our heredity already runs (A FAMILY LOOKS LIKE A FAMILY).
- CRUSADER KINGS, MASSIVE CHALICE, ROGUE LEGACY: play the heir, the house carries. Sequential,
  no flip; useful for what carries and what dies with a person.

### Two worlds side by side, one button (the flip without time)
- A LINK TO THE PAST (light/dark world, a mirror), SOUL REAVER (material/spectral), METROID
  PRIME 2. THE LESSON: the same ground drawn twice, and a puzzle is the difference between the
  two drawings.

## 2. WHAT EVERY ONE OF THEM AGREES ON (the rules I would bet on)
1. THE FLIP IS INSTANT AND IN YOUR HAND when it is fun (Tentacle, Titanfall, Dishonored, Oracle
   of Ages); it is a walk to a place when the designers needed to slow you (Ocarina). Our
   default: in the hand, on the phone.
2. THE PAY-OFF IS A THING STANDING WHERE YOU LEFT IT, not a stat. A bean is a plant. A town
   list met is a town standing. A flag is a flag.
3. SHOW THE BAD FUTURE EARLY. Every one of these games lets you see the ruin before you can
   fix it, and that is the motive for the whole game.
4. THE FUTURE RE-DERIVES, THE HANDS DO NOT. What you carry and what you have done in the
   future survives; the world around it is rebuilt from the past.
5. EVERY ERA NEEDS ITS OWN TO-DO. Day of the Tentacle keeps three lists alive so no era is
   empty; Dishonored's one level proves an era with nothing to do is dead weight.
6. THE COST IS REAL. Every flippable era is a whole map kept alive. Dishonored built one level.
   Tentacle got away with it because the mansion is one building. Our valley is 9,216 cells,
   three times. That is the first thing DYNASTY's school must price with PLUMBER.

## 4. THE HOLES DYNASTY'S SCHOOL MUST CLOSE (pull holes, per CLAUDE.md)
- If act 3 is the Angel era and it is derived from act 1's ledger, what is the FLOOR? A player
  who does nothing in act 1 must still have an act 3 that can be played, or the flip is a
  punishment screen. Dark Chronicle's answer: the ruined future is still a place with people
  and jobs, just a worse one.
- Does time run in an act while he is not in it? Tentacle: no, frozen. Ocarina: no. Our 120 BPM
  clock and the day cycle need a rule. Default to research: frozen when not looked at.
- What does the Animal (act 1) DO that seeds a city? DYNASTY Q1 (what you can do with no hands)
  is the start of that answer.
- Saving: three ledgers, one save.
