## 1. THE TENSION THIS ROW ARRIVES WITH, AND RESOLVING IT IS THE FINDING
Two earlier rounds appear to contradict each other on exactly this question:
- **Round 1**: the animal's verb set is eight things, **and not one of them needs
  a hand.** So the animal cannot play the human's game.
- **Round 10**: the rules **must not change** in the last act, because expertise
  is domain-specific and a new rule set makes a beginner of a master.
Both are right, and the resolution is that **THE TWO FOLDS ARE NOT THE SAME KIND
OF FOLD**, which nothing in this study has said before.
```
FOLD 1  ANIMAL -> HUMAN    the player has ~5 hours invested (round 1 measured
                           eight verbs as a five-hour game). Losing that is
                           CHEAP, and losing it is the strongest teaching
                           device the game will ever have.
FOLD 2  HUMAN  -> ANGEL    the player has ~90 hours invested. Losing that is
                           UNACCEPTABLE and the research says it cannot be
                           recovered inside one act.
```
> *** THE FIRST FOLD CHANGES THE VERBS. THE SECOND ONE MUST NOT. THAT IS NOT AN
> INCONSISTENCY, IT IS THE ONLY ARRANGEMENT THE LEARNING RESEARCH ALLOWS. ***

## 4. THE REAL AISLE -- WHAT HANDS ACTUALLY BUY, AND IT IS NOT STRENGTH
The distinctive human physical act is **throwing**, and the measurement is
striking. Only humans regularly throw projectiles with high speed and accuracy;
some primates throw occasionally and their anatomy is a compromise that cannot do
both parts of the motion. The mechanism is **elastic energy stored in the
tendons, ligaments and muscles crossing the shoulder**, released to power the arm.
> **IT IS THE FASTEST MOTION THE HUMAN BODY PRODUCES**, and the anatomy for it
> appears together about two million years ago.
So the human's signature is not being stronger than an animal. It is **acting at
a distance, with an object.** In our list that is `shot`, and it is the verb the
animal most obviously cannot have.
### AND THE ANIMAL'S SIGNATURE, FROM ROUND 1, IS THE MIRROR OF IT
A tracking dog reads which way somebody went from three to five consecutive
footprints, by comparing how strong the smell is between them, because scent
decays. **A track has a direction because it has an age.**
> *** THE ANIMAL KNOWS THE PAST. THE HUMAN REACHES THE DISTANCE. THE ANGEL SEES
> THE WHOLE THING AND CAN TOUCH NONE OF IT. ***
That is the three arcs as ways of playing, in one line, and every third of it is
measured rather than invented.

## 6. *** THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG ***
The instinct behind "three ways of playing" is three verb sets: an animal game, a
human game, an angel game. **Measured against our own code and the learning
research, that is one change too many.**
> **THERE ARE THREE GENERATIONS AND ONLY ONE VERB CHANGE.**
And it lands in the place nobody would have put it: **at the FIRST fold, the
cheap one, five hours in, not at the dramatic one.** The most structurally
violent moment in a hundred-hour game is the one the story treats as a prologue
ending, and the moment that looks like a transformation is the one that must
change nothing at all.
### AND ONE MORE THING THE SIDE-BY-SIDE SHOWS
Seven of the animal's eight verbs map onto a human verb we have already built.
**The animal generation is not a separate game. It is our game with the nose
switched on and the hands switched off.** That is a far smaller build than
"design gen 1", and it is the strongest argument this lane has produced for gen 1
being buildable at all.
