## THE SHORT VERSION
**Take the state away and people do not talk rougher. They talk more carefully.
Where nobody can recover what is stolen from you, courtesy is armour, a fixed
saying is the only place a rule can be kept, violence gets named sideways, and the
first question anybody asks you is your name and whose you are. Act one should be
the most formal-sounding of our three eras, not the least. Measured against 617
films, our own text is currently six standard deviations UNDER the law-abiding
baseline on exactly the courtesy this era should have most of.**

## 1. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG, AND THE GENRE ADVICE MANUFACTURES THE CLICHE
The instinct in the phrase "instinct before rules" is to write act one feral: short,
blunt, stripped, threat-first. The standard craft advice for this genre says exactly
that in as many words. A representative guide: "the easiest way to create dialogue
is to write it and then cut all the unnecessary words," which it says "naturally
creates the kind of terse, economical speech that lawless characters would use." The
same body of advice lists "the strong, silent, gruff, cowboy protagonist" as the
genre's most tired cliche. It does not notice that its own technique is the machine
that builds that cliche. Cut every unnecessary word from every character and you have
not written a lawless world, you have written one man twelve times.

The real record goes the other way, and it is measured rather than asserted.

**The keystone measurement.** Cohen, Nisbett, Bowdle and Schwarz ran an experimental
ethnography on the American South, the classic case of a place whose people relied on
an honour code because the state could not reach them. A large man walked down a
narrow corridor straight at the subject, and they recorded how close he got before
the subject gave way:
- **Northerners: about 1.4 m (5 feet), insulted or not.** The law-culture baseline.
- **Southerners who had NOT been insulted: 2.7 m.** They yielded at nearly DOUBLE
  the distance. That is the default, and the default is extreme deference.
- **Southerners who HAD been insulted: 0.94 m.** Once the switch flips they go
  closer than anybody in the study.

One population, one experiment, both ends of the range. The polite end is the
resting state. The authors' own reading is the important part for a writer: southern
politeness is a response to southern aggression, because a place where people are
more likely to TAKE offence is a place where you had better not GIVE any.

**And the mechanism is our own locked economy.** The literature is consistent about
where honour cultures come from: herding societies, where "a family's wealth and
livelihood could be stolen from them in one fell swoop," wealth that is portable and
vulnerable rather than rooted in the ground, in places where "the governing body is
weak or nonexistent and thus cannot prevent or punish theft." That is BATTERIES ARE
THE MONEY (Paolo 9/4) plus no state, which is act one exactly as it is already
locked. Our own economy predicts this register. We did not have to choose it.

**One more word, from the source.** Elijah Anderson studied the American place that
trusts formal law least and called what he found an ETIQUETTE: "a powerful and
pervasive form of etiquette governing how people learn to negotiate public spaces,"
built on respect, which is "almost an external entity that is hard-won but easily
lost, and so must constantly be guarded." Etiquette is the word the evidence chose.

## 2. WHAT PEOPLE STOP SAYING, AND WHAT COMES IN ITS PLACE. SIX CHANGES.

**2.1 THE WHOLE VOCABULARY OF RECOURSE DIES.** Every sentence that hands the
consequence to a third party stops working: I will call the police, that is illegal,
I will take you to court, I will report you, I have rights, I will have your licence.
None of it is available, because there is nobody at the other end of it. This is the
single largest deletion, and it is subtractive, which makes it easy to get right and
easy to forget. The consequence now has to sit inside the sentence or it does not
exist.

**2.2 THE FIRST QUESTION IS WHO YOU ARE AND WHOSE YOU ARE.** In Lebanon the identity
card's religion line at a militia checkpoint could kill you, and the reported magic
words at one were a name and a home town: "My name is Omar and I am from Beirut."
Not what do you want. Not where are you going. Who are you, and whose. In this era a name
works as a document rather than a courtesy, and handing one over is a transaction
with a price. Our own build already understands half of this and does not use it.

**2.3 THE FAMILY ANSWERS, NOT THE PERSON.** Under Somali xeer, elders "pay and
collect diya (blood money) from clan members" when one of theirs kills one of
another's, with compensation collective by principle. Under the Albanian Kanun a
besa is generational: your father's pledge becomes yours. So a threat is aimed past
the person at their house, a settlement is spoken by an elder on their behalf, and
the sentence "this is between you and me" is a thing only a law culture can say.

**2.4 A RULE HAS TO LIVE INSIDE A FIXED PHRASE.** Xeer is oral, "passed down orally
from one generation to the next," with "oral codifications memorized in poetry and
proverbs." The Kanun was transmitted verbally for centuries. This is not colour, it
is storage: with nothing written down, the only durable container for a rule is a
sentence built to be repeated word for word. So lawless speech is thick with
formulas, and quoting one is how a person cites the law at you. The improvised,
every-line-fresh register the genre advice produces is the one thing a place with no
writing cannot afford.

**2.5 VIOLENCE GETS NAMED SIDEWAYS, NOT PLAINLY.** Gambetta's study of criminal
communication is entirely about how people identify and verify trustworthiness when
no contract can be enforced, and why they stay cryptic while doing it. Two forces
push the same way. Words become evidence, and face has to be protected because an
escalation cannot be un-escalated. The result is euphemism as the default register
for the worst things. A blunt threat is not the tough version, it is the version
where somebody has already decided to go through with it.

**2.6 ORDINARY PEOPLE BORROW THE CRIMINALS' VOCABULARY WHOLESALE.** Russia in the
1990s is the clean case. The rise in crime pushed criminal slang from a marginal
space into general use, and "to live by ponyatiya," originally prison speech for the
convicts' own code, took on the broad meaning of living by unwritten but generally
accepted rules. Halliday's 1976 paper on anti-languages gives the mechanism: a
society organised against the dominant order relexicalises, and specifically
OVERLEXICALISES, the areas it cares about most, generating many words for one thing
in exactly those places. When the state goes, the anti-language stops being anti. It
just becomes how the valley talks, and the overlexicalised areas are the map of what
the valley is frightened of.

## 3. WHAT A PROMISE SOUNDS LIKE WHEN NOTHING ENFORCES IT
The row asks this directly, and the best-documented answer is the Albanian besa. The
word is close to untranslatable; the nearest English is "to keep the promise," and
it sits somewhere between a sworn oath, a binding promise, protection and faith. In
the Kanun it is described as **the highest authority, and without besa the Kanun has
no enforcement mechanism at all.** The whole legal order of a stateless mountain
society rests on it.

Three properties matter for writing:
- **The cost is the family's name, not a fine.** Breaking it is a permanent stain on
  the house, not a penalty with a number.
- **It is generational.** Your father's pledge becomes yours.
- **It is a status as much as an act.** A man of besa means a man you can trust your
  life and family to, so giving one both binds you and claims something.

The generational part is a gift to a game built on three inherited generations. **A
promise given in act one can still be owed in act two by somebody who never made
it.** That is a mechanic rather than a line, and a line can carry it.

## 4. THE GAMES HALF, WRITTEN WITH NO BORROWED NAME ATTACHED
I may not cite a reference game Paolo has not named, and the ones he HAS named belong
to other departments (combat, interface, sound, campaign, city transition), so citing
any of them here would be the same violation as inventing one
(laws/BOHEMIA_ADDENDUM_A_REFERENCE_GAME_BELONGS_TO_ONE_DEPARTMENT_9_5_26.md). So the
craft half is stated as mechanics in plain words:
- **A greeting that works as a password.** The safe-conduct phrase is real behaviour
  (2.2) and it is also a clean gate: know it and the encounter opens, miss it and it
  does not.
- **Asking the name before the business.** An encounter that opens by pricing your
  identity teaches the world faster than any explainer.
- **Options gated by WHO you are, not what you know.** In a law culture a player
  unlocks lines with information. In an honour culture they unlock with standing.
- **A promise the game remembers across a generation.** Rare and expensive, per 3.
- **The failure to avoid** is threat-first writing, where every hard character opens
  at maximum. It flattens a cast into one voice, and the measurement in 1 says it is
  also simply wrong about people.

**READING THE HITS BY HAND CHANGED TWO OF THESE NUMBERS, which is why the rule is
print the hits and never just the rate.**
- **A BROKEN RULER, CAUGHT AND FIXED.** The first address-term pass returned 134 of
  our lines and -8.6 sd. Reading the hits, the top one was "Easy, easy. Don't touch
  that, it's live." The pattern `\bdon\b` matches inside "don't", because an
  apostrophe is a word boundary. It was inflating both sides. Removed, and the honest
  figure is the 16 above.
- **AND 16 IS STILL TOO KIND.** Reading all 16, most are REFERENTIAL, not spoken to
  anybody: "my brother," "my sister," "the checkpoint boss." Genuine address to a
  person's face is about five lines in 2,442: one "hermano," one "boss," and three
  "friend."

Three more counts, hits read:
- **THE VOCABULARY OF RECOURSE: 4 lines in 2,442, and all four are one argument
  about whether taking lake water is legal.** We have already, by instinct, written
  a world with no recourse in its mouth. That deletion is done.
- **NOT ONE LINE ASKS THE CHECKPOINT QUESTION.** Zero lines contain "who's asking,"
  "where are you from," or "whose block." The eleven name lines are all about a name
  being EARNED over time ("That's twice. Three times and I'll learn your name"),
  which is good and is a different thing. In a valley made entirely of faction
  ground with checkpoints in its own fiction, nobody is ever stopped and priced.
- **EUPHEMISM: 10 lines, and they are the strongest thing in this measurement.**
  "There's water till Thursday. After Thursday there's a conversation." "A
  conversation. A polite one, usually in daylight, usually with somebody who doesn't
  raise their voice." That is finding 2.5 already on the page. It is our proof the
  register works in our own voice.

**THE SUMMARY OF THE MEASUREMENT.** We got the subtraction right and the addition
wrong. The law words are gone, the family is already in the sentence, and the
euphemism is excellent. What is missing is everything the real record says fills the
hole: the courtesy, the address, the oath, the fixed saying, and the name at the
border. Six standard deviations under a film baseline is not a style choice, and the
era that should carry the most of it is the one we call the animal era.

## 6. WHAT SCHOOL LEAVES ME HOLDING FOR ROUND TWO
1. **ACT ONE IS THE MOST FORMAL OF THE THREE ERAS.** Courtesy is armour, and the
   default is exaggerated deference with a switch behind it.
2. **THE RECOURSE WORDS ARE GONE AND WE ALREADY DID THAT.** The consequence lives
   inside the sentence or nowhere.
3. **THE NAME AND THE HOUSE ARE THE PASSWORD, AND NOBODY IN OUR BUILD ASKS FOR
   THEM.** The first question is who are you and whose are you.
4. **A RULE ARRIVES AS A FIXED PHRASE**, because a repeatable sentence is the only
   storage a place with no paper has.
5. **THE WORST THINGS ARE SAID SIDEWAYS**, and we can already do this.
6. **A PROMISE IS A BESA: it costs the family's name and it outlives the man who
   gave it**, which is the one finding here that reaches all the way to act two.

AND THE TEST FOR ROUND TWO, which the row sets: fifteen act-one lines that would
sound WRONG in act two. Findings 1, 3, 4 and 6 give the wrongness a reason. A line
is act-one if act two could answer it with an institution.
