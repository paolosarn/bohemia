# BOHEMIA COORDINATOR SCHOOL, ROUND 8 OF 10: ONE LOOK ACROSS MANY HANDS (10/10/26)

## THE QUESTION
How do the best art directors and pipelines keep one look when many artists and many purchased packs feed the same game, and how does a coordinator make that enforceable rather than hoped for? Bohemia has five art lanes, 87 purchased HD tile packs with 1,927 tiles Paolo approved, and his complaint that the art we made looks nothing like the art we bought. The coordinator's answer so far is THE REFERENCE TWIN (rules 82, 82a, 87). This round asks what the twin is still missing. Source note: the search budget ran out mid-round and most sites could not be fetched from this machine, so each finding rests on the search summaries; Slynyrd's Pixelblog and the Overhype dev blog archive could not be reached and are owed reading.

## WHAT I FOUND
1. A usable style guide is short and numeric: a palette with hex values and roles, character proportions, an outline policy, a shading model, and the pixel density the assets target. Source: Bugnet, "How to write an art style guide", https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-write-an-art-style-guide. Why it matters here: our twin record names a bar (dark outline with a lit edge, 30 colours per 1000 px, authored wear, a shadow) but it lives in one record, not in a one-page guide every cook reads before drawing.
2. The most useful page of a guide is the do/don't page: near-misses annotated, because boundaries teach better than prose. Source: same Bugnet article. Why it matters here: the ground twin sheet already has three near-misses (flat stamp, no dark line, repeated square); that sheet should become the first page of the guide, not a one-round exhibit.
3. Every published art bible studied specifies a light direction with the ambiguity spelled out. Source: Makko, "What four public game art bibles actually say", https://blog.makko.ai/what-public-game-art-bibles-say/. Why it matters here: the packs were painted with one light; a cook that picks its own light will never match them, and no gate checks light today.
4. Outline rules are binary and never mixed: always 1 px dark, or never any, across the whole game. Source: Sprite AI, "2D pixel art style guide", https://www.sprite-ai.art/blog/2d-pixel-art-style-guide. Why it matters here: the twin sheet measured our dark line share at 0.013 to 0.040 against the pack's 0.050 to 0.074; the pack chose outlines, so the game has outlines, everywhere, with no cook exempt.
5. The unification pass for bought assets has an order: source from one artist or pack series first, recolour and retexture to the project palette, normalise detail density, then light everything under the game's own lighting and colour-grade. Recolouring is "an afternoon per pack with batch tools". Source: Bugnet, "How to avoid the asset store look", https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-avoid-the-asset-store-look. Why it matters here: we did the opposite, cooking fresh beside the packs; the fix is a pack-first pass with a batch palette tool, not more drawing.
6. Players notice three things in stitched asset art: different texel (pixel) densities, clashing levels of stylisation, and lighting baked for different conditions. Source: same Bugnet article; a Goblin Factory postmortem reports "textures clashed, poly counts varied wildly" after mixing sources, via https://www.wayline.io/blog/asset-packs-vs-kitbashing. Why it matters here: those are the three numbers the twin sheet should print on every piece: pixel density, edge detail, light direction.
7. One engine-level grade over everything is the last unifying step (Unity's Post-Processing Stack, Unreal's Post Process Volumes). Source: https://www.wayline.io/blog/unity-asset-pack-visual-consistency. Why it matters here: analog horror stays at the source, never a filter, but one shared final grade over pack and cook alike is how pros hide the seam; it is a tool, not the look.
8. Battle Brothers is a one-hand game: creative director Paul Taaks "takes care of everything visual", including art direction, UI design, illustrations and concept art. Source: Bilbao Games Conference speaker page, https://en.bilbaogamesconference.com/paul-taaks/. Why it matters here: the reference Paolo names most was never a committee; our five cooks must behave like one hand, which means one person (DIRECTION) holds the pen on every verdict.
9. Overhype redrew its own game to stay consistent. Dev Blog 88 says the style moved from a light-hearted comic approach to a more serious and gritty one, the team "redid most of the assets to raise quality", and a new key visual was needed because "the character looks don't match our ingame assets anymore". Source: https://www.moddb.com/news/dev-blog-88-the-key-visual. Why it matters here: the makers of our reference game threw out their own early art when it drifted; our rule 87 revamp is the same move and should be said that way to the lanes, not as a punishment.
10. Paint-overs are the lead's native tool. A DICE art director listing says written or verbal notes work "but visual sketches, paint-overs or mock-ups are even better", and the director reviews the in-game experience with the other directors and leads. Source: job listing, https://vakanser.se/jobb/art+director+140/. Why it matters here: DIRECTION's "pass or back with three plain words" is weaker than a paint-over; a back should come with the corrected pixels, or a crop of the pack tile that shows the fix.
11. Riot's card art role includes "checking artwork for consistency, polish, and fidelity to the League of Legends IP" and holding "a consistent quality bar". Source: Riot Games listing, https://www.riotgames.com/en/j/8138239. Why it matters here: consistency review is a named job with a named bar at big studios; it is not something every artist self-checks.
12. Pixel Logic (Michafrar, 242 pages, "Images > Text") covers line art, anti-aliasing, colours, readability, dithering and subpixeling; a cluster is a group of same-coloured pixels forming a shape and messy clusters make an image hard to read. Sources: https://michafrar.gumroad.com/l/pixel-logic and https://80.lv/articles/pixel-logic-a-pixel-art-tutorial-book/. Why it matters here: the pixel bible gate should name clusters and banding by their names so a cook is held to a craft rule, not a vibe.
13. Pedro Medeiros publishes his pixel-art rules as animated GIFs, including entries on resizing pixel art and on anti-alias and banding. Source: Creative Bloq, https://creativebloq.com/news/artist-makes-animated-gif-tutorials-to-teach-you-pixel-art (the GIFs themselves are on his Patreon and the Studio Miniboss blog). Why it matters here: the rule he teaches on resizing is the one we broke most: scale pixel art only by whole numbers and never mix pixel sizes in one frame.
14. One dithering style per piece, ordered or error-diffusion, never both; dither everywhere and the art "starts looking noisy and muddy". Source: Divoom, https://divoom.com/blogs/setup-ideas/pixel-art-dithering-when-to-use-and-stop. Why it matters here: the packs are painted, not dithered; a cook that dithers reads as a different hand instantly.
15. Consistency can be measured, not just eyeballed: compare hue and saturation histograms with Earth Mover's Distance, read luminance polarity from the median and the darkest 10 percent, use SSIM for structure (1.0 is identical), and validate palettes mechanically (PixelPal has a `validate` command that fails any image using colours outside a given palette and a `convert` that remaps onto it). Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.19918, https://imagecompressor.com/blog/ssim-binary-search, https://github.com/vulpivia/pixelpal. Why it matters here: the twin sheet already prints four numbers; these are the next three, and the palette validate is the one gate that cannot be argued with.

## WHAT THE BEST DO THAT WE DON'T
- They write the look down on one page with numbers before anyone draws. We have the bar scattered across a record, a law and a gate; no cook can recite the light direction, the outline rule or the pixel density of the packs because nobody extracted them.
- They start from the bought asset and unify it; we start from a blank canvas and hope it lands near the bought asset. Five lanes cooked fresh roads, props and boards while 1,927 approved tiles sat in text banks nobody's tool read.
- They have one hand on the pen. Overhype is one visual director; Supergiant is one art director. We have five cooks and one director who sees the work after it exists. The director should pick the pack twin and the crop BEFORE the cook draws, not grade afterwards.
- They give back pixels, not words. A back from DIRECTION is three words; a lead at DICE paints over. Three words cannot show a lit edge.
- They measure the seam. We measure colours and edges on one sheet, once. Nobody prints pixel density, light direction or a palette-validate result per piece per round, so drift is found by Paolo's eye, which is the most expensive detector we own.
- They re-grade everything at the end under one light. We have an analog horror bible at the source and nothing at the end, so pack tiles and cooked tiles never pass through the same final hand.

## THE CHANGES FOR THE COORDINATOR
1. THE ONE-PAGE LOOK CARD. DIRECTION writes one page, extracted from the approved packs by measurement: palette (hex, pulled from the 1,927), light direction (measured from pack shadows), outline rule (dark line with lit edge, always), pixel density (the pack's own pixels, whole-number scaling only), material rule (authored wear, no speckle, no dither), colour floor (30 per 1000 px). Test: every cook record this round cites the card by rule number, and a cook that cites a value not on the card is red.
2. TWIN BEFORE THE COOK, NOT AFTER. Every cook job names its pack twin (bank, pack, index) in the job line before drawing; a job with no twin named does not start unless the record says the pack lacks it. Test: PLUMBER's pack gate reads the job line and the tool's banks_used; a tool with no twin read is red.
3. THE THREE NUMBERS ON EVERY PIECE. The twin sheet tool prints, per piece, beside the pack's number: colours per 1000 px, edge detail, dark line share, saturation, and now pixel density, light direction (shadow vector) and a palette-validate pass or fail against the pack palette. Test: a piece more than 25 percent off its twin on any number cannot enter VOTE; the sheet is in the suite.
4. A BACK IS A PAINT-OVER. When DIRECTION sends a piece back, the back carries either a paint-over at the same pixels or a crop of the pack tile with the difference boxed. Test: a back with words only is counted as no review by the cook-every-round gate.
5. ONE FINAL GRADE, MEASURED. One shared colour grade runs over pack tiles and cooked tiles alike at the end of the pipeline, set once by DIRECTION from the analog horror bible, so no piece ever ships ungraded next to a graded one. Test: the demo's fight board, settlement picture and map stop each pass through the same grade function, and the suite fails if any surface draws from an ungraded buffer.

## ONE RULE
THE LOOK IS A NUMBER BEFORE IT IS A DRAWING. Nobody draws until the pack twin is named and its numbers are on the card, and nothing reaches the vote until its own numbers sit beside the twin's and match.

## FOR PAOLO, IN PLAIN WORDS
The best studios write the look down as a few numbers on one page before anyone draws, and they start from the art they bought instead of drawing beside it. We did it backwards, so our stuff never matched your packs. From now on every piece starts from a pack tile you approved, gets measured against it, and only goes to your vote when the numbers match.
