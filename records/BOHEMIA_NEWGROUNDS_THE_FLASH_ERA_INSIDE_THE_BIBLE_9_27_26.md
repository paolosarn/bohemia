## 1. WHAT THE ERA WAS (the page, no titles)
The early-2000s flash portal was HAND-MADE WEB ANIMATION: one person or
three, drawing, animating, scoring and coding a thing in a bedroom and
posting it the same week. Its look and sound, as a craft:
1. HAND-MADE AND PROUD. Uneven lines, visible construction, the
   author's hand everywhere. Imperfection was not a defect, it was the
   SIGNATURE - you could tell who made a thing at a glance.
2. SPRITE-AND-TWEEN MOTION. Bodies were puppets of a few parts; motion
   came from moving and rotating pieces between HELD POSES, snappy
   eases, 12 to 24 frames a second, anticipation exaggerated because
   frames were expensive. Cheap was a style, not a shortage.
3. LOUD. Big silhouettes, thick outlines, saturated accents on flat
   fills, chunky buttons, gradients everywhere the author could put
   one. Everything read at a glance because nothing was subtle.
4. CRUDE ON PURPOSE. Fearless, juvenile, violent, funny; low fidelity
   worn as a badge. The energy said: made by a person, not a company.
5. THE SOUND: clipped music loops, doorbell-quality effects, voices
   recorded on a desk mic an inch too close. Rough, present, HUMAN.
6. SMALL-TEAM CONSISTENCY: because one author did everything, a piece
   was coherent all the way through - art, motion and sound agreed.

## 2. THE CUT: WHAT SURVIVES INSIDE THE BIBLE, WHAT DIES (like FFX 9/20)
SURVIVES, and where it lands:
- THE HAND-MADE SIGNATURE -> the grime machine and the craft laws.
  Wear drawn by a hand at bake time IS the era's mark (R10 already
  bans the shader); a tile whose damage has authorship beats a clean
  one. "Put more life into it" means MORE HAND, never more filter.
- PUPPET MOTION WITH HELD POSES -> the rig and the envelope law. Our
  animation is already sprite-and-tween at twelve keys a bar; the era
  BLESSES the held pose and the snappy ease (and R3's long hold makes
  stillness content, which the flash era understood better than any
  studio: holding a pose was free, so poses were WORTH holding).
- LOUD IN SILHOUETTE -> the runway poles and the contrast rule. Loud
  lives in SHAPE and the one accent (card 2B's two-channel rule), a
  big pose readable at arm's length - never in flooding the palette.
- THE DESK-MIC VOICE -> SOUNDS' lane, one line: a voice recorded too
  close by a person, not a studio, fits the broadcast premise exactly
  (the announcement was made by somebody, and you can hear the room).
  Their [analog horror sound] row owns the execution.
- SMALL-TEAM CONSISTENCY -> the bible itself: one taste written down,
  every lane cooking to it, is the era's production truth at our size.
DIES, and why:
- THICK OUTLINES: our corpus is pixel-native with no outline language
  (style card 2C); an outline pass would be a costume.
- GRADIENT ABUSE: R4, no mood gradient, ever. The era's gradients are
  exactly the lens the bible bans.
- COMEDY GORE AND THE JUVENILE REGISTER: rule 20b (not a monster) and
  20g (fun carries the tie, but the tone is dread, not splatter).
- CHUNKY GLOSSY WEB CHROME: the no-slop object law and the act cards
  own the UI; a shiny replay button is the vibe-coding tell with a
  2003 haircut. (Our loading door already does the era's PRELOADER
  one better: it is diegetic - the dead utility's console, not a web
  page's spinner.)
