## THE LAW (rule 348)
1. EVERY FULL-SCREEN VIEW LIKE THIS IS MADE TWICE: one tall (9:16) and one wide (16:9), each its own composition (never one picture
   cropped or squeezed into the other), with enough picture past the edges that it FILLS any real screen (a tall phone, a laptop) with no
   black bars and no stretch. It switches the moment the phone turns or the window changes, with a cross-fade, never a jump (rule 324).
2. THE START SCREEN SHOWS FOUR THINGS ONLY: the word BOHEMIA, under it POST ECONOMIC APOCALYPSE, and NEW GAME and CONTINUE. Nothing else:
   no settings (you reach the settings once you are playing), no version text the player can see, no other button.
3. THE VIEW IS FIRST PERSON: you are looking through the camera itself, immersive, the analog horror Crashbox HBO series feeling at the
   source (rules 301, 342), never a filter laid on top.
4. QUIET: the feed's sound sits low under everything (his 9/21 word on the room tone: 'very, very low').
5. WHICH FEED (his 'slideshow, or a random roll each time you open it, I'm not sure'; the coordinator's default, his to change): a random
   feed each time the game opens, never the same twice running; and if you sit on the start screen, it cuts to the next camera after
   about 40 seconds the way a real security monitor cycles its cameras (the cut itself is part of the horror: a roll, a frame of static,
   the next room). Both of his ideas at once, and it is what a real monitor does.
6. HIS STAR STANDS: this is the thing he starred; the work is to make it fit, not to make it different.
