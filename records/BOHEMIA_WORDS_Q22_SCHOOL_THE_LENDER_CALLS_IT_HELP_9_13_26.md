## THE SHORT VERSION
**A lender never uses the word debt. He uses the word help, and the threat is the
sentence he leaves out. The one real field experiment on reminder wording found that
using the person's NAME moves payment and that moral appeals do nothing at all,
which is the opposite of every instinct for a card like this. WORLD already got the
naming right by instinct. And the measurement that matters most is not about words:
the debt card lives on the walked city, the interface word book is harvested from
the demo, so THE CARD'S WORDS HAVE NEVER BEEN READ BY ANY CHECKER THIS LANE OWNS.**

## 1. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG, AND IT HAS A CONTROL GROUP
The instinct for a card that lists who you owe is to make it mean something: to
remind the player that people are counting on them, that this is how the block
holds together, that it is only fair. That is a moral appeal, and it is the one
thing that has been tested properly here and failed.

Saulitis (2024), in Digital Health, ran a field experiment with a debt collection
agency in Latvia on **9,196 individuals with unpaid hospital bills**. Reminders were
randomly assigned by text and email, comparing a generic reminder against a
**personalised** message carrying the recipient's name, against messages appealing
to **social norms**, against messages appealing to **contribution to a public good**.

- **Personalised messages, the ones with the person's name in them, significantly
  improved payment against the generic reminder.**
- **The social-norm and public-good appeals did not significantly move payment at
  all.**

So the thing that works is being addressed as a specific person, and the thing that
does nothing is the moral frame a writer reaches for first.

**AND WORLD ALREADY GOT THIS RIGHT WITHOUT THE STUDY.** The card's own comment says
"One sentence per row, NAMING THE LENDER, which is the whole row." Every line on it
opens with who. That is the finding, already shipped, and this round's job is not to
take credit for it but to avoid undoing it and to say plainly that the naming is the
load-bearing part rather than a formatting choice.

## 2. WHAT THEY NEVER SAY OUT LOUD, WHICH IS TWO THINGS
**2.1 THEY NEVER SAY DEBT. THEY SAY HELP.** The ethnographic literature on informal
moneylending describes lenders who straddle the formal and the local: they will
accept the paperwork of the formal system while using **the language of community
help and trust** in their actual dealings. The relationship is narrated as help
given, never as credit extended. This matters for a card because the LEDGER'S word
and the LENDER'S word are different words for the same event, and which one the card
uses decides whose side the card is on.

**2.2 THEY NEVER NAME THE CONSEQUENCE.** A protection demand is phrased as payment
for protection from unspecified other parties, where the protector is himself the
threat, and the legal literature is explicit that vague intimation is the normal
form and that a covert threat is still a threat. Two forces produce this: a named
threat is evidence, and a named threat is also a LIMIT, because once you have said
what will happen you have promised not to do worse. **An unstated consequence is
unbounded, and it costs nothing to leave it unstated.**

The Lebanese generator case is the same shape from the household side. Residents
describe dreading the monthly visit of the bill collector, and one Achrafieh
resident's words are the whole relationship: **"The generator owner is a thug.
Everyone knows it, but there is nothing we can do. He makes his own law in full view
of everyone."** Nobody is quoting a threat, because none was made. Bills ran from
roughly 300 to 1,000 dollars and were estimated at 44% of average household income
in 2023, and 88% for the poorest households, and the collector is often a relative
sent round. The pressure is total and the language is administrative.

## 3. HOW A SECOND REMINDER DIFFERS FROM A FIRST, AND THIS IS THE WEAK PART
The row asks this directly and **I could not find a good source for it, so it is
written here as a derivation rather than as a finding, and the next round should
treat it as the least supported thing in this record.** What the evidence in 2.2
supports is a direction: if the first reminder works by leaving the consequence
unstated, then escalation is movement toward SPECIFICITY. Not volume, not threat
vocabulary. The second reminder knows more: a date, a place, a name, a thing the
lender has noticed. It does not get louder, it gets closer.

## 4. A CARD IS NOT A PERSON, AND THAT IS THE REAL CRAFT QUESTION
Everything above is about somebody speaking. The row's actual subject is a LEDGER,
and a ledger has no face, cannot be interrupted, cannot time its arrival and cannot
be argued with. So the question a writer has to answer first is **whose voice the
card is in**, and there are only three answers:
- **THE LENDER'S.** Then it says help (2.1) and it is warm and it is the most
  frightening version, because the warmth is the mechanism.
- **THE PLAYER'S OWN RECKONING.** Then it is a man counting at the end of a day, and
  it can be honest in a way no lender would be.
- **NOBODY'S.** A flat administrative voice. This is what most interfaces default to
  and it is the only one of the three with no point of view, which in a game about
  who owes whom is the one choice that says nothing.

The card today is mostly the third with flashes of the second, and that is the
finding round two has to act on.

**5.2 THE THREE SHIPPED ATTEMPTS, READ AGAINST THE FINDINGS.** The card builds one
sentence per row:
- favour: `<WHO> gave you something for nothing, <once/twice/N times>. Nobody has
  mentioned it yet.`
- loan: `<WHO> lent you a battery on a handshake. You pay one back a night.`
- rent: `<WHO> let you stay on their ground without paying, <times>.`
under the headers `YOU OWE THEM`, `THEY LENT YOU BATTERIES` and `YOU WENT SHORT ON
THEM`.

**WHAT IS ALREADY RIGHT, and it is most of it.** Every row opens with the lender's
name (finding 1). No amount anywhere, only a count, which is his ruling and happens
to match the register. And **"Nobody has mentioned it yet" is finding 2.2 exactly**:
the consequence is unstated, and the sentence is more frightening for it than any
threat would be. That line is the best thing on the card.

**WHAT THE FINDINGS ARGUE WITH.** The headers are second-person and accusatory, and
"gave you something for nothing" is the ledger's honest word for it, not the
lender's. No real lender describes his own help that way (2.1). The card therefore
speaks in nobody's voice while using a scolding register, which is the combination
section 4 calls the one choice with no point of view.

**5.3 AND THE WORDS THE LENDER WOULD USE ARE ABSENT FROM THE WHOLE CORPUS.** Zero of
3,093 spoken lines describe a loan or a favour as having helped somebody out. Two
lines in 3,093 carry anything like a moral appeal, and reading both, neither is
about money. So we have neither the register that works nor the one that fails: the
vocabulary simply is not there yet.

## 6. WHERE THE STANDARD ADVICE IS WRONG
Search the craft of collection wording and the top results are collections-industry
content marketing offering confident numbers: respectful language boosts repayment
"by up to 30%", aggressive wording drops it "15 to 20%", empathy workshops produced
"20% higher" recovery. **None of those carries a study behind it that I could
reach**, and they sit beside the one real experiment whose finding is narrower and
more surprising: the name works, the moral appeal does nothing. The advice is not
wrong about being decent. It is wrong in the way it is usually wrong, by attaching
invented precision to an agreeable idea.

## 7. WHAT SCHOOL LEAVES ME HOLDING FOR ROUND TWO
1. **THE NAME IS THE LOAD-BEARING PART**, with a control group behind it. Never
   trade a name for a category.
2. **THE MORAL APPEAL DOES NOTHING.** No line on this card tells the player what
   kind of person this makes them.
3. **A LENDER SAYS HELP, NEVER DEBT**, and the warmth is the mechanism.
4. **THE CONSEQUENCE STAYS UNSAID.** Unbounded is stronger than stated, and the card
   already has one line proving it.
5. **ESCALATION IS SPECIFICITY, NOT VOLUME** (weakly sourced, flagged as such): the
   second reminder knows a date, a place or a thing that was noticed.
6. **A CARD HAS TO PICK A VOICE.** Lender, or the player's own reckoning, or nobody.
   Today it is nobody in a scolding register.
7. **AND THE CARD IS UNCHECKED TEXT**, which is a job and not a line.
