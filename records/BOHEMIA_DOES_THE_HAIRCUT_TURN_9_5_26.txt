9/5/26, CHARACTER lane. [hair sheet] HAIR-REF-EIGHT-FACINGS.

HIS ORDER, 8/25, still open on the board: "LOOK ONLINE FOR PIXEL HAIRSTYLES IN
ALL 8 DIRECTIONS AND WE CAN GO FROM FUCKING THERE." The dispatch turned that into
REFERENCE FIRST, BEFORE ANY MORE COOKING: study what those artists do at the back
and the three-quarter, put the reference beside our styles, THEN cook.

WHAT WAS ALREADY HELD, and why it was not enough. The hair gate proves every cut
renders NON-EMPTY in all eight and that the front and back shapes differ ACROSS
the set. Both are true. Both are a low bar: "non-empty" is satisfied by a scalp
painted on a head, and "they differ from each other" is satisfied by 24 equally
flat cuts as long as they are flat in different places. Neither asks whether a
cut does what a haircut does when the person turns around.

RULE 1 -- VOLUME IN PROFILE. Hair sits ON the skull, so from E and W a cut with
any length grows the head OUTLINE: it paints where the bare head had nothing.
  cuts that add ZERO pixels outside the bare skull in profile   0 of 24   (0%)

RULE 2 -- THE FACE-FRAME IS GONE FROM BEHIND. From the front the face punches a
hole in the mass; from the back there is no face, so the same cut covers MORE.
  cuts with LESS hair from behind than from the front            0 of 24   (0%)

RULE 3 -- THE NAPE. Every real cut is defined at the back by where it STOPS on
the neck. Measured BELOW the base of the skull on the N facing -- the row where
the silhouette pinches before it widens into the shoulders, read off the art.
  cuts with NOTHING on the neck from behind                      0 of 24   (0%)

RULE 4 -- THREE-QUARTER IS NOT FRONT. A three-quarter is its own drawing. If a
cut's 3/4 picture is the FRONT or the BACK picture slid sideways, the head
never turned; the body swapped underneath the same hair.
  cuts whose SE/SW/NE/NW picture IS the front or back picture    0 of 24   (0%)

RULE 5 -- EIGHT FACINGS, NOT TWO. How many of ONE cut's eight pictures actually
differ from each other, comparing the PICTURE and not where it sits.
  8 different picture(s) of 8    24 cut(s)   100%

THE REFERENCE HALF OF THE ORDER, because he said LOOK ONLINE FIRST

  Five entries landed in the haircut shelf this round (HAIR-04..HAIR-08), all
  about the eight facings, which the three entries already there did not cover:
    HAIR-04  the canonical pixel turnaround is EIGHT SEPARATE DRAWINGS laid out
             as one sheet; a facing you cannot tell from its neighbour is not one.
    HAIR-05  THE FINDING THAT ARGUES AGAINST US, kept on purpose. Shipped
             top-down games routinely draw FOUR directions and let the diagonals
             reuse them, and a side view is normally MIRRORED, not redrawn. So
             "the back three-quarters reuse the back" is a normal trade in the
             craft. It is wrong HERE for two reasons: we already pay to render
             eight, and mirroring is exactly what ours were NOT doing -- NE and
             NW were the SAME picture, not reflections. We were not even taking
             the cheap correct answer.
    HAIR-06  head anatomy: the neck emerges from UNDER the occipital bone and the
             skull tapers toward the spine before the neck starts. THAT TAPER IS
             THE LANDMARK the hairline is placed from, and hair overlaps the ear
             and the neck. This is what corrected the nape ruler below.
    HAIR-07  from behind the hairline is a GRADIENT, thicker at the nape, finer at
             the crown; the edge that reads is the bottom one, on the neck.
    HAIR-08  a three-quarter shows depth a head-on view hides completely, so its
             silhouette sits BETWEEN head-on and side-on and is asymmetric.

  AND THE NUMBER CAME FROM HIS OWN ART, NOT FROM THE INTERNET. Measured off
  hair/curtain-bob, the one style he painted and has never criticised: hair leaves
  the skull by 0 px head-on and 2 px side-on, so a three-quarter is 1. Reference
  gives the SHAPE of the rule; his art gives the NUMBER (8/3, standing).

WHAT CHANGED IN THE GENERATOR, and the control that proves it

  ROOT CAUSE: genHair had THREE facings -- front, back, profile -- and the game
  has eight. NE, N and NW took the same branch at every line. The front
  three-quarters escaped only by accident: SE and SW have FACE pixels, so the
  generator could read which way he was looking off the art. Turn away from the
  camera, the face is gone, and it had nothing left to tell left from right.

  TWO FIXES. A three-quarter reads the turn off the FACING when there is no face
  to read it off the art, and gets HALF the profile's silhouette on the away side.
  A cut that ends at the skull carries ONE CELL of hair down onto the neck, inset
  to the neck's width, so the nape is where the nape is.

  THE CONTROL, and without it none of the numbers above mean anything, because
  one of the rulers moved this round. The SAME corrected ruler, run against the
  build on origin/main from before the fix:
        no nape                18 of 24  ->  0 of 24
        3/4 is the front/back  19 of 24  ->  0 of 24
        pictures out of eight    6 or 7  ->  8 on all 24
  Red then, green now, same ruler. Held by gates/hair_gate.js, which takes ALPHA=
  so anyone can re-run that control.

  ON THE SHIPPED DEMO, not only the workshop: 300 citizens asked of the real
  picker, 281 wear hair, all 24 cuts appear, and 281 of 281 both turn between N
  and NE and carry a nape.

A CHECK THAT WAS WRITTEN THIS ROUND AND WITHDRAWN

  HAIR-05 says a side view is normally MIRRORED, so the obvious next check was
  that NE should resemble NW-mirrored more than it resembles the flat back. It
  went red on four cuts by a whisker (WOLF CUT 0.83 against 0.95). THEN THE
  CONTROL KILLED IT: run the identical test on the BARE BODY WEARING NO HAIR --
        bare NE vs mirrored NW  0.849      bare NE vs N  0.862
        bare SE vs mirrored SW  0.837      bare SE vs S  0.851
  The naked rig fails it by the same margin, so the ruler was measuring THE RIG,
  not the haircut: a three-quarter body is not a mirror of the other three-quarter
  body because the arms, hands and their layer order resolve per facing. NOT A RIG
  BUG -- a body holding its arms differently on two facings should not mirror.
  What is dead is the claim that hair can be judged that way. A TEST WHOSE SUBJECT
  FAILS IT WITH THE THING BEING TESTED REMOVED IS TESTING THE SUBSTRATE.

READ THIS BEFORE DRAWING A CONCLUSION. A BUZZ CUT CORRECTLY SCORES ZERO ON RULE
1 -- shaved hair does not leave the skull, and a rule that fails a buzz cut is a
broken rule, not a broken cut. What matters is the SHAPE OF THE WHOLE SET: if
nearly every cut scores zero, the wardrobe has no volume in it anywhere, and
that is the thing he has been looking at since August. A NUMBER IS NOT A FINDING
UNTIL YOU KNOW WHAT IT IS COUNTING (9/5, the round the brown boots were called
an accent).
