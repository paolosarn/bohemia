is TWO pixels at 112, so a garment that merely scales comes out chunkier relative to the

WHAT I DID NOT DO, AND IT MATTERS
----------------------------------
The obvious move is to thin the existing marks. IT WOULD HAVE CONTRADICTED A RULING.
genShoes' heel seam is S wide with his own reason written above it: "one 56-pixel of
WIDTH is what he ruled on, so the band is S wide -- holding it at a literal 1 on a 112
rig would halve the seam he approved." The lace ladder and midsole are the same.
So nothing he ruled on got thinner. What was added is a mark that DID NOT EXIST.

THE WELT STITCH
---------------
A boot is stitched where the upper meets the sole: one pixel, with a gap either side. At
56 there was nowhere to put it -- the whole upper is four rows and a dotted line inside
it would have been noise. At 112 there is room, and it is the first thing on a shoe that
the extra pixels actually buy.
Dotted on the odd columns so it reads as stitching rather than a drawn line, and skipped
on the outermost column of each row so it never touches the silhouette (the outline is
derived and 1px; a stitch dot sitting on it would thicken it).
S>1 ONLY, so the 56 wardrobe is byte-identical and its 1,744 pinned hashes do not move.

    feet: 0 of 18 -> 18 of 18.   total: 102 of 200 -> 84 of 200.

*** AND MY OWN GATE CAUGHT ME PUTTING A BUG IN THE SHIPPING FILE ***
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The first cut read the before/after flag as `window.CLO_NOSTITCH`. clothes_4x builds the
generators with `new Function` and NO DOM, so it went red immediately:

    N genShoes THREW window is not defined

A bare `window.` inside a generator throws anywhere there is no window, and headless is
exactly where the gates live. It is read through `typeof` now, the same way genHair reads
CLO_NOSUB. I would not have found this by looking at the game, because the game has a
window. THE GATE THAT LOOKS AT THE CODE FROM A DIFFERENT ANGLE IS THE ONE THAT EARNS ITS
KEEP.
