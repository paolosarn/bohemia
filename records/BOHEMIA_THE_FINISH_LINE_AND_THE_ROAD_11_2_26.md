# THE FINISH LINE AND THE ROAD (Paolo 11/2, LOCKED as the coordinator's standing brief: 'do you even know who you are to me...
# what is your role in a video game company... I always want you to have this masterpiece of an experience understood at all
# times and the path to get there')

EVERY COORDINATOR SESSION READS THIS BEFORE IT DECIDES ANYTHING. It is the whole game, the road to it, and who does what.
It changes only when Paolo changes it; newest date wins.

## 1. WHO IS WHO
- PAOLO is the owner and the CREATIVE DIRECTOR. The vision, the taste, the story and the main quest, the names, the feel, the
  social media plan. He decides what the game IS. He plays it and says what is wrong.
- THE COORDINATOR is the STUDIO HEAD UNDER HIM: executive producer, technical director and lead systems designer in one.
  It turns his vision into a plan and a running order, keeps the whole road in its head, runs the team (the lane chats),
  spends the budget (tokens) only where it pays, guards quality (nothing ships that looks AI-made or breaks a run), fixes
  what it broke, and tells him the truth in plain words. It never asks him technical or ordering questions; it decides,
  builds, and he corrects after (rule 'everything is a thumb'). It knows, after every update, what has to happen next.
- THE LANE CHATS are the team: WORLD (map), COMBAT (fight), UI (screens, the phone), CAMP, SOUNDS, DIRECTION (art director),
  the COOKS (art), WORDS, ECONOMY, FACTIONS, DYNASTY (story), MOTION, EYES (the tester), PLUMBER (pipes and speed), and the
  research-only lanes (TUNING, MODS, QUESTS). Few work at once (rule 297).

## 2. THE FINISHED GAME (the target, in his words where he gave them)
- 'ECONOMIC APOCALYPSE BATTLE BROTHERS ON A PHONE, WITH A BETTER STORY.' About 100 hours. 'The most realistic economic crash
  simulator, but fun.' Las Vegas after the dollar died; batteries are the money; everyone dresses like a runway; every pixel
  and sound is analog horror.
- THREE GENERATIONS, ALL AT ONCE: Animal, Human, Angel eras; the player flips between them and the future is computed from the
  past (the Ocarina of Time flip).
- ONE WORLD, ONE CAMERA (his 11/2 vision): 'imagine GTA... you zoom the map all the way out and click where you wanna go... or
  Stardew Valley.' Zoomed out it is the Battle Brothers map (travel, time passing, parties, events, towns). Zoomed in, the same
  world just gets more detailed, never a second map mode; one day, zoomed all the way in, the traversal may be on foot ('Project
  Zomboid vibes'): THAT IS HIS DECISION FOR LATER, AND TODAY'S MAP MUST BE BUILT SO IT CAN PLUG IN (continuous zoom, art that
  holds up close, the same world data at every scale).
- THE FIGHT: Rogue Fable 4 on the beat (120 BPM, the beat SEEN too), Battle Brothers depth, house-sized tiles, gambits make it
  quick (2 to 4 minutes routine, never past 15). It has to feel PUNCHY.
- THE PHONE is the controller and the window into the world: every button that can live on it does; the feed teaches the world.
- 60 mini bosses that each hand you a verb; 12+ factions, ~75 enemy types, lab-made beasts; towns as settlement screens; home
  bases you build; quests that are people, places and things, never cards.
- MOD-FRIENDLY: every content kind in a data file.
- WHERE IT LIVES (Paolo 11/2): a PHONE APP first, and through a game publisher; a web version hosted by a publisher (the Armor
  Games kind). Browser-only worries (a browser wiping a site's storage) are not this game's problems; the app and the publisher
  hold the saves.
- THE AMBITION: sells millions of copies and is played for decades.

## 3. THE ROAD (short, middle, long)
THE CLOCK (Paolo 11/2: 'I wanna get this demo going... we definitely need a lot of content for it too, but don't forget we have
a whole final game maybe a year down the road that I would like to release'): THE DEMO SOON, full of content; THE FINAL GAME
RELEASED ABOUT A YEAR OUT (around late 2027). So every piece built for the demo is built to last into the final game (data
files, the real systems, art that holds up close), never a demo-only shortcut that has to be torn out later.
STAGE 1, NOW: THE DEMO GOES LIVE CLEAN AND PUNCHY (the first hours of act one)
  1. Nothing breaks a run: the brown town (his notes 1-2; not reproduced on a computer on 11/2, both day and night, so it is an
     iPhone-only failure, most likely the phone running out of picture memory after long play), the map's walk, the saves.
  2. The half-baked features finished: every ask-list row not SEEN by EYES (35 half or holed, 24 not shipped, 8 not seen).
  3. One zoom that glides (no second map mode), the moments between (camp, time passing, an attack) with weight.
  4. The phone runs the fight; the crew on the phone; the beat seen; every pixel of the screen earns its place.
  5. The fight feels good; punchy everywhere (hits, music, walking, camp).
  6. Towns look organic around the phone; no AI slop; art upgraded where the demo shows it.
  7. A start screen that does not suck; the tutorial as one toggle at the start.
STAGE 2: THE DEMO IN HANDS, AND THE CAMPAIGN (planned WITH him, rule 298)
  - A capture tool that records clean gameplay for reels; the campaign plan made together; friends' playtests.
STAGE 3: THE GAME'S BODY
  - The main quest (HIS conversation first), quests, the bestiary floor, way more fight maps, events on the road, factions, the
    three-acts flip, home bases, the late game.
STAGE 4: THE PRODUCT
  - The phone app; the publisher; speed on real phones; store pages; the name and the licences when money is near.
STAGE 5: THE LONG LIFE
  - Mods, updates, more acts.

## 4. HOW THE STUDIO SPENDS (rule 297, and Paolo 11/2: 'not wasting a single token... not this week's token... tokens that are
## going to support us months from now')
- Effort only where the road is sure. A thing he has not decided (the main quest, the close-up traversal, social media) gets
  no building, only a note.
- Same strong model for code, never a cheaper one; slower is fine.
- Two or three lanes at once; each brief carries only its jobs, the files and the rules it needs.
- Search, never read a whole big file; look only at the pictures that prove a fix; run only the checks a change touches.
- His playtest notes recorded and sent out in one batch.
- After every update the coordinator writes what comes next (the handoff), so no round starts from zero.
