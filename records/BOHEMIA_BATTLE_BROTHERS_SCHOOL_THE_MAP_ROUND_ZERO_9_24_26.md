## 1. HOW THE BATTLE BROTHERS MAP WORKS (measured off the game and its dev blogs)
- THE MAP IS GENERATED PER CAMPAIGN: villages near fertile land and rivers, towns, guardposts and keeps
  at strategic spots, roads that snake around dark forests. A typical world: ~17 settlements aligned to
  three noble houses (2 city halls, 3 town halls, 6 village halls, 2 strongholds, 2 castles, 2 manors),
  plus camps and ruins that spawn and are cleared.
- THE PARTY IS ONE MARKER. You tap a spot or a place; the company walks there in real time; you can
  pause. Speed depends on terrain (roads fast, swamp and hills slow), on load, and on night (slower,
  and your sight radius shrinks, so ambushes happen at night).
- TIME PASSES AS YOU MOVE, and it costs: provisions are eaten, wages are due every day, tools repair
  gear and medicine heals wounds while you walk. Standing still is not free either. THE CLOCK IS THE
  PRESSURE, not a quest log.
- YOU SEE WHO IS COMING. Other parties roam the map in the open, and every party leaves TRACKS you
  can follow or avoid, with a different footprint per kind (men, orcs, beasts). You choose fights by
  walking into or away from them.
- PLACES ARE WHERE THE SERVICES ARE: market (buy and sell, prices differ per town and shift with
  supply and with what your actions did nearby), armourer and weaponsmith, recruits with backgrounds
  and hidden traits, a temple to heal faster, training grounds, a tavern for rumours. A town carries
  temporary MODIFIERS your actions change (clear the bandit camp and the trade route is no longer
  ambushed; a raided village has no food).
- CONTRACTS come from the places: escort a caravan, clear a camp, hunt a beast, deliver, hold a
  position; each has a TWIST (the client lied, the target moved, a third party shows up). Pay is
  negotiable and pushing too hard costs reputation with that house (his 9/21 ruling already reads
  this: [haggle like bb]).
- EVENTS pop while travelling or camping: a short situation with an illustration and 2 to 4 choices;
  outcomes land as morale, injuries, items, money, a brother leaving or a new trait. Many events are
  keyed to the men you carry (backgrounds, traits, relationships), so the company generates its own
  story.
- AMBITIONS: a light goal the company sets itself (make X crowns, win Y fights) with a small
  reputation reward; the campaign never tells you what to do next.
- REPUTATION is two numbers: renown (how strong you are known to be, which gates contracts and
  prices) and standing per noble house (which gates who hires you and who hunts you).
- THE LATE GAME IS A CRISIS: after a while a war between houses, a greenskin invasion or an undead
  scourge sweeps the map and changes what every town needs.

## 2. WHAT PLAYERS REMEMBER, AND WHAT THEY COMPLAIN ABOUT
Remembered: the company as a cast (named men with pasts, a brother who dies and is missed), the
tension of a night march with something's tracks crossing yours, the twist in a contract, a market
that made a haul worth the walk. Complained about: the mid-game grind of the same contracts, a map
that reads the same everywhere after fifty hours, travel dead time, and a UI that hides numbers
players want (why the pay is what it is).

## 3. THE SHAPE FOR US (defaults, his to knock down in VOTE)
- THE MAP IS THE CITY VIEW WE HAVE. One squeeze out is the valley with the cracked phone; it becomes
  the map: the party marker (him and the companion, one marker), tap a place, travel with the clock,
  roads faster than dirt, other groups visible and leaving tracks (COOK [footprints] already did the
  prints; FACTIONS [tracks read] knows whose). One game mode: the same buttons.
- THE STREET IS THE PLACE. Arrive and you are on the walked street of that block, one step per
  house, people with names at doors, the fight on house tiles. Battle Brothers' town menus become
  buildings you walk to (the shop that is still lit, the battery shed, the pump).
- THE CLOCK IS THE PRESSURE, WITH OUR MONEY: a day of travel eats a battery (EVERYTHING COSTS ONE); a
  companion costs one a day; the debt of one a night (ECONOMY Q49) is the wage.
- CONTRACTS ARE ASKS: a person in a place asks for a thing (A QUEST IS PEOPLE, PLACES AND THINGS), pay
  is negotiated the BB way, and every ask carries one twist (the one wrong detail, re-aimed at the
  road).
- EVENTS ON THE ROAD, from a mouth with a portrait, two or three choices, never in the first minute.
- CRISES ARE OUR ACTS: the flip to act 2 and act 3 (rule 31) is where a Battle Brothers crisis would
  sweep the map; the factions moving without him IS the crisis.
- WHAT WE DO NOT COPY: hand-illustrated event cards (ours are a face and a mouth), the fantasy
  bestiary, a fight that copies BB's (RF4 leads, his 9/24 words), and the same-everywhere map (the
  valley is Las Vegas, real places, faction colour on the ground).

## 4. WHAT ALREADY EXISTS THAT THE MAP CAN USE
The zoomed-out valley and the seam (RUN [way back]); the cracked phone in that view (UI); territory
colours on the ground (FACTIONS); tracks and whose they are (FACTIONS [tracks read], COOK
[footprints]); places with names (WORLD's shops, sheds, pumps, the Stratosphere, the Sphere); the
companion on foot (PEOPLE); the deed and standing machine (PEOPLE [weights shape]); the loan
(ECONOMY Q49); the asks generator (QUESTS); a day clock and night (WORLD). What does not exist: a
party marker that travels on a tap, travel time by terrain, an event on the road, a place you ENTER
from the map.

## 5. THE HOLES EVERY LANE'S SCHOOL MUST CLOSE
- Rule 24 says one mode: the map, the street and the fight share one HUD. What is on it in all three?
- Rule 21 says the person is one size: on the map he is a marker; where does the law's "walked or
  fought surface" end? (The map is neither; ruled here: the marker is not the person.)
- The demo is one map with a few places: which few, and how far apart in seconds of travel?
- Travel dead time on a phone: Battle Brothers players complain of it on a PC with a mouse; on a
  phone a 40-second walk is a put-down. What fills it (the phone feed? the companion talking?).
