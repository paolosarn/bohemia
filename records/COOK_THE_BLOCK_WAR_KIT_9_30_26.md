## 2. THE SHEET GUESSED WRONG IN BOTH DIRECTIONS, WHICH IS WHY COUNTING BEAT GUESSING
The sheet's guess was "we have the city, we lack the nature." Measured:
- **THE SHORE is the second best stocked terrain we own (1,409)**, not a gap. Water, edges and
  banks were built long ago for the city's pools and canals.
- **THE HILLS has 590, and 323 of them are MOUNDS** -- the one kind the whole fight rests on.
- **THE REAL EMPTY ONES ARE CITY**: the freeway 39, the landfill 37, the trailer park 35, the
  airport 21. Open desert at 11 is the barest thing we own, but it is one terrain, not the half.
- **COVER IS THE THINNEST KIND ANYWHERE: 389 against 2,899 blockers.** A board with blockers and
  no cover is a board with nowhere to stand, which is the opposite of a Battle Brothers fight.
- **LIGHT is 99 and WEATHER is 36** across all fifteen terrains put together.

So this round cooks the city cover and the city blockers he named, which is where his
correction and the count agree.

## 4. WHAT IS PROVED, NOT CLAIMED (each refuses the run)
- **EVERY PIECE IS ITS REAL SIZE** at his density, checked against a metre table;
- **COVER IS NOT A BLOCKER, AND IT IS MEASURED**: under a man's chest (1.3 m) you can shoot over
  it, over it you cannot;
- **A MOUND IS SOMETHING YOU STAND ON**: both mounds carry a flat top;
- **EVERY PIXEL IS HIS**: on a family ramp of the 7/28 bank or on one of his own tiles;
- **NOTHING IS STAMPED**: nine pieces, nine different pictures.

## 5. FOUR THINGS THE GUARDS AND THE LOOKING CAUGHT
1. **I FILED TWO PIECES WRONG BY EYE AND THE TAPE CORRECTED ME.** A cart corral is 1.05 m and a
   propane tank is 1.20: both under a man's chest, so both are COVER, not blockers. And that
   forced a better fact into the bank: **a piece carries two things, not one.** Height decides
   whether you can shoot over it; whether you can walk through it is separate. A corral is cover
   that also stops a man, which is a real board piece and not a contradiction.
2. **THE CONTAINER AND THE TRAILER WERE THE SAME PICTURE.** Both dressed in his wall tiles, both
   came out tan brick boxes standing side by side on the card. A container is painted steel (his
   asphalt ramp, corrugations doing all the reading, rust where the rain sat); a single-wide is
   pale ribbed metal with a seam down the middle, skirting and a step.
3. **THE ROOF DECK WAS A BLANK RECTANGLE.** High ground has to read as somewhere you would
   stand: it is gravel with a parapet round it now, lit on the north-west lip.
4. **THE LABELS COLLIDED FOUR DEEP.** A jersey barrier is 3 m wide and its name is nine
   characters longer than the piece. Numbered on the picture, named in a legend.

And once more, the guard that has now bitten this lane twice: **a colour check against the bare
family ramps is stricter than his own approved art.** The barrier failed on eight colours that
are the concrete ramp plus two, which is his tiles' own sun offset, measured on this lane's row
on 9/28. The check reads his ramps AND his tiles now.

## 6. THE ANALOG HORROR LINE (rule 20)
Everything in this kit is something somebody left. The corral still has its carts in it, the
tank is still full, and the house that burned did it long enough ago that the ash has stopped
moving.

## 7. [bb the overworld is battle brothers] -- rule 33(f), (g) and (j)
**reference/library/battle_brothers/02_COMBAT_RULES.md**, the BOARD and TERRAIN lines: "a fight
is generated from the map terrain where it happens", blockers scattered by rules, height the one
bonus. BB's kit per terrain is small on purpose, three or four blocker kinds, because a board
reads when its vocabulary is short. Taken: nine pieces, not ninety.

**WHAT WE DO DIFFERENTLY (rule 39b, so nobody can call it a rip-off):** BB's blockers are
nature's (trees, rocks) and its settlements are wood. Ours is a CITY THAT ALREADY FELL: every
blocker in this kit is something a person put there for a reason that no longer exists, and half
of them are climbable, so our board has a vertical layer BB's flat field never had.
