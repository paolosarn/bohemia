# PAOLO 10/11: IS THERE NOT ONE THING IN THE UNIVERSE I CAN BUY ONCE TO MAKE YOU A BETTER GRAPHIC ARTIST (verbatim, voice-to-text)

> "Is there not a single thing in the universe that I can make a one time purchase for to make you better as a graphic artist?"

## The honest frame first

Nothing bought once changes the chat's own hand. A chat draws by code and sees the result afterwards, small. Every one-time purchase that works is one of two things: A HAND (a model or an editor that makes or fixes the pixels) or A TEACHER (reference the hand is taught from, or rules the chats encode into the gate). Both kinds exist. Ranked by how much they change the picture, with what they cost once.

## The list (one-time, no meter, no card on his computer unless he picks number 5)

1. THE MODEL TAUGHT ON HIS PACKS (a LoRA). About $2 to $5 once: one to three hours of a rented RTX 4090 at roughly $0.30 to $0.50 an hour on a community GPU cloud, or $0 on a free Kaggle GPU session. The result is a 50 to 200 MB file in the repo that draws only in his packs' style, at his packs' pixel floor. It runs afterwards on the free runners. THIS IS THE BIGGEST SINGLE THING. It is the gun from the last ruling, loaded with his ammunition.
2. ASEPRITE, $19.99 once. The pixel editor every pixel studio uses. Scriptable with no screen, so the chats run real pixel tools (outline, dither, palette swap, onion skin, eight-frame sheets) instead of code that places squares. The cleanup hand in step 6 of the ten steps.
3. MORE PACKS, $10 to $60 each, once. The one lever that already worked (rule 87). Every pack is both reference for the twin and training food for number 1. Specific holes to fill: wrecked vehicles at 45 degrees, hand-held guns at the 96-pixel floor, head-only portraits in one style, eight-facing people and beasts.
4. THE TUTOR BOOK: Pixel Logic by Michael Azzi, $10 once. Its rules (line weight, banding, pillow shading, palette ramps, readable silhouettes) get written into the pixel tutor skill and the clean gate, so the machine refuses the mistakes the book names. Gives rules, not eyes.
5. A GRAPHICS CARD BOX, about $300 to $600 once. A used RTX 3060 12 GB is about $290; a used small gaming PC with one inside is $450 to $600. It sits under a desk, always on, as this repo's own runner. Pictures in seconds instead of minutes, the LoRA retrained whenever the packs grow, no cloud at all. He said no graphics card on HIS computer; a separate box is a different thing, and it is HIS call, not a recommendation. Without it, the free runners do the same work slower.
6. AN ARTIST, ONCE, FOR THE SEED SHEET: $300 to $800 for a style sheet (one house, one person in eight facings, one gun, one vehicle, one portrait, the palette). That sheet becomes the twin for everything and the first training food for number 1. The cheapest way to buy taste once.

Together, 1 through 4 is about $45 plus packs. 1 through 6 is about $1,000 to $1,500 once, with no meter ever again.

## What does NOT exist

- A plugin or file that makes the chat itself draw well. There is none, anywhere, at any price.
- A one-time licence for Retro Diffusion's web model, PixelLab, or Scenario. They sell meters or months.
- A good pixel-art model that runs fast on a processor alone. Fast means a graphics card, somewhere; slow and free is the processor.

## The ruling, as filed (rule 108 ROUND FIVE: THE ONE-TIME LIST)

The six numbers above are THE shopping list; nothing else is put to him. Number 1 (the LoRA) and number 2 (Aseprite) are the default recommendation. Numbers 3, 4, 6 are good and cheap. Number 5 is his alone. The free road (the gun on the runners) runs whether he buys nothing or everything. He answers with numbers; the chats never ask again.
