# "BRO WHAT DO YOU MEAN" — I ASKED HIM TO JUDGE SOMETHING HE COULD NEVER SEE
## 8/11/26, CITY lane

I asked Paolo the same question six times: **"the dead — too many, too few, or
about right?"** He finally answered: **"BRO WHAT DO YOU MEAN"**, and he was right
again. This is the SECOND badly-formed question I have put to him in two days
(the first was `records/BOHEMIA_I_ASKED_HIM_A_QUESTION_HE_COULD_NOT_PARSE_8_11_26.md`).

## WHAT I DID WRONG, AND IT IS NOT THE WORDING THIS TIME

The wording was fine. **The question was unanswerable because the thing it asks
about is invisible.** I never checked that before asking, six times.

Measured — a walking player at HC=44 on a 390x844 phone sees about 9 x 19 tiles,
so a screen is 171 of a cell's 16,384 tiles. Averaged over twelve seeds per type:

| district | outdoor remains per cell | per screen | **screens walked per body** |
|---|---|---|---|
| suburb | 0.8 | 0.009 | **115** |
| desert | 1.2 | 0.012 | 82 |
| arterial | 2.7 | 0.028 | 36 |
| downtown | 3.0 | 0.031 | 32 |
| medical | 8.0 | 0.083 | 12 |
| freeway | 14.3 | 0.150 | 7 |

**The suburb is 2,582 of the valley's 9,216 cells** — the ground he walks most.
One body every 115 screens is not a density he can have an opinion about. It is
an absence.

## AND THE DENSITY IS NOT WRONG

That is the uncomfortable part. It is exactly what the death math produces:
131,000 dead, 60% still lying where they fell, spread over 84.9 km². **Realistic,
evenly distributed, and therefore nearly invisible on foot.** Nothing is broken.

I tried to fix the QUESTION by shooting the same street at three densities
(0.30 / 0.60 / 1.00). The pictures were useless — at a zoom wide enough to show
several bodies, each body is a few pixels; at a zoom where a body reads, you see
one screen's worth of ground and the density difference is invisible. **Those
shots were deleted rather than shipped**: a picture that does not show what its
caption promises is worse than no picture, and slices/look/ is published.

## THE REAL CHOICE, WHICH IS HIS (REALISM FIRST, 8/4)

The law: the realistic option leads and wins by default; realism is sacrificed
only for fun or genuine interest, **and that trade is his**.

- **A — LEAVE IT.** The maths stays honest. The dead live on the freeway, at the
  hospital, in the sealed rooms. A suburb street is empty, which is true.
- **B — MORE.** Raise the count until a suburb reads. Costs ~10x the death math:
  the valley would hold more bodies than people who died in it.
- **C — SAME NUMBER, CLUMPED.** Keep the valley total exactly right and stop
  spreading it evenly. Six or eight together every ~15 screens instead of one
  alone every 115. **Arguably MORE realistic, not less**: people die together —
  in a room, at a checkpoint, around the last water — and an even sprinkle is the
  one distribution a mass-mortality event never produces.

C is the recommendation, because it is the only option that costs neither the
arithmetic nor the sight of them.

## THE LESSON, AND IT IS A GENERAL ONE
**Before asking him to judge a quantity, measure whether he can perceive it.**
A question about something invisible is not a hard question, it is a broken one,
and it burned six of his turns.
