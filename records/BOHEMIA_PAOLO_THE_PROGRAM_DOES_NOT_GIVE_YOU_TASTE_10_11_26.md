# PAOLO 10/11: THE PROGRAM DOES NOT GIVE YOU TASTE (verbatim, voice-to-text)

> "OK, so I appreciate that we would have a program that would make you a more professional pixel artist but that doesn't necessarily mean you're gonna be a better one because it looks like if I just give you this fucking program you still might be making shit that looks like shit because you think it looks good and you don't know what the hell is going on with your life so I need you to explain what else we can do to make you a better pixel artist"

## What it is

The tenth round of rule 108 and the truest sentence in it. A tool makes the chats more PROFESSIONAL (clean outline, right palette, right size). It does not make them BETTER, because the fault is upstream: the chat thinks its picture looks good and it does not know. Research page 03 says the same in numbers: a maker grading its own work is unreliable; pairwise comparison by a separate judge is reliable; absolute scores hallucinate.

## The ruling: A CHAT'S OPINION OF ITS OWN PICTURE IS NOT IN THE LOOP

The way to make a chat a better artist is to take its taste OUT of the chain and put taste in from three places it cannot fake:

1. THE PACKS DECIDE, NOT THE CHAT. The twin gate measures every piece against his packs with thresholds taken from the packs against themselves. The chat never gets to say 'looks good'; the number says inside or outside, and outside never reaches his tab.
2. THE CHAT DOES NOT DRAW. The picture comes from a 3D model under one camera (physics supplies the proportions), from parts drawn big and shrunk (the rule supplies the pixel), from a cut of his pack (the pack artist supplies the taste), or from the model taught on his packs. The chat's hand is not where the look comes from.
3. A SEPARATE JUDGE, PAIRWISE, NEVER THE MAKER. When two candidates exist, a different chat (EYES) compares them side by side against the twin with one question ('which is closer to the pack in X?'), never a 1-to-10 score, and never the chat that made them. Cap three rounds, keep the best, revert on a worse one.
4. HIS TAP TEACHES THE MACHINE. Every NO lands in the graveyard with one fault tag from the catalogue (banding, pillow shade, wrong size, wrong light, off palette, mushy, too clean, reads as a toy). The catalogue is a gate list: a tagged fault becomes a measured check the same round, so the same mistake cannot reach him twice (A DEAD SHAPE DOES NOT COME BACK). His taste enters the system once per fault, not once per picture.
5. THE SQUINT. Every piece is shrunk to 8x8 and 16x16 and must still read as its thing (the squint gate exists for districts; it extends to every asset). Silhouette is the one thing a human eye checks first and a chat checks never.
6. ONE THING AT A TIME UNTIL FINAL (rule 101). Fewer pieces done to FINAL beat many pieces done to 'fine'. Taste accumulates in the FINAL pile, and every new piece is measured against the FINAL pile as well as the packs.
7. A HUMAN SEED, ONCE, IF HE EVER WANTS IT. An artist's one style sheet ($300 to $800) is the cheapest way to buy taste once; it becomes the twin for everything. His packs already play this part for the ground and props; the sheet would do it for people, beasts and faces.

## What a chat can and cannot gain

A chat cannot acquire taste; nothing on the shopping list changes that and nobody should pretend otherwise. What it CAN do is stop being the judge, stop being the hand, learn each fault once from his NO, and borrow taste from the packs, from physics, from the rule and from him. That is what 'a better pixel artist' means for a chat: a chat whose opinion is never what decides.
