## WHICH FINDINGS CHANGED THE LINES, AND HOW
Seven, and the first one reshapes every ask in the set.

**1. THE ASK WORKS AND THE ASKER DOES NOT KNOW IT, SO NOT ONE ASK IS WRITTEN AS A
PLEA.** People underestimate by as much as 50% how likely a stranger is to agree.
**The instinct is to write an ask as a case being argued. All ten are written as
people getting a sentence out**, because the evidence says the outcome was never in
much doubt. The tension sits before the words.

**2. THE COST OF ASKING IS WHAT MAKES REFUSING EXPENSIVE, SO EVERY ASK SHOWS ITS
PRICE.** Helpers are moved by the social cost of saying no, and that cost scales
with what the ask cost the asker. **An errand that costs the asker nothing is a task
with a face on it.** This is also the answer to the row's "without being a quest
marker", and it came out of putting the craft complaint about fetch quests next to
the psychology.

**3. RESOURCEFULNESS FIRST, SO NOBODY LEADS WITH HELPLESSNESS.** The testimony names
resourcefulness before desperation. **Ask 1 opens with what this man normally fixes
himself.** Every ask arrives wrapped in evidence that the person is usually fine.

**4. THE PROUD ASK WORST, SO THE CLUMSIEST ASK BELONGS TO THE WORST OFF.** High
self-esteem predicts LESS help seeking under threat. **Ask 2 is deliberately the
weakest sentence in the section** and it belongs to the man in the deepest trouble.
The 9/4 round would have given him the best speech.

**5. THEY ASK THE STRANGER BEFORE THE NEIGHBOUR, AND THAT IS NOW SAID OUT LOUD.**
Help from a peer is the most threatening kind, because a peer is the comparison.
**Ask 3 states the mechanism in the character's own mouth: four doors would open,
and then he would live next to it.** This is the finding that hands the game its
player role, so it earned a line rather than a footnote.

**6. SURVIVAL FATIGUE, SO THE LONG TROUBLE IS THE HARDEST TO SAY.** Ask 4 is nine
weeks in and the man has LOST the speech he had in month one. **That is the opposite
of a rising dramatic curve and it is what the research describes.**

**7. SOMEBODY SAYS THEY CANNOT PAY IT BACK.** Measured across 1,669 lines, not one
person in this valley has ever said it, in a game whose entire premise is that
nobody can repay anything. **Ask 5 exists to end that.**

## AND TWO THINGS SCHOOL PRODUCED THAT ARE NOT IN THE ROW'S BRIEF
**THE SURPRISE BEAT.** If compliance is underestimated by half, then the accurate
moment after a yes is not gratitude. **It is astonishment, and gratitude arrives a
second later.** There is a short section for it, and it is three lines this lane
would never have thought to write.

**AND THE NON-ASK, WHICH IS THE TRUEST THING IN THE SECTION.** Combine findings 4
and 6 and the most accurate version of this scene is the one where the ask does not
happen: a man walks up, talks about the weather, and leaves. **Nothing is requested
and everything is visible.** If the player only understands it three streets later,
the scene worked.

**PLUS THE ASK THAT IS SECRETLY AN OFFER**, which is how real people dodge the whole
cost: "I have more beans than I can keep cold, you have the cold room." Nobody
admits needing anything and both people know exactly what happened.
