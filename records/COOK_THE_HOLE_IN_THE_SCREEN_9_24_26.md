## THE SQUARE, AND HE WAS RIGHT ABOUT THE CAUSE WITHOUT NAMING IT
The loading yard was drawn AFTER the service ring, with a comment saying it was drawn after
"so the ring cannot eat them". The result was the opposite of the intention: THE YARD ATE THE
RING. Its hard square corner ended up butted straight against the curve of the ball with no
road in between, and a flat rectangle jammed into a curved form reads as pasted on top, which
is the word he used.

AND REORDERING WAS NOT ENOUGH, WHICH THE RENDER SHOWED AND THE ORDER DID NOT. There is a
three-tile gap between the ball's edge and the service road's inner kerb, and the yard's
corner landed in it, so the ring was drawn and the square still touched the ball because
neither of them owned that gap. A piece of ground on this plot is either inside the road or
outside it. These two are outside it, so they are now clipped to outside it: the yard's
nearest tile is 117.4 tiles from the centre against a road whose outer edge is 117.

AND A FLAT BLOCK OF ONE TONE READS AS A SWATCH WHEREVER YOU PUT IT, which is the other half of
what he was looking at. The yard has a kerb round it now and loading bays painted on it
(TG-05), and the plant room has an edge. One tone with a hard border is a colour sample; an
edge and some marks make it a place.

## THE HOLE
A hole in a screen is not a circle cut out of it. An LED wall is panels on a frame, so when
one lets go it takes a PANEL-SHAPED BITE and the edge tears: straight along the lattice on two
sides, ragged where the skin gave. So: a panel-sized bite near the top of the ball, north-west
of the crown where the sun still lands; its inside is the darkest tone on the plot because
there is nothing behind a screen but the frame; and a torn lip on the sun side where the
peeled skin bends up and catches the light. The lip follows the same rule every mark in this
game follows -- open ground to the north or west means the sun is on it -- so it agrees with
the footprints, the kerbs and the ball itself.

It is 12 m across on a 157 m ball, which is a thirteenth of it: tiny, his word, and the one
wrong thing in an ordinary frame (AH-01 R1). No pucks are drawn where the panel is gone.
