# THE FOUR CIVICS — RESEARCH, THEN BUILD
**8/2/26. WORLD lane. Paolo: "do big brain online research if you need to then execute."**

Four districts were still generic: city hall, courthouse, terminal, chapel. All four were
built from *typology* — "what a city hall looks like in general" — and typology in the Mojave
produces a New England town hall with a clock tower standing on a green lawn. So each one was
researched to a REAL BUILDING IN THIS VALLEY and rebuilt on it.

The rule that came out of it, and it is now in three gates: **A NUMBER TAKEN FROM THE REAL
BUILDING IS A FACT THE MACHINE CAN HOLD. A NUMBER INVENTED ON THE DAY IS DECORATION.**

---

## 1. CITY HALL → LAS VEGAS CITY HALL
**Elkus Manfredi Architects, 2012, 495 S Main St.**

| what the research gave | what the district draws |
|---|---|
| Two shapes that MERGE inside the lobby: the curvilinear Council Chamber and the angular seven-storey glass office block | one building — the round chamber lands on the block and merges into it |
| A dynamic canopy over the plaza entrance carried on a single **160-foot column** | the entry canopy, OVERHEAD, on exactly one mast |
| A solar "tree farm": **33 tubular columns**, 25–35 ft, each carrying a PV panel, ~7% of the building's energy | **exactly 33 solar trees**, counted by `cityhall_gate.js` in all six placements |
| LEED Gold; the façade a metaphor for the Colorado River, the chamber for the Indian Springs Preserve | not drawn — that is symbolism, not geometry, and inventing it would be inventing canon |
| An attached parking structure | the deck on the east, its floor lighter because the sun never got in |

**KILLED:** the clock tower (a New England town hall) and 28% dead-green LAWN.

## 2. COURTHOUSE → LLOYD D. GEORGE U.S. COURTHOUSE
**CannonDesign, 2000, 333 Las Vegas Blvd S. One of three GSA Honor Awards for Architecture in the country.**

| what the research gave | what the district draws |
|---|---|
| **L-SHAPED**, 450,000 sq ft | the L, wrapping its own plaza in the elbow |
| A three-storey **ROTUNDA** public lobby under a **60-foot cable-truss glass dome** | the rotunda at the elbow; from above the dome reads as a bright RING, because most of the glass is gone |
| A dramatic steel and aluminium canopy **PROJECTING FROM THE TOP OF THE BUILDING**, shadowing the plaza | the canopy, OVERHEAD, with **no masts** — it cantilevers, and that is the whole point |
| Clad in **22 ft × 10 ft precast panels** and beige limestone | the panel joint grid across both roof plates |
| The **FIRST federal building** built to post-Oklahoma-City blast-resistance requirements | the wide empty SETBACK and the BOLLARD LINE. Standoff distance IS the security, so here — and only here — empty ground is correct, and the gate says so out loud so nobody "fixes" it |

**KILLED:** the portico colonnade, the monumental steps, the cupola, the lawn, and a 35.6% monoblock.

## 3. TERMINAL → BONNEVILLE TRANSIT CENTER
**2010, 101 E Bonneville Ave, downtown. LEED Platinum.**

| what the research gave | what the district draws |
|---|---|
| **16 on-site vehicle bays** | exactly 16, sawtooth, each box STEPPED against its neighbour so a coach pulls straight out instead of reversing |
| **Solar-panel shade structures** over the bays | the photovoltaic deck over the platform and the bay noses — and it leaves the walking strip open, because shading the whole platform made the platform stop existing |
| **7 on-street loading points** beyond the 16 | seven painted kerb marks outside the property line |
| **~100 double-stacked bike racks** + a repair stand | exactly 100, two banks, two bikes high |
| 2-storey ~20,000 sq ft head house, fully enclosed waiting area, **curved lines** | the curved concourse merged into the bar, glazed end to end |

**KILLED:** the schedule-board clock tower and 26% dead-green LAWN. Declared `vehicular:true`
(WALKABLE-LAND exception, 7/20): at a transit centre the vehicle surface IS the venue — and the
exemption is not a licence for a bare apron.

## 4. CHAPEL → kept as a CHURCH, corrected to the ground it stands on

The cruciform plan was always right and it stays. What was wrong was everything around it, and
both faults are findings Paolo made on *other* districts:

- **THE GREEN.** "memorial garden" and "dead landscaping" were lawn-green across a fifth of the
  plot. A Mojave churchyard is not a lawn: it is a walled **MEMORIAL COURT** of decomposed
  granite with a **COLUMBARIUM** wall of niches, because in this ground you do not dig graves,
  you build a wall and fill it. That is the correction, and it is grounded, not decorative.
- **THE MONOBLOCK.** 33.9% of the plot was one code called "sidewalk". A walk is the APRON that
  hugs a building, so it is now **computed from the building** rather than guessed at, and what
  it used to cover is a **DEAD ORCHARD** — a mission planting is what a parish did with ground
  it could not build on. **Renaming emptiness never fixes it. Putting something there does**
  (renaming "sidewalk" to "gravel" moved the monoblock and fixed nothing; the gate now checks
  the 30% cap directly so that dodge cannot work again).

## WHAT WAS DELIBERATELY NOT DONE

window, several small chapels and a gazebo on one lot — is the most Las Vegas building type there
is, and nothing in the valley has it. It is not built, because `chapel` is defined in canon as a
CHURCH with a memorial garden, and changing what a district type *is* is a contents decision.
