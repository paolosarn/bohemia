# PAOLO 9/28/26: WHEN A MAN DIES IN ONE ACT, WHAT HAPPENS IN THE OTHERS (voice-to-text, verbatim, to the coordinator)

"OK, but remember you know like people have a 20% chance to permanently die in the company like what happens
if they die earlier in an earlier act like do they die in the future or their descendent die in the future but
what if I'm in combat in the future and then they die does that mean the one in the past doesn't and I just have
to do some extra recruiting in the future like"

## THE ANSWER (from the rule the three-acts law already has for the city, s2(c): the past rewrites the future's
## WORLD, never the future's own deeds; time in the derive flows one way, past to future)
1. A MAN DIES IN ACT 1 -> his line is gone from act 2 and act 3 the next time you flip forward. No heir, unless
   he already had a kid before he died (a man who has been with the company long enough, or came with a family in
   his background, leaves one). A death in the past costs the future. That is the whole point of keeping men alive.
2. AN HEIR DIES IN ACT 2 (you are fighting in the future and he goes down) -> the man in act 1 is FINE. Nothing
   flows backwards. The act-2 death is act 2's own ledger. You recruit extra in act 2, the Battle Brothers way.
   Act 3 loses that heir's line the next time you flip forward.
3. YOU FLIP BACK TO ACT 1 AFTER THAT and get the act-1 man killed -> act 2 re-derives: the heir never existed.
   Whatever that heir DID in act 2 stays done on the company's ledger (the Day of the Tentacle rule: the past
   rewrites the world, never the future's hands), but the man is not there any more. You recruit again in act 2.
4. YOU RECRUIT A NEW MAN IN ACT 1 LATER -> the next flip forward shows his heir in act 2's roster, prepared from
   him. The heirs list is derived every flip, like the city.
5. THE MAIN CHARACTER NEVER DIES (37g), so the line itself never breaks; only the company's lines do.
DEFAULT NUMBERS (TUNING [recruit odds] researches; all in the table, all in VOTE): an heir exists if the man
survived the act, or died after 60 game days with the company (long enough to have a life), or carries a family in
his background; heirs arrive at 15 to 35 years old (35 years between acts); an heir carries roughly half the
parent's traits and the family's gear (37g).
