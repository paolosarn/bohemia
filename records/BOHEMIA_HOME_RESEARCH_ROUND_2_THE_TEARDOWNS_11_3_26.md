# HOME RESEARCH, ROUND 2: THE TEARDOWNS (rule 349; ask line 359)

RESEARCH ONLY. The five from round 1 (records/BOHEMIA_HOME_RESEARCH_ROUND_1_THE_SURVEY_11_3_26.md) that fit Bohemia best, picked because
each sits inside a game of travel and fights like ours, or is the one-screen base the most people ever played. Battle Brothers first, as
always. Facts are from this round's searches (sources at the foot); TAP COUNTS ARE OUR READING OF THE FLOW THE SOURCES DESCRIBE, not a
measurement, and say so. A researched game is a source, never a reference: what we take is told in our own words.

## 1. BATTLE BROTHERS: THE RETINUE, THE AMBITIONS, AND THE HOME ITS PLAYERS BUILT
- THE SCREEN: no home. A retinue screen off the world map: five slots of non-combat followers, 'several times that many' to choose from
  (dev blog #127). A cart picture with the five seats.
- A CHANGE: open the retinue, pick a follower, pay, he takes a seat; swap any time for a cost (about 3 taps, our reading).
- DEPTH AND EASE: depth is WHICH five of many, each changing one rule of the campaign (the surgeon: a fallen man is far likelier to live
  with a permanent injury instead of dying, dev blog #128; the recruiter: 10% off hires, 50% off tryouts). Ease: five seats, one choice each.
- UPKEEP: none after the hire price (players call it expensive).
- GROWTH AND PROGRESS: slots unlock with RENOWN; some followers need a deed first (the drill sergeant: retire a brother with a permanent
  injury). AMBITIONS are goals the company sets itself, now with a tooltip of requirements and rewards (dev blog #136); they build renown.
- A SECOND SCREEN: none.
- THE ITCH: the deed that unlocks the follower (you did something, now someone joins).
- WHAT PLAYERS ADDED: the STRONGHOLD mod (Nexus 324): your own fortress on the map, built from the retinue screen once the cart is fully
  upgraded; a strong force from nearby comes to destroy it; connect it by road and it sends caravans that bring food back; later versions
  add more bases at rising renown, an attached hamlet and stored brothers. A Steam thread (5/2024) recommends it for 'the excess crowns of
  the mid to late game'. Other mods: retinue promotions, ten slots instead of five; ambition overhauls; they broke when DLCs landed.
- WHAT PLAYERS HATED: the price; the waiting for the right ambition (stash slots were moved to plain money for that reason, dev blog #136);
  that nothing you own is at stake (the reason the Stronghold mod exists).
- WHAT WE TAKE: the deed opens the door (a person comes to live with you because of what you did); a small number of seats, each one
  changing a rule you feel; the home opens LATE, once the company can hold it; your neighbours come for it; a road to it pays.

## 2. DARKEST DUNGEON'S HAMLET
- THE SCREEN: one painted town, about nine buildings, the hub between expeditions (Wikipedia; the wiki's town page). It fits a phone held
  sideways with every building a button; upright it would need to scroll or stack.
- A CHANGE: tap a building, tap its upgrade, pay (about 3 taps, our reading); using a building is tap the building, pick a hero, pick a
  service.
- DEPTH AND EASE: each building has a few upgrade tracks (the Guild's skill levels and Training Regimen at -10% a level; the Blacksmith's
  gear and its cost cut; the Stagecoach's roster size). No placement at all: the town is drawn once, the depth is the order you upgrade in.
  Players argue that order in every forum (four different orders in round 1's results): the choice IS the game.
- UPKEEP: heirlooms and deeds pay upgrades; gold pays every service (stress relief, gear, skills) each time.
- GROWTH AND PROGRESS: the same picture, each building visibly upgraded; the 2026 DLC added districts (Wikipedia).
- A SECOND SCREEN: each building opens its own panel (a room you look into), not a walkable interior.
- THE ITCH: going back between runs to spend what you brought home, and choosing what comes next.
- WHAT PLAYERS HATED: the grind for deeds and heirlooms ('deeds are a big bottleneck for the most valuable hamlet upgrades'); the late game
  once everything is upgraded ('more gold than you can ever spend'); players asked for cosmetic upgrades as late sinks (Steam, 2015).
- WHAT WE TAKE: one painted place drawn ONCE, a tap on a building is everything, upgrades visible in place, a late sink that is looks
  (customizing) so money always has somewhere to go.

## 3. XCOM: ENEMY UNKNOWN'S ANT FARM
- THE SCREEN: the HQ seen side-on as a cutaway, 'We call this the ant farm' (Jake Solomon); 'a living diorama' (Destructoid). A cutaway is
  tall: on an upright phone it fills the screen better than any top-down town.
- A CHANGE: pick a cell, excavate, then install a facility (about 4 taps, our reading); each room costs more than the last.
- DEPTH AND EASE: adjacency: two power stations side by side give more, a lab beside a workshop gives discounts (CNN, Giant Bomb). Easy to
  read, but a reviewer found it easy to exploit ('the adjacency bonus for uplinks made the upgrade buildings not needed', Game-Wisdom).
- UPKEEP: monthly funding and power.
- GROWTH AND PROGRESS: digging down; the base fills.
- A SECOND SCREEN: each facility opens to its own room scene.
- THE ITCH: watching your people work in the diorama between missions.
- WHAT PLAYERS HATED: 'brutal, beautiful, emotion-wringing turn-based storytelling muffled by flat base design' (PC Gamer, 87); fans of
  the 1994 original missed the depth that was cut; one base for the whole world felt 'artificially restricting' (Techgage).
- WHAT WE TAKE: a cutaway is the natural upright picture; neighbours that help each other (realistic: the purifier beside the main, the
  kitchen beside the garden); and a warning: if the base is only a menu of rooms it reads FLAT next to the fights.

## 4. FALLOUT SHELTER
- THE SCREEN: a vault cross-section, 'an ant farm look'; it scrolls and zooms on a phone as it grows down.
- A CHANGE: tap the hammer, pick a room, tap a free cell (about 3 taps, memory, unconfirmed); drag a dweller into a room (1 drag);
  rooms built side by side merge into one bigger room.
- DEPTH AND EASE: few room kinds, levels, merges and who you put where; every action is a tap or a drag.
- UPKEEP: power, food and water drain all the time; collect by tapping a room.
- GROWTH AND PROGRESS: floor by floor down one shaft; dwellers to a cap of 200.
- A SECOND SCREEN: the wasteland you send dwellers into (a log, not a place).
- THE ITCH: 'play sessions surpassed 70 million times a day' in week one (Bethesda).
- WHAT PLAYERS HATED: the waiting ('you put dwellers into the right room and you wait'); the late game: the last room unlocks at 100
  dwellers of 200, then nothing new (Game Informer, 8/2015); selling junk loot is a chore (TechRaptor); balanced only for the early game
  (GamingBolt); mixed on Metacritic.
- WHAT WE TAKE: the bars that drain, shown on the screen; one tap to collect; people placed by a drag. WHAT WE LEAVE: waiting for its own
  sake, and running out of new things halfway.

## 5. SUIKODEN'S CASTLE (and Ni no Kuni II's Evermore, the same idea)
- THE SCREEN: your army's headquarters; Evermore is a kingdom grown from the ground up.
- A CHANGE: in Suikoden you do not build: a person you recruited moves in and opens a room (the bath, the shops, the storage); the castle
  level comes from the story and how many of the 108 you found (Gensopedia). In Evermore you build a facility and assign a citizen; a
  facility without one stalls (64 buildings, 103 citizens).
- DEPTH AND EASE: the depth is WHO you found; the ease is that you never place anything.
- UPKEEP: none (Suikoden); Evermore's money accumulates by the hour.
- GROWTH AND PROGRESS: the castle levels up in steps you see; Evermore's kingdom level caps at 4, each building at 3 or 4.
- A SECOND SCREEN: the castle IS walked inside; Evermore opens at chapter 4, a third of the way in.
- THE ITCH: find all 108 for the best ending (Game8).
- WHAT PLAYERS HATED: missable recruits (the castle you could have had); Evermore's chapter-4 lock.
- WHAT WE TAKE: the journey's people are the settlement (rule 347(e) exactly): each one you bring opens one thing there and you can see it.

## WHAT THE FIVE SAY TOGETHER, FOR OUR ONE SCREEN
1. DRAW IT ONCE, CHANGE IT IN PLACE: the place is a painted picture; the lots are fixed; a tap on one is the whole interface.
2. DEPTH = WHICH and WHO, not WHERE: which upgrade first, which look, which people; never free placement.
3. A DEED OPENS A DOOR: a boss's prize, a person who joins because of what you did.
4. SHOW THE BARS: the daily batteries, food and water on the screen every time you open it.
5. A LATE SINK THAT IS LOOKS: materials, paint, signs, the name, so money always has a place to go.
6. NEIGHBOURS HELP NEIGHBOURS, realistically: the purifier beside the main, the kitchen beside the garden, the panels beside the tank pump.
7. THE PLACE IS AT STAKE: neighbours come for it (Battle Brothers' players added exactly that), and a road to it pays.
8. AN INSIDE SCREEN COMES LATER, as a step (Evermore at a third in; XCOM's rooms; his act-two idea).
