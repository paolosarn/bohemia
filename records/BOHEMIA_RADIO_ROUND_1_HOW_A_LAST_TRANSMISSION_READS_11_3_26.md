## 1. THE FORMS, AS THE INSTITUTIONS PUBLISH THEM (the conventions, not the content)

A. INVESTIGATION TRANSCRIPTS (cockpit voice recorder exhibits, NTSB and peers; legends read off published exhibit cover pages)
   - A clock on every line (local time, to the second). Time is the spine; nothing is summarised.
   - A SOURCE tag and a SPEAKER tag: RDO (keyed on the radio), CAM (picked up by the room's microphone), HOT (a crew
     member's own live mic); -1, -2 for the speakers in order, -? for a voice nobody could name.
   - Editorial marks, in brackets, in the same calm type as the words: * an unintelligible word, # an expletive,
     ( ) a word the transcriber is not sure of, [ ] the transcriber's own note ([sound of ...]), ... a pause.
   - It ends with one flat line from the transcriber, not from anybody in the room: the end of the recording.
   - What makes it unbearable is the FORMAT ITSELF: a clerk's calm typing out a room where people are not calm.

B. AIR TRAFFIC CONTROL PHRASEOLOGY (the standard phrases every pilot and controller learns)
   - Who you are calling, then who you are, then the message. The number first, the feeling never.
   - Fixed words: say again, standby, roger, unable, how do you read, readability one to five,
     MAYDAY MAYDAY MAYDAY (three times, always), PAN-PAN for less.
   - The readback: the other side says your instruction back to you. A readback that does not come is the horror.

C. EMERGENCY DISPATCH AND SCANNER TRAFFIC (fire and police radio, as scanner listeners hear it)
   - Unit numbers, not names. Short keys. 'Copy.' 'En route.' 'On scene.'
   - 'Emergency traffic, emergency traffic' clears the channel; everyone else goes quiet; the channel tone.
   - A crew in trouble gives where, who, what they were doing, what they need (fire services teach it as LUNAR).
   - The roll call (a personnel count): the dispatcher calls each unit; each answers. One unit does not.
   - THE OPEN MIC: a radio keyed and left keyed (a thumb, a body, a fall on the button). No words; the room on the air.

D. WARTIME RADIO AND UNIT LOGS (the duty log a signaller keeps)
   - Date-time groups, grid squares, call signs; 'contact', 'send help', 'out'.
   - The log is kept by somebody who is not in the danger, in handwriting that does not change.

E. MODERN WARFARE 2, NAMED BY HIM FOR THIS ONLY (rule 353b; the Washington DC levels: the defence of the city and the
   evacuation). Studied from secondary sources (fan analyses of the 'Of Their Own Accord' radio chatter and the hidden
   evacuation broadcasts in 'Whiskey Hotel'); nothing quoted. What it does that he felt: the war happens to OTHER units
   on the radio while you are busy. You do not see them fall; you hear them call, and you hear the calls stop. The radio
   is the only window onto everyone you could not help. That is our 4(a) exactly: the town you did not defend.

## 2. WHAT MAKES THEM UNBEARABLE (the seven things, each one a rule below)
1. PROCEDURE HOLDS WHILE THINGS FAIL. The words stay in the form (call sign, location, count) while the facts get worse.
2. THE REPEATED CALL. The same call sign, three times, with the gap getting longer. No answer is the loudest line.
3. THE CALM VOICE GETTING LESS CALM, in small steps: a word dropped, a number wrong, a name used instead of a call sign.
4. THE MUNDANE DETAIL. Someone asks for a door code, a count of blankets, whether the generator is on.
5. THE OPEN MIC. A channel keyed with nobody talking: breathing, a room, something far off. Nothing jumps.
6. THE CUT. No ending line from the people. The transcriber ends it, flat.
7. THE SILENCE AFTER. The carrier is gone; the scanner hunts; nothing comes back. That gap is the scene.

## 3. THE RULES WE WRITE BY (RADIO's own; every line in records/target/bb/radio_lines.json obeys them)
R1. A TIME STAMP ON EVERY LINE, game clock to the second (02:14:07), and a SOURCE: GATE, ROOF, CLINIC, OPEN, or the
    town's channel. Speakers by post, never by full name (rule 353c and the scanner page's own 'no names').
R2. 120 CHARACTERS MAX a line (rule 350), whole on the phone; most under 60. Radio is short keys.
R3. NO EXCLAMATION MARKS. EVER. Alarm words in the same register as a door code (bible R5: the dead institution's type).
R4. THE TRANSCRIBER'S MARKS ARE THE PHONE'S: [unintelligible], [key, no voice], [open mic], [carrier lost], and the last
    line is the phone's, not a person's: END OF TRANSMISSION. A bracket is the machine talking; it is calm.
R5. EVERY FALL HAS FOUR VOICES IN ORDER: THE GATE (procedure, numbers), THE ROOF (sees it coming, then sees it),
    THE CLINIC (the mundane need: a count, a door, a supply), THE OPEN CHANNEL (keyed, nobody, then the cut).
R6. ONE CALL GOES UNANSWERED, AT LEAST, AND IT IS REPEATED: the gate calls the roof, the roof does not come back.
R7. NO GORE, NO SCREAM WRITTEN OUT, NO MONSTER (bible: not a monster). The worst thing is reported as procedure or is
    not reported at all. The silence carries it.
R8. SPANGLISH ONLY WHERE A PERSON WOULD (the 8/25 law): a word slips under stress, a name for a saint, a 'mijo'.
    Never on the transcriber's lines (the machine does not speak Spanglish; the player does not either, 9/22).
R9. EACH TOWN SOUNDS LIKE ITS PEOPLE: the Church prays procedure, the Mob counts money and doors, the Reds use
    comrade and the count of hands. The town is the {town} fill; a town without its own voice gets the plain set.
R10. RARE AND QUIET (353e): only when a town you hold falls with nobody at its gate; never in the first minute; never a
    card; the phone is its mouth; the radio sound sits under the music, never over it.
R11. NEVER A REAL LINE. Every line is written fresh for Bohemia. Nothing paraphrased from a real transcript either.

## 4. THE SOUND, AND THE ONE THING WE COULD NOT GET (said out loud)
- The radio's OBJECTS are real recordings from our CC0 bank (reference/library/sound_bank/esc50_cc0, every source cited):
  the talk switch is a real microswitch click; the far siren, the fire, glass, steps, breath and a bell are real
  recordings heard THROUGH the radio, the way an open mic picks up a room.
- A CC0 RECORDING OF A REAL HANDHELD'S STATIC WAS NOT REACHABLE from this box (it reaches GitHub, npm and PyPI only;
  Freesound, Wikimedia and the Internet Archive are blocked; seven CC0 sound repos on GitHub were searched by file name, and
  the two files called 'static' sit in digital and glitch folders, neither described as a radio recording). So the hiss and the squelch tail are made THE WAY A REAL HANDHELD MAKES THEM, not as a texture:
  the receiver's own thermal noise through a narrowband FM discriminator, a carrier at the town's signal strength, the
  noise squelch with its real closing delay (the 'kssht' after every key-up), de-emphasis, a 300-3000 Hz voice band and a
  small speaker. That is the radio's real physics (rule 353f 'or its real physics'), and it is NOT the dead sand recipe:
  near threshold an FM receiver throws CLICKS (Rice's FM click noise), so the static is impulsive and changes with the
  signal, which band-limited noise never is. The gate measures exactly that. When a machine can reach Freesound, a CC0
  recording of a real handheld replaces it, by name, and nothing else changes.

## 5. THE VOICES (353f: researched, brought to him, nothing ships until he hears them)
Three honest options; two or three samples each go in the VOTE tab; the text and the radio sound ship without a voice.
A. THE SQUIGGLE ON THE RADIO. People speak the squiggle (rule 10b of the analog horror law, built from real recorded
   syllables, public domain), keyed through the same handheld. In law today; the words are the phone's transcript.
B. REAL PEOPLE READ OUR LINES. Performers (him, friends, hired voices) record our lines on a phone; the same radio
   treatment. The sample uses the only radio-shaped thing real people have already recorded for us under licence: the
   ten digits (AudioMNIST, MIT), so it is a call sign called again and again, unanswered. Real voice, our words.
C. NO VOICE. The key, the carrier and the squelch, timed to each line's length; the words only on the phone. The cheapest,
   the most analog horror, and what the game plays until he picks.
