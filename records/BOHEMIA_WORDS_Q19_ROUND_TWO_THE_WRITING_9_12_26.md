## WHICH FINDINGS CHANGED THE LINES, AND HOW
Six, and the first one deletes the scene I would have written without school.

**1. NOBODY COLLAPSES, SO NOT ONE LINE IN THE SET IS A STAMMER.** School's hardest
result is that the whole point of holding evidence back in a real interview is to
stop people REVISING to fit what you just showed them. The revision is so reliable
that a professional technique exists for no other purpose. **Every first line after
the catch in this section is calm and absorbs the new fact**, and the collapse, which
does happen, got demoted to one rare block that is explicitly marked as rare. Before
school, the collapse was going to be the default and the revision was going to be
the variant. That is backwards, and the section is now built the other way up.

**2. THE REVISION USES ONE OF EIGHT MOVES, AND THE CHOICE IS THE CHARACTER.** Four
excuses and four justifications, catalogued in the sociology since 1968. This is
what turned a writing question into a structure. **The centrepiece of the section is
the SAME man, caught for the SAME cable, in the SAME second, answering eight
different ways** -- one per move. Read the eight in a row and eight different people
appear out of one crime. That is not a stylistic flourish, it is the finding made
visible, and it hands PEOPLE a per-character setting rather than a line to reuse.

**3. THEY PROTECT THE IDENTITY, NOT THE STORY, WHICH IS WHERE THE REVISION LADDER
CAME FROM.** An account is offered so a person can go on being who they were a
minute ago. The tell is that they will surrender facts cheaply and never surrender
the self-description. **So the ladder gives him three proofs in a row and he concedes
something on every rung** -- the Tuesday, then the spring, then the bag -- while the
picture of himself never moves. He is not losing ground. He is spending it.

**4. THE APOLOGY THAT WORKS IS THE ONE THAT COSTS, SO THERE ARE TWO OF THEM AND ONE
IS DELIBERATELY BAD.** Ranked components: owning it first, offering repair second,
plain regret in the middle, asking forgiveness last. **The WEAK apology in the
section is built only out of the two weakest components**, which is exactly the
apology film writes, and the STRONG one is built only out of the two that cost. The
difference is legible in one read: the weak one asks you for something, the strong
one hands you something. Without the ranking I would have written the weak one and
thought it was the emotional version.

**5. THE CATCH IS A COLLISION BETWEEN TWO STATEMENTS, WORTH ABOUT SEVEN TIMES A READ
OF A FACE, SO THE PLAYER BRINGS A SENTENCE.** This is the finding with the biggest
design consequence and it is why the section opens with the collision instead of the
answer. **The player holds what he said Monday and what she said Friday, and the
whole catch is the match.** Nothing in the set lets anybody be caught by looking at
them, and "the player catches somebody by reading their face" is now a written
refusal. This is the half that pairs with the QUESTS row.

**6. OUR CAST APOLOGISES ONLY FOR EXISTING, WHICH IS A VOICE TO PLAY AGAINST.**
Measured in round one: eight sorries in the build and every one is for a mannerism,
not for an act. **So the last block is a man who apologises twice for talking over
people and then flatly refuses to apologise for the thing he actually did.** The
contrast only works because the reflex apology is already the house voice. A finding
about our own text became a character.
