CHARACTER lane, 8/25/26.  Clause 1 of HAIR AT FOUR TIMES THE PIXELS.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT HE SAID
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"you just have to be intentional with the hairstyles making them looking good and
 the same and coordinated from all angles."

CLAUSE 1 of laws/BOHEMIA_LAW_HAIR_AT_FOUR_TIMES_THE_PIXELS_8_25_26.md, written the
same day. A hairstyle is not eight drawings. It is one object seen eight ways.
Turning the head may change what you SEE; it must not change WHICH HAIRCUT HE IS
WEARING.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE DEFECT, AND IT WAS SITTING INSIDE A GREEN GATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    SHOULDER LENGTH   11 pixels of hair below the jaw facing S, SE and SW.
    LONG LOOSE        ZERO facing E, NE, N, NW or W.

Two of the fifteen canon styles had a real fall of hair from the front and nothing
at all from any other angle. That is not a subtle drift, it is the exact failure
clause 1 names -- a mane from the front and a crop from the side.

WHY genHair DID IT. `sideF` is not only a hairline; it is HOW A STYLE'S LENGTH IS
WRITTEN DOWN. SHOULDER LENGTH carries side 1.60 and LONG LOOSE carries side 2.40,
meaning the mass runs past the jaw. Two separate earlier fixes -- THE BACK IS THE
WHOLE SKULL (8/1) and PROFILE GETS THE WHOLE SKULL TOO (8/2) -- both wrote:

    var sideBot=(back||prof)?hBot:(hTop+fr(hTop,hBot,sideF)+S-1);

`hBot` is a FLOOR for a cropped style and a GUILLOTINE for a long one, and nothing
ever asked which. Both fixes meant "at least the whole skull". The code said
"exactly the skull". That is the branch-disagreement the law predicted, found by
building the ruler the law demanded.

*** AND THE GATE THAT WAS SUPPOSED TO HOLD CLAUSE 1 WAS GREEN THROUGH ALL OF IT. ***
It pinned the hair's own AREA between adjacent facings at 62%. Area is the wrong
ruler and it is wrong for a reason worth keeping: from the front you see a face and
two curtains, from behind you see a whole skull of hair, SO AREA SHOULD SWING.
Pinning a quantity that legitimately moves cannot catch a quantity that must not.
HOW FAR THE HAIR FALLS is a property of the object and not of the view. That is the
ruler clause 1 actually needed, and it is now the one that is pinned.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SECOND BUG UNDERNEATH IT: THE LENGTH DIAL WAS NOT REACHING THE RENDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SHOULDER LENGTH carries 1.60 and LONG LOOSE carries 2.40 -- a 50% bigger dial. Both
drew the SAME 11 pixels of fall.

Below the jaw the shared narrowing walks the two edges toward each other by up to
2*S over six rows, and a curtain on a real head measures about 2*S wide. The taper
ate the entire curtain, so every style's fall stopped at the same row whatever
length it asked for. The longer style was not longer, and had not been for as long
as the code has existed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AND backEx WAS THE ONE LENGTH IN THIS GENERATOR THAT NEVER LEARNED ABOUT 112
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every other measurement in genHair goes through fr(), which divides by S and
multiplies back. `backEx` was raw pixels. So when the rig doubled:

    WOLF CUT's back length   1.00 head-heights at 56  ->  0.64 at 112

The haircut got SHORTER because the canvas got BIGGER. In cells now, like everything
it sits beside. Same family as every other 4x units bug, and an identity change as
much as a scale one.

It was also an ON/OFF SWITCH on a thing that turns: full length on seven facings,
zero on the front, so a fall appeared out of nowhere in the single notch between SE
and E. It now ramps -- half head-on, three-quarters at the diagonals, all of it
side-on and from behind. Which is his own 8/1 ruling: "with more of the longer
hairstyles even from the front I would like to see you know a couple pixels of hair
... even if it is in the back of the head."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
*** I DREW IT WRONG TWICE BEFORE I DREW IT RIGHT, AND ONLY LOOKING CAUGHT EITHER ***
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. FIRST REPAIR: widen the curtain to survive the taper. That walked the two
   curtains inward until they MERGED. Long hair rendered as one rat's tail down the
   sternum and WOLF CUT as a SOLID SHEET ACROSS THE CHEST -- the exact thing the
   curtain rule exists to forbid. Every number I had said the length was now
   correct, and the length WAS correct.

2. SECOND REPAIR: anchor the fall to the head instead. span() at the jaw is the
   narrowest row of the whole skull, so the curtains pinched in at the jawline and
   the fall picked up again at full head width underneath it. The hair DETACHED
   from the head and floated off the sides of his shoulders with a gap where his
   ears are. Again every number said fine.

Hair leaves the head at the temples and falls straight down past a jaw narrower
than it is. That is the third version and it is the one that ships. VERIFY ON THE
REAL SURFACE is not a formality; it is the only instrument that caught either of
these, and this is the second time this week that has been true.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AND A FIX THAT TRADES ONE RULING FOR ANOTHER IS NOT A FIX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Long falls are long straight edges. Clause 3 (8/1, LOCKED) -- hair is little off
shapes, never straight lines -- went 19.5% -> 21.1% the moment the falls got their
length back.

NOT SOLVED BY LOOSENING THE PIN. Free-hanging hair MOVES MORE than hair pressed to
a skull: wob() fires on one row in four, which is a hairline lying on bone and far
too calm for a fall swinging in air. A second independent step on the same cell-row
grid, on both edges of each curtain and on every facing's fall, took it back to
19.5% and made the falls read as hair instead of ribbon. Deterministic, seeded off
the style name, so an NPC never shimmers.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MEASURED, BEFORE AND AFTER (scale-free: head-heights of fall)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    SHOULDER LENGTH   S 0.50 / E 0.00 / N 0.00   ->   0.50 on all eight
    LONG LOOSE        S 0.50 / E 0.00 / N 0.00   ->   1.27-1.30 on all eight
    and LONG LOOSE is now genuinely longer than SHOULDER LENGTH, which it was not
    before on ANY facing.

    worst one-notch length change, all 15 styles   1.273  ->  0.300
    a sheet of hair across the chest, front facings   -   ->  0 (pinned)
    hair reaching the jaw from behind and side-on    -   ->  15 of 15, 5 facings
    clause 3 straightness, six open-air facings    19.5%  ->  19.5% (unmoved)

The 0.300 that remains is ASH SWEEP turning SW to W, and it is a back-of-head length
coming into view as he turns. That is anatomy, not a defect, and it is named as such
rather than engineered away.
