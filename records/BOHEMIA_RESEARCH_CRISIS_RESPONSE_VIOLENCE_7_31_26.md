## THE FINDING, AND IT IS THE ANSWER TO WHAT HE ASKED

> **VIOLENCE IS TRAUMATIC WHEN IT MAKES WORK AND COSTS YOU SOMETHING. IT IS GORY
> WHEN IT JUST LOOKS WET. THOSE ARE TWO DIFFERENT DIALS AND MOST GAMES ONLY HAVE
> THE SECOND ONE.**

Crisis Response is disturbing because of two structural decisions, and neither of
them is an art decision:

**1. THE GOAL IS NOT TO KILL. IT IS TO NOT KILL.** "Least possible loss of life as
the desired outcome" inverts the entire incentive of a shooter. Every trigger pull
is a **failure you are choosing**, not a score you are farming. You cannot make
killing feel bad while also making it the win condition — that is the contradiction
almost every "mature" shooter dies on, showing you a sad cutscene about the men you
had to shoot to reach it.

**2. A HURT PERSON IS A CLOCK, NOT A CORPSE.** "Working against the clock to save
them" means the shot does not resolve when the body falls — **it opens a task.** The
aftermath is the gameplay. That is what turns a kill from a full stop into a
consequence you have to carry, and it is why the violence sticks.

And the third, which is the mechanism under the feeling:

**3. THE BODY IS A LEGIBLE SYSTEM YOU WATCH FAIL.** Pulse, breathing, blood oxygen.
A number you understand, going the wrong way, in something that was a person a
second ago. **That is more upsetting than any amount of red**, because gore is a
texture and a falling pulse is a story with an ending you can see coming.

## WHY THIS EXACTLY MATCHES "NOT GORY BUT TRAUMATIC"

**All three of those work at zero gore.** A pulse readout falling is traumatic. A
scream that stops mid-breath is traumatic. Someone begging before you decide is
traumatic. **None of it needs a single red pixel.** Gore is the cheapest and
weakest tool in the box, and it is the only one most games reach for — which is
precisely his instinct, and the research backs it.

The corollary, and it is the part that will be tempting to skip: **traumatic
violence costs the PLAYER, not the corpse.** If a killing does not take something
from you — time, standing, a companion's opinion, a bandage you now do not have —
then it is spectacle no matter how it is drawn.

## WHAT IS IN THE FRAMES

**Frame 1 — the aftermath.** A prone body on grey concrete beside a green dumpster
and stacked tyres. Cream/pale shirt, dark patterned sleeves, gloves, treaded boots.
Limbs at slack angles, not a posed death. **A rifle lies on the ground well clear
of the hands.** A plain white-on-black caption box, centred near the top:
**"Drop your weapons and come out!"**

**Frame 2 — mid-ragdoll.** The body is **AIRBORNE and fully extended**, horizontal,
arms and legs trailing behind, head down and lost in shadow. A dropped weapon and
what reads as a bipod or stand tumbling below it. Letterboxed black bars top and
bottom.

## SIX NOTES

**1. THE DARKNESS DOES THE WORK, AND IT IS THE WHOLE "NOT GORY" TRICK.** Both
frames are nearly silhouette. If there is blood in either one I cannot see it. The
scene is so dark that the violence is legible as SHAPE and not as detail — which
means the trauma survives and the gore never arrives. **We already own this:**
CLUSTERED POWER (only ~12% lit), LIGHT = TERRITORY, nobody patrols the dark. Our
darkness is already canon and it is already the gore suppressor. That is a free win
nobody had connected.

**2. VALUE, NOT COLOUR, IS WHAT MAKES A BODY READABLE IN THE DARK.** The only thing
you can actually parse in either frame is the **pale shirt**. Everything else is a
dark mass. A body in a dark scene needs one bright garment value or it disappears.
Directly actionable for ART, and it is a wardrobe note as much as a lighting one.

**3. THE DROPPED WEAPON IS ITS OWN OBJECT.** In both frames the gun has left the
hands and landed separately. Cheap, and it says "this person is finished" without a
single frame of gore.

**4. SPEECH IS A PLAIN CAPTION, NOT A BUBBLE.** "Drop your weapons and come out!" is
white text on a black box. No portrait, no name plate, no styling. **That is the
delivery mechanism for the screaming and the begging he wants** — and it costs
nothing to build, which is the point. It is also a *demand*: the loud part of that
game is negotiation, not gore.

**5. *** THE ANTI-REFERENCE, AND IT IS THE MOST IMPORTANT NOTE HERE: THE CAMERA. ***
Crisis Response is a SIDE-SCROLLER. Both frames are flat side-on. Bohemia's
**45 DEGREE ART LAW** mandates the world's three-quarter view, and our own ragdoll
law says *"Falls respect all 8 facings... This is WHY the 8 directions exist."* A
side-scroller ragdoll has exactly one facing. **THE REGISTER PORTS. THE GEOMETRY
CANNOT.** Anyone who takes these frames as an art target instead of a feel target
will produce a flat side-on death and break a locked law.

**6. A TENSION WITH OUR OWN LOCKED RAGDOLL — FLAGGED, NOT DECIDED.** Frame 2 shows a
body that was **LAUNCHED**: airborne, fully extended, limbs trailing. Our locked
direction is the opposite register — *"a real body, not rubber. Weighty, stiff
joints, falls and settles like a corpse"*, with *"don't go overboard"* written into
it. Those are two different feels. **This is ANIMATION/CHARACTER's call and not
mine, and it is not a question for Paolo either until that lane looks at what is
already built** — the law says the Verlet ragdoll is BUILT and verified settling
across all 8 facings, so the honest next step is somebody comparing the live thing
to this frame, not a new law. **FLAGGED FOR ANIMATION/CHARACTER.**
