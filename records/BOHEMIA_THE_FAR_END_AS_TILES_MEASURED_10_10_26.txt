THE FAR END AS TILES -- MEASURED  (COOK, 10/10/26, rules 65, 60, 77)
==============================================================================

ROUND 1 PAINTED A SHEET AND NOTHING IN THE REPO PICKED IT UP. That is where
this round started: a thing nobody can use is not shipped, whatever its guards
said. So the first job was to find out why, in the shipped map, not by guessing.

*** WHAT THE MAP ACTUALLY DRAWS (engine/bohemia_valleymap.js) ***
  paintCell   one overmap cell into 128 x 128. A district kit paints its real
              block grid. A ROAD cell FILLS ALL 128 x 128 WITH TARMAC and rules
              lane lines on it. A TERRAIN cell -- mountain, desert, wash, every
              piece of land in the valley -- fills ONE FLAT COLOUR and throws
              ninety random translucent rectangles on top.
  paintValley "THE WHOLE VALLEY, one flat image at 1 pixel per cell."

  SO THE FAR END IS 9,216 PIXELS, scaled up to fill his phone.
  Battle Brothers is 2,073,600. THAT IS 0.44% OF BATTLE BROTHERS, and rule 60 is him
  asking for that number since 10/1. There is no mountain painting in the map at
  all, only a fill and a speckle, which is why the valley reads as a tan dome. It
  was never going to be fixed by a better flat colour.

AND THE ROAD CELL IS THE SAME CATEGORY ERROR THIS LANE MADE AND CAUGHT IN ITS OWN
FIRST SHEET LAST ROUND: a cell is 96 m of world saying a road runs through it,
not 96 m of tarmac. Filled solid, 2,543 arterials make a printed circuit board. I
fixed that in my sheet and then found the shipped map does it too.

*** SO ROUND 2 IS NOT ANOTHER SHEET. *** A single big picture asks RUN to throw
away a per-cell painter and composite an image: a rewrite on their side, and
seed-locked on mine. What drops into the painter they already have is TILES.

WHY SIXTEEN PIXELS A CELL, AND IT IS NOT A TASTE:
  96 cells x 16 px = 1,536, and 1,536 squared is 2,359,296.
  That is the smallest whole number of pixels per cell that puts THE WHOLE VALLEY
  ON ONE SCREEN AT BATTLE BROTHERS' COUNT. Fifteen just clears it; sixteen is the
  power of two above. It is 256 times the 9,216 the map draws now.

*** CUT FROM ONE PAINTING, WHICH IS WHAT MAKES THEM JOIN (rule 77). ***
  Paolo 10/5: "the tiles aren't speaking to each other... these things should
  conjoin easily like Legos". The cheap way is to draw each tile alone and hope.
  This paints the WHOLE valley once with round 1's own brushes -- ridged folded
  noise for the ranges shaded by slope under one sun, alluvial fans off the
  mountain mask, braided washes, the city's grain -- and CUTS it on the cell
  grid. Tiles cut from one painting meet by construction.
  AND THE GATE MEASURES IT ANYWAY, laid from the set alone: the seams jump
  26 down and 35 across, against 14 inside the tiles themselves.

THE SET: 1115 tiles over 565 signatures, one per (what the cell is, and what runs
out of its four sides). Edge types: range, basin, wash, water, road, spine,
runway, yard, field, city.

HOW RUN USES IT, IN ONE LINE: paintCell stamps the tile whose (self,N,E,S,W)
matches the cell and its four neighbours, instead of filling flat. Nothing about
the loop changes. The bank carries that sentence in its own how_to_use field.

[bb the overworld is battle brothers] reference/library/battle_brothers/
  01_WORLDMAP.md, the WORLD MAP and TERRAIN lines: BB's map is hand-authored
  terrain art at the screen's own pixels, and you read forest from hills from
  swamp across the room without a legend. Ours is 9,216 pixels of flat fill and
  speckle, which is the whole gap in one number. WHAT MOVES THAT THEIR PICTURE
  DOES NOT: BB's terrain tiles are drawn once for one fixed world. Ours are CUT
  FROM A PAINTING OF THE VALLEY THE ENGINE ROLLED, so the ranges that meet across
  a seam are the same range, and the same brushes cut a different set next seed.
