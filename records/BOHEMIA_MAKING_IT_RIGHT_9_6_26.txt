THE RULE, AND IT IS THE ROW'S OWN: THE PERSON WHO WAS WRONGED DECIDES.
makeRight takes ONE MIND. There is deliberately no valley-wide absolution and no
way for the actor to clear their own name -- witness() refuses to record a deed
about yourself, so there is no mind for a player to call this on. Somebody else
who watched the same thing is NOT settled by your forgiver's choice, because it
was never theirs to give away. That asymmetry is the feature, and it is the
claim that stops this being an eraser.

FORGIVEN IS NOT FORGOTTEN, AND THAT IS NOT SENTIMENT, IT IS THE DEFINITION.
The forgiveness literature treats forgiveness as a drop in negative motivation
toward the offender, never as forgetting. So the deed STAYS in the mind carrying
a `right` mark, forceOf returns 0 for it, and madeRightBy can always say what
happened and how. becauseOf still works. Nothing is deleted.

FOUR WORDS, ONE MECHANISM: settled / paid / forgiven / spared, which are four
stories for the same event -- whether they got something, whether they simply
let it go, whether you had them and did not. The word is recorded so a surface
can say which and so he can rule them apart later. All draft:true.

*** AND "WOULD THEY?" IS DERIVED, NOT A DIAL, WHICH IS THE BEST PART. ***
A button that always works is an eraser, but "how forgiving is this person"
would be a tuned number and numbers are his. So it is asked of what THEY saw:
they will square the old thing only if everything else they know about you
already comes out positive. Nothing new is invented -- it is this web's own
arithmetic with the grudge taken out of the sum.
  IT MAKES THE RIGHT LOOP. "Make it right" stops being a button and becomes the
  literal instruction: go and do something for the person you wronged, in front
  of them, and then ask. Somebody whose only knowledge of you is the bad thing
  will not forgive you, which is true and is the harder, better version.
  AND IT IS GROUNDED: the most consistent finding in that literature is that
  amends raise forgiveness while severity lowers it. Both fall out of this sum
  already -- a heavier grudge needs more good to outweigh it -- with no
  threshold anybody had to pick.

THE WEB LEARNS IT HAPPENED, on the paths the grudge travelled: carryRight drops
a SECOND-HAND grudge when the person it belongs to squares it ("she's squared it
with him" really is how a retold grudge dies), and stops dead at anybody who saw
it with their own eyes.
