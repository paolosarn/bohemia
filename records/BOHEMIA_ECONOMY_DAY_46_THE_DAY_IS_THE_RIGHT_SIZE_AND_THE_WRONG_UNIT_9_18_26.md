## 1. THE ANSWER: **RIGHT SIZE, WRONG UNIT.**

### THE SIZE IS RIGHT, AND IT IS IF ANYTHING SLIGHTLY SMALL

    CARACAS, without transit   commuters walk 7 km ONE WAY, over an hour, to work
                               = 14 km round trip, 778 lots, BEFORE DOING ANYTHING ELSE
    NEW ORLEANS, after Katrina survivors walked THREE MILES (4.8 km, 267 lots)
                               through four feet of water to the Superdome
    OURS                       8.6 km a day off the roads, 17.1 on them

**A Caracas commuter's round trip is 1.6 times our entire day, and it buys them nothing but
arriving at work.** So the day is not too big. Against the crisis record it is modest.

### AND THE UNIT IS WRONG, WHICH IS THE ACTUAL FINDING

**Nobody plans in kilometres. Every documented threshold in the planning record is a TIME,
and every one of them is under half an hour:**

    10-minute catchment              800 m       44 lots
    15-minute city                  1.25 km      69 lots
    20-minute neighbourhood          1.7 km      93 lots
    30-minute walk                   2.5 km     139 lots
    "walkable" to a school           800 m       44 lots (1.6 km for older children)

**The daily radius is not how far a person CAN go. It is the distance past which they stop
bothering** — and Caracas is the proof, because when the walk became an hour each way the
response was not that people walked further:

> *"Riders have a hard time affording them and **find themselves walking or staying
> home**."* Over **90% of buses not operating** in some regions.

**They walked, or they did not go.** The 7 km commute is the exceptional, resented,
once-a-day trip that makes people quit the job at the end of it. It is not the radius. **The
radius is about 2 km, and it is 139 lots.**

### SO, FOR RUN'S REACH CARD

Our player's **daily radius**, out and back, is **4.3 km off the roads and 8.6 km on them**
— he can reach **47% of the way across the valley and still get home.** That is correct and
generous. But a reach card that says *"you can walk 8.6 km"* is answering a question nobody
asks. **The card should show the half hour: about 2.5 km, 139 lots, 27% of the way across
the valley** — because that is the unit humans plan in and the only one they act on.
Everything past it should feel like the Caracas commute: **possible, resented, and a thing
you stop doing.**

## 2. *** TWO THINGS THE ARITHMETIC FOUND THAT NOBODY ASKED FOR ***

### ONE. A WHOLE GAME DAY OF WALKING IS FOUR MINUTES OF REAL TAPPING.

One step per beat at 120 BPM is **two steps a second.**

    a day off the roads    478 steps =  239 s =  4.0 minutes
    across the valley      511 steps =  256 s =  4.3 minutes
    a day on the roads     950 steps =  475 s =  7.9 minutes

**THE FIVE MINUTES is the only measure of the game (Paolo 9/13), and a whole game day of
walking is four of them.** That is not a complaint, it is a scale nobody has put next to the
law: **the demo's entire measure is about one game-day of walking, and crossing the valley
end to end is 4.3 minutes of it.** Every design argument about how far a day should reach is
also an argument about how much of his five minutes it eats.

For comparison, in the same unit: the **Caracas round trip is 6.5 minutes of tapping**, the
**half-hour radius is 1.2 minutes**, and the **10-minute catchment is 24 seconds.**

### TWO. THE BEAT AND THE STATED MULTIPLE DISAGREE BY TEN TIMES.

THE STEP IS A HOUSE says: *"ONE STEP IS ONE LOT = 18 m = about 2 minutes at walking speed,
**real-time travel 24x faster**."*

    2 game-minutes in half a real second      = 240x
    2 game-minutes at the stated 24x          = 5.0 real seconds per step
    one step per beat at 120 BPM              = 0.5 real seconds per step

**The two numbers cannot both describe steps taken on the beat.** Either a travel step is
not a beat step, or the 24x is the wrong multiple. **I am not saying which** — that is the
lane that owns the lattice, and rule 12 says measure before assuming. But the law was itself
corrected by measurement three days ago, and this is the same class of thing sitting one
line further down it.

## 3. THE DELIVERABLE FOR RUN'S CLOCK AND REACH CARD

> **THE DAY IS THE RIGHT SIZE. DO NOT CHANGE IT.** 8.6 km off the roads is modest against
> the record — a Caracas commuter without transit walks **7 km each way, over an hour,
> 14 km round trip, which is 1.6 times our whole day** and buys them only arriving at work;
> New Orleans survivors walked **three miles through four feet of water** to the Superdome.
> **What is wrong is the unit on the card.** Every threshold in the planning record is a
> **time**, never a distance, and all of them are under half an hour: a 10-minute catchment
> (800 m, **44 lots**), the 15-minute city (**69 lots**), the 20-minute neighbourhood
> (**93 lots**), the 30-minute walk (2.5 km, **139 lots**). **So the reach card should show
> the half hour, not the day:** about **139 lots**, which is **27% of the way across the
> valley** and **1.2 minutes of real tapping**. The day stays 478 lots and stays reachable;
> it is just not the number a person makes a decision with. **And the far market should feel
> like the Caracas commute**, because that is what the record actually documents: when the
> walk became an hour each way people did not walk further, **they walked or they stayed
> home**, with 90% of the buses gone. **A daily radius is not the distance a person can
> cover, it is the distance past which they stop bothering** — and building that is free,
> because it is already true: 4.3 km out and back is 47% of the valley, so half the map is
> already "not worth it today" and nothing on screen says so.

**The project-level hole, round 32 of naming it.** These gates check that a part does what
it says. Nothing checks that two parts agree, that a part keeps working for as long as the
game lasts, that it is the right part to have, or that the parts form a loop that closes.

distance, a walking speed and a real-time multiple, and **the multiple does not follow from
the other three.** A gate that re-derived every stated ratio from the values beside it would
have caught it the day the law landed — and that law had *already* been corrected once by
measurement, three days ago, which is the best argument there is for such a gate.
