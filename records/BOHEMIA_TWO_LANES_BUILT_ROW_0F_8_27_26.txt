TWO LANES BUILT ROW 0F AT THE SAME TIME, AND NEITHER COULD SEE THE OTHER
8/27/26 - RUN lane. A coordination finding, written by the lane that lost.
================================================================================

I built the feedback card. So did somebody else, in the same hours, in the same
file, for the same backlog row.

I found out when a rebase produced a conflict whose two sides were both
implementations of row 0f:

    <<<<<<< HEAD
      /* __CITY_FEEDBACK__ -- AND WHICH BUILD, WHICH THE CITY HAS NEVER KNOWN. */
    =======
      /* __THE_NOTE_KNOWS_THE_BUILD__: and the build, for the note to carry. */
    >>>>>>>

Two lanes, four hours apart, independently discovered that the city could not
read the build stamp off its cross-origin parent, and independently fixed it by
putting the build on the boot handshake that already existed. Same bug, same
diagnosis, same fix, same day, twice.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE CALL: THEIRS WINS AND MINE IS DELETED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Not reconciled. Not merged. DELETED, entire: the patch tools, the gate, the
record, the card. This is the ONE ID, ONE WHOLE PERSON rule from 8/27 applied to
myself -- when two mechanisms exist for the same thing, THE YOUNGER ONE IS
DELETED, not reconciled -- and theirs is on main, so mine is the younger one.

AND IT IS ALSO THE RIGHT CALL ON MERIT, WHICH MATTERS MORE THAN THE PRECEDENCE.
Theirs is better than mine in three ways I did not think of:

1. IT HAS A BLACK BOX. engine/bohemia_blackbox.js records the session as it
   happens -- how long they really played, across how many sittings, which beat
   they got to, and HOW LONG THEY STALLED THERE. Mine asked the tester to
   remember. Theirs does not have to ask.
2. ITS FIRST QUESTION IS BEHAVIOURAL, NOT EMOTIONAL. "WOULD YOU SEND THIS TO
   SOMEBODY?" with its own note: "People who love a thing send it to somebody;
   people who are being kind say they had fun." That is a better instrument than
   my ten emotion words, because it asks about an ACTION rather than a feeling,
   and an action is harder to be polite with.
3. ITS CONFUSION QUESTION GIVES THEM A PLACE. "WHERE DID YOU NOT KNOW WHAT TO
   DO?", with the options built from that tester's own session, against my free
   text box. Their note says it: "Not 'was it confusing', which is a yes or a no
   and neither one is a place."

It also persists across sittings, captures the device string raw rather than a
label somebody guessed, and delays its door five seconds so the ending's last
line has time to land before anything asks the player a question.

I was proud of mine four hours ago. Theirs is better and that is the end of it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ONE THING MINE HAD THAT THEIRS DOES NOT, HANDED OVER RATHER THAN INSERTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Their black box measures how long the session REALLY was. It never asks how long
it FELT.

That gap is worth something specific, and it is the one piece of research I would
hand across:

  THE PLAYTEST LITERATURE names "did you have fun?" as shallow enough to delete
  outright, and offers a replacement: ASK HOW LONG IT FELT. Felt short means they
  were enjoying it. Felt long means they were confused, bored or awkward.
  DURATION NEGLECT (already researched in this repo, in the demo gap list) says
  how long it actually was barely registers in memory.
  PUT THOSE TOGETHER: felt-minus-real is not an opinion. It is the closest thing
  to an objective read a feedback card can take, and the tester hands it over
  without knowing they did. THE BLACK BOX ALREADY HOLDS THE "REAL" HALF. One
  tapped question -- 5 / 10 / 20 / 30 / 45+ -- completes it.

I AM NOT ADDING IT. It is their system, they are mid-flight in it, and reaching
into somebody else's card to insert a fourth question is exactly the collision
this record is about, committed a second time on purpose. It is written down
here and in the handoff, and it is theirs to take or leave.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT ACTUALLY WENT WRONG, AND IT IS NOT EITHER LANE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ONE SYSTEM, ONE SESSION is the law and BOTH LANES OBEYED THE PART THEY COULD SEE.
Neither of us edited a system we knew somebody else was in. The failure is that
NEITHER OF US COULD SEE IT.

  - The demo gap list routed 0f to RUN. Both of us are working RUN-adjacent
    surfaces, and the row does not say which session holds it.
  - The handoff is the shared state, and it is written AT THE END of a turn. Both
    cards were four hours of work each, so both existed long before either
    handoff entry did.
  - A backlog row is a claim on WORK, not a claim on a FILE, and nothing in the
    repo turns one into the other.

*** THE COST WAS ABOUT FOUR HOURS OF MY TURN AND IT WOULD HAVE BEEN WORSE IF THE
RUN HAD SHIPPED. *** Two feedback cards in one build, both firing off the same
ending, would have been a worse outcome than either card alone -- and the merge
was clean enough on the surface that a lane in a hurry could have resolved the
conflict "both sides" and shipped exactly that.

WHAT WOULD HAVE CAUGHT IT, cheapest first, and none of these is mine to impose:
  1. CLAIM THE ROW BEFORE BUILDING IT, not after. A one-line write to the handoff
     at the START of a build, naming the row and the file, costs nothing and is
     visible to every lane that fetches.
  2. A REUSE CHECK AGAINST origin/main, NOT JUST THE LOCAL TREE. I ran the right
     check -- it is what found the encounter director this morning and what
     stopped me rewriting the ending this afternoon -- but I ran it against MY
     checkout, which was already stale. `git fetch` first and the grep would have
     found __CITY_FEEDBACK__ before I wrote a line.
  3. That second one is the actionable one and I am adopting it in this lane
     regardless of what anybody else does: BEFORE BUILDING ANYTHING, FETCH, THEN
     GREP MAIN.
