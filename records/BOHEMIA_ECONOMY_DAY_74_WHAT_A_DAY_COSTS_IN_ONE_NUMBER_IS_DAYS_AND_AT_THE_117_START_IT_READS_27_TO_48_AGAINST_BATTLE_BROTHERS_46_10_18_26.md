## 2. THE REAL RECORD: WHEN PEOPLE UNDER PRESSURE NEED ONE NUMBER, THEY PICK DAYS
- **THE ARMY'S UNIT IS A DAY OF SUPPLY.** A 'day of supply' is 'a quantity (as of food, clothing or
  ammunition) taken as the average daily requirement of a body of troops in a given situation'
  (Merriam-Webster); Australian Army planning defines it by 'the daily consumption of a known
  dependency' and uses it 'to determine the broad stocking levels of the force' (as quoted in search
  extracts; the DoD Dictionary entry itself was not opened from here). Stocks are counted in days,
  not in tins.
- **SHOPS DO THE SAME DIVISION:** days of supply is stock on hand divided by average daily demand, the
  standard inventory reading (any operations glossary; the arithmetic is the definition).
- **THE CLINIC IN 2020.** The CDC/NIOSH PPE Burn Rate Calculator (cdc.gov/niosh, released in the COVID
  response, a spreadsheet and a phone app) took box counts of gowns, gloves and masks and returned an
  estimate of how many days the supply would last from the average used per day, for clinic staff
  counting boxes in a crisis. It needs two days of counts before it gives a rate. The one number the
  federal government handed frightened people was DAYS.
- **THE KIT EVERY HOUSEHOLD IS TOLD TO KEEP:** one gallon of water a person a day, for at least three
  days (Ready.gov, as quoted by the state and university pages that cite it; a 2009 archived ready.gov
  checklist says the same). A gallon is 3.8 litres; our water need is 4 (section 3c). THREE DAYS is the
  floor the record writes down most often.
- **THE OTHER NAME FOR IT: RUNWAY.** A business counts its cash in months: cash divided by the burn.
  It is the same division with money on top and time underneath.
THE LESSON: the one number is TIME, never money and never a count; the burn is the average of what
was really used; and the line most often drawn under it is three days.

## 3. OURS, MEASURED ON MAIN: THE NUMBER IS HALF BUILT IN TWO PLACES THAT DO NOT KNOW EACH OTHER
**3a. THE LIVE MAP (slices/BOHEMIA_CITY_WORLD.html, the surface the demo is cut from): EVERYTHING
COSTS ONE.** At nightfall the day eats ONE food ('day:ate' spends one 'resources', 'the people who
depend on you ate'), the night burns ONE battery for every lit circuit you hold ('night:power'), and a
finished job pays ONE (payForToday). The amount 'is 1 and cannot be passed in' (his EVERYTHING COSTS
ONE, 8/15). So on the live map the FOOD count already IS a count of days: a day eats one. The bar
draws the six as counts (sixPlate): batteries and food read the purse, and MEDS, ROUNDS, TAPE and
WATER 'have no player count anywhere in the game, so they read null and draw a dash'. One more thing
measured: 'fight:plate' ('the plate you wore at the bell is spent') also spends 'resources', so the
bar's FOOD is eaten by the day AND by every fight; it reads as days of food only on a day with no
fight.

**3b. THE STASH SCREEN (RUN TWO, slices/BOHEMIA_STASH_SCREEN.html): THE DAYS ARE ALREADY THERE, ONE
PER PILE, AND NO PAGE OF THE GAME OPENS IT.** It divides each pile by its rate and prints 'N DAYS'
beside it: food (headcount x 2 x terrain), meds (one a day per open injury), batteries (the wage,
each man's daily wage / 10, paid at noon); rounds read in shots, tape in repair. Red at 2 days or
fewer ('.dl.low', the only colour rule on main). Water: 'NO RATE in the data yet (ECONOMY owes it);
shown as a count, never a made-up day'. git grep: no slice but the screen itself names it, so the
days a player could read are behind a door the game never opens.

**3c. THE WATER RATE THE SCREEN WAITS FOR IS ON MAIN, IN THE WRONG FILE. PROVES US WRONG (this lane's
debt).** engine/bohemia_economy.js GOODS.water: need 4.0 litres a person a day ('3L sedentary, 6-8
desert labor; 4 mixed'), and gates/economy_gate.js holds it inside the real band, 3 to 6 litres. Day
69 priced four litres at ONE battery at the counter. records/target/bb/stash_rates.json, which is THIS
lane's file ('ECONOMY [what the stash is]'), carries food, tools, medicine and ammo and no water key,
and engine/bohemia_stash.js daySpend() returns food, medicine, ammo 0 and tools 0: no water and no
wage. The engine's own day leaves out the two lines that are half of Day 72's week (21 of 42).

## 4. THE NUMBER: DAYS
**THE DEFINITION, IN HIS WORDS' SIZE:** how many more nightfalls the crew gets through at today's
spend, if nothing else comes in.
**HOW IT IS WORKED, from what is already on main (nothing new is counted):**
1. TODAY'S SPEND: food a day = headcount x 2 x the ground (stash_rates.json foodByTerrain); water a
   day = headcount x 4 litres (GOODS.water.need); meds a day = one per open injury (only when somebody
   is hurt); the wage a day = the crew's wages (zero when nobody draws one).
2. CARRIED FIRST: the stash lasts S days, the fewest among the piles being spent (food / food a day,
   water / water a day, meds / meds a day when somebody is hurt).
3. THEN THE PURSE AT THE COUNTER: once a pile is gone, its day is bought at Day 69's counter (a ration,
   one man one day, ONE; four litres ONE; a dose ONE) out of the batteries, and the wage comes out of
   the batteries every day. DAYS ends on the first day the batteries cannot cover what that day needs.
   Closed form when the piles run out together: DAYS = S + floor((B - wage x S) / (wage + counter cost
   of a day)), or floor(B / wage) if the wage runs the purse dry inside the stash. The exact one is the
   clock's own day loop, which is what the screen should call (it already has day()).
4. ON THE LIVE MAP (3a), the same rule is two divisions: DAYS = the smaller of FOOD (a day eats one)
   and BATTERIES / lit circuits held (a night burns one each); with no circuit held the batteries do
   not run down at night, and the number is the food.
**WHY GROSS, WITH NO INCOME COUNTED.** The number never lies on a day nobody picks you (Day 49: the
corner does not pick every man every day), and every battery that does come in makes the number jump
on the spot. The jump is rule 117(c)'s visible win: the number going UP when he does something good.
The clinic's calculator does the same: it divides by what was used, not by what might arrive.
**WHY DAYS AND NOT BATTERIES.** Section 2: everyone under pressure picks time. And it is Battle
Brothers' own move (food shown in days) taken one step further: ours folds the crowns-against-wages sum
he does in his head into the same number. Rule 117(b): fewer numbers shown, one clear next thing.

## 6. WHEN IT TURNS RED
**THE PROPOSAL (one colour, no amber; TUNING holds the digit):** the number is RED when
(a) DAYS is 3 or fewer (the 72-hour floor, section 2), or
(b) on the map, the CARRIED days S are fewer than the trip to the nearest counter plus one (you would
    run dry on the road before you could buy; the trip is the map's own days: Day 73's trips run 0.12
    to 1.09 days on WORLD's land, and the far towns past the rim 1.4 to 3.9, the land file's 'beyond').
Otherwise it is plain. It never blinks and never sounds before (a) or (b) is true; the second votes
(Paolo 9/23: NOTHING IS FORCED AND NOTHING HAPPENS IN THE FIRST SECOND) mean a crew should not open the
game already red, which only the start purse can fix (RUN; Day 72).
**THE CANDIDATES FOR (a):** 3 (the record's floor) or 2 (the stash screen's '.dl.low' on main). Three
is the easier one: it warns a day sooner.
**AGAINST BATTLE BROTHERS:** it has no colour; the warning is a man's face going sour, then desertion
rolled every four hours, and an unpaid man walks after about three days. Ours: red at 3 days before
anything runs out, then TUNING's lever (five unpaid days, the face sours a day before). So a man walks
at the earliest 8 days after the number turns red, where Battle Brothers gives about 3 and no colour.
That is rule 117(a), measured.
**THE WIN THE OTHER WAY (rule 117(c)).** When the number climbs out of red (a stack bought, a job paid,
a camp's road cleared), the colour going back to plain is the win, and it is the only time the bar
should make a sound about money. UI owns how it looks and sounds.
