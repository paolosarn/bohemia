## WHY THE SHOP IS REDONE INSTEAD OF A NEW KIND

the family's rules existed. It breaks three of them: it shows a ROOF PLAN (the study page: his pack never draws one),
it floats (DIRECTION 10/10: "no shadow, the houses float"), and its top-to-face proportion was a guess. A placeholder
is never edited (rule 101c), so the shop is a NEW asset on the family's code and the flat one is superseded (rule 92,
redoOf on the card). He has not voted on the flat shop; the card quotes the words it is redone on.

## HOW THE PACK DID IT, APPLIED (rule 100c; the study page records/BOHEMIA_COOK4_HOW_THE_PACK_DID_IT_THE_PLACES_10_10_26.md)
- THE ROOF IS THE MEASURED ONE, AGAINST ONE STOREY. The three ratios are read off his #30 and #26 by the tool on every
  run. They are taken against a single storey's wall, not the two-storey wall: at 45 degrees the roof's seen depth is
  the building's depth, and a two-storey shop is no deeper than a one-storey ranch. Taken against two storeys the roof
  would read nine metres deep. The tool asserts the roof height equals the ranch's.
- THE SLATE EAVE UNDER THE SLATE ROOF. The family's shared roof function lays the clay eave strip under every field,
  which is wrong under slate (the garage ranch carries it and stays as it is, rule 101c). The shop has its own slate
  roof with his slate strip #15 at the foot; the next family member in slate takes the same function.
- EVERY SURFACE IS HIS TILE AT ITS OWN PIXELS (rules 82a, 105, 108); A PART THAT EXISTS ONCE IS LAID ONCE: the door,
  the three upstairs windows, the awning, the roll-down, the chimney. Only the wall, the roof and the string course run.
- OUR PAINT IS WEATHER, WEAR AND LIGHT ONLY: the shade under the eave, under the awning and under the string course,
  the one-pixel brown-black outline, the contact and cast shadows.
- ANALOG HORROR, THE WRONG THINGS FROM THE WORLD'S STATE: the flat above has one window barred and one boarded; the
  shop is half shut, its roll-down down beside an open door; the chimney has no smoke.
- WHAT THE PACK NEVER DID AND WE DID NOT EITHER: no roof plan (the flat shop's was the last), no gable end facing the
  viewer, no thatch.

## THE THREE DIFFERENCES FROM THE TWIN (rule 82, on the card)
1. HEIGHT: his pack house is one storey; ours is two with a string course between, the roof at one storey's ratio.
2. FRONT: his has one door and one window; ours 3.8 m of glass under his stall's awning, a door a man fits, one roll-down.
3. GROUND: his floats on a clear tile, as the flat shop did; this one sits on a contact and a cast shadow.

## WHAT IS NOT DONE, SAID PLAINLY

- THE AWNING HIDES THE DOOR'S TRANSOM: at 2.45 m the canvas hangs over the glass and the entry, as a real awning does;
