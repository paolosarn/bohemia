# THE GROUND TELLS YOU WHAT HAPPENED
COOK lane, [footprints] round 2, 9/23/26. Session cook-mce6r5.
His words, from the first votes: "make pixel art of footsteps that lead you to the conclusion."

## WHAT ROUND 1 LEFT UNDONE, AND THE DEFECT IT SHIPPED WITH
Round 1 (207df29d) cooked the boot: one sole, 9 x 20 px, pressed into four grounds as a
DEPRESSION lit by the same north-west sun as every other tile, borrowing the ground's own
colours and inventing none. That half is right and stands.

The second half of his sentence was not built. A line of identical boots crossing a swatch
is a texture; it leads nowhere. A trail only says something when the marks in it DISAGREE:
this bit walked, this bit ran, here it stopped, here it went down, here something was
pulled away.

AND ROUND 1 SHIPPED A DEFECT I FOUND BY LOOKING, NOT BY MEASURING. Its walk turns through
about 230 degrees and EVERY BOOT IN IT POINTS NORTH, because the print was stamped at the
path position and never rotated to the heading. Halfway along that trail the boots walk
sideways; by the end they walk backwards. Every guard in that tool was green. A trail whose
feet point the wrong way does not merely look wrong, it says the wrong thing, which is the
one failure this row exists to prevent. Fixed here: a print points along the line of travel
with a real toe-out angle.

## THE FIVE MARKS
All five are CUTS of round 1's boot. Not one new foot is drawn, which is also how the real
thing works: a running print is the same sole with only its front in the ground.

  WALKED     the full sole; feet on two lines 0.40 of a boot apart; toes out 10 degrees;
             a step of 2.6 boot lengths
  STOOD      the pair, side by side, pressed near full depth, one of them twisted
  RAN        the ball and toe only, the heel never lands; the step at 5.2 boot lengths;
             the two lines closed to 0.07; ground thrown backward behind each push-off
  WENT DOWN  the same boot pressed through the angles a pivot passes through, which is how
             a crescent really forms
  DRAGGED    two continuous furrows, six px wide, a heel mark at the head of each

THE PROPORTIONS ARE REAL AND THEY ARE IN BOOT LENGTHS, not pixels, so they stay true at any
size: a 0.70 m walking step against a 0.27 m boot is 2.6; a 0.10 m walking base (inside the
measured 8-12 cm adult range) is 0.40. Round 1's 52 px stride and 8 px straddle already sat
exactly on those ratios, so no number moved. What was missing was the ANGLE.

## THE NUMBER THAT IS ALLOWED TO BE QUOTED
"Pixels pressed" is not it. Round 1 quoted 1,060 of them on prints that were invisible. The
fight-floor card he voted DOWN quoted 9,635 colours going to 57 on a change he could not
see. Both numbers were true and neither was the question. The question is HOW FAR THE GROUND
MOVES WHERE THE MARK IS, against the spread that ground already has:

  ground                        the mark moves it by    the ground's own spread
  dust                          23.2 of 255             16.3
  the sidewalk                  40.1 of 255             28.1
  the road (asphalt)            27.7 of 255             38.2

AND THAT THIRD ROW IS THE FINDING. ON CRACKED ASPHALT THE MARK IS SMALLER THAN THE ROAD'S
OWN NOISE (27.7 against 38.2), so a footprint does not read on the road and no amount of
depth will make it, because the depth available is capped by ONE PALETTE PER FAMILY. This
agrees with the picture, it agrees with the reference (prints survive in soft ground), and
it is why the card shows dust and sidewalk and not asphalt. The tool measures the road
anyway and prints it, because the honest answer belongs on the record whether or not it is
the picture he is shown. The tool now REFUSES a ground whose mark moves it by less than 12.

## THE CARD, AND THE RULE IT WAS BUILT TO
A CARD MUST SHOW THE THING DOING ITS JOB AT THE SIZE HE SEES IT (the rule taken from his
"No difference" on cook-the-ground-you-fight-on-9-21). Laying the whole trail out at once
gave a picture that was 80% empty ground with specks in it, because a real trackway IS long:
five walking steps alone are thirteen boot lengths. Showing it whole means showing it small,
and small is what he voted down. So the card does what a person does at a scene: it walks the
trail and stops at each thing. Four windows on ONE trail, every window sized to hold its own
segment, all four at the same magnification so the sizes can be compared, then the same four
on the sidewalk.

THE CARD'S GROUND IS ONE TILE REPEATED, on purpose: the mark is the subject and a mixed
ground under it is a second variable in a picture asking one question. The numbers above are
taken on the REAL mixed ground, which is the harder test.

## FOUR THINGS THE PICTURE CAUGHT THAT THE NUMBERS DID NOT
1. THE STORY RAN OFF THE FIELD and the pivot landed outside the picture entirely. The press
   reported it as zero pixels, which reads exactly like a mark that does not work. There is
   now a geometry guard that fails with the coordinates instead.
2. A ONE-PIXEL MASK IS ALL LIP. The press decides light from shape: a pixel with open ground
   to its north or west is the sunlit lip. Pressing a furrow dot by dot therefore drew the
   whole furrow in the ground's BRIGHTEST tone, a raised thread instead of a scored groove.
   Built as one mask it gets a bright lip and a dark body.
3. AND THE WIDTH, NOT THE DEPTH, IS WHAT MAKES A FURROW READ. On a diagonal, BOTH long edges
   answer yes to the lip test, so a thin furrow comes out almost all lip: two bright rails.
   It took two tries to see that as a width problem. Six pixels is a dragged heel's real
   width and leaves the body outweighing the lip two to one.
4. THE LAYOUT WAS CALCULATED AND THE CALCULATION WAS WRONG by a factor of two on the wide
   panels, so they drew on top of each other. The canvas is now measured, not calculated:
   draw onto a tall sheet and cut it where the drawing ends.

None of those four is a second attempt at the same thing. Each is a different defect found
by looking at the render, and the count is worth keeping because the pattern is now thirteen
rounds old in this lane: THE NUMBER AND THE PICTURE EACH CATCH WHAT THE OTHER CANNOT, and a
round that only runs one of them ships blind.

## THE WEAKEST PANEL, NAMED
HE RAN is the thinnest of the four and it is thin for a true reason: a running print is
SMALLER than a walking one (85% here) and the gap between prints is twice as long, so a
window big enough to show the gap is mostly ground. That is what a real run leaves. If he
wants the run to read harder, the honest lever is the thrown material behind the push-off,
not a bigger print, because a bigger print is the thing the reference says is wrong.

## REFERENCE CHECK
Four new entries in the library, reference/library/tracks/INDEX.md, index rebuilt 89 -> 93:
  TRK-01 the trackway's four measurements (forensic podiatry / gait): step length, walking
         base 8-12 cm, foot progression angle.
  TRK-02 walking against running in the impression itself (Naples, Making tracks): the gap
         grows, the toe drives deeper, the heel lightens or never registers, so a running
         print is SHORTER than a walking one.
  TRK-03 trail width: the two lines of feet CLOSE as speed rises until a runner's prints
         land on one line, so the reader sees the pace change in the pattern first.
  TRK-04 the marks that are not prints: drag furrows, the pivot crescent, and material
         thrown backward; the continuous marks carry the EVENT, the prints carry the person.
Plus AH-01 (R1 the ordinary frame one wrong thing, R3 the long hold, R4 the light was in the
room), TG-03 and TG-04 for the families and their ramps.

## ANALOG HORROR LINE (rule 20 section 9)
The horror is that the person is not in the frame. Every mark is a still fact left behind on
empty ground, lit by the same sun as the kerb beside it, and the picture is finished by the
viewer working out what happened to somebody who is no longer there.
