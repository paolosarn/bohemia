# BB STUDY, DAY 3 — DUMB ORDERS, AND WHAT GUNS DO TO A CHESS GAME
# (8/28/26, coordinator. The biggest day. Two halves: the order language
# for nineteen bodies you never touch, and the problem he named himself.)

## PART A — THE ORDERS

## A1. HIS DESIGN HAS A NAME, IT IS TWO HUNDRED YEARS OLD, AND IT IS A
## MILITARY DOCTRINE
He described it as "a GO, SLOW DOWN type of vibe... so simple I barely
wanted it to be kind of like the gambit system."
**THAT IS AUFTRAGSTAKTIK — MISSION COMMAND.** The defining principle is
stated the same way everywhere it is taught: **you give INTENT, NOT
DETAILED INSTRUCTIONS.** A commander says what must be achieved; the
subordinate decides how. It came out of Prussia's reforms after Napoleon
beat them in 1806, and the lesson they drew is a sentence he could have
written himself:
> **"Centralized, ornamental command loses to speed and initiative."**
### *** THE FINDING THAT CHALLENGES WHAT WE BELIEVE ***
I have been treating the simple order system as a CONCESSION — dumb it
down so normal people can play. **THE DOCTRINE SAYS THE OPPOSITE.**
Decentralised intent is not the weaker system that is easier to use. It
is **THE STRONGER SYSTEM WHEN THINGS ARE FAST AND CONFUSING**, and the
reason given is exactly our situation:
> "In complex, fast environments, **the person at the edge often has the
> best information** to solve the problem."
In a twenty-a-side fight the player CANNOT see what is happening on the
far flank. Telling that body exactly what to do would be worse
information, not better. **SO THE SIMPLE ORDERS ARE NOT A DUMBING DOWN.
THEY ARE THE CORRECT COMMAND SYSTEM FOR THE SCALE HE CHOSE**, and the
deep-rule alternative would be a commander micromanaging men he cannot
see, which is the thing armies spent two centuries learning not to do.

## A2. AND THE DOCTRINE ANSWERS HIS "NO MANUAL CONTROL" WITHOUT
## CONTRADICTING IT
He ruled there is no take-over-a-body button, ever. The obvious worry is
what happens when a squadmate does something stupid.
**THE DOCTRINE ALREADY HAS THE ANSWER AND IT IS NOT MANUAL CONTROL:**
> Commanders "**would intervene when subordinates were doing something
> clearly unsound.** They would add or delete assigned tasks, or **change
> their intent**, as they saw fit."
**YOU NEVER TAKE OVER A BODY. YOU CHANGE THE INTENT.** That is one order,
it costs one beat, and it is historically what real commanders did inside
this exact system. It is also the recourse that makes no-manual-control
survivable rather than infuriating.
And the doctrine's other caveat is a design note we should keep: it varied
by "group maturity" — telling the green ones, delegating to the veterans.
**A NEW BODY MIGHT NEED A NARROWER ORDER THAN A VETERAN.** That is a
progression axis that costs no interface.

## A3. WHAT THIS MAKES THE ORDER SET
Not rules with targets and priorities. **INTENTS.** A handful of words a
person understands with no tutorial: go, hold, slow down, fall back, get
behind them, stay with me. **ONE SYSTEM DRIVES EVERYBODY** — his ruling —
so the enemy is running intents too, which means reading the enemy is the
same skill as commanding your own, and nothing has to be taught.
**AND LEGIBILITY IS SOLVED BY THE SIMPLICITY ITSELF.** With four words on
screen the player can always answer "why did they do that," because he
can read his own order at a glance. A deep rule language would have made
39 bodies unreadable, which is the failure mode that kills systems like
this.

## PART B — THE GUNS

## B1. THE PROBLEM HE NAMED
Battle Brothers is chess **because it is melee**: a front line, adjacency,
zone of control, a reason to close the distance. **GUNS DELETE THE FRONT
LINE.** If everyone has range, nobody has to walk anywhere.

## B2. *** THE STANDARD FIX IS COVER AND OVERWATCH, AND ITS DOCUMENTED
## FAILURE IS THE EXACT THING HE IS TRYING TO ESCAPE ***
The genre's answer to guns is cover plus a hold-fire-until-they-move
mechanic. It works, and it has a well-known cost, and the cost has a
name: **TURTLING.**
The design literature is blunt about what turtling does:
> "**Matches take longer than optimal**... if both players are playing
> defensively, game lengths can go on for a really long time. Matches are
> **less interesting, often un-interactive early on**, and endgames tend
> to be a huge sudden cascade that **involves little decision-making**."
And on the pacing mechanism specifically: giving units the option to
spend more of their turn aiming "**encourages a slower sense of pace**,"
because players hoard actions to shoot better.
**READ THAT AGAINST HIS SENTENCE.** He wants "one battle, not a 40 minute
long chess match." Turtling produces long, un-interactive matches that end
in one sudden cascade. **SO ADDING COVER AND OVERWATCH WOULD TRADE A MELEE
CHESS MATCH FOR A SLOWER SHOOTING ONE.** That is the trap on this half of
the day and it is worth the whole day to have found it.

## B3. THE HISTORICAL ANSWER IS BETTER, AND IT IS THE ONE ALREADY IN OUR
## LAW
When firearms arrived, armies did not abandon the spear and hide. **They
put the two together, and each arm covered the other's weakness:** massed
fire from a distance, and a solid block of long spear points keeping
cavalry and chargers off the shooters. That arrangement held for two
centuries. **And it ended the moment the gun could also fight up close** —
at which point the shooters no longer needed protecting and the whole
formation dissolved.
**SO THE LAW WE ALREADY WROTE IS THE RIGHT ONE, AND NOW WE KNOW WHY IT IS
LOAD-BEARING RATHER THAN FLAVOUR: GUNS MUST BE BAD IN YOUR FACE.** That
single constraint is what keeps a reason to close, keeps somebody needing
somebody else, and keeps position meaningful once range exists.

## B4. AND WE ALREADY OWN THE ANTI-TURTLE KIT — WE TOOK IT ON 8/17 AND
## NEVER CONNECTED IT TO THIS
Three of RF4's machines exist in our canon precisely to stop the sit-still
pattern, and all three are POSITIONAL PRESSURE FROM ENEMY DESIGN rather
than juice:
1. **THE 50% AGGRO SHOUT** — kills the safe, repeatable single pull.
2. **THE PRIORITY TARGET IS AT THE BACK** — winning means ADVANCING past
   the nearest threat, so hiding loses.
3. **SUPPORT AI THAT KEEPS LEAVING YOUR LINE OF SIGHT** — the thing you
   must kill will not stand still and wait.
**PLUS OUR OWN, FROM THE RF4 ENEMY DOSSIER: THE CLOCK IS OUR PLINKER.**
Animals have no ranged attack, so the constant pressure that says HURRY
comes from time and terrain — the day advancing 0.084 per cell, heat as
the daily condition, terrain that kills. **A TIMER IS PRESSURE THAT NEVER
MISSES AND CANNOT BE HIDDEN FROM.**
**TURTLING IS ONLY RATIONAL WHEN WAITING IS FREE. NOTHING IN THIS GAME
LETS WAITING BE FREE.** That is the answer to the gun problem, and every
piece of it is already ruled.

## B5. THE TWO HALVES MEET
Intents plus anti-turtle pressure produce the fight he described: you
point nineteen people at something in four words, the world refuses to let
anybody stand still, and you spend your own beats on the one body you
control. **NOBODY IS SOLVING A PUZZLE. EVERYBODY IS COMMITTING.**

## 5. WHAT I WOULD ROUTE ON DAY 5 (not routed today, per his order)
- The order set is INTENTS, a handful of words, one system for the
  player's company, the enemies and the factions.
- Changing an intent costs one beat. There is never a take-over button.
- Order breadth may narrow for green bodies and widen for veterans.
- **GUNS ARE BAD IN CLOSE — a hard constraint on every weapon, forever.**
- **NO COVER-AND-HOLD MECHANIC WITHOUT PRESSURE THAT PUNISHES WAITING**,
  and we already have four sources of that pressure.
- Every number in all of it is his.
