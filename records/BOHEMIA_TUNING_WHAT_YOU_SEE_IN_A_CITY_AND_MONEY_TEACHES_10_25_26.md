## 1. HOW FAR PEOPLE REALLY SEE IN A CITY (the sources first, then the numbers for RUN)

THE FOUR THINGS SIGHT DOES, EACH WITH ITS OWN DISTANCE (they are not one number, and that is the finding that matters most)
- SEE THAT SOMETHING IS THERE (a person-sized thing moving): about 1.5 km by day. A target-acquisition model of the unaided eye
  (arXiv 1904.11678, 'Performance modeling of electro-optical devices for military target acquisition') puts certain detection of
  person-sized targets at about 1.5 km in daylight and about 500 m under a full moon, falling off sharply past that.
- TELL WHAT IT IS (people, how many, armed): about 300 to 400 m. The range-estimation tables soldiers learn by eye: 'at 200 metres clear in
  all detail, colour of skin and equipment identifiable; at 300 metres clear body outline, face colour good, remaining detail blurred'
  (An Cosantoir, the Irish Defence Forces' journal); 'at 400 yards the body outline is clear, but remaining detail is blurred' (USMC,
  0300-M16-1008, unknown-distance engagement). Past about 400 m you see a group, not what it carries.
- KNOW WHO (a face): tens of metres. Loftus and Harley (2005, Psychonomic Bulletin and Review, 'Why is it easier to identify someone close
  than far away?'): blur and distance are the same to the eye; at about 3 m the eyelashes go, at about 61 m (200 ft) 'the eyes can't be made
  out', at about 152 m (500 ft) the head is a blur. At night under street lamps the lighting standards design for facial recognition at
  4 m, 10 m called ideal (Fotios and Raynham; Rombauts et al., 100 tests on a real street: recognition falls below about 0.6 lux on the
  face, nothing gained above 25 lux).
- SEE A LIGHT: kilometres. Krisciunas and Carona (2015, arXiv 1507.06270, 'At What Distance Can the Human Eye Detect a Candle Flame?'):
  a candle flame is as bright as a just-visible star at about 2.6 km. A fire, a torch, a lamp in a window or a running engine's lights
  are brighter than a candle. AT NIGHT YOU SEE THEIR LIGHT LONG BEFORE YOU SEE THEM.

NIGHT WITHOUT POWER (most of the valley in act one: CLUSTERED POWER, 12% of circuits live)
- Full moon: about 500 m to see that something is there (the same model). Starlight only: a field test on military targets (DTIC) found a
  tank detected at about 190 yards (170 m) under starlight and 900 to 1,000 yards (820 to 910 m) under moonlight; a man is far smaller
  than a tank, so under starlight he is a shape at tens of metres. The Night page in the library says the same in Battle Brothers' terms:
  'View range is decreased at night, both in combat and on the worldmap' (bb_all/1191_Loading Screen Tips; the fight board's -2 of 7).
- A dark-clothed person on an unlit road is noticed by drivers under about 30 m with low beams (Olson and Sivak, as cited in an MDPI 2026
  conference paper); dark clothing cuts detection about 60% (a UAE University thesis on pedestrian visibility).

AROUND CORNERS: NOTHING. Sight in a street network is LINES, not a circle. Space syntax calls the fewest and longest lines of sight in a
street network its axial lines, and an isovist (what you see from one spot) reaches far only along the street you stand in and sharply down
the cross street at an intersection (the drift measure 'rises sharply toward a major avenue... highest at the ends' of a linear path;
Bushehr isovist study, IJAUP 2012; the MDPI axial-line paper). A wall, a house or a parked bus ends the line.

LAS VEGAS IS A CITY OF LONG STRAIGHT LINES. Its arterials follow section lines (Wikipedia, 'Section line road': 'much of the Las Vegas Valley,
all major thoroughfares run along section lines'), so an arterial every mile (1,609 m), a collector every half mile (2,640 ft maximum,
Las Vegas municipal code via a code aggregator), local streets no more than 660 ft (200 m) apart, and the suburbs inside them curl into
cul-de-sacs. Straight for miles on the arterials and the Strip; a few hundred metres at most inside a subdivision.

FROM A ROOF: the horizon from a height h is about 3.57 x the square root of h kilometres (h in metres): a one-storey roof (4 m) 7 km, a
two-storey roof (6 m) 8.7 km, a ten-storey hotel (35 m) 21 km. The valley is mostly one and two storeys, so on a roof the street rule lifts
and the limit becomes the eye's own: about 1.5 km to see people in every direction, farther for lights and dust.

THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG: the circle we hold is thirteen times too big for a city. FACTIONS' draft base sight
(records/BOHEMIA_FACTIONS_HOW_FAR_A_COMPANY_SEES_ON_BATTLE_BROTHERS_MAP_10_18_26.md, map.sight.base_fraction_of_width) is a tenth of the
map's width, 50 blocks: 19 km at 384 m a block. The eye sees that a party is there at about 1.5 km, 4 blocks, by day, and knows what it is at
1 block. Battle Brothers never prints its base radius (FACTIONS grepped 2,256 pages), so 50 was a pick, and it is the wrong one for a city:
it is a desert-plain number. What the city gives instead is a SHAPE: long lines down the arterials and the Strip, nothing past a corner,
your own block around you, and at night only the lights.

WHAT IT COSTS IN PLAY (named, not decided): at RUN's measured road pace (about 260 blocks a game day, records/BOHEMIA_THE_REGION_RUN_10_16_26.md
via FACTIONS) a party 4 blocks away is about 22 game minutes off; at the 1.22x default and about two real minutes a day that is about two
seconds of real time. Battle Brothers' answer is not a bigger circle; it is the MEET card (rule 151(e)): a party in sight stops you and asks
ENGAGE or FALL BACK. So the honest numbers above and 151(e) together are the design: you see little, and what you see always gives you the
choice. The lights at night (7 blocks) and the tracks (FACTIONS) are what give warning past the street.

  three men 54 crowns a day, 44 days of doing nothing; hire three more and it is 666 crowns, 19 days; hire six more and the purse is gone.

THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG: HE SAID "FED AND PAID", AND NOBODY IS PAID.
The bill feeds the crew (2 a man a night: a ration and four litres) and charges NO WAGE. engine/bohemia_goodbros.js gives every man at the
post wageChargedHere: 0 with the comment "bought once, rule 56, applied here too"; the one table carries it as hire.wage_charged 0, owner
"paolo (the ruling)". Rule 56's words are "THE RETINUE IS BOUGHT ONCE, no wage": the retinue is Battle Brothers' followers (the cook, the
scout, the paymaster), not the men who fight. It was a lane's stretch of a ruling about followers onto the company, and his 10/24 words
(151(b): "wages, food and upkeep stay felt") are newer. What it costs: with no wage a sellsword eats the same 2 a night as a beggar and a
level-11 killer the same as a recruit, so the half of his lesson that says a big company "would hurt my ability to hire better people"
is gone: better men cost nothing more to keep. The roster already prints each man's wage ("N BATTERIES A DAY"); nothing charges it.
