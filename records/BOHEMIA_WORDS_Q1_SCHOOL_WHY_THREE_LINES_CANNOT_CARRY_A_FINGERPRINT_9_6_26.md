## 1. THE HARD NUMBER THAT REFRAMES THE WHOLE QUESTION
The row's test is three lines each. Our own corpus measures a median NPC line at
**11 words**, so three lines is **about 33 words.**

Now the science of telling two writers apart from text alone:

    reliable authorship attribution        1,000 words is weak, 2,000 is better
    the low end forensic studies work at   105 to 341 tokens
    what this question gives a reader      about 33 words

**THIRTY-THREE WORDS IS A THIRD OF THE ABSOLUTE FLOOR AND ABOUT TWO PERCENT OF THE
RELIABLE THRESHOLD.** And the field's own answer to short texts is to JOIN them into
one longer corpus, which is exactly what a scene cannot do.

**SO WHATEVER LETS A READER TELL TWO PEOPLE APART IN THREE LINES, IT IS NOT
IDIOLECT.** Idiolect does not fit in 33 words. Naming what actually does the work is
the whole job of this school round.

## 2. AND THE FIELD IS NOT SURE IDIOLECT IS USABLE AT ALL
Forensic authorship analysis rests on the assumption that every native speaker has a
stable, distinctive linguistic fingerprint. But there is **growing concern in the
field that idiolect remains too abstract to be of practical use.** The thing our
question takes for granted is contested by the people whose job depends on it.

That is not a reason to abandon the question. It is a reason to stop expecting the
distinctiveness to live in the grain of the language, and start asking what else
carries it.

## 3. THE INVERSION, WHICH IS THE CENTRAL FINDING OF THIS SCHOOL
Authorship attribution runs on FUNCTION WORDS, and the reason is stated plainly in
the founding work: **"We need variables that depend on authors and nothing else."**
Function words are chosen precisely because they are **topic-independent**. "The"
and "and" have no synonyms, so they cannot be swapped for stylistic reasons and they
survive a change of subject. Content words are rejected as wasteful because their
use depends on topic rather than style. (Why function words work this well is,
by the field's own admission, still a mystery.)

**NOW READ THAT AGAINST WHAT A DIALOGUE WRITER NEEDS.**

    FORENSICS WANTS            A WRITER NEEDS
    unconscious features       features a reader notices in one pass
    stable across topics       features that ARE the topic
    hard to imitate            instantly legible, on purpose
    1,000 to 2,000 words       33 words
    the author stripped of     the situation, the listener and the want
    situation and audience     doing most of the work

**THE BEST TOOL FOR AUTHORSHIP ATTRIBUTION IS THE WORST TOOL FOR CHARACTER VOICE,
AND THE 9/4 ROUND USED IT.** It measured our people with function-word stylometry,
the instrument built to ignore exactly the thing that makes a character a character.
That is the specific error school exists to catch, and it is mine.

## 4. WHERE THE ROW'S OWN TEST IS WRONG, AND IT IS WRONG HALF THE TIME
"You can tell them apart with the names removed" is the standard screenwriting test
and it is good for strangers. **It is wrong for people who know each other, and the
evidence is quantitative.**

Language style matching measures similarity in FUNCTION WORD use between two people
in a conversation, which is the same dimension stylometry uses. It predicts
closeness:

    40 speed dates      pairs above median matching mutually wanted contact
                        33.3% of the time, against 9.1% below median (OR 3.05)
    86 couples          still together at three months: 76.7% above median
                        against 53.5% below (OR 1.95)

**PEOPLE WHO ARE CLOSE CONVERGE, ON EXACTLY THE DIMENSION THAT SUPPOSEDLY CARRIES
IDENTITY.** So a family at a table SHOULD be hard to tell apart, and the correct,
accurate, better-written version of that scene is one where they sound alike.

**AND THAT MAKES DIVERGENCE THE DRAMA.** The person at the table who has stopped
matching is the person who has left, and the reader feels it before they are told.
This lane already wrote a grief dinner (Q8) with four distinct voices, on the
assumption that distinct is always better. **The science says the family should
converge and the one who does not is the story.** That is a real correction to a
shipped scene and it came from school.

## 5. THE STANDARD ADVICE, AND THE PROOF FROM OUR OWN BUILD THAT IT FAILS
The standard advice is the catchphrase or verbal tic: one word or phrase a character
repeats. The published craft objection is that overused tics make a character
one-dimensional and the dialogue forced. **True, and too soft.**

**HERE IS THE HARDER OBJECTION, MEASURED IN OUR OWN CORPUS BY THIS LANE (Q13): FIVE
DIFFERENT SPEAKERS IN FIVE DIFFERENT QUESTS INDEPENDENTLY SAY A VERSION OF "THAT
ISN'T A THREAT", WITH THE WORD "ARITHMETIC" IN THREE OF THE FIVE.** Nobody planned a
tic. A rhetorical move the writer liked propagated across five characters and made
them one person.

**SO A TIC DOES NOT MAKE A CHARACTER DISTINCT. A TIC IS THE WRITER'S FINGERPRINT,
NOT THE CHARACTER'S, AND IT SPREADS.** That is the most useful thing in this school
round because it is measured, it is ours, and it happened while we were being
careful.

The better formulation in current craft writing is three things instead of one:
**opposing syntax, behaviour under stress, and avoidance patterns**, checked with
blind reads before the writing is locked.

## 6. THE THING A PERSON NEVER SAYS, WHICH THREE SOURCES REACHED SEPARATELY
The row names it. Craft writing on distinguishing characters names it as **avoidance
patterns**. And game-writing practice names it as **bright lines: what this person
will not discuss, and how they refuse.**

Three independent sources landing on negative space is the level of agreement this
lane trusts, and it is the one channel that works at 33 words, because **an absence
is legible immediately and cannot be borrowed by another character the way a phrase
can.** A tic spreads across a cast. A refusal cannot: the moment a second person
refuses the same subject, that is a fact about the world, not a copy.

## 7. WHAT THE BEST GAMES DO, AND AN HONEST NOTE ABOUT THIS HALF
This is the thin half of the round and I will say so rather than pad it. Published
craft writing on game dialogue voice is mostly generic, and the searchable material
now skews toward persona prompting for generated NPCs. The one durable idea in it is
the bright-line persona above.

**THE BETTER GAMES EVIDENCE I HOLD IS MEASURED, NOT WRITTEN**, and it comes from
this lane's own corpora work: 617 films, 304,446 lines; a shipped game script of
29,213 lines; 199,740 turns of real tagged speech. Those let me compare what a
medium DOES rather than what its blogs SAY, and that comparison has already produced
this lane's sharpest numbers.

One caution from the craft side that belongs in school as a counterweight to my own
instinct: **game dialogue does not have to be realistic, and mimicking real
conversation often produces flat, lifeless lines.** This lane has spent seventeen
rounds pushing our numbers toward the real-speech corpus. Round two of this question
has to hold both: the real numbers are a diagnosis, not a target.

## 8. WHAT I NOW BELIEVE CARRIES A PERSON IN 33 WORDS
Not conclusions to write from yet, which is round two. This is what school leaves me
holding, in order of how much weight the evidence puts on each:

1. **WHAT THEY WILL NOT TALK ABOUT.** Legible instantly, cannot spread across a
   cast, and three independent sources point at it.
2. **WHAT THEY WANT FROM THE PERSON IN FRONT OF THEM.** Situation and audience are
   what forensics deliberately strips, which is precisely why they are available to
   a writer and free.
3. **WHAT THEY EXPLAIN THINGS WITH.** The source domain, which this lane measured in
   Q9 and found we have zero of. It is topic-dependent, therefore useless to
   forensics and ideal here.
4. **HOW THEY BEHAVE WHEN PRESSED**, which Q2 measured.
5. **RHYTHM AND LENGTH**, which Q4 and Q14 measured, and which is the only one of
   these that overlaps with what stylometry can see.
6. **AND LAST, VOCABULARY**, which is where the standard advice starts and where our
   own corpus proves it leaks between characters.

**THE ORDER IS ROUGHLY THE REVERSE OF THE STANDARD ADVICE, AND THAT IS THE FINDING.**
