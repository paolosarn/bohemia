# WHAT A CHILD OF THE ANARCHY DECADE CARRIES AS A HABIT, NOT A NUMBER
DYNASTY, row [animal era] (Q23). 10/10/26. MODE: SCHOOL (one record, no code). Real-world findings tagged (recall); the fleet cannot fetch pages.

## 0. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG, MEASURED
An heir's traits come from ONE place: half of the parent's own traits and the parent's trade (`was.keeps`). I ran the derive on a parent with no traits and a front-of-house trade (a card dealer): the heir has **no traits at all**. The era the heir was raised in appears in 0 of the heir's fields. So the child of the anarchy decade and the child of any other decade are the same person, and the game's own sentence for act one ('instinct before law', his 9/7 'the vibes, not actual animals') leaves no mark on anyone. Meanwhile the Battle Brothers mechanism we already translate has exactly the hook: traits gained by EVENTS (Paranoid, Insecure, Impatient, Dastard, in the library's 06_BACKGROUNDS): a life leaves a trait, the player learns it by playing.

## 1. WHAT A CHILDHOOD INSIDE A REAL COLLAPSE DOES TO AN ADULT (recall)
- DEPRESSION BABIES: people who lived through a slump as young adults stay more cautious with money for decades, hold less risk, distrust banks, and keep the habit after the money returns (Malmendier and Nagel, 2011; Elder, Children of the Great Depression, 1974: the youngest children were hit hardest, the teenagers coped).
- HYPERINFLATION: survivors of Weimar and of later hyperinflations tend to spend money the day they get it and to hold hard things (goods, gold, land) instead of savings: the paper lied once.
- FAMINE AND SIEGE: people who were hungry as children hoard food, finish the plate, and never leave the last of anything (Dutch Hunger Winter follow-ups).
- WAR AND ANARCHY: children raised where nobody could be trusted sleep lightly, watch the door, and read strangers first. They are not weak: they are fast at the thing they had to be fast at.
- WHAT IS NOTICEABLE IS THE HABIT, never a number: a person's refusals and reflexes, which family and friends can name in one line.

## 2. WHAT THE HEIR RAISED IN ACT ONE SHOULD CARRY (proposal, draft:true, three habits, each a refusal or a reflex)
1. HOARDS: will not sell the last of a thing (a store of food, rounds, batteries is never spent below a floor; the wealth is in the pile). A refusal at the shop.
2. SLEEPS LIGHT: reads a stranger first; the first read of any meeting or fight is his (never ambushed at the door). A reflex.
3. TRUSTS NO PAPER: refuses an IOU or a promise to pay; only batteries, goods or a hand shake count. This is the dollar-died habit, said as a person.
Each is a HABIT the heir is shown to have (one line on the company screen and one spoken line, WORDS, draft:true), picked by the parent's era and trade, never a stat bonus: hoarding is a cost in options, sleeping light costs sleep, no paper costs credit. Habits fade only if the player lives against them for a long time (the second generation's own habits, formed in act 2's rebuilt valley, replace them in act 3).

## 3. HOW GAMES LEAVE AN ERA ON A CHARACTER (Battle Brothers is the allowed reference)
The campaign layer's lesson: a trait is a short, two-edged word the player discovers, gained by what happened to the man. So the engine is already shaped: `traits` on the heir. The change is that the derive adds ONE era trait from a small table keyed on (the act the heir grew up in, the parent's trade): act one gives the three above; act two (reclaimed, techy) gives different ones (trusts the machine too much, an over-careful reader of records); that second table is another round's work.
