THE RULING, re-read in full before touching anything: Paolo does not paint
the female rig -- Claude produces her as a CANDIDATE through the standard
verdict pipeline (thumbs, not auto-canon). The male paint stays sacrosanct:
the female body is DERIVED via a documented, regeneratable TRANSFORM, never
hand-repainted. Timing was his call ("we don't even have to do it now") --
the marathon's own menu is what activates it this turn.

THE HARD TECHNICAL QUESTION, answered by direct code read before writing
anything: seg() (the bone transform every pose/animation runs through) is
explicitly ANISOTROPIC -- "WIDTH LAW (Paolo 7/2/26): the perpendicular axis
NEVER scales (width never changes)." This means feeding a different
skeleton through the EXISTING skinner can restretch bone LENGTHS but can
NEVER change torso WIDTH -- the addendum's own build plan (narrower
shoulders, waist taper, wider hips) is architecturally impossible through
skeleton changes alone. It requires reshaping the PAINTED REST-SPACE PIXELS
themselves before they ever reach the skinner. Also confirmed by direct
code read: the body's actual RGB color is never stored per-pixel at all --
BAKED.layers[dir][partId] is just an array of PIXEL INDICES (which part
each rest-space pixel belongs to); color comes procedurally from
skinRampFor()+bshade at render time, keyed off part id + skinTone. This
made the transform tractable: no texture to preserve, only the WHICH-PART-
OWNS-THIS-PIXEL index sets need reshaping.

THE TRANSFORM (documented, regeneratable, per the addendum's own spec):
for each of the 8 directions, every torso (part 4) pixel at (x,y) is
remapped to x' = round(cx + (x-cx)*scale(t)), where cx = that direction's
own waC (waist-center) skeleton joint x, and t = the pixel's normalized
position from the torso's top row (shoulder, t=0) to its bottom row (hip,
t=1). scale(t) is a 4-point piecewise-linear profile taken straight from
the addendum's build plan: shoulder 0.82 (narrower), chest 0.92 (adjusted),
waist 0.72 (a real taper -- the tightest point), hip 1.18 (wider). The
shoulder/arm chain (parts 5+7 for the left arm, 6+8 for the right, plus
the shL/elL/handL and shR/elR/handR skeleton joints in both BAKED-shape
skeleton AND pose) is rigidly translated by the same shoulder-row delta so
the arms reattach at the new torso edge with no seam -- deltas came out
tiny (0 to +-1px per direction, since the profile at t=0 is a modest 0.82).
Nothing else moves: same 56px frame, same skeleton joint SET, same part
ids, same pose-delta clip functions (they're skeleton-relative, confirmed
by an earlier direct read of applyPose() -- rotations/translations/
compression fractions, nothing hardcoded to an absolute pixel).

WIRING: BODY_RIGS gained FEMALE:1. rebuildFromRig() (the ONE PACKAGE LAW

v2 REBUILD (7/25/26, same day) — PAOLO KILLED v1 ON SIGHT
==============================================================================
Paolo, looking at the live build: "What if i told u its dogshit and doesnt
look like a female body at all." He was right. Rebuilt from scratch, and
THREE REAL DEFECTS came out of it -- this was not just a taste miss:

1. THE SHELF. v1 reshaped ONLY the torso (part 4). But in this rig the HIPS
   ARE THE LEG TOPS (parts 9/10 rows 32-38), which v1 left 100% male. So the
   "wider hips" only widened the torso's bottom two rows and then snapped back
   to an unchanged male lower body -- a shelf, not a hip.
2. THE OCCLUSION. v1 put its hip flare at rows 31-34. The arms hang at rows
   17-34 and the hands sit at 34. Head-on, the outer silhouette in that whole
   band IS THE ARMS -- so the single female cue v1 had was completely hidden
   behind them in every arms-down pose. Nothing you do to torso width between
   rows 17 and 34 is visible from S or N. The flare has to live BELOW the
   hands (row 35+) to be seen at all.
3. THE COVERAGE BUG (caught by the gate, not by eye). The parts genuinely
   OVERLAP in the source data -- on the hip row the torso and both legs claim
   the same pixels and draw order resolves it at render time. The first rebuild
   collapsed each row into one {x: part} map, so the legs clobbered the torso
   and THE TORSO SILENTLY LOST ITS ENTIRE BOTTOM ROW on S/E/N/W. Garments key
   off part 4, so that was a real garment-coverage bug, not a cosmetic one.
   Fixed by tracking membership per part and sharing only the row EXTENTS.

WHAT v2 DOES DIFFERENTLY:
 - reshapes torso AND both legs as one continuous silhouette, rows 16->48
 - puts the hip/thigh flare at rows 35-42, BELOW the hands, where it is seen
 - hard waist pinch (0.66) -- the cue that reads best head-on, because it
   opens a visible gap between the torso and the hanging arms
 - DIRECTION-AWARE: on profiles and diagonals the depth axis is on screen, so
   it adds a real front-edge BUST bulge and a back-edge GLUTE bulge. v1 had
   neither, and these are the strongest cues available on those 6 facings.
   Front side is detected from the face centroid vs the torso centroid, so it
   is correct per direction without hardcoding.
 - does NOT thin the arms and barely narrows the shoulders. An earlier pass
   did both and the result read as a SMALLER PERSON (a child), not a woman --
   shrinking everything uniformly is not feminising, it is just scaling.

NOW A REAL TOOL: the derivation lives at tools/bohemia_female_rig_transform.py
(`--inject` writes FEMALE_BAKED straight into the alpha), with every control
point named and tunable at the top of the file. That satisfies the addendum's
own requirement -- "a TRANSFORM with a spec, so it can be regenerated, tuned
by his notes, and gated" -- which the v1 scratch script did not. The gate now
asserts the tool is checked in, so the shipped data can never drift from a
spec nobody can re-run.

HONEST CEILING (flagged for Paolo, not worked around): after four visual
passes on the REAL render surface, v2 is clearly better than v1 and the bugs
are genuinely fixed, but width-tuning a 56px male body whose torso is a
featureless box and whose sides are occluded by hanging arms has a low
ceiling. It reads as a slim figure with a waist and hips; it does not read as
UNMISTAKABLY a woman. The constraint doing the capping is the addendum's own
"derive her from the male paint by transform" clause. Lifting that -- letting
her be authored as her own painted 8-direction body, which is still Claude
producing her, not Paolo painting her -- is a CANON-LEVEL change and is
[PENDING, Paolo's call]. Do not lift it unilaterally.

the LOWER body. So v3 keeps every cue v2 introduced and only pulls the mass
down, rather than re-deriving anything:

  thigh splay peak 2.0  -> 1.0   (per side, px)
  glute peak       1.9  -> 0.9
  glute (leg rows) 1.9  -> 0.9

v4 (7/25/26) — PAOLO'S BUG LIST + THE RESEARCH HE ASKED FOR
==============================================================================
Paolo: "In some parts of the animations the leg looks like it's chopping off
from the rest of the body... rework the shadow textures, clean it up... from
some angles where it's naked there's like stray pixels flying off next to the
butt on some of the cardinal directions... definitely want to fix where the
nipples are... do some big brain online research to find out how the studios
translate male bodies into female bodies... we're getting closer though."

--- THE THREE BUGS, ROOT-CAUSED (all real, none cosmetic) ---

1. "THE LEG LOOKS LIKE IT'S CHOPPING OFF." Not a pose bug -- a SHADING bug with
   a geometry cause. The ANATOMY border rule outlines any body pixel with empty
   space above it. v3's thighs splayed WIDER than the torso above them, so the
   exposed leg tops took the dark border tone and a hard line got drawn straight
   across the hip, reading as the leg detaching. Verified failing on exactly the
   cardinal facings he named (S/E/N/W). FIX: PELVIS COVER -- the torso base must
   span at least as wide as the thigh row beneath it, always. The pelvis flares
   before the legs do, like real anatomy. Applied to the last TWO torso rows so
   the pixels it adds can never themselves become strays, and applied AFTER the
   cull so the cull can't eat it. Machine-locked in the gate, all 8 directions.

2. "STRAY PIXELS FLYING OFF NEXT TO THE BUTT." Real: the resample stranded a
   pixel whenever a stretched row's edge landed away from its neighbours. Found
   them exactly where he said -- row 31 (hip) on NE/NW/SW, plus row 20 shoulder
   strays. FIX: a cull of any pixel with <=1 same-part orthogonal neighbour, in
   the SOLID parts only. Three sub-bugs surfaced while building it, each caught
   by measurement rather than eye:
     - the cull ate the 1px torso apex on the pure profiles -> ROW PRESERVATION
       (a cull may never empty a row outright)
     - the shoulder scale squeezed a male 2px row to 1px, which IS the stray
       shape -> MINIMUM-WIDTH CLAMP (if the male row had >=2, keep >=2)
     - the cull ate the NECK's outer column (a 4x2 strip whose corners
       legitimately have one neighbour) -> cull scoped to solid parts only,
       the same "never eat thin intentional art" rule the engine's own
       refineSkin already observes
   Result: ZERO strays in torso and both legs on all 8 facings -- cleaner than
   the male baseline, which carries 1-3 on NE/NW.

3. "FIX WHERE THE NIPPLES ARE." ROOT CAUSE: the render path read
   BAKED.skeleton directly, so on the FEMALE body every skeleton-anchored
   detail was still measuring against MALE landmarks. Placement was a hardcoded
   shoulder+5 row and a fixed +-3 column. FIX: a rigSkel(d) accessor returning
   the ACTIVE rig's skeleton, wired into all three sites that had hardcoded
   BAKED (nipples, the shoulder blend, and the GARMENT CONTACT LAW -- that last
   one was silently wrong for every garment on the female). Placement is now
   MEASURED: the row is the widest chest row in the upper torso (bust line on
   her, pec line on him -- correct for both), the columns are a fraction of that
   row's actual half-width. Male output is unchanged in practice.

--- THE RESEARCH (he asked for it; it changed the design) ---
Full report in the session log. The two findings that mattered:

 (a) NOBODY DERIVES A FEMALE BY TRANSFORMING A MALE. Studios either author a
     separate female body on a shared skeleton (Skyrim, FFXIV, MetaHuman, Sims,
     Stardew, LPC) or cross-fade two hand-sculpted morphs (Daz, MakeHuman --
     verified in MakeHuman's own source: the targets are hand-authored vertex
     deltas, not a procedural transform). Pure squash/stretch is explicitly
     rejected: affine scale PRESERVES ratios, and dimorphism IS ratios. This is
     direct evidence for the ceiling flagged in v2 and it is still [PENDING
     Paolo] whether to lift the addendum's derive-by-transform clause.
 (b) THE GENDER SIGNAL IS THE SHOULDER-TO-HIP RATIO, NOT HIP MASS.
     Three independent source families converge: male 1.43-1.48, female
     1.27-1.33. Shoulder 0.86x (0.857/0.884/0.893). Ribcage 0.86x.
     Hips 1.00-1.05x -- real female hips are barely wider in absolute cm.
     And verbatim: "prefer NARROWING THE SHOULDERS over widening the hips;
     widening hips is what produces the thick failure mode." That is precisely
     the BBL Paolo saw. v2/v3 bought the hourglass with hip mass; the sourced
     answer buys it with ribcage taper.

v4 RETUNE to the sourced numbers: shoulders 0.80 -> 0.86, ribcage 1.00 -> 0.88
(v3 sat at full male width -- this was the real miss), waist 0.66 -> 0.72
(between the 0.75 stylized and 0.90 realistic values), hip 1.06 -> 1.02, thigh
splay halved again to 0.55 peak, bust trimmed (it is a LATE detail, never the
signal -- "if the ratio still reads male, adding a bust reads as a man with
breasts"). NEW: the NECK is now reshaped too, 0.86x, a high-confidence sourced
cue v1-v3 never touched.

HONEST LIMITS, not worked around:
 - the neck's 0.86 lands on the same integer at 3-4px, so it does not visibly
   change yet. The code is right; the resolution is the limit.
 - HEIGHT is the strongest cue still unused: sourced at 0.92x body with a 0.96x
   head, i.e. she should be 3-4px shorter while the head barely changes. Not
   done because it moves foot placement and the ground/occupancy contract, and
   that is not a change to make blind late in a session. This is the single
   highest-value next move on her.
 - shading conventions for a bust at small scale: the research could NOT find
   rigorous sources. Only "highlight the cloth, not the skin". So the shading
   work here was limited to the defects with identifiable causes (the hip
   border line, the strays) rather than inventing a convention.
 - research also warns bone PIVOTS must move when hips widen or the rig gets
   candy-wrapper twist. Our hip change is now small (1.02x) so this is not yet
   biting, but it is the thing to watch if the hips ever grow again.
