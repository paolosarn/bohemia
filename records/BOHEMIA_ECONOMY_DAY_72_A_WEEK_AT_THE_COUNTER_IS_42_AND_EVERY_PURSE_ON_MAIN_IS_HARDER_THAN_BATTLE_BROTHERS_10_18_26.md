## 1. HOW BATTLE BROTHERS DOES IT: WEEK ONE COSTS A NEW COMPANY 294 CROWNS AND NO FOOD
Sources: bb_all/0002_A New Company.txt, 0827_Game Guide.txt, 0427_Companion (Background).txt,
1608_Provisions.txt (reference/library/grok/wiki/PAGES_ALL.tar.gz).
- A New Company starts with three Companions. Low funds: 1,900 crowns, 10 tools, 20 ammo, 10
  medicine, and 2 stacks of Ground Grains in the cart (50 provisions). The Game Guide's generic Low
  is 1,500 crowns with the same 50 provisions.
- A Companion's base wage is 14 crowns a day (the background page), times 1.1^(level - 1) for the
  first eleven levels (the Game Guide's wage formula). Level 1 is the floor of that, so 14 is the
  cheapest a Companion can cost.
- Food: 2 a man a day on plains (Provisions page; stash_rates.json foodByTerrain plains 1.0).
  Three men, seven days: 42 provisions. THE CART ALREADY HOLDS 50. Week one's food is prepaid.
- So Battle Brothers' week one: 3 x 14 x 7 = 294 crowns of wages, 0 crowns of food, 0 of tools,
  ammo or medicine until the first fight (the piles are prepaid too). At ten to one: 29.4 batteries.
- From week two the food is bought: 42 provisions is 1.7 grain stacks at 50 crowns, about 84 crowns.
  A steady week is about 378 crowns (37.8 batteries).
- RUNWAY with no contract taken: 1,900 / 378 = about 5.0 weeks (Low); Medium 2,400 / 378 = about 6.3
  weeks; the Game Guide's generic Low 1,500 / 378 = about 4.0 weeks. Battle Brothers hands a new
  player about FIVE WEEKS OF DOING NOTHING before the purse is empty. Every contract on top of that
  is profit against the clock.
- What Battle Brothers' men do NOT do: earn. The company pays them; only contracts pay the company.

## 2. OURS, MULTIPLIED OUT: A QUIET WEEK FOR A CREW OF THREE IS 42 BATTERIES ON PLAINS
Inputs, all on main: Day 69's ladder (a ration is one man one day at ONE battery; four litres of
water is ONE; a dose ONE per injury per day; a tape point ONE; five rounds ONE; a bandage TWO; the
bread stack of 25 units 6 or 7 by the rounding, Day 57's open line; grains 5); food 2 units a man a
day times terrain (stash_rates.json: plains 1.0, desert 1.5); water four litres a man a day (the
economy gate's grounded band, 3 to 6 litres around our 4); the loan of Day 49 (one handed over, one
back a night until paid, never grows: engine/bohemia_lend.js HANDSHAKE 1, DUE_A_NIGHT 1).
Twenty-one man-days (three men, seven days).

| food | 21 rations = 21 | 42 units = 2 bread stacks = 12 or 14 (2 grain stacks = 10) | 63 units = 31.5 rations = 32 | 63 units = 3 stacks = 18 or 21 (grains 15) |

**2a. A week with one fight in it**, a worked example (the quantities are an illustration, not a
number for the table): one bandage (2), one man hurt three days (3 doses, 3), ten rounds (2), two
tape points to patch a plate (2). Plus 9. A plains week with one fight is about 51 at the ration
counter. The fight is a small line; the plate and the cup are the big ones. That matches Battle
Brothers' own shape (wages and food dwarf tools and medicine in its Game Guide's money section).

**2b. What seven days of work pays.** A day's work pays one battery a man (payForWork,
PAYOUT.COMPLETE). Three men picked every day of the week: 21. Day 49 already found the corner does
not pick every man every day; 21 is the ceiling, not the expectation.

**2c. The gap, read across.** Plains at the ration counter: 42 out, 21 in at best, 21 SHORT A WEEK.
By the stack: 10 to 14 short. Desert at the ration counter: 32 to 43 short. A working crew of three
loses between ten and forty-three batteries a week before it ever fights for pay. That is Day 49's
'one short every day' scaled to a crew, and it is now a number TUNING can see.

## 3. THE POOR PAY MORE: BUYING BY THE RATION COSTS 1.5 TO 2 TIMES BUYING BY THE STACK
*** CORRECTED 10/18 BY DAY 77 (section 6.1): the ration is 2.4 to 2.5 times the grain stack for every crew of two
or more (1.4 for a man alone, whose stack rots); Bristol's 490 pounds holds no pack-size line (fuel tariffs 233,
area 84, credit 55, the meter 38); the food record's size premium is about 6 to 9 in 100. 'The real record exactly'
below is withdrawn: ours is 16 to 25 times it. ***
Same 42 units of food on plains: 21 batteries by the ration, 10 to 14 by the stack. The floor of one
battery, applied to a ration, is a premium of half again to double over the wiki's own stack price.
Nobody designed that; it falls out of Day 69's rule (sell the unit the floor can reach). And it is
the real record exactly:
- The poverty premium: the University of Bristol's 'Paying to be poor' (Davies, Finney and Hartfree,
  2016) put it at about 490 pounds a year for a low-income UK household, much of it from buying in
  small units and on the meter.
- Loose cigarettes, the single sachet of shampoo, the one-day phone top-up: every collapse case this
  lane holds (Zimbabwe's candy change, Venezuela's single tablets, Day 1 and Day 69) is a counter
  selling the small unit at a worse rate because the buyer cannot reach the big one.
So the ration counter is not a bug to fix. It is a lever: the day a crew can afford a stack, food
gets 30 to 50 percent cheaper, and that is a visible win (rule 117(c): every good choice gets one).

## 4. THE REAL RECORD: NOBODY STARTED THE FIRST WEEK AT ZERO
Cases from this lane's own pages; each line names where.
- **ARGENTINA, 1 DECEMBER 2001 (Day 1, 2a).** The corralito capped cash at 250 pesos a week, at the
  one-to-one peg still 250 dollars. Shop sales fell 50 to 70 percent. The cap was a week's cash at a
  time, not nothing: what broke was income and the bank, not the plate. (INDEC's food line for a
  family sat well under the weekly cap; the exact 2001 figure is to be checked against INDEC's series
  before TUNING cites it.)
- **LEBANON, OCTOBER 2019 (Day 1, 2d; Day 17).** The banks shut for about two weeks from 17 October,
  then reopened with informal caps of a few hundred dollars a week. The minimum wage of 675,000 lira,
  about 450 dollars at the peg, fell to about 7 at the street rate by 2023; the survival food basket
  rose 56 percent in seven months. Week one: a person had what was in the house and what the cap let
  out. A day's pay bought a day's food and nothing more.
- **VENEZUELA, 2016 (Day 1, 2c; Day 69, 2b).** The tags came off and the cashier said the number.
  A CLAP food box held about 12 dollars of goods; a teacher's pay of about 15 dollars a month stood
  against a family food basket of about 500 (33 times, the lane's Venezuela figures). Week one of
  any household there began with a box, not with zero.
THE SHAPE: every real case starts a person with about A WEEK (Argentina's cap, Lebanon's cap, the
CLAP box), not with nothing and not with a month. The record supports a small purse.

## 5. THE FINDING: UNDER RULE 117 EVERY PURSE ON MAIN IS HARDER THAN BATTLE BROTHERS
Rule 117(a) (his 10/18): every felt number starts EASIER than Battle Brothers', the sliders go up
from there, and Battle Brothers' default sits at our HARD end; a page that sets a number harder than
Battle Brothers' without his word is a NO before he sees it. The fair way to compare two games
with different money is RUNWAY: weeks of doing nothing before the purse is empty.

| purse | where it is on main | ours, idle weeks (plains, ration counter, 42 a week) | ours, weeks with every man working (21 short a week) | Battle Brothers at the same rung |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | the demo (RUN 2490fc8; bohemia_origins.js 'NO STARTING MONEY') | 0 | 0 | none |
| 3 | the settlement screen standalone | half a day | one day | none |
| 190 | origins.json A New Company 'bare' (BB Low at ten to one) | 4.5 | 9 | 5.0 (Low) |
| 240 | origins.json 'thin' (BB Medium) | 5.7 | 11.4 | 6.3 (Medium, BB's default) |
| 290 | origins.json 'full' (BB High) | 6.9 | 13.8 | 7.7 (High) |

Read it plainly: the two purses anyone actually plays with (0 and 3) are harder than anything Battle
Brothers ever does. Even the data file's own Battle Brothers numbers come out a little HARDER than
Battle Brothers on the idle measure, because our crew of three must buy water as well as food, by the
ration, from the first night, where Battle Brothers' cart arrives full. They come out EASIER only if
the men work, because our men earn and Battle Brothers' men are paid.

So rule 117 places Battle Brothers' default (Medium, 6.3 idle weeks) at our hard end, and the first
number on main past it is origins.json's own 'full' 290 (6.9 idle weeks). That is the arithmetic, not
a pick: TUNING holds the digit and his slider decides where the default lands.

cart). Seeding the six piles (a week of rations and water in the stash at the door) is the other way to be easier than Battle Brothers without touching the battery number, and it is how the real record
did it (the CLAP box was food, not money).
