HOW I GOT THERE, BECAUSE THE ROUTE IS THE LESSON
------------------------------------------------
HEAD FOLLOWS RIG is a ratchet: it pins how far the drawn head strays from the rig
and lets that number only shrink. Across the 112 flip it moved 13 -> 64. I converted
both to 56-equivalent cells, found the chin row had gone from exact to two cells
wide, reasoned that Scale2x had been rounding that corner off and hiding it, and
published a claim about THE SKINNER -- which RIG LAW names the most dangerous code
in the repo.

Then I went to fix it, and started by asking what the ratchet actually compares.

IT COMPARES THE RIG'S PAINTED **FACE** AGAINST EVERY SKIN-COLOURED PIXEL ON THE ROW
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Part 2 is the face. The drawn row also carries part 1, the HEAD, which is wider than
the face everywhere except the cheekbones -- and on the chin rows part 3, the NECK.
All three are skin. Measured on the real frame, row by row:

    row   rig(face)   posed(head+face)   +neck   drawn skin   verdict
    y12       12            16             16        16       skin == head+face EXACTLY
    y14       16            20             20        20       skin == head+face EXACTLY
    y20       20            20             20        20       skin == head+face EXACTLY
    y28       12            16             16        16       skin == head+face EXACTLY
    y30        8             8             12        12       skin == head+face+NECK exactly
    ...
    UNEXPLAINED PIXELS, ACROSS EVERY ROW: ZERO

Every "deviation" the ratchet has ever reported is the head being wider than the
face. Anatomy, not error. And what moved across the flip was not the skinner: it was
how much of the HEAD EDGE got counted once the upscaler stopped rounding corners off
it.

*** A RULER COMPARING TWO DIFFERENT REGIONS CANNOT BE REPAIRED BY RESCALING IT ***
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
That is the part worth keeping. My first pass this morning DID improve that gate: I
turned a pixel count into a ratio so it would read the same at any resolution, wrote
the reasoning out, and mutation-tested it in both directions. It was a well-built,
carefully calibrated instrument pointed at the wrong thing, and every one of those
good habits made the wrong answer MORE convincing, not less. Scale-free and wrong is
still wrong.

The tell I walked past: the numbers were suspiciously tidy. Every deviating row was
off by exactly 4 at 112 and the zero rows stayed exactly zero. A real skinner defect
does not come out constant across the whole face; a REGION MISMATCH does, because it
is just one region minus another.

The renderer reproduces his painted head exactly. Pinned at ZERO, which is a ratchet

SAMPLED AT THE REST PHASE ON PURPOSE. At a moving phase the skinner is SUPPOSED to

THE PIXELS ARE STILL CHECKED. The gate's jaw-EDGE tone assertion is untouched: on the
rows his art puts the dark anatomy index on, is the edge pixel darker than the one
