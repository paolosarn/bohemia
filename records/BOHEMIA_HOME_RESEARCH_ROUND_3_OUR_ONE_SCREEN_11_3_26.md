# HOME RESEARCH, ROUND 3: OUR ONE SCREEN, THREE DIRECTIONS (rule 349(e); ask line 359)

RESEARCH ONLY. Three ways Bohemia's settlement can be ONE screen (tall and wide, rule 348), the realistic one first, each SHOWN as a
mock of our screen and told in plain words. The mocks are cut from town pictures we already ship (COOK's tall towns) with marks laid on
top; NOTHING NEW IS MODELLED and every picture says MOCK. Tool: tools/bohemia_home_research_mocks_11_3_26.py. Pictures:
records/target/home_research/mock_a.webp, mock_b.webp, mock_c.webp; the VOTE card's picture vote_one_screen_abc.webp (A, B, C side by side).
Built on round 1 (records/BOHEMIA_HOME_RESEARCH_ROUND_1_THE_SURVEY_11_3_26.md) and round 2 (..._ROUND_2_THE_TEARDOWNS_11_3_26.md).

WHAT ALL THREE KEEP (rules 343 and 347, his words): it is optional; it opens once the company can hold a place, never in the first minute;
the first planting asks "are you sure? This is permanent" once; the bosses' prizes stand there (YOUR KITCHEN from THE POT, YOUR MAIN from
THE TAP, YOUR PURIFIER from THE FILTER, every later boss the same way); the journey's people come to live there and each opens one thing;
the daily bill is on the screen every time you open it (batteries, food, water); everything is a tap on the screen, never free placement;
neighbours can come for it, and act two and act three show it grown or fallen. Stardew Valley only for the feel of a place that is yours;
Battle Brothers has no home (our twist; its own players built one, round 2).

## A. THE BLOCK YOU TOOK (realistic first; his own words, 11/2: "the same real estate as the other settlements")
- WHAT IT IS: one real Las Vegas place you plant on (a walled cul-de-sac behind its block wall and gate, a strip mall, a school), painted
  exactly like every town's screen, with about TWELVE FIXED LOTS in it (the yards, the garages, the open ground, the roofs, the gate).
  The mock is the Custom crew's yard with twelve lots numbered and the three prizes where they would stand: the kitchen in the garage, the
  main at the road, the purifier beside it (a purifier wants the main next to it: real).
- WHAT YOU TAP: a lot. It shows what stands there, its level, its look.
- WHAT YOU CUSTOMIZE: what stands on each lot (from what you have built or won); its level (three or four, each one drawn); its material
  and paint (three or four looks inside the colour law); its sign; the place's name (tap the gate); who lives in each house and their job.
- WHAT IT COSTS: a daily battery bill beside the company's; food and water per person (real: 15 litres and 2,100 kcal a person a day,
  Sphere); the panels, the beds and the prizes pay some back.
- HOW IT GROWS: in place. A lot wakes up, gets its next level, a second storey goes up, panels go on the roofs, the wall is rebuilt.
  Never past the frame.
- A SECOND, INDOOR SCREEN: yes, in act two (the clubhouse or the storm drain under the block), his "indoor system as an upgrade".
- LEARNED FROM: Darkest Dungeon's hamlet (one painted place, upgrades seen in place), Suikoden's castle (the people fill it), Clash of Clans
  (a fixed plot that fills up), Battle Brothers' players' Stronghold mod (your own place on the map, attacked, fed by a road).
- THE RISK: twelve lots on an upright phone are small; the tall picture has to keep them a thumb wide (RUN TWO's 44-point rule).

## B. THE CUTAWAY (one building, open)
- WHAT IT IS: your settlement is ONE building seen cut open from the side (a dead casino's parking garage: concrete decks, ramps, the top
  deck in full sun). The mock enlarges the open concrete frame from the Reds picture: roof (panels and the tank), floor 3 (your people's
  rooms), floor 2 (YOUR KITCHEN), floor 1 (YOUR PURIFIER and YOUR MAIN where the pipe comes in), the basement for act two.
- WHAT YOU TAP: a floor's room. It shows what room, its level, its look.
- WHAT YOU CUSTOMIZE: which room on which floor, its level and look, the building's sign and name, who lives on which floor.
- WHAT IT COSTS: the same bill; a building also needs its stairs and its pumps (water up a floor costs power: real).
- HOW IT GROWS: up a deck or down into the basement.
- A SECOND, INDOOR SCREEN: it IS indoors and outdoors at once; act two opens the basement (dig down).
- LEARNED FROM: XCOM's ant farm, Fallout Shelter, This War of Mine and Sheltered.
- THE RISK: no town in the game is a cutaway, so it leaves "the same real estate as the other settlements"; a cutaway that is only a
  menu of rooms reads flat next to the fights (PC Gamer on XCOM: "muffled by flat base design").

## C. THE RING (one heart, rings out)
- WHAT IT IS: one heart in the middle (YOUR MAIN from THE TAP, or a battery bank) and the lots in rings round it. What the heart reaches
  (the water's pressure, the power's reach) is how far your settlement fills; past it the ground is dark. The mock rings the Volunteers'
  ground: eight lots reached, the next ring dashed.
- WHAT YOU TAP: the heart (feed it, upgrade it) or a reached lot.
- WHAT YOU CUSTOMIZE: what stands in each reached lot, its level and look, the heart's kind, the name.
- WHAT IT COSTS: the heart's daily batteries rise with every ring (the next ring in the mock: +6 lots, +4 batteries a day).
- HOW IT GROWS: ring by ring, inside the one view.
- A SECOND, INDOOR SCREEN: none needed; a later heart (act two's) can change what the rings are.
- LEARNED FROM: Frostpunk (a city in circles round one generator, the heat zone grows and the coal bill with it).
- THE RISK: the outer ring's lots get small on a phone; a perfect circle is not how a Vegas block is laid out (realism).

## THE PICK
Realism first: A leads (a real place, the towns' own picture system, his own "same real estate" words), B is the strong alternative (it fits
an upright phone and has the inside built in), C is third (the cleanest upkeep story, the least real layout). He picks with one letter in
the VOTE tab (card home-one-screen-abc-11-3). After his letter: the build (the stopped build's piece tool on branch home-stopped-build-11-3
fits A and C as it is; B needs rooms instead of lots), then RUN TWO's screen, then the data file of what stands on a lot and what puts it
there.
