## RULE 388. THE MYSTERY MEAT (don't ask, don't tell)
- Real floor: in the siege of Leningrad about 2,000 people were arrested for cannibalism; in the Ukrainian famine of 1932-33 about
  2,500 were convicted of it. When food runs out it happens, and nobody talks about it.
- Ours: it is common and never said. The stew on the Strip is 'mystery meat': mostly the Mob's clone meat and whatever beast came in,
  and when a batch dies with the power (rule 375's duct tape), the pot still has meat in it. Everybody eats it; nobody asks.
- Quests: finding out what is in the stew, whose people go missing near the vendor's yard, a buyer for the dead; the Church against it,
  the Mob selling it, a family that will not eat it and starves. Choices, never a lecture.
- Analog horror at the source, never gore: the menu board's chalk, a ring in the bowl, the smell told in a line, the butcher's freezer
  with one more shape in it than there should be.
- QUEUED (QUIET DAYS): DYNASTY or WORLD (the lore), WORDS (the menu boards, the feed), QUESTS research (the library's food quests).

## RULE 389. THE BEAST MATH (no number edited; the realistic answer is the default)
ROUGH MATH (the coordinator's, from his own count; draft):
- His main man: 100 or more down in under 30 days, about 3 a day. If he does a quarter of the company's killing, the company drops
  about 400 a month, 13 a day; over an act (300 to 450 days, rule 84) about 4,000 to 6,000. If a third are beasts: 1,300 to 2,000
  beasts an act, from one company, and the valley has others fighting too.
WHAT THE VALLEY CAN REALLY HOLD:
- Dogs: about one for every four Americans; the valley's 2.3 million people had about half a million. If 19 in 20 died with their
  owners, 25,000 are left, and they eat the dead and breed every year. Coyotes, ranch cattle and Nevada's wild horses and burros (about
  30,000 in the state today) on top. Killing two thousand an act is nothing to them.
- Lab beasts: a clone farm runs 'a hundred, keep three' (rule 359) on power the Mob barely has: tens a year, never thousands.
- Clones can breed (Dolly the sheep had six lambs). The FAST breeders grow: a wolf has 4 to 6 pups a year, a bison a calf a year, a
  hog two litters of about 6; thirty dire wolves fed by the dead can be a thousand in about ten years. The SLOW ones cannot: a bear
  has two cubs every two or three years, a sloth or a lion fewer; dozens in the whole valley.
THE DEFAULT (realism first):
1. Most beast fights are the ferals that the dead feed: dogs, coyotes, hogs, cattle, horses and burros. They are many, and that is real.
2. The fast-breeding lab lines (dire wolves, bison, the hog lines) are common enough to hunt every week.
3. The slow lab giants (the short-faced bear, the sloth, the sabre-tooth, the American lion, Camelops) are RARE: a named hunt, a few an
   act, worth the trip (Battle Brothers' unholds and lindwurms are rare the same way).
4. THE LOOP: more dead, more food, more beasts and more vultures (rule 386); a valley he fights in hard fills with them. The dead feed
   the beasts, the beasts feed the people, the people feed the stew (rule 388).
- QUEUED (QUIET DAYS): BEASTS and WORLD (which beast, how often, by place), TUNING (the rates in the one table). No number is changed
  until he says.

## RULE 388 AMENDED AGAIN: IT IS HOW PEOPLE SURVIVED, AND THE SHAKES (ask line 425)
HIS WORDS: "Cause to be honest, it is perfectly good meat that could go to waste bro and if I have to feed my family personally bro,

- The stew is survival, said plainly: the people alive in the valley lived through the famine years on it.
- THE SHAKES ARE REAL (the coordinator's hole, draft:true, his to keep): kuru, among the Fore people of Papua New Guinea, spread through
  eating the brains of their dead at funerals: a prion disease (mad cow's family) that shows five to twenty years or more later, with
  trembling and fits of laughing, and kills. So the survivors' one rule: NEVER THE HEAD. The ones who broke it shake years later, and in
  act two and act three (the future computed from the past) the shaking shows in whoever ate wrong: a mark a player can read on a face.
- 'No farming' holds for act one: the Mojave gets about four inches of rain, and without power nothing pumps the lake; his settlement's
  garden bed and water tank (his 10/1 picks) are how farming comes back.
