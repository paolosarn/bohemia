# THE CARCASS: HAUL IT, TELL SOMEBODY, OR LEAVE IT (Paolo 11/4, the quiet days; VAMILY rule 392; ask line 427)

## THE REAL FLOOR
- A male mountain lion weighs about 60 to 70 kg; the American lion (rule 345's Ice Age lion) was about 250 to 350 kg. About half a
  carcass is meat a person can eat. One Ice Age lion is over 100 kg of meat; a man eats about half a kilo a day: one lion feeds a
  company of ten for about three weeks, or a block for days.
- Mojave heat: a carcass has to be gutted within about an hour and cooled or it starts to spoil; in summer meat turns in hours, not days.
  The vultures (rule 386) and the coyotes find it first. Butchering a big animal takes a few hours of work.
- Battle Brothers: its beasts leave TROPHIES (pelts, teeth, hearts) for the taxidermist, and a hunt pays by the contract; nobody hauls the
  meat. Ours adds the meat, because in this valley people are starving (rules 359, 388, 389).

## RULE 392. THE CARCASS IS A THING ON THE MAP, AND IT ROTS (draft:true; his to change)
1. After a beast fight the bodies stay where they fell, on the map (the fight's place), never 'bite-size pieces in the bag'.
2. A CLOCK YOU CAN SEE: fresh, turning, gone (Mojave hours: about a day at most, less in summer); the vultures circling over it (rule
   386) are the timer; coyotes and dogs come to it, so a carcass left too long draws a fight.
3. THREE WAYS TO CLAIM IT (choices, never a card: on the phone and in the settlement screen):
   a. HAUL IT: the camp's shopping cart (his idea; the cart is already one of the camp's pieces): the carcass rides in it as ONE heavy
      good, the party travels slower while it hauls, and at a town it sells to the butcher or the Mob's stew for batteries, or it feeds
      his company (days of food in the stash).
   b. TELL SOMEBODY: post the spot from the phone to a town that is hungry; their own hunters go and get it, and they pay him in
      batteries when it comes in fresh (his idea: 'the location of the corpses for immediate retrieval'). A hunt job can ask for this
      up front: more pay if the meat comes home.
   c. LEAVE IT: the vultures and the coyotes take it; a few days later a pack of something hungry lives near there.
4. DRESSING IT TAKES TIME: a few hours on the clock at the kill (a camp action, the men's own animation), more meat if he spends them,
   less and faster if he does not.
5. HOW IT DIED counts (draft): a clean kill keeps more meat than one torn apart.
6. Early game, this is real money: a lion's meat is a lot of batteries to a new company, and a reason to take the hunt.
7. The loop of rule 389: beasts feed the people; what is left feeds the beasts and the vultures; and the stew (rule 388) is never far.
