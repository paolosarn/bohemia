## WHAT THE CRAFT SAYS (the research)
- **Material is irregular pixel noise.** Stone, rust, fabric and dirt read as those
  materials largely BECAUSE of irregular noise. Remove the noise and the eye is
  given nothing to name, so it reads the surface as plastic.
- **Vary all three, not one.** Hue, saturation and brightness together get far more
  out of a few colours than brightness alone. Shifting HUE between neighbouring
  values is what makes a small palette look rich; a ramp that only darkens looks
  flat no matter how many steps it has. That is precisely what a low colour-density
  number measures.
- **A perfectly even tile announces that it is a tile.** Repeated regularity is the
  tell; the fix is small dark patches, incidental detail, and three or four variants
  placed semi-randomly.
- **Dithering is a density dial, not a texture.** Alternating two colours reads as a
  third; biasing the mix slides the blend. Contemporary styles often drop it on
  purpose for clean flats, which is a STYLE CHOICE and has to be made deliberately,
  not arrived at by cooking clean and never comparing.

## THE HONEST BOTH-SIDES
Our own law 0 says a tile is DECISIONS, not a shrunk painting, and some of the
cleanness is the craft working on purpose. A 0.16x median is not proof of bad art.
But 0.03x on the ground the player looks at for a hundred hours, plus ten tiles lit
from the wrong corner, is not a style either. It is drift, and drift is what the
COMPARE law was written to catch.
