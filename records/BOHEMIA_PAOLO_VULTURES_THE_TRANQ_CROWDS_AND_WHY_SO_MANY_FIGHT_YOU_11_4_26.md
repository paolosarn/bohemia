## RULE 386. VULTURES (his sister's idea, his yes)
- Real first: the turkey vulture rides the Mojave's thermals all over the valley today, and the California condor, brought back from 22
  birds in 1987, flies again a few hundred miles east (the Grand Canyon, Vermilion Cliffs). In a valley where billions died (rule 360)
  the vultures are the one animal that is doing well. They do not attack the living (realism): they wait.
- In the game: circles of vultures over the dead (rule 360's places), over a fight that just ended, over a hunt's kill, over a boss's
  yard (the Valheim sign of rule 243); they follow a company that leaves bodies behind it (the more he kills, the more ride over his
  party marker on the map: the world sees what he is). Analog horror at the source: a shadow sliding over a camera feed, the sound of
  wings landing on a roof, a bird on a dead man's chest that does not fly when you come.
- Draft, his to change: a condor is rare and an omen (a feed post when one is seen).
- QUEUED (QUIET DAYS): BEASTS (the birds as real models through the rig), WORLD (the circles on the map, the ones that follow him),
  WORDS (the feed's posts), SOUNDS.

## AN IDEA, NOT LOCKED: THE TRANQ CROWDS ('fentanyl homeless zombies')
- His idea, 'keep this in mind'. The real thing under it: fentanyl cut with xylazine ('tranq') leaves people standing bent and frozen,
  with wounds that rot; Kensington in Philadelphia is filmed for it every day. In a valley with no clinics it would be worse.
- The coordinator's honest note: a crowd of real addicts as things to kill is the one place this game could punch down at real people
  in trouble now; the realism-first version keeps them PEOPLE, and the horror comes from that (Battle Brothers' lesson for its dead
  that walk is 'many, slow, no fear': the same mechanic can wear a human face).
- Three ways, his pick (asked in the reply): A) people on the real drug, still people: slow, numb, many, they do not run and do not
  chase far, most are best walked around, some are dangerous; B) a lab-made street drug from the collapse (the vat years' chemistry,
  rule 359) that makes users feral and horde-like: Battle Brothers' undead lesson in a human skin; C) not an enemy at all: part of the
  dead and the living on the streets, scenery and stories.
- Nothing is built until he picks.

## RULE 387. WHY SO MANY FIGHT YOU (justify the fighting; keep the fighting)
- He likes the volume ('we're fighting a lot... I want that'); the lore must explain it. Over 100 men down to one man in under 30 days.
- The real floor: Port-au-Prince's gangs held about 80 percent of the city by 2024 and killed thousands a year; in the Thirty Years' War
  (Battle Brothers' own model) a mercenary company lived by fighting and was hunted by everyone it robbed; a crew with guns and
  batteries in a dead economy is the richest thing on any road.
- The coordinator's draft of the reasons (draft:true; his to change):
  1. MOST FIGHTS ARE WORK: he takes contracts and hunts (the jobs): he is paid to go and fight; the kill count says how many were jobs.
  2. HE IS WORTH ROBBING: batteries are the money (9/4); a company carrying them, guns and medicine is a caravan to every hungry crew
     (Battle Brothers' brigands go for caravans and companies the same way).
  3. HE IS KNOWN: clout (renown) draws the ones who want to test him and the factions who want him gone (Battle Brothers' renown brings
     harder fights); a feed post when a crew is coming for his name.
  4. THE DEAD HAVE FRIENDS: a crew he cut down has kin and a boss; some come back for him (a vendetta mark, the feed's own posts).
  5. THERE IS NOTHING ELSE: the vats died in a week (rule 359), the stores are empty; fighting is the job left, for everyone.
  6. NOT EVERY MAN DOWN IS DEAD: struck down is a wound for his side (20 percent death); theirs too can be left alive, run, or give up,
     and the count can say killed, wounded, ran (Battle Brothers counts kills only).
- QUEUED (QUIET DAYS): DYNASTY or WORLD (the lore page), WORDS (the feed's reasons and the kill count's words), TUNING (how many fights
  a month a company really meets, against Battle Brothers' own pace). Ask line 420.
