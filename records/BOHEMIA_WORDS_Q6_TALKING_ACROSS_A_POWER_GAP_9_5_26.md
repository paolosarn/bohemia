# WORDS Q6 -- TALKING ACROSS A POWER GAP
# VAMILY research round, 9/5/26, lane WORDS (words-8dqrnq). MODE: RESEARCH.
# The question, verbatim: "Talking across a power gap. Sociolinguistics of address:
# how a boss talks to a hand, a stranger to a fortress, a camp kid to anyone. Test
# lines for one exchange at each gap."

## THE ANSWER IN ONE LINE
**A power gap is never visible in one line, only in the ASYMMETRY between two, and
our valley has no asymmetry at all: in 1,669 spoken lines there are exactly TWO
upward-address lines, and one of them I wrote myself.**

## 1. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG

    marker                  all 1,669 spoken lines
    upward address          0.1%     (sir, ma'am, boss, jefe, mister)  -- TWO LINES
    downward address        0.7%     (kid, son, hermano, man)
    politeness marker       1.0%     (please, thank you, excuse me)
    bare command            2.6%
    softened request        0.3%     (could you, would you, mind if)

Here are both upward lines in the entire build:

    "...and it stays between us. Who's going to tell them, boss? Me?"
    "That's a tile now, boss. Not a pad."

**Nobody in Bohemia has ever said sir, ma'am, mister or jefe.** Everyone speaks to
everyone as an exact equal.

And the world is NOT flat. The cast already carries the hierarchy:

    power side   keeper 43, watch 27, sergeant 21, owner 16, pastor 15,
                 foreman 11, holder 9, boss 8, blue_boss 8, red_boss 6, elder 6
    hand side    worker 20, hauler 20, neighbor 20, scav 19, neighbour 12,
                 tenant 10, busker 10, canvasser 8, runner 5

**Bosses, a foreman, a sergeant and a watch all exist and none of them is spoken to
differently from a scav.** The world model has the gap. The language does not.

## 2. THE SCIENCE, AND IT REFRAMES THE JOB
**Brown and Gilman (1960), the pronouns of power and solidarity.** Their finding is
not that powerful people are addressed politely. It is that a power gap is
**NON-RECIPROCAL**: the superior gives the familiar form and receives the polite
one. Equals exchange the SAME form, whichever it is. Solidarity is symmetrical;
power is asymmetrical.

**Brown and Ford (1961)** put the same thing in American English, where the choice
is first name against title-plus-last-name: two reciprocal patterns running from
acquaintance to intimacy, and one NON-reciprocal pattern governed by age and
occupational status.

**THE CONSEQUENCE FOR US, AND IT IS THE WHOLE ROUND: A POWER GAP CANNOT BE WRITTEN
INTO A LINE. IT CAN ONLY BE WRITTEN INTO A PAIR.** Every previous round in this
lane has been measuring single lines. This one cannot be. What the player reads as
rank is the DIFFERENCE between how A speaks to B and how B speaks back, and if you
only ever ship one side of the exchange there is no rank in the scene at all.

**Brown and Levinson's politeness theory** then gives the dial. The weight of an
imposition is **W = D + P + R**: social Distance plus relative Power plus the Rank
of what you are asking for. The heavier the sum, the more indirect the phrasing,
running from bald-on-record ("Move"), through positive politeness ("Do me a favour,
move"), negative politeness ("Sorry, could you move?"), to off-record hint ("I
can't get past"). **That is a three-input formula for exactly how a line should
bend, and it is buildable: we already know distance, we already know faction rank,
and the ask is the quest's own.**

## 3. THE THREE GAPS THE QUESTION NAMES

    A BOSS TO A HAND        P high, D low, R low. Bald on record and downward
                            address. "Take the small stuff first, kid."
                            The hand answers upward and softened.
    A STRANGER TO A FORTRESS P high, D HIGH, R high. The heaviest sum in the game,
                            so the most indirect: no imperative, no name, a
                            reason offered before the ask.
    A CAMP KID TO ANYONE    P low, D low, R low, and the kid does not know the
                            rules yet, so the comedy and the danger are the same
                            thing: the kid speaks to a sergeant exactly as he
                            speaks to a scav. THAT is how you show a gap exists --
                            by writing somebody who ignores it.

The third one is the free one. **A child who addresses everybody identically makes
every adult's asymmetry visible by contrast**, and costs one character.

## 4. AND THE RULER BROKE AGAIN, THE FIFTEENTH TIME
My first address counter reported **7.2%** upward address and printed a list of
"upward" lines that were all things like "Don't touch that, it's live." The pattern
included Spanish `don` as a title, and `\bdon\b` matches the **don** inside
**don't**. Every contraction in the game was scoring as deference.

Fixed by requiring an address term to sit in ADDRESS POSITION (after a comma,
before a comma, or standing alone as the clause), which dropped the honest figure
from 7.2% to **0.1%**. Caught by reading the printed lines instead of trusting the
percentage. The rule this lane keeps relearning: **print the hits, never just the
rate.**

## 5. WHAT THIS SAYS TO DO, WHEN THE LANE RETURNS TO BUILD
1. **SHIP EXCHANGES, NOT LINES, WHEREVER RANK MATTERS.** One side of a conversation
   cannot carry a power gap. Our `exchange` kind exists and holds 148 lines with
   ZERO address terms of either direction; that is where this work goes.
2. **THE PAIR IS THE UNIT: WHAT HE CALLS YOU, WHAT YOU CALL HIM.** If both sides
   use the same form, they are equals, and the scene has said so whether or not
   anybody meant it.
3. **W = D + P + R AS A DIAL, NOT A TABLE.** Distance and power are already in the
   world model and the ask belongs to the quest, so the bend can be computed rather
   than hand-written per pair.
4. **ONE CHILD WHO ADDRESSES EVERYONE THE SAME.** Cheapest possible way to make the
   whole hierarchy legible.
