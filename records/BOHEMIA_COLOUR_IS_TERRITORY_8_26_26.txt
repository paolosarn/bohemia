--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS IS REALISM, RESEARCHED THE DAY HE SAID IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
His reference is exact and the history holds it up:
  - colours signal allegiance, claim territory and IDENTIFY RIVALS AT A DISTANCE,
    and that distance is the entire function
  - the practice began as PRACTICAL NECESSITY in dense urban areas: it lets a
    group project presence WITHOUT VERBAL CONFRONTATION
  - the choices were OPPOSITIONAL -- the Bloods took red deliberately to contrast
    the Crips' blue. A colour only means something against another colour
  - the origins were MUNDANE AND MATERIAL: one account of the Crips' blue is
    simply which bandanas the local surplus store had in bulk
  - and they are EASY TO TAKE OFF if you are stopped
That last one is a mechanic waiting to happen: wearing your colours is a decision
with a cost, made every time you go out.

================================================================================
PART TWO: THE FACE TALKS
================================================================================
"every time you speak to someone, their portrait will pop up on screen so you
 feel like you're relating to them... I said facial animations too, bro, like
 talking and shit and, like... from eyebrows moving"

THREE KNOBS ON renderFace, all optional, all defaulting to EXACTLY the approved
face -- which is the only way to add performance to art that is already approved:

  MOUTH   'closed' | 'mid' | 'open' | 'wide'
  BLINK   0 (open) .. 1 (shut)
  BROW    -1 (lowered, angry) .. +1 (raised, asking)

*** THE MOUTH IS DRIVEN BY THE LETTERS THEY ARE ACTUALLY SAYING. *** Not a random
flap. The line is walked one sound at a time and each sound picks a shape, so a
person saying "oye, mira" makes an O and a person saying "no se" does not, and
THE SAME LINE ALWAYS LOOKS THE SAME. Spanglish is IN the line so it is in the
mouth: accented vowels map with their plain twins and n-tilde closes like an n.

FOUR SHAPES, NOT PRESTON BLAIR'S TEN. His chart is the standard and it is for a
face with a thousand pixels. THIS MOUTH IS NINE PIXELS WIDE AND THREE TALL. Ten
shapes into nine pixels is the same mistake as the overworld face scale he killed
on 8/11 -- asking geometry to express what it has no room for. Four that READ
beat ten that do not, and that is the 8/25 hair law in a different organ: draw in
the pixels we actually have.

EVERY NUMBER IS MEASURED FROM PEOPLE, NOT INVENTED:
    a spontaneous blink is 100-400ms, mean ~285ms
    people blink 15-20 times a minute at rest, 3-7 while concentrating,
      2-10 seconds apart
    speech runs 4-5 syllables a second, so a shape holds about 200-250ms
250ms is inside that speech range AND exactly half a BEAT at 120 BPM, so the
mouth lands on the same grid as everything else in the game (120 BPM LAW).
A LISTENING FACE BLINKS MORE THAN A TALKING ONE, which is the measured result and
also the right read: somebody mid-sentence is concentrating.

DETERMINISTIC, ALWAYS. Everything keys off the person's id. No Math.random, so
the same person blinks on their own rhythm every time you meet them and nobody in
a crowd shimmers -- the same rule the hair wobble lives under.

AND THE PICTURE LIED ON ITS FIRST CUT, for the fourth time this week. The blink
row asked facePerform for a person whose blink was not due in that 285ms window
and drew eight identical open eyes -- a picture saying "the blink does nothing"
about code that works. The row now walks the blink through its own shape AND
prints the schedule separately, so neither claim can hide behind the other.
