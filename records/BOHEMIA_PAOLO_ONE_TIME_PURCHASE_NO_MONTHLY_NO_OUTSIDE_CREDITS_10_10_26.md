> "So yeah, I'd rather prefer something that can just get implemented into my repo. I make a one time purchase you know and then it's just doing awesome stuff within my repo to be honest like I'm not bro you know a monthly thing but I don't want anything that uses outside credits you know I just want something that uses our credit or even saves a lot of credits because it's really good at what it does instead of being some dog shit seventh grader coder like you are."

## WHAT I HEARD (rule 91c; correct me with one word)

1. Buy once, own it, it lives in the repo and runs there. No subscriptions.
2. Nothing that burns a third party's credits per image or per sound. It runs on our own time (the chats' sessions) or on his machine.
3. Good tools should save credits, not spend them, because they do in one call what a chat fumbles for an hour.

## THE LIST, RE-CUT TO ONE-TIME OR FREE (from the two research rounds)

ONE-TIME:
- Aseprite, $19.99 once (https://dacap.itch.io/aseprite): the pixel editor every pixel artist uses; headless and scriptable, so a chat writes the Lua and Aseprite assembles sheets, locks palettes and exports eight directions.
- Retro Diffusion's Aseprite extension, $65 once (https://astropulse.itch.io/retrodiffusion): the same pixel-art model, running LOCALLY inside Aseprite, unlimited, no credits. The catch, said plainly: it runs on a graphics card in the computer it is installed on. The chats' cloud machines have no graphics card, so this runs on his computer; the chats write the scripts and the prompts, he (or a script on his machine) presses go, the output lands in the repo.

FREE, DOWNLOADED ONCE INTO THE REPO'S LIBRARY FOLDER:
- Sonniss GDC Game Audio Bundles (every year; royalty-free, commercial, no attribution), Freesound CC0 recordings, EchoThief room impulse responses (derivative work allowed).
- Pedalboard (Spotify, Python) with Chow Tape Model for the analog horror chain; pixelize and MakeItPixel for the palette snap; bfxr for UI clicks; ImageMagick for the seam tests.
- The free trial credits of Retro Diffusion's and PixelLab's online APIs: one-time by nature, used for the day-one test only, then never again (they are outside credits).

DROPPED, BY HIS RULE: Retro Diffusion's online credits, PixelLab's subscription, ElevenLabs, Scenario, Mubert, Soundly, Boom Library subscriptions. (Boom's single libraries are one-time purchases and may come back if a specific sound is missing.)

## THE HONEST LINE

Pixel art of pack quality does not come out of code alone; that is what failed for three months. It comes out of a model or an artist. With no monthly and no outside credits, the model has to run on a graphics card he owns, and the chats drive it through Aseprite. If his computer has no graphics card, the one-time route for new art is a one-time artist commission for the hero set (the research priced it at $500 to $2,000), and the chats' job becomes assembling his packs and the commissioned pieces, which is free and deterministic and saves credits.
