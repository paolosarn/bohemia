================================================================================
ROOT CAUSE, AND THE METRIC DID NOT SEE IT
================================================================================
First thing I checked: the numbers. UP averaged edge 18.31 / grain 61.2%; DOWN
averaged edge 15.98 / grain 59.1%. Nearly identical, both deep inside tolerance.
The style target — the thing this whole lane is built on — had NOTHING to say about
why he rejected seven of them. That is worth writing down: a density ruler measures
whether a tile is as rich as his purchased art. It cannot see STRUCTURE, and it
cannot see anything about how a tile behaves when the wall is fifty of them long.

Then I looked, which is what should have happened first.

*** ONE HERO FEATURE, STAMPED AT EXACTLY 44px PITCH, FOREVER. ***
The cook drew ONE face tile per design and the run repeated it down the whole wall.
So the single crack — or on stucco, a single bright weed rosette — baked into that
tile reappeared on EVERY CELL, in the same place, all the way along the block. An
identical hard-edged mark on a perfect grid does not read as damage. It reads as a
rendering fault. "Looks like it's glitching out" is a precise description of what
was actually on screen, not a vague impression.

WHY THIS NEVER SHOWED UP BEFORE: his purchased ground library is shuffled fifteen
tiles deep per cell, so the repeat pitch is fifteen cells and invisible. The wall's
pitch was one.

WHY EXACTLY THOSE SEVEN: tan slump and split-face have strong block coursing, and a
regular masonry grid OUTRANKS the stamp — the eye reads the coursing and files the
crack as chipping. Stucco and precast are flat fields where the stamp is the only
structure there is, so it becomes the whole read. All three stucco colourways went
down. Both flat precast colourways went down. The one precast he kept (#3) is the
darkest, where the crack has least contrast to work with.

THE FIX, AND IT IS STRUCTURAL, NOT A DIAL
  1. EIGHT face and EIGHT base variants per design, shuffled by a 2D hash of the
     cell — the same way the ground has always shuffled his bought tiles. There is
     no visible period left at all.
  2. MOST VARIANTS CARRY NO DAMAGE. One face in four is allowed an event. A wall is
     not a road: his concrete is crazed edge to edge because thirty years of traffic
     did that, and a garden wall gets a crack here and a crack there.
  3. STUCCO IS "STUCCO OVER BLOCK" AND THE "OVER BLOCK" WAS MISSING. A real Vegas
     garden wall is skim coat over CMU and the coursing telegraphs through it,
     especially after thirty years of sun. Drawn as a flat field it gave the eye
     nothing to hold except the one crack. Both stucco materials now carry ghost
     coursing at the block cook's own 11x22 module.

================================================================================
THE GATE: HE LIKED IT AND THUMBED IT DOWN, AND THE CARD IS WHY
================================================================================
"The gate assembly stuff actually looks decent" next to three thumbs down is not a
contradiction — it is a report that the CARD was wrong, not the art. To fit both
gate kinds in one strip I put the STEEL LEAF on the coping row and the OPEN MOUTH
on the row directly below it. So he was looking at a single opening that was barred
across the top and empty underneath: two different gates stacked, which is exactly
"glitching out" again, and this time I built it into the judging surface myself.

The game never did this — the kind is seeded per plot, so a neighbourhood is all
steel or all open. The card invented the defect.

FIXED: the two kinds are separate strips now, each drawn the way a plot actually
wears it.

LESSON, AND IT IS THE SAME ONE TWICE IN ONE PAGE: a judging surface that shows art
in an arrangement the game never produces is not a judging surface. It manufactures
verdicts about things that do not exist.
