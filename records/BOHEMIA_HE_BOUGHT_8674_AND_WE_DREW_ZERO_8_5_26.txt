THE BLOCKER EVERYONE ASSUMED WAS IMAGINARY

The masters are ~96px and the art cell is 44px, and that mismatch is why 180MB of
purchased art sat dark: the no-resample law was being read as "art must be
cell-sized."

IT DOES NOT SAY THAT. It says AN ART PIXEL IS A WHOLE NUMBER OF SCREEN PIXELS.

A 96px prop blitted at the run's own integer zoom step is perfectly legal. It
simply spans about two cells -- which is exactly what an object the size of a
market stall SHOULD do. Nothing had to be re-cut, re-cooked or re-bought.
Nobody checked.

carrying his UP verdict. Placed by SURFACE, which is mechanism and not content:
a road gets wrecks, a sidewalk gets street furniture, dead ground gets the desert
and what blew into it, an open lot gets barricades, stalls and goods. He decided
what exists; this only decides that a rock belongs on dirt.

  street 18 · wreck 18 · trash 18 · crate 18 · dead 18 · barrier 18 · camp 18

One cell in nine. Never under his feet, never in a doorway, never inside a
building, never on a driveway (a car has to get up it). PURE PAINT: nothing here
touches passability or occupancy, because a decorative object that quietly made a
cell impassable would break pathing across the valley and read as a world bug.

THREE CUTS, AND WHAT THE RENDER CAUGHT THAT THE NUMBERS DID NOT

CUT 1 drew nothing, twice, while every source check passed. I invented the
world's vocabulary out of my own head TWICE -- first tile ids ('yard_0'), then a
prefix ('yard') -- when the dossier actually calls that ground "dead-ground
(yard)". Fixed by SAMPLING the real names across eight districts before writing
the map. Same lesson as the pack names an hour earlier: build the filter from his
data, never from my assumption.

CUT 2 put a CAR-SIZED GLOWING POTION JAR and a two-metre backpack on a suburban
lawn. 'jars, bottles and items' was UP 47 and 'survival props' UP 49, so the
filter took them.
*** HIS VERDICT WAS NOT WRONG. MY READING OF IT WAS. *** The sweep says every
asset was "individually judged IN CONTEXT", and the context for a jar is a shelf
and for a backpack is a pickup. A VERDICT ON AN OBJECT IS NOT A LICENCE TO RENDER
IT AT ANY SIZE. Those are LOOT, a different system, and they are out of the
scenery pool until something picks things up.

CUT 3 put wooden market barrels and slatted crates on a Las Vegas front lawn.
Right tiles, wrong WHERE. Split into TRASH (bags and debris, which blow anywhere)
and CRATE (market goods, lots and swap meets only). Same tiles, same verdicts,
different placement.

Only the third render is what shipped. The numbers were green at cut 1.
