## THE THING I HAVE TO SAY FIRST

**MACHINE PARTY IS A 3D GAME. BOHEMIA IS PIXEL ART. YOU CANNOT HAVE ITS
GEOMETRY, AND YOU DO NOT NEED IT.**

Machine Party is low-poly 3D, built in Blender, described by reviewers as "stark
PSX-like." Bohemia is hand-painted pixel art at a fixed three-quarter 45 degree
view. If the plan were "copy the look," the plan would be dead on arrival.

But that is not what is actually going on when you look at that game and want
yours to look like it. Almost nothing that makes Machine Party look the way it
looks is about polygons. The list below is what I found, and **six of the eight
findings are resolution-independent** — they are rules about shape, dirt, value
and darkness that a pixel artist executes exactly as well as a 3D artist. One of
them is, I think, the direct answer to why our world does not look consistent
yet.

The other lesson, the RF4 lesson, still applies: this record describes what that
game does. It does not import it.

## ROUND 1 — WHO MADE IT, AND WHAT HE BELIEVES

**Machine Party**, Steam app id **4108000**, released **30 July 2026**. By **Mike
Klubnika** (Estonia, solo dev of *Buckshot Roulette* and *Unsorted Horror*) with
co-developer **GDeavid**, published by **Oro Interactive**, announced at the 2026
Triple-i showcase. 2–4 player, 15 lethal minigames. Industrial soundtrack by
**Alex Peipman**. Very Positive on Steam (~89%).

Klubnika is the whole reason the look is coherent, because he has said out loud
what the rules are. Three statements matter, and they are design laws, not art
tips:

1. **THE OBJECTS ARE DESIGNED BY A FICTIONAL ENGINEER WHO DOES NOT CARE HOW THEY
   LOOK.** On Buckshot Roulette: all the machines were made with very sharp
   angles, and **no coat of paint to make them look pretty — just the bare
   minimum to get the job done** from the fictional engineer's side.
2. **BRUTALISM IS THE ARCHITECTURAL REFERENCE.** Most of his environments are
   industrial; brutalist architecture is a huge inspiration. Everything is
   designed to fit a specific purpose, and **it does not think about the user
   experience.**
3. **A MACHINE BUILT TO HARM IS ALREADY SINISTER.** His words: if a machine is
   made to harm something then it is already pretty sinister, because it is an
   emotionless thing with no mercy. And in both games it mattered that **there is
   no failsafe** — you cannot stop them.

That third one is a horror rule, not a paint rule, and it is worth writing down
because it is free. He gets menace out of *what the object is for*, before a
single texture is applied.

## ROUND 2 — HOW HE ACTUALLY MAKES THE PIXELS

This is the round with the finding I care about most.

- **TOOLS:** Blender for models. **Paint.NET and Texture Ripper** for textures.
  He **photographs some textures himself**, mainly of electronics.
- **HE CHOSE 3D TO GET LIGHTING FOR FREE.** He was interested in 2D art with
  lighting and shadows, and found that working in Blender let him **focus on
  shape and textures while the computer handled lighting**. That is a very
  honest statement of what 3D bought him — and it is the one thing we have to
  buy by hand.
- **HE DOES NOT UV UNWRAP PROPERLY, AND NOBODY NOTICES.** Verbatim shape of it:
  you can get away with not properly unwrapping objects, having textures look
  weird and stretched in places, and nobody thinks "that texture is stretched"
  because **it fits in with the aesthetic's grunginess**.
- **★ THE UNIFYING GRIME PASS.** When he had finished all the textures in
  Buckshot Roulette, he went back and **added dirty, grimy leaks to every
  corner** of every texture — and the effect was that it **blends the entire
  thing together rather than having different objects.**
- **NAMED INFLUENCE:** PilotRedSun's animations — a lo-fi look and sound that
  made him appreciate **how cool stretched textures can look**.

Read that fourth bullet again, because it is the answer to a problem we
actually have. Our world does not read as one place. The instinct is to fix it
by making each asset better. **His fix was a single consistency pass applied to
everything, after the assets were done.** The grime is not decoration. The grime
is the thing that makes fifty separately-authored objects look like they have
been sitting in the same building for twenty years.

And the bullet above it is the permission that comes with it: **once the grime
pass is consistent, individual imperfection stops being visible.** For a factory
that has to output hundreds of tiles, that is not a small thing. It says the
route to a consistent world is not a higher bar per tile.

## ROUND 3 — THE PALETTE AND THE VALUE STRUCTURE

- **THE PALETTE IS TINY AND IT IS WARM-DEAD.** The community-catalogued Buckshot
  Roulette palette is **five colours**: `#5a0d0d`, `#5c2616`, `#ad5437`,
  `#d69700`, `#ebdec7` — near-black oxblood, dark rust, a mid clay, one ochre,
  one bone. That is a dark-red-to-bone ramp with **exactly one warm accent** and
  **no cool colour at all**.
- **HE ACCEPTED A LEGIBILITY COST TO KEEP THE GRADE.** Players report that the
  colour-coded shells look faded and hard to tell apart, and blame the post-
  processing and lighting. There are literally Steam threads about it. He did not
  brighten them. **The mood outranked the readability of a gameplay-critical
  item.** That is a real decision with a real price, and he paid it.
- **DARK IS THE DEFAULT AND LIGHT IS THE EXCEPTION.** Machine Party reviews land
  on the same words repeatedly: gritty low-poly, **dark lighting**, claustrophobic
  nightmare, grim, mechanical, "industrial, almost clinical." One review reaches
  for *Nightmare Kart* and *Twisted Metal* as the visual family.
- **THE LOOK IS DELIBERATELY ANTI-CHEERFUL, AND THE MUSIC ENFORCES IT.** Reviewers
  noted that an industrial soundtrack under a *party game* is the tell: it signals
  the game is not chasing the bright cheerful aesthetic that owns that genre. The
  art direction wants to feel **genuinely threatening rather than cartoonishly
  friendly.**

## ROUND 4 — MOTION, FRAMING, AND WHAT THE VIOLENCE LOOKS LIKE

- **★ THE ANIMATION READS AS STOP-MOTION.** The most-repeated praise in reviews
  is that the animation has a **wonderfully tactile quality that often makes the
  action look like stop-motion.** Not smooth. Not interpolated. Held, weighted,
  stepped.
- **THE SETTING IS A TEST FACILITY, AND THE ART SELLS THAT FIRST.** Players are
  customizable **test subjects**; reviewers say the clinical industrial art
  direction "fits the test subject framing well." The look establishes the
  premise before any text does.
- **THE HARM IS MACHINE-SHAPED, NOT WOUND-SHAPED.** The 15 minigames are buzz
  saws, gatling guns, minefields, steamrollers, escalators, firearm-assembly
  races, competitive pea eating. Train dodging. Outrunning a steamroller.
  Sprinting up a down escalator over "whatever is at the bottom." **The dread is
  carried by the apparatus and by what is about to happen** — not by rendered
  damage on a body.
- **THEY CUT THE FLUFF ON PURPOSE.** The devs say they deliberately cut dialogue,
  transitions and the board-game scaffolding that minigame collections usually
  have, to make it frantic and back-to-back with almost no wait times.

That third bullet is the same conclusion the Crisis Response study reached from
the opposite direction, and the two now agree: **the threat does the work, not
the gore.** `laws/BOHEMIA_ADDENDUM_TRAUMATIC_NOT_GORY_7_31_26.md` already says
gore is never the mechanism. Machine Party is a shipped, Very Positive, extremely
violent game whose horror is almost entirely *anticipation of an emotionless
machine with no failsafe*. That is corroboration, not a new direction.

## WHAT TRANSFERS TO A PIXEL-ART 45 DEGREE GAME

Six things, and every one of them is a rule about authoring, not about rendering.

**1. NO PAINT. Every object is designed by somebody who did not care how it
looked.** Sharp angles, bare minimum to do the job, zero decoration that is not
functional. This costs nothing to adopt and it is already true of our fiction: a
city ten years into a dead grid does not have anybody prettying anything up.

**2. ONE GRIME PASS OVER EVERYTHING, AFTER THE FACT.** Dirt, streaks and leaks in
every corner of every tile, authored as a single consistent pass across the whole
bank — not per-asset flavour. This is the finding that most directly addresses
"the world does not look consistent."

**3. A FIVE-COLOUR RAMP AND ONE WARM ACCENT.** Narrow, desaturated, warm-dead,
no cool colour unless it is doing a specific job. Pixel art does hard palette
discipline better than 3D does — this is the one place we are structurally
advantaged.

**4. DARK IS THE DEFAULT. LIT IS THE EXCEPTION, AND THE EXCEPTION MEANS
SOMETHING.** We are already here in law and not yet in the render: CLUSTERED
POWER puts ~12% lit, LIGHT = TERRITORY makes the lit part owned, nobody patrols
the dark. Machine Party is what that law looks like when the art actually commits
to it.

**5. STEPPED, HELD ANIMATION — NOT SMOOTH.** Stop-motion tactility is what you
get from a low frame count with real weight, and it is the native strength of
pixel animation. We also already have the grid for it: the 120 BPM LAW quantises
everything to the BEAT, and I-MOVE-YOU-MOVE means the world is stepping anyway.
Holding a pose on the beat instead of smoothing through it is the same move he is
being praised for.

**6. THE MENACE IS IN WHAT THE OBJECT IS FOR.** An emotionless machine with no
failsafe is frightening before it is drawn. Free horror, and it aims at the
apparatus rather than at blood.

## WHAT DOES NOT TRANSFER

- **The geometry.** Low-poly 3D silhouettes are not our silhouettes and chasing
  them would fight the 45 DEGREE ART LAW, which is the thing that makes our
  corpus cohere.
- **The camera.** His is in the room with you; ours is a fixed three-quarter view
  over a city. The side-scroller camera is already the named anti-reference
  (`records/BOHEMIA_RESEARCH_CRISIS_RESPONSE_VIOLENCE_7_31_26.md`) and this does
  not change that.
- **Computed lighting.** His own reason for going 3D was to let the computer do
  the lighting. We paint it. Every lit-versus-dark decision in Bohemia is
  authored by hand, so our version of his darkness has to be a *palette and value*
  discipline, not a light source.
- **Fog and depth blur.** Atmospheric depth cues that rely on a Z buffer. Our
  depth is stacking order and value, full stop.
- **The legibility sacrifice.** He let a gameplay-critical item go hard to read
  to protect the mood. Bohemia is a phone game read at arm's length in daylight —
  SUN MODE exists because of exactly this. **We take his palette discipline and we
  refuse his readability trade.** Noted as a deliberate divergence so nobody
  later "fixes" our contrast back down thinking it was an oversight.
