The sources below are cited from recall (marked), the way the coordinator marks its own; each is a published book or manual a
reader can check, and each gives the acts a reason. No reference game is added: Battle Brothers is read for how it does the
moment (rule 33), and nothing else.

## HOW BATTLE BROTHERS DOES IT (rule 33, recall, marked)
The dead stay where they fell on the battle map until the fight ends; then the loot screen is a flat panel (the stash beside
what was found) and the map is gone. Nobody is shown doing anything after the last blow. Ours keeps the board and puts his men
on it behind the panel.

## WHAT THE RECORD SAYS (cited, recall, marked)
1. CONSOLIDATE AND REORGANIZE (US Army FM 3-21.8, The Infantry Rifle Platoon and Squad, 2007, the chapter on the attack:
   consolidation and reorganization). The minutes after taking the ground are drilled: security all round first (men face
   out and look), the chain of command re-set, AMMUNITION REDISTRIBUTED and weapons reloaded, CASUALTIES TREATED, the dead and
   prisoners SEARCHED for weapons, documents and kit, and the ACE report (ammunition, casualties, equipment: counting what is
   left). -> LOOK ROUND, RELOAD AND COUNT, TEND, LOOT.
2. TACTICAL FIELD CARE (Committee on Tactical Combat Casualty Care, TCCC guidelines, the phases Care Under Fire and Tactical
   Field Care). Under fire nothing but a tourniquet; once the shooting stops, the wounds are found and dressed: pressure on the
   wound, a bandage wrapped and pulled tight, a man kneeling over another, talking to him. -> TEND (kneeling by a wounded
   friend), BIND (a bandage wrapped round a cut and pulled tight, the end in the teeth).
3. THE BACKLASH (Dave Grossman with Loren W. Christensen, On Combat, 2004: the parasympathetic backlash after the adrenaline
   dump). When it stops, the body pays: the hands shake, the knees go, men sit or bend over with their hands on their knees,
   breathe hard, wipe their faces, some laugh at nothing, some go quiet. -> CATCH HIS BREATH (bent, hands on his knees; up;
   looking at his own hands; the forearm across his face).
4. STRIPPING THE DEAD (E. B. Sledge, With the Old Breed at Peleliu and Okinawa, 1981: Marines going through the enemy dead for
   pistols, flags, watches and worse minutes after a fight; Ernst Juenger, Storm of Steel, 1920: men plundering a taken
   trench's dugouts for food, tobacco and kit; Waterloo, the field stripped of boots, clothes and teeth the night after, told in
   Bernard Cornwell, Waterloo, 2014). Looting is not a game's invention, it is the first thing that happens. -> LOOT: down on a
   knee by the body, the hands in its pockets and pouches, something pulled out and looked at, pocketed, a look round.
   BOHEMIA's twist (BATTERIES ARE THE MONEY, Paolo 9/4): what comes out of the dead man's pocket is a BATTERY.
5. THE SMOKE AND THE TALK (Sledge, the same book: the cigarette after, the quiet talk of who is hit and what comes next;
   S. L. A. Marshall, Men Against Fire, 1947, cited for the behaviour only, its numbers are disputed: men talk the fight over
   right after, and the talk is how a unit counts itself). -> SMOKE, TALK (two of them, turns), the town's own acts.

## THE ACTS (what this lane makes; the call RUN FOUR plays)
NEW, through the rig, the town's player, the same 57 px a metre:
- LOOT    down on his right knee by the body (it lies before him), his hands in its pockets, a battery pulled out, held up and
          looked at, pocketed at his hip, a glance over his shoulder, both hands back in.
- TEND    down on a knee by a wounded friend (who lies before him), one hand pressing on him, the other on his own knee, his
          head down to him, a look round for help, a hand on his chest, a nod.
- BIND    down on a knee, binding his own left forearm: the arm across him, the right hand wrapping over and under, the end
          pulled tight with his teeth, the hand opened and closed to try it. A dirty strip on the forearm.
- BREATH  standing, bent over with his hands on his knees, up, looking at his hands, the forearm across his face, a look
          round (the backlash, the shaking held as two keys a pixel apart).
THE TOWN'S, as they are: SMOKE, TALK (a pair). THE WALK between the bodies is his walk sheet (RUN TWO's slices/rig_walk), steered
by RUN FOUR between the dead. RELOAD and the gun check are the fight's own (ANIMATION's PReload / LReload in slices/rig_hold, the

## THE LOOK (the Crashbox HBO style he named, rule 115(d), and analog horror AH-01)
Held keys three a beat at 120, no tween, stiffness as the point; the quiet is in the holds (a man stays bent over his knees for
a whole slot); nothing flashes. The acts loop, each with its own breaths and a few variants, so a scene that sits behind the
loot screen for a minute never shows two men in step.
