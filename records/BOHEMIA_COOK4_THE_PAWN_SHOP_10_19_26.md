## The building
The Las Vegas pawn shop: after the casino, the Strip's and Fremont Street's oldest storefront: one storey of stucco,
the display glass behind bars, a steel door, the name on a fascia box. In this valley the dollar died and the
batteries are the money (9/4): the pawn shop is the bank now, where a man turns what he owns into what the valley spends.
- THE FRONT: 14 m of his cracked plaster (Wall tiles 1 #0, the ranches' own wall) to a 3.8 m plate; four of his barred
  windows (5. #20) as the display glass, sills low at 0.6 m, two each side; his steel door (4. #9) under a transom of
  his steel plate (#10) to 2.1 m.
- THE SIGN: a 7 by 0.9 m fascia box over the glass of the plain half of his framed steel panel (#13), its own rim top
  and foot, its face blank (the packs hold no lettering; a name is his to give), its shadow on the wall as light.

  grooves (the second pass's roof); one of his rooftop units (15. Street props #29, the motel's) on it.
- THE QUEUE: in front of the door, his post-and-rail (7. Fences and palisades #2, 1.7 by 1.3 m at 1:1) laid as a bank's
  switchback: three rows 2.2 m apart (a lane a person walks; 1.6 m stacked the rails into a pen), the gaps at alternate
  ends.
