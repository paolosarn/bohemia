## THE ANSWER IN ONE BREATH
The fighting stays. It is honest for five reasons that are each real somewhere today or in history: most fights are work you took,
you are the richest thing on any road, your name draws men who want it, every man you drop has people who come looking, and for a
boy in a dead valley a gun is the only job. Two more make the NUMBER honest, and they are the finding of this page: not every man
down is dead (in real gun wars two or more are wounded for every one killed), and the valley never empties because it is the only
water and power for hundreds of miles, so the road keeps bringing new men to fill the dead men's places.

## 1. THE MATH FIRST (the finding that proves us wrong, measured against canon)
- HIS COUNT, from rule 389's rough math: his main man drops about 3 a day; the company about 13 a day; 4,000 to 6,000 an act (an act
  is a year, rule 84). Two thirds people, one third beasts: 2,700 to 4,000 PEOPLE an act from one company.
- THE VALLEY'S LIVING: 65,000 people (band 45,000 to 80,000), laws/BOHEMIA_ADDENDUM_HOW_MANY_PEOPLE_7_29_26.md ruling 1.
- SO ONE COMPANY PUTS DOWN 4 TO 6 PERCENT OF EVERYONE ALIVE IN THE VALLEY EVERY ACT, and over three acts 12 to 18 percent, alone,
  if every man down is dead and every one of them lived here.
- AGAINST THE REAL FLOOR:
  * Haiti in 2024, one of the most violent places on earth: at least 5,601 people killed by gang violence in the whole country
    (UN human rights office, January 2025), most of them in and around a capital of about three million. That is under 0.2 percent
    of the capital a year, from every gang together. Our one company alone runs at 20 to 30 times that rate.
  * The Thirty Years' War, 1618 to 1648, the war Battle Brothers is built on: the German lands lost about 15 to 20 percent of their
    people over thirty years (Britannica's summary of most historians), more than half in the worst regions such as Wurttemberg, and
    most of it to hunger, plague and flight, not battle. Under one percent a year, all causes.
- THE VERDICT: as he plays it, the count is not realistic IF every man down is dead and every one was born in the valley. Both of
  those "ifs" are false in the real world, and fixing them costs no change to how often he fights:
  (a) THE WOUNDED. In America's own big wars about two to two and a half men were wounded for every man killed in battle (the
      Civil War's Union army about 2 to 1, the Second World War about 2.3 to 1; Department of Veterans Affairs, America's Wars).
      With that ratio, 100 men down is about 30 dead, 70 hurt or run. The company's 2,700 to 4,000 people down an act become about
      800 to 1,200 dead, 1.2 to 1.8 percent of the valley: still a war zone (six to nine times Port-au-Prince), which act one, the
      anarchy decade, is meant to be.
  (b) THE ROAD. Canon already says why the dead are replaced: the valley is a functioning oasis and people cross the desert for its
      power and water (laws/BOHEMIA_STORY_MASTER_7_18_26.md, THE WORLD), and the population is the FOOD number, the carrying
      capacity, not a headcount of the people who happened to survive here (HOW MANY PEOPLE, ruling 1(a)). A valley held at its food
      number refills: every man who dies frees his share for the next one off the road. Most of the men who fight him were not born
      in Las Vegas; they came last month from a dead Kingman or a dead St. George. (DRAFT, this page's hole pulled: canon gives both
      halves and nobody had joined them.)
- No number is changed by this page. TUNING owns whether the count's split (dead, hurt, ran) needs a number in the one table.

## 2. THE SEVEN REASONS, EACH ON ITS REAL FLOOR
The coordinator's six (draft, 11/4) checked against the record, and a seventh from the math.

1. MOST FIGHTS ARE WORK. He takes jobs and hunts; he is paid to go. REAL: the armies of the Thirty Years' War were raised on credit by
   military contractors (Wallenstein, Mansfeld) and fed by the "contribution system", levies forced on the towns they stood in instead
   of taxes; a foot soldier's pay of about 4 thalers a month was barely a living, so men fought because fighting was the trade and the
   side money came from it. In Haiti the gangs are paid the same way, by work: kidnapping for ransom (close to 1,500 kidnapped in
   2024, UN) and extortion collected street by street. Battle Brothers: the contract board is where most of its fights come from.
   IN THE GAME: the board's job says who pays and why in one plain line, and the count can say how many of his fights were jobs.
2. HE IS WORTH ROBBING. Batteries are the money (9/4); a company carrying batteries, guns and medicine is the fattest caravan on the
   15. REAL: the Cartel's canon is chokepoints and a prey-tax on the caravans (records/factions/BOHEMIA_FACTION_CARTEL.md); the army
   that sacked Magdeburg in 1631 came in hungry, unpaid and after plunder; Haiti's gangs hold the roads in and out of the capital.
   IN THE GAME: road events where a crew wants the cart, not him; a toll he can pay instead of fighting.
3. HE IS KNOWN. Clout draws men who want to test the name and factions who want him gone (Battle Brothers' renown raises the
   contracts it offers and the enemies it sends). REAL: in a city run by gangs a name is territory; a crew that beats a known crew
   takes its streets and its taxes. IN THE GAME: a feed post when a crew from outside is coming for his company's name.
4. THE DEAD HAVE FRIENDS. REAL, AND THE OLDEST PICTURE OF IT: Jacques Callot's print "The Peasants Avenge Themselves" (plate 17 of
   The Great Miseries of War, 1633, made during the Thirty Years' War): farmers lie in wait for the soldiers who wrecked their homes,
   kill them and strip them to their shirts. And now: Haiti's Bwa Kale movement, from April 2023, when neighbourhood residents killed
   about 160 suspected gang members in its first month (the rights group CARDH) and the UN counted some 310 by mid-August, and the
   neighbourhoods braced for the gangs' revenge. Revenge runs both ways and never ends on its own. IN THE GAME: a crew he broke comes
   back with a name; the feed carries the sister's post.
5. THERE IS NOTHING ELSE. The vats died in a week (rule 359), the stores are empty, the farms do not exist in the Mojave without
   power. REAL: in Haiti children are 30 to 50 percent of gang members (UN estimates), and gang recruitment of children rose about 70
   percent in a year (UNICEF, late 2024); they collect the extortion money and watch the police. The Cartel's own dossier: "Nobody
   joins for the violence. They join because somebody finally had a job for them." IN THE GAME: many of the men who fight him are
   boys, and the game says so plainly, never as a lecture.
6. NOT EVERY MAN DOWN IS DEAD. His side already has it: struck down is 20 percent dead, else a long injury (the 9/27 law). Theirs
   should read the same way: a losing side breaks and runs (rule 366), and the wounded are carried off. REAL: the ratio in (a) above,
   and in the armies of that age a captured soldier often changed sides, which commanders on both sides expected of the lower ranks.
   IN THE GAME: the count says DEAD, HURT, RAN (section 3).
7. THE ROAD KEEPS BRINGING THEM (new, from the math, draft:true). The valley is the only water and power for hundreds of miles; the
   road brings the desperate, armed and thirsty; a stranger is not a bandit until the water is refused. This is why the fighting does
   not empty the valley, and why so many of the faces are new.

## 3. HOW HE FEELS IT (never a card, never a lecture: the board, the feed, the dead's friends, the birds, the count)
- THE BOARD: every job says, in one line, who pays and why it is worth a fight. Draft lines (draft:true):
    "The Caravans want the narrows open by Thursday. Six men at the overpass. They have been there since the rain."
    "Church work. Somebody has been taking the blankets off the gym floor at night. Bring the blankets back."
- THE FEED (rule 372: a real person typed it; rule 353's guide: plain facts, no exclamation marks), draft:true:
    "heard the {company} took the wash bridge with six guys. we got seven. see you saturday"
    "if you're the ones with the cart full of batteries, my cousin says hi. he's the one at the narrows"
    "the {company} came through. 14 went down at the swap meet. the clinic has 5 of them. the church has the rest"
- THE DEAD'S FRIENDS: a crew he broke is marked and may come back with the name of the man who did it (the kill tally already keeps
  each man's BEST foe: the same record can keep whose friend he killed). Rare, never in the first hour, one at a time.
- THE BIRDS: rule 386's vultures follow a company that leaves bodies. Real hunters and scavengers watch circling vultures to find a
  kill; so do people. THE BIRDS OVER HIS MARKER ARE WHY OTHERS FIND HIM: the world sees where the killing is and comes to it.
- THE COUNT (rule 316 keeps 'n KILLS · n FIGHTS, BEST: ...' per man today; Battle Brothers counts kills only). Proposal, draft:true,
  words for WORDS and the fight's knowledge for COMBAT:
    per man      "41 DOWN · 12 DEAD · 22 HURT · 7 RAN"        and his BEST line as it is
    the company  "THE COMPANY: 412 DOWN. 131 DEAD. 247 HURT. 34 RAN."
    why          "WHY THEY FOUGHT: 61 JOBS · 22 ROBBERS · 9 FOR A NAME · 6 FOR A FRIEND"
  The split needs the fight to know which of theirs died, which were left breathing and which ran off the edge; that is COMBAT's and
  TUNING's to decide (whether theirs use the same 20 percent rule as his men is a number for the one table, not for this page).

## 4. QUEST SEEDS (draft:true; a quest is people, places and things, never a card, rule 19; each line from a mouth with a face)
a. THE BOX FLAP (the dead have friends). A woman selling shoes at the swap meet keeps a list on the flap of a shoe box: the men the
   company put down at the wash bridge. Two are crossed out in a different pen. She is not looking for him. Her brother is.
   Choices: find the brother first and pay for the dead (batteries), meet him when he comes, or let it come. Line, draft:true:
   "My brother has your name on a box. I told him the box is for shoes."
b. THE TABLE AT THE NARROWS (he is worth robbing). A folding table across the 15 where it narrows, a kid writing in a notebook, men on
   the overpass. Three batteries a head, or go through them. Line, draft:true:
   "Three a head. The kid writes it down. Nobody has ever asked for a receipt."
c. THE BOY WHO STAYED (there is nothing else). After a fight one of theirs is alive behind a car, sixteen at most. His crew kills
   runners. Choices: sign him on, walk him to the Church's gym, or leave him. Line, draft:true:
   "I can carry. I carried for them. I don't eat much."
d. SEVEN FROM PAHRUMP (he is known). A crew from over the mountains, outside the valley, comes because of the feed: they want the name, not the cart.
   They send the date first. Line, draft:true: "We don't want your stuff. We want people to say it was us."
e. THE ONES WHO CAME FOR WATER (the road keeps bringing them). Strangers off the road from a dead town to the south, armed, thirsty,
   polite. Choices: walk them to the Church's line, sell them water, or hold the pump. Line, draft:true:
   "We left Kingman with eleven. We're nine. We'd like to buy water, and we'd like to not have to ask twice."
f. THE CHURCH'S BOOK (not every man down is dead). A deacon comes to the camp with the funeral book to write the names from the swap
   meet. His count says 14; her book says 9. Line, draft:true:
   "The book says nine. The clinic says five more are sleeping. Your number is wrong in a good way."

## 4b. WHAT THE GRANDKIDS HEAR (rule 412, Paolo 11/6, 'they'll tell a story about your granddad'; added the same round)
- THE NUMBERS BECOME THE STORY (412(b)). What the game already keeps on a man (the kill tally's kills and his BEST foe, the deeds and
  who saw them, engine/bohemia_deeds.js) is what a settlement tells his grandson in act two and act three, from a mouth with a face at
  the bar, never a pop-up. The DOWN / DEAD / HURT / RAN split of section 3 is what makes the telling honest: a town can say he spared
  men as well as that he killed them.
- WHO TELLS IT DECIDES HOW (412(c)): the town that hired him tells the jobs; the town he robbed or the crew whose friend he dropped
  tells the dead. The same hundred men, two stories, by the relation each place kept.
- WHAT NOBODY SAW STAYS BURIED (the witness rule, engine/bohemia_memory.js): a fight with no survivor and no watcher fades; only the
  vultures saw it. Draft lines, draft:true, act two:
    "Your granddad? He cleared the narrows for the Caravans. Six men on the overpass. My father was the kid with the notebook."
    "Ask at the swap meet, not here. Here we just remember the box."

- He fights a lot and keeps fighting a lot; no rate is lowered (his words: 'I want that').
- No new faction (rule 374): every crew that comes for him is a faction we have or a crew off the road.

## 6. ROUTED (proposals only; the coordinator adds jobs)
- WORDS: the board's one 'who pays and why' line; the feed's reasons; the count's words (section 3).
- COMBAT and TUNING: theirs struck down read as dead, hurt or ran; a man who runs off the edge counts as ran; whether the count needs
  a number in the one table (the wounded ratio, theirs at 20 percent dead or not).
- FEED: the vendetta post and the 'coming for the name' post as triggers, rare, never in the first hour.

- CONTENT: the toll at the narrows and the strangers for water as road events.
- FACTIONS: which of our factions send which crews (the Cartel's tolls, the Remnants' permanent war, the Mob's guarantee).

## 7. [bb] AND THE ANALOG HORROR LINE
- [bb] Battle Brothers explains its fighting the same way without saying it: brigands raid caravans and companies on the road,
  renown raises the contracts and the men sent against you, and its world is a war with no front. It counts kills only; a broken
  enemy who flees off the map is simply gone. Ours keeps all of that and adds the two things its count never says: who they were
  and why they came.
- ANALOG HORROR, at the source: the list on the shoe box flap, two names crossed out in a different pen; the count that does not add
  up and the deacon who corrects it. Never gore; the silence after.
