## 0. THE QUESTION, AND THE ANSWER IN FIVE LINES
If the gun is how you win (rule 120(a)), the bullet is a cost from the first fight. What does a fight's ammo cost in the
first week, and what keeps a careful shooter from going broke in the first hour?
1. A FIGHT'S ROUNDS ARE SMALL MONEY. Measured on the live fight with guns in the crew's hands (section 4): one gun spends
   4 to 5 rounds a first-week job, two guns 8 to 9, three 7 to 12. At Day 69's five a battery that is 0.8 to 2.4
   batteries: 1 to 8 in 100 of the job's pay, a tenth to a quarter of a nine-battery day.
2. SO THE PRICE CANNOT BREAK HIM. In every cell of the first hour (four kits, two schedules, mean and bad-luck shot
   counts, even zero rounds at the door and every fight lost) the purse never falls under 179 of the door's 240. TUNING's
   10/10 finding for Battle Brothers ('price never binds') holds for ours, measured.
3. WHAT BREAKS HIM IS THE EMPTY PILE. Today every origin starts with ZERO rounds: the pile is dry at the first fight, so
   every shot is bought at a counter he may not be near. THE PICK: 60 ROUNDS A GUN IN THE STASH AT THE DOOR (the hard end
   40, Battle Brothers' own Medium for its one shooter; the easy end 80, its High), refilled after every fight from the
   pile, one round a shot on every gun, five a battery loose or fifty for ten in a box, and six from every body searched.
   With 60 a gun no crew is dry inside the first hour on the mean; a three-gun crew never; the first week's rounds cost
   nothing in cash.
4. AND THE BIGGER FINDING, FOR COMBAT: ON TODAY'S FIGHT EVERY GUN YOU ADD LOSES. Three men on a one-skull job: two pipes
   and a sledge win 93 in 100; pipe, pipe, rifle 87; one pipe and two rifles 69; three rifles 44. A lone wolf with a rifle
   wins 0 of 300. A gun cannot fire at the man beside it (rule 83) and the crew walks into reach. Rule 120(a) is the
   opposite of what the fight does. TUNING's [the first kit is guns], shipped the same hour on its own bench, found the
   same (three against three: pipes 87, today's gun rows 56) and its draft guns (two shots a turn, the butt at one tile)
   win 86; a draft gunman fires about 4 shots a routine fight, the same as today's rows here, so the pick holds.
5. THE CAREFUL HAND IS PUNISHED TOO: for three rifles, holding every shot under 50 in 100 saves 37 rounds in the first
   hour (7 batteries) and loses 25 batteries of pay, because the fights it lets run are lost. Until the fight pays the good shot (rule
   117(c), rule 120(b)), 'careful' costs men, not money.

## 1. HOW BATTLE BROTHERS PRICES A SHOT
Sources (reference/library/grok/wiki/PAGES_ALL.tar.gz): bb_all/0055_Ammunition.txt, 1616_Quiver of Arrows.txt,
1617_Quiver of Bolts.txt, 1140_Large Powder Bag.txt, 0935_Handgonne.txt, 0766_Fire Handgonne (5 fatigue).txt,
1678_Reload Handgonne (20 fatigue).txt, 0460_Crossbow and Firearms Mastery.txt, 1166_Light Crossbow.txt,
1782_Shoot Bolt (5 fatigue).txt, 0002_A New Company.txt, 0827_Game Guide.txt (Economy), 1244_Market prices for various
items.txt; records/target/bb/stash_rates.json ammo (GROK_134, PASSED). The web check (battlebrothers.fandom.com, search
extracts only; the page itself would not open from here) agrees on every number below.
- ONE PILE, POINTS NOT BULLETS: a bundle is 50 points, worth 100 crowns (2 crowns a point; ten to one, 0.2 battery).
- WHAT A SHOT TAKES OUT OF THE PILE: an arrow or a bolt 1 point, a handgonne shot 2, a throwing weapon or a fire lance
  charge 3.
- THE QUIVER IS THE MAGAZINE AND THE PILE IS THE WALLET: quivers of 10 (large 14), powder bags of 5 (large 7); every
  quiver, bag and bundle REFILLS AFTER THE BATTLE from the pile, never in the fight; an empty pile leaves them empty.
- THE GUN COSTS DOUBLE A SHOT AND FIRES HALF AS OFTEN: Fire Handgonne 3 AP, Reload 9 AP, so it fires every other turn
  until Crossbow and Firearms Mastery (reload 6 AP, every turn). Per turn the handgonne and the crossbow both spend about
  one point; a bow on Quick Shot spends two.
- THE GUIDE'S OWN WARNING: 'Ammunition expenses can rise up fast if several recruits are using Bows with Quick Shot. It
  is advised to mostly stick to Crossbows in the early game... use only 1 Ammunition per attack.'
- THE START: A New Company carries 40 points at Medium (20 Low, 80 High) beside 2,400 crowns; ONE of its three
  Companions is the ranged one, with a light crossbow ('almost a full turn to reload'). Its first fights are two men in
  front and one shooter behind.
- THE COUNTER: supplies sell at the town's buying modifier (1.04 to 1.22 in the guide's examples, sometimes 1.5), so a
  bundle is 104 to 150 crowns; an Arrow-Maker's Shed makes it 'unusually low'. The wiki calls 80 to 100 a good deal.
- THE TWO RETURNS: ammunition 'can be looted after battle', and the Scavenger follower 'will recover some Ammunition
  used by the player during battle'. Enemy bows have no count at all (an old developer reply, UNVERIFIED).
- WHAT A FIGHT SPENDS (players' own words, unverified): a late-game archer 'hardly ever' uses more than one and a half
  quivers (about 15 points, 30 crowns; a Steam guide); one player says 20 to 50 points a fight for a company.
THE LESSON: Battle Brothers makes the shot cheap and the PILE the thing you watch. A fight of its one early shooter is
about 9 crowns, a sixth of its 54-crown day. You run out before you go broke.

## 2. THE REAL RECORD: A ROUND IS PRICED AGAINST A DAY'S WORK, AND THE CAREFUL SHOOTER IS THE ONE WHO HITS
All from search-engine extracts of the cited pages; none of the pages opened in full from here (DNS and the proxy refused
smallarmssurvey.org, nyc.gov, ammo.com). Quoted second-hand; the arithmetic marked 'mine' is this page's.
- THE CRASH MULTIPLIES THE ROUND, NOT THE GUN. US 9mm: about 15 to 17 cents a round in late 2019, 64 to 81 cents at the
  2021 peak (Ammo.com's index; Outdoor Life 2025 on Ammunition Depot data; Athlon Outdoors 2021: 60 to 80 cents against
  17). Four to five times in a year of panic, with no money dying at all.
- WHERE THE MONEY DID DIE, THE LAW WAS THE BOTTLENECK. Caracas 2019: about a dollar a bullet, a magazine about 15,
  against an average income of 6.50 a month (AP, 28 May 2019). Venezuela had stopped civilian sales in 2012 (AP via CTV)
  and its 2013 Disarmament Law caps a permit holder at 50 rounds a year a gun (Article 64; ALC Abogados).
- A WAR MARKET'S PRICE: Small Arms Survey 2013 'Price Watch' (Lebanon, Pakistan, Somalia, 2011 to 2012): an AK round 20
  cents to 1.20 dollars; Lebanon's ammunition price index tracked Syrian deaths at a correlation of 0.93 (Florquin, European
  Journal on Criminal Policy and Research, 2014); a 7.62 round in Erbil fell from 3 dollars to 1 after the 2014 airstrikes
  and rose whenever the line fell back (VICE News); Taiz about 50 cents (AFP 2019); Mogadishu 50 cents to a dollar (Al
  Jazeera 2013); Balkh late 2023, 7.62x39 at 12 to 15 afghani, 17 to 21 cents (Small Arms Survey 2024); Darra Adam Khel
  about 10 cents (NBC 2017).
- A DAY'S WORK IN ROUNDS (mine): Balkh, a casual labourer's 317 afghani (WFP, June 2024) buys 21 to 26 AK rounds;
  Caracas 2019, a day's pay buys a fifth of one; a US minimum-wage day bought about 387 rounds of 9mm in 2019 and about
  90 at the 2021 peak. Battle Brothers: a Companion's 14-crown day buys 7 bolts. OURS: a man's one-battery day buys 5
  rounds at five a battery: a little dearer than Battle Brothers, four to five times dearer than a working war market,
  twenty-five times cheaper than the place where the state stopped selling them. Realism-first reads this as RIGHT for a
  valley where the dollar died: a war-market price, not a banned one.
- CARTRIDGES WERE MONEY, ONCE, FOR REAL. Ethiopia: 'cartridges are also currency' (Statesman's Year-Book 1913; Hansard,
  18 May 1936); in 1928 a bullet was a fifth of a Maria Theresa thaler and the French 'S.F.M.' stamp was prized
  (American Numismatic Society); around 1900 28 to 32 empty cases made a thaler, about 6 after the 1919 arms embargo
  (MoneyMuseum). The Old West 'a cartridge for a shot of whiskey' has no source before about 2003 (Snopes); Sarajevo's
  siege money was cigarettes, not bullets (FAMA). Batteries are our money (9/4); a round is a good with a price, not a
  second coin. The record says that is the usual case.
- WHAT A FIGHT SPENDS. NYPD: 297 shots in 47 gunfights in 2009 (about 6 an incident, mine) and 368 in 33 in 2010 (about
  11); in 2011 two thirds of officers fired 5 or fewer; the hit rate 18 in 100 in gunfights, 30 when nobody shot back
  (RAND 2008, via PolitiFact). Hunters: 1.2 shots a deer in South Carolina, 1.6 in Ontario (MeatEater; year unverified);
  5.07 shots a duck bagged (SEAFWA 1992), about 5.4 in Missouri, 1979 (USGS). US snipers in Vietnam 1.3 to 1.7 rounds a
  kill (Lanning, via a secondary source); the '50,000 a kill' figures are disputed and lump in training (The Independent
  2005, John Pike of GlobalSecurity, not GAO). A US rifleman's basic load is 210 rounds, seven magazines of 30 (a
  secondary source); at Wanat, 2008, one soldier fired about 360 in half an hour (AP 2009).
THE LESSON: a careful shot is one to two rounds a kill (the hunter, the sniper); a scared crew is six to eleven an
incident and hits one in five. The difference is not the price of the round, it is who is holding the gun. A game that
wants the careful shooter to win has to make the careful shot the one that lands.

## 7. WHAT PROVES US WRONG
1. **RULE 120(a) IS THE OPPOSITE OF THE FIGHT.** Every gun added to a fresh crew lowers the win (93, 87, 69, 44 at one
   skull; 64, 57, 31, 12 at two) and raises the dead (6.7 to 31.3 a hundred fights); a lone wolf with a rifle wins none
   of 300. The rifle cannot fire at the man beside it (rule 83's two-tile floor) and AUTO's rifles hit 35 to 40 in 100.
   The kit that loses least is Battle Brothers' own: two in front, one shooter behind.
2. **THE CAREFUL HAND LOSES.** Rule 117(c) wants every good choice to win visibly; on today's fight the fewer, better
   shot (55 to 62 in 100 against 35 to 40) loses more fights in every kit. The good shot has to be the important shot
