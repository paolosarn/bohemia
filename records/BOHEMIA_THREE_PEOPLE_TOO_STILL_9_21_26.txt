WHAT THIS LANE IS ALLOWED TO COOK WITH, AND IT SHAPED THE ANSWER. COOK owns garment
ART; this lane owns THE BODY, which is the dials and the pairing. So the wrongness
could not be a new sinister prop. It had to live in PROPORTION and in WHAT SOMEBODY
CHOSE TO PUT ON. That is the better constraint: an ordinary wardrobe worn wrong is
more analog horror than a new object would be, and rule 20 bans the filter anyway.
NOTHING NEW WAS DRAWN. Every garment is already canon on the rail; every dial is the
rig's own. If the tone cannot be reached with the wardrobe we have, it is a filter.

AN ORDINARY CITIZEN STANDS BESIDE THEM IN THE PICTURE. One thing being wrong is only
legible next to the thing being right, and "too tall" means nothing alone.

SEVEN PER CENT END TO END. Real adult height runs about a third between the short
and the tall. At the very top of the slider a man is three per cent above average
and nobody can see that. THE RIG HAS NO USABLE HEIGHT RANGE. Shipping it would have
put a label claiming something the pixels do not show into the one place he judges
things, which is this lane's own [names lie] defect one screen further out.
