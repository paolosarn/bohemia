## ★ HE WAS RIGHT AND I OVERSTATED THE SHOE FINDING. CORRECTING IT.

> "UR TELLING ME SHOES HAVE A TEN YEAR LIFE CYCLE BEFORE THEY ARE UNWEARABLE. THATS BULLSHIT
> LMAO."
> — Paolo, 8/7/26

**He is substantially right and I oversold it.** What the sources actually support, and what I
stretched:

**TRUE:** polyurethane midsoles hydrolyse on a five-to-ten-year clock **from manufacture**, they
degrade in storage, warmth accelerates it, and the failure is invisible until the sole crumbles.
That is real and it is sourced.

**WHAT I DID WRONG:** I generalised "PU midsoles" into **"every shoe in the valley."** That does
not follow.
- **Most sneakers use EVA foam**, not PU. EVA compresses, hardens and loses cushioning over
  years — it does **not** crumble the way PU does. Ugly and flat, still wearable.
- **Leather boots with stitched or welted soles last decades** and are the one kind you can
  actually re-sole.
- **Rubber outsoles dry-rot and crack**, but slowly.

So in a valley that had millions of pairs of shoes, **you would absolutely find wearable
footwear.** His instinct beat my citation.

**THEREFORE THE BOOT IS KILLED.** And it dies by **my own rule from last pass**: *if you cannot
name the wall without inventing it, there is no boss.* I invented that wall, then defended it
with a half-true finding, and it took him one line to knock it over. **That is the second time in
two passes** — THE FIXER died the same way — which makes it the pattern to watch in this lane, not
a one-off.

**WHAT SURVIVES, because it is still true and still interesting:** *specific* things rot on
timers you cannot see, and PU soles are a real example. That belongs as **world texture** — a
crate of unworn boots that turns to chalk in your hands is a great five-second beat — **not as a
boss.** Nobody builds a progression gate on it.

## THE FINDINGS THAT STILL GOVERN, CARRIED SO THIS FILE STANDS ALONE

**FOOD IS FIRST.** The rebuilding literature is blunt: **it does not start with technology, it
starts with FOOD** — survivors spend years just stabilising food production. That is why THE SOIL
(19) opens act 2, which is also his ruling (*"this should be the beggining of act 2 pls"*).

**GUNS ARE EASY, BULLETS ARE NOT.** A slam-fire pipe shotgun is documented as two pieces of pipe
and an end cap with a nail, buildable **trivially** with very limited skill — so pipe weapons are
ACT 1, exactly as he guessed. **What is genuinely hard is AMMUNITION**, because primers need real
chemistry, which is why that bottleneck sits in act 2 under THE CHEMIST and not under a
metalworker.

**THE FIVE WHO DIED OF "IM CONFUSED", AND THE REASON.** THE WATCH (*"DELETE THIS IS ASS"*), THE
ARCHITECT, THE SIGN, THE STILL and THE FIXER. **Four of the five died because the LOCK was
invented to justify a boss I wanted** — THE FIXER's was a flat lie, since nothing in the game
replaces parts. **If you cannot name the wall without inventing it, there is no boss.** THE BOOT
makes it five, this pass.

**THE SCHOOL CARRIES THE BIGGEST STAKE ON THE LADDER**, because he said he could not see its
gameplay: three generations is canon, and without it **your heir starts at zero** every single
time. Beat it and a run's progress survives the death of the person who earned it.

## CARRIED FORWARD, BECAUSE THE LIVE DOC HAS TO STAND ALONE

**THE RULE:** one boss = one lock = one thing that was impossible and now is not, and **no two
bosses may open the same lock.** Every LOCK is written as an **impossibility**, never a noun
somebody owns. `gates/boss_ladder_gate.js` holds it across all 63.

**VALHEIM'S FORSAKEN POWERS ARE PASSIVE BUFFS, NOT KEYS** (−60% stamina, −25% damage taken, a
sailing tailwind). What actually gates Valheim is the biome's ore — the "material vibes" framing
he killed. **So this ladder is NOT Valheim's mechanism, IT IS METROID'S** — lock-and-key ability
gating, where each ability answers a different class of obstacle.

**ACT 3 IS SLIGHTLY FUTURISTIC AND NOT JUST RECOVERY, AND THE REASON IS THAT THE REBUILD WAS THE
TAKEOVER.** Nobody conquered Night City: it was rebuilt by the corporation that supplied the
rebuild, and **supplying the rebuild is how they came to own it.** So act 3's upgrades arrive
with an owner attached — a bill, not a reward — and the player builds the trap themselves. The
counter is his own: **THE PRESS, because print is not scrapable data.**

**HABITABLE IS DEFINED:** every district carries a **FILTH level**, and while it is high its
**population cap is ZERO**. A settler will accept a bed there is what habitable means. THE DRAIN
(23) is the only thing that lowers it.

**TRANSPORT IS NOW A SPINE:** bicycle (9) → cart (36) → cleared roads (37) → car (45) → rail
(46) → lifts (52) → flight (56). The research is why it earns that — **you cannot have
metallurgy without transport networks.**

**FOOD IS FIRST:** rebuilding does not start with technology, **it starts with FOOD**, which is
why THE SOIL opens act 2.
