0. THE ONE SENTENCERogue Fable IV is not a damage game. It is a POSITION game with a damage readout, andalmost every system in it exists to make geometry more powerful than statistics.Once you see that, all 83 tips collapse into about nine machines.

1. ACTION ECONOMY: THE FREE-MOVEMENT BUDGETThis is the foundation everything else stands on.The base rule. One action per turn. Attacking ends your turn. Moving ends your turn.Waiting is a legal action and is frequently the correct one.The exception that makes the game. Speed Points. Sprinting moves you WITHOUT endingyour turn. That means SP is not movement, it is a currency that buys free actions outsidethe turn economy entirely.The regen rule is the sharp part. SP regenerates on every 5th global game turn, on a fixedworld clock. It is NOT a per-use cooldown that starts when you spend. 2. THE KITE LOOP: THE ACTUAL CORE VERBStrip the tips down and the intended fight shape is a loop, not an exchange:!. Engage, dump your abilities". Abilities go on cooldown, you are now weak#. Disengage using SP, terrain, and line of sight$. Kite until cooldowns recharge%. Re-engageThe game says outright that you will often need to kite around a level to let abilitiesrecharge, and that a single speed point is enough to kite almost indefinitely.Why this is smart. In most turn RPGs, running out of resources means you lose. Here,running out of resources means the fight enters a different PHASE. Retreat is not failure,retreat is a move. That converts "I am out of cooldowns" from a death sentence into apuzzle.The cost of it. It only works if the level is big enough and connected enough to run laps in.Kiting is a level-design dependency, not just a combat rule. A cramped room deletes theentire core verb. The game clearly knows this, which is why so many tips are aboutcorridors, corners, and pillars.Design principle extracted: if your combat loop requires retreat, your level generator has ahard obligation to guarantee retreat is possible. Combat design and map generation are thesame system wearing two hats.

3. MOVEMENT ASYMMETRY: HOW DISTANCE ISMANUFACTUREDKiting cannot work if the enemy moves as well as you do. So the game builds inasymmetries you can exploit.Diagonal versus orthogonal. Slow enemies can only move orthogonally. You can movediagonally. Every diagonal step you take costs them more than it costs you. You areliterally generating distance out of pure geometry, with no resource spent.Slow speed exploitation. You can use an enemy's slow diagonal movement to open athree tile gap specifically to bait a charge ability.Liquids. Water blocks sprinting, fast movement, and movement abilities. This is terrainthat switches OFF the free-movement budget. You use SP on land, then put waterbetween you and the pursuit. Terrain that disables the most powerful system in thegame is enormously strong, and it cuts both ways.Flight and levitation. A traversal state on its own countdown, tracked separately fromthe potion duration that grants the stat. Two different numbers that players are explicitlywarned not to confuse. Slightly clumsy, but it means flight is a windowed resource, not amode.Design principle extracted: movement asymmetry is a cleaner difficulty lever than statinflation. Making an enemy orthogonal-only is more interesting than giving it more HP, and itteaches the player something durable.

4. VISION IS THE MASTER SWITCHThis is the highest leverage system in the entire game and it is not presented as one system.It is scattered across a dozen tips. Pull them together and the architecture is obvious.Line of sight gates, at minimum:Ranged enemies cannot shoot without vision of the playerShamans need vision of BOTH the player and the ally to place a totemSummoners need vision to call alliesHealers only heal enemies they can seeAggroed enemies only shout to alert others if the player is in visionEnemies never spot a sprinting player at allSo a single wall simultaneously disables ranged damage, enemy buffing, enemyreinforcement, enemy healing, and aggro propagation. One variable, five systems.And then the game lets you MANUFACTURE walls. This is the part worth stealingconceptually:Fire plus water makes steam clouds that block visionA sleep bomb blocks line of sight, and a sleeping body plugs a corridorCloud walls get summoned behind the player, so your own positioning determines wherethe wall landsPortable, craftable, positional line of sight turns a static level property into an active playerability. The map stops being a fixed board and becomes editable mid-fight.Design principle extracted: pick ONE variable that as many enemy systems as possibledepend on. Then give the player tools to control that variable. You get combinatorial depthwithout writing combinatorial content.Bohemia relevance: Bohemia's cover system and the pop-out-of-cover behavior in theDead Eye Dial already put line of sight at the center of the combat read. The open noteabout cover popping being spammable in 1v1 is a vision-gating question wearing a differenthat. 
[PENDING, Paolo's call] whether enemy support behaviors in Bohemia get gated onvision the way RF4 gates them.

5. AWARENESS AND AGGRO: A THREE LAYER MACHINENot one stealth stat. Three separate layers, each with its own rule.Layer 1, detection radius. Base 6 tiles, reduced by 1 per point of stealth. Displayed on themap as rings. Legible, countable, plannable.Layer 2, awareness state. An unaware enemy showing one question mark will NEVER spotyou. Showing three question marks, it has a chance to. Two distinct states with two distinctrules, communicated with a single glyph count.Layer 3, alert propagation. When an enemy aggros it has a 50% chance to shoutimmediately. It only shouts if the player is in vision. Other enemies only respond to a shoutwithin 10 tiles.Stack those three and you get real infiltration play out of very little code:Aggro one enemy at maximum range and the shout cannot reach the pack, so you peelone target off a groupIf the coin flip comes up no-shout, you can retreat out of vision with SP and the alertnever propagates at allSprinting is a hard immunity to being spotted, so the free-movement budget is ALSO thestealth budget, which means one resource is doing double dutyEven 1 point of stealth stops sleeping enemies from waking, so the player chooses whenthe fight starts by removing the stealth item on purposeThat last one is the sharpest. Stealth is normally a defensive stat. Here it is a trigger switch.You take your armor off to start a fight on your terms.Design principle extracted: a binary spotted/unspotted stealth system has no decisions init. Split detection, awareness, and propagation into three separate rules and the samefiction produces a genuine tactical language.

6. TERRAIN AS THE PRIMARY WEAPONThe environment is not decoration and it is not a modifier. It is the strongest damage sourcein the game, and it deliberately does not scale off your stats.Unstable terrain. Characters on it take 50% more physical damage. The tip explicitlyframes it as a two-part play, avoid standing on it yourself while leading enemies onto it.Same tile, opposite meaning depending on who is on it.Pits. Knocking an enemy into a pit instantly kills it, framed openly as the answer to highHP tanks. An enemy charging into a pit dies instantly too. This is a total bypass of thedamage system.Walls. Lunging enemies take damage from hitting a wall, and you can bait the lunge bystanding in line when the cooldown resets.Traps. Kite enemies through them.Cursed floor. Heals undead enemies. Terrain that is advantageous for THEM, whichmeans terrain reading is mandatory, not optional.Corpse denial. Destroying skeleton bodies with terrain prevents resurrection, andsimply standing on a skeleton prevents it too. The floor itself becomes contested spaceafter the kill.Why the pit rule matters so much. It means power level and solvability are decoupled. Aweak character with good positioning can kill a tank. That is the single most importantproperty in a roguelite, because it means a bad item run is not automatically a dead run.Skill has somewhere to go when the numbers do not show up.Design principle extracted: guarantee at least one kill channel that ignores the player'sdamage stat entirely. It is the difference between a roguelite that respects skill and one thatjust checks your loot.Bohemia relevance: Bohemia's KILL zone already deals exactly 100 damage through thearmor stat rather than flagging an instakill, which is a deliberately different philosophy froma pit. Worth noting the contrast rather than resolving it. 
[PENDING, Paolo's call] whetherBohemia ever wants a true stat-independent environmental kill, or whether the dial is meantto be the only kill channel in the game.

7. PUBLISHED DETERMINISM: THE AI IS AN OPEN BOOKThis is the most unusual choice in the whole corpus and the one most worth thinking hardabout.RF4 tells the player its own AI rules and its own exploits, plainly:Enemies attack in a fixed order, left to right, top to bottom, closest first, producing thehit order W, N, S, E, NW, SW, NE, SEMost ranged enemies have a 25% chance to retreat if you are within three tilesChanneled abilities leave the enemy vulnerable for a turnEnemy pathing routes around summoned bombs toward clear paths, which can be usedto steer them and can also backfire and trap youTurrets fire in one direction at a time, so zigzagging forces them to turn and SP gets youa free hitA summoned firestorm expands and damages its own caster if you stay close behindterrainSpawned enemies award no XP, and a spawner gives the same XP whether it dies fromdamage or from exhausting its spawnsAnd then it teaches the attack order ENVIRONMENTALLY. The gladiators in the tutorialattack in that exact sequence so you can watch it, and the blood spatter around the practicedummy is arranged in the same pattern so the floor itself is a diagram. The tip then asks youto notice, rather than telling you to memorize.What this buys. Mastery becomes knowledge rather than execution. The skill ceiling riseswithout the input demands rising at all. A player who has read the board can beat a fightthat a faster player cannot. That is a very specific audience promise, and it is completelycompatible with a slow turn-based game on a phone.What it costs. Nothing can be surprising twice. Every deterministic rule is a rule the playereventually stops thinking about. Determinism buys depth on first contact and spends it overtime. Games that go this route must keep adding new deterministic rules, because the oldones become free.Design principle extracted: deterministic AI plus published rules equals a game aboutknowledge. Hidden AI plus randomness equals a game about adaptation. These areopposite promises and a game has to pick one. RF4 picked knowledge and never wavers.

8. DAMAGE MATH: BOUNDED VARIANCE AND VISIBLEBREAKPOINTSDamage rolls between 50% and 100% of the listed value. A 20 damage weapon deals 10 to20.The tip then does the player's arithmetic for them: 10 HP enemies always die in one hit,most 20 HP enemies need two.That is the entire point of the 50% floor. Because the roll cannot go below half, the playercan plan against the WORST case instead of the average. Kill counts become knowablerather than hopeful. Randomness is preserved for texture, but it never invalidates a plan.Crits stack across sources. Sleeping and unstable both grant critical hits on physicaldamage, and crits from different sources stack. Two stacked crits kill in one hit. So the critsystem is not a damage bonus, it is a combo system. Two positional setups multiply into abreakpoint you could not otherwise reach.Design principle extracted: put a floor under damage variance. Unbounded low rolls donot create tension, they create unplannable turns, and the player correctly responds bydisengaging from planning entirely.

9. STATUS EFFECTS ARE POSITION CONTROL, NOT DAMAGEAlmost nothing in the status list is about dealing damage. It is all about denying the enemyits turn or reshaping the board.Sleep bomb, one item, at least five jobs:Blocks line of sight, preventing enemy attacks outrightPlugs a corridor with a sleeping body so you can shoot the rest with clear aimCancels berserk, so timing it AFTER the enemy berserks strips the buffBlocks cloud attacks if placed before the enemy attacksSleeping enemies release constricting effects, so it is also a cleanseKnockback: prevents enemy actions entirely while you deal damage. The recommendedloop is wait for cooldowns, knock back to deny actions, damage, re-sleep, repeat. Enemyturn denial as the primary defensive stat.Corners: when an enemy follows you around a corner it creates the room you need to knockit back and land an AOE strike without taking damage. The corner is not cover, the corner isa spacing tool.Design principle extracted: one item with five geometry-dependent uses beats five itemswith one use each. It rewards understanding over inventory, and it costs a fraction of thecontent budget.

10. THE ECONOMY OF RESETSA quietly deep system with a genuinely unusual centerpiece.Energy shroom restores SP and MP and resets ALL ability cooldownsHealing shroom restores HP and cures physical ailmentsFood restores 50% of HP, MP and SP, explicitly recommended mid-fightShrooms can be destroyed by shooting them, so the reset resource is destructible andtherefore contestableAnd then the big one. Leveling up restores all cooldowns. Investing a talent point restores acooldown. An amnesia potion can be used to forget a talent and refresh the cooldown twice.That means LEVELING UP IS A COMBAT ABILITY. Progression and combat are the samesystem. A held level up is a full reset you can detonate at the worst moment of a fight.Combined with the spawner XP rule, you can deliberately time two spawner deaths so thelevel ups land mid-fight and carry you through the remaining enemies.That is a progression system with a tactical trigger on it, which is rare and very strong.Against it: the corpus also states plainly that it is better to use consumables early than todie with a full inventory. The game knows its players hoard and tells them not to. Worthnoting that a tip is not a fix. If players hoard, the system is teaching hoarding somewhere,and text does not undo that.

11. TARGET PRIORITY DOCTRINEThe game repeatedly teaches "kill the cause, not the symptom."Kill the totem, do not fight through the buffKill the spawner, the adds are worth no XP anywayBreak the healer's sight instead of out-damaging the healPrioritize the back line, use SP to get line of sight, burst them downEvery one of those is the same lesson with a different skin, which is exactly how a lessonshould be taught. Repetition across contexts, not repetition of words.

12. HOW THE GAME TEACHES, WHICH IS ITS OWN SYSTEMThree distinct teaching registers in the corpus, and the split is deliberate.Register A, explicit mechanical instruction. Bulleted, numeric, states keys andpercentages. Used for anything the player could not possibly derive: the SP regen parity, the50 to 100% damage band, the 6 tile detection radius, the 10 tile shout range, the 25%ranged retreat chance.Register B, the riddle line. A single unbulleted poetic sentence with no mechanicalvocabulary at all. Not all paths to destruction are direct. The right angle can make all thedifference. Even hunters grow weary of the hunt. These appear where the room itself is thepuzzle. They point at a solution without naming it, so the player still gets the discovery.Register C, environmental demonstration. The gladiator drill and the blood spatterteaching the attack order. No text at all, and it is the most memorable teaching moment inthe entire set.The escalation is the design. Register A for what is unguessable, Register B for what isguessable, Register C for what is showable. Most games use only A. RF4 grades the registerto the thing being taught.Design principle extracted, and this is the one I would fight for: the teaching registershould be chosen by whether the player COULD derive the rule unaided. Tell them whatthey cannot derive. Hint at what they could. Show them what the room can demonstrate.Never explain something the floor could have shown.

13. WHAT I WOULD PUSH BACK ONNot everything here is worth taking, and a teardown that only praises is useless.The tip volume itself. 83 screens of instruction is a lot of reading placed between theplayer and the game. Some of it is doing work the fiction should have done. When a tip hasto say do not click the pit tiles, use numpad, graphics slightly broken, the tip has become abug report with a border around it.Duplicate and near-duplicate tips. The sprint tip appears twice with only close distanceversus create distance changed. The auto-explore tip appears twice identically. Cheap tofix, and the repetition trains players to skip boxes, which then costs you the tips thatactually mattered.A real contradiction the game creates for itself. One tip says base SP regeneration is 1SP every 5 turns, which reads as a personal per-use timer. A later tip corrects this to aglobal every-5th-turn clock and explicitly says it is not 5 turns after use. Those are twodifferent systems, and the player learns the wrong one first. If a rule is subtle enough toneed a correction later, state it correctly the first time.Determinism has a shelf life. Covered above. Published fixed rules are enormouslysatisfying to learn and then become invisible. This is a real cost, not a nitpick, and it is themain structural risk in the whole approach.

14. THE NINE MACHINES, COMPRESSEDFor quick reference. This is the whole corpus reduced to its load-bearing parts.!. A free-movement currency on a global clock, which makes timing the skill". A retreat-and-recharge combat loop, which makes level shape a combat system#. Movement asymmetry, which makes geometry generate distance for free$. Vision as one variable gating five enemy systems, plus tools to manufacture it%. Three-layer awareness, which turns stealth into a fight-start trigger&. Terrain kills that ignore your damage stat, which keeps bad runs solvable'. Published deterministic AI, which makes mastery knowledge instead of execution(. Bounded damage variance, which makes breakpoints plannable). Status effects as turn denial and board editing, one item doing five jobs15. OPEN FORKS FOR BOHEMIAListed, not decided.
