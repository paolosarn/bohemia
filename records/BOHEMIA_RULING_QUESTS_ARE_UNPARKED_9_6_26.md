## THE DECISION
**QUESTS IS UNPARKED. THE SHAPE IS B WITH A'S SPINE: the sixty-boss ladder is the
main line, and everything around it is asked for by the systems, not written.**
1. **THE SPINE IS THE LADDER.** 60 bosses, 53 already wired, one verb each. That is
   the story and it carries the three generations. Nothing procedural touches it.
2. **EVERYTHING ELSE IS ASKED FOR BY THE WORLD.** Nobody writes a side quest. A
   person you know, in a place with an owner, wants something they cannot get,
   because of a system that is already running: a debt with a name on it, a power
   bill, a border they cannot cross, a shelf that emptied, a family that owes.
3. **THE VERBS ARE THE ONES WE HAVE.** No quest introduces a verb the game does not
   already do. If an ask needs a new verb, it is a boss, not a quest.

## THE RESEARCH THAT SHAPED IT, INCLUDING AGAINST MY OWN PICK
I went looking for why generated quests usually feel like filler, because that is
the risk in B and I would rather find it now:
- The failure is not the generator. It is **lack of meaningful impact on the world
  and its people**. Generated quests read as filler when finishing one changes
  nothing that the player can see.
- What makes a task feel worth doing is **perceived task value**: the player's
  belief that the thing they did mattered. That belief comes from VISIBLE
  CONSEQUENCE, not from the writing.
- The upside nobody mentions: generation stops the game from ever handing you the
  same errand twice, which is the other way content dies.
Sources: [Deriving quests from open world mechanics (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.00341),
[Game Developer: procedural quest generation, industry outlook](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/procedural-quest-generation-current-and-future-industry-outlook),
[The quest in a generated world](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228771322_The_quest_in_a_generated_world),
[Towards procedural quest generation: a structural analysis of RPG quests](https://www.academia.edu/2600453/Towards_procedural_quest_generation_A_structural_analysis_of_RPG_quests).

## SO THE ONE RULE THAT MAKES B WORK
**AN ASK THAT CHANGES NOTHING VISIBLE IS NOT AN ASK.** Every generated ask must
move something the player can SEE afterwards, in a system that already exists: a
block changes hands, a debt clears or is called in, a shelf refills, a person moves
house, a light comes back on, a rumour about you changes. If a candidate ask cannot
name what it visibly changes, it does not get offered. That single rule is the
whole difference between this and filler, and it is the thing to gate.
