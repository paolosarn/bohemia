## THE SHORT VERSION
**The ask almost always works, and the person asking has no idea. People
underestimate by as much as HALF how likely a stranger is to say yes. So the scene
is not about whether help arrives. It is about what it costs to open your mouth,
and our whole cast never once says that out loud.**

## 1. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG, WITH THE MECHANISM
Six studies, experimental and in the field, published in 2008:

    **people underestimated by as much as 50% the likelihood that others would
    agree to a direct request for help**

And the sixth study found WHY, which is the part a writer can use:

    **help seekers were less willing than potential helpers to appreciate the
    SOCIAL COST OF REFUSING. They attended instead to the INSTRUMENTAL COST OF
    HELPING.**

**THE ASKER AND THE HELPER ARE DOING DIFFERENT ARITHMETIC.** The asker is totting up
what saying yes will cost the other person: an hour, a walk, a battery. The helper
is not thinking about that at all. The helper is thinking about what kind of person
says no to that, standing right there, in front of them.

**SO THE ACCURATE SCENE IS THAT THE ASK LANDS AND THE ASKER IS ASTONISHED.** Every
instinct says to write the tension as "will they help", and the evidence says that
tension is in the wrong place. **The tension is entirely in the approach.**

## 2. HOW SHAME ACTUALLY SHAPES IT, AND IT IS BACKWARDS FROM THE OBVIOUS
The threat-to-self-esteem model: asking for help means admitting you cannot cope,
so a request is a threat to your own standing. Two consequences, both
counterintuitive, both writable.

**FIRST: THE PROUD ASK LESS, NOT MORE.** People with HIGH self-esteem are more
sensitive to that threat and seek LESS help under self-threatening conditions.
**So the man who most obviously needs it is the one who will not open his mouth**,
and the one who asks easily is not the one in the worst trouble.

**SECOND, AND THIS ONE DECIDES OUR WHOLE DESIGN: IT IS WORSE TO BE HELPED BY SOMEONE
LIKE YOU.** The research on donor and recipient similarity supports the model: help
from a peer, on something that matters to you, is the most threatening kind, because
a peer is the comparison. Help from someone distant costs your standing far less.

**SO A MAN ASKS A STRANGER BEFORE HE ASKS HIS NEIGHBOUR.** And that hands this game
its player's role for free: **the player is the safest person in the valley to ask,
because the player is nobody's peer.** A stranger who walks up a street is not
somebody you have to sit next to for the next thirty years.

## 3. THE REAL TESTIMONY, AND THE FIRST WORD IS NOT THE ONE I EXPECTED
A study of food bank use in northeast Scotland named five recurring themes:

    **resourcefulness, desperation, shame, gratitude, powerlessness**

**RESOURCEFULNESS LEADS.** People in the worst position do not present themselves as
helpless, they present themselves as competent people in a bad week. That is exactly
what the self-esteem model predicts: **the ask arrives wrapped in evidence that the
asker is normally fine.**

Two more things from the same body of work.

**THE LADDER OF ALTERNATIVES, in their own order.** Asked what they would do without
help, people said: **rely on family, borrow, go into arrears on rent or bills, or
simply go without.** That is the order of asks, and going without sits alongside the
others rather than last. **A person who has gone without is not a person who failed
to think of asking. Going without was one of the options.**

**AND SURVIVAL FATIGUE**, named as a distinct barrier: cognitive weariness and
psychological exhaustion that makes help-seeking HARDER. **The longer the trouble
lasts, the less able somebody is to ask about it**, which is the opposite of the
dramatic curve a writer reaches for. It also lands on this lane's Q2 finding about
exhaustion, from a completely different literature.

There is also a stigma finding worth keeping: people underuse help because of
misperceived eligibility as much as shame. **Half of not asking is not knowing you
are allowed to.**

## 4. THE GAMES HALF, AND THE ONE IDEA IN IT IS THE SAME IDEA
The craft writing here is thin and mostly generic. One observation in it is sharp,
and it turns out to be the science from the other side: the reason a fetch request
rings hollow is that **the person asking could obviously have done it themselves.**

Put that next to section 1. The helper is moved by the social cost of refusing, and
**that cost scales with what the ask cost the asker.** An errand that costs the
asker nothing generates no cost for refusing it, so it is not a request at all, it
is a task assignment with a face on it.

**SO THE ANSWER TO "HARD TO SAY NO TO WITHOUT BEING A QUEST MARKER" IS: MAKE THE ASK
EXPENSIVE FOR THE PERSON ASKING.** The player says yes because refusing means
watching somebody pay that price for nothing. **Not because an icon appeared.**

**DOES ANYBODY SAY THEY CANNOT REPAY?**

    pattern hits    1
    real hits       0

The single hit is a false positive: a mother saying "I have eleven days of what I
trust" is talking about her food stores, not about repayment. **In a game whose
entire premise is that the economy collapsed and nobody can repay anything, nobody
ever says they cannot repay.**

**AND THAT IS PRECISELY THE THING THE SCIENCE SAYS MAKES AN ASK HARD.** Inability to
reciprocate is what turns help into a threat to the person receiving it. **We have
built a world of people who cannot pay each other back and given them no words for
it.**

## 6. WHAT SCHOOL LEAVES ME HOLDING FOR ROUND TWO
1. **THE ASK WORKS. WRITE THE APPROACH, NOT THE VERDICT.** The tension is before the
   sentence, not after it.
2. **THE ASK COSTS THE ASKER, AND THE LINE MUST SHOW THE PRICE**, because that price
   is the only thing that makes refusing expensive for the player.
3. **RESOURCEFULNESS FIRST.** The ask comes wrapped in proof that this person is
   normally fine. Nobody leads with helplessness.
4. **THE PROUD ASK LAST AND ASK WORST.** The person in the deepest trouble has the
   worst ask in them, not the best.
5. **THEY ASK THE STRANGER BEFORE THE NEIGHBOUR**, and the player is the stranger.
6. **THE LONGER IT HAS GONE ON, THE HARDER IT IS TO SAY.** Survival fatigue, not
   rising drama.
7. **AND SOMEBODY HAS TO SAY THEY CANNOT PAY IT BACK**, because right now nobody in
   this valley ever has.
