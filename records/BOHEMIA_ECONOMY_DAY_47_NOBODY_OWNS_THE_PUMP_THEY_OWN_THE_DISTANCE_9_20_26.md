## 0. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG

The row asks **who guarded the pump**, and round 44 set this up by finding the pumps
standing dark. The obvious build is a faction that **seizes the pump**.

**Nobody in the record seizes a pump.** They own the land the pipe crosses, or they own the
truck.

Karachi is the whole business in one sentence:

> Tanker drivers pay off the water board, the police, *"and **the 'landlords' — men who own
> the land where the government pipes are punctured, and who build valves to allow the
> drivers to pump the water**."*

The plant stays public. It stays broken. **The money is in the last mile**, and it is not
small: **272 million gallons a day** moving through 30-plus illegal hydrants, at ₨4,500 per
thousand gallons, **₨1.22 billion a day and annual revenue potentially over ₨447 billion** —
run by *"political VVIPs, government functionaries, water board personnel, military
personnel, hydrant contractors, tanker owners, police, Rangers, and community-level
strongmen."*

Caracas is the same shape with a truck instead of a valve: **municipal pipes leak over 60%
before reaching a tap**, so families buy from *camiones cisterna*.

**OWNERSHIP OF WATER IS NEVER OWNERSHIP OF THE SOURCE. IT IS OWNERSHIP OF THE DISTANCE
BETWEEN THE SOURCE AND THE MOUTH.**

And Cape Town proves the same shape from the side where the state held: the Day Zero plan
was **200 collection points**, **officials posted 24 hours a day to keep order**, and **the
army standing by to protect the supply.** When water gets scarce the thing that needs
guarding is not the reservoir. **It is the queue.**

## 2. WHAT IT COST A HOUSEHOLD, IN OUR MONEY

**Caracas: working-class households spend 15 to 25 percent of monthly income on water.**
Against our ladder, where a day's work pays one battery and a month is thirty:

    15% of a 30-battery month  =  4.5 batteries  =  one battery every 6.7 days
    25% of a 30-battery month  =  7.5 batteries  =  one battery every 4.0 days

**WATER IS ONE BATTERY EVERY FOUR TO SIX DAYS.** That is sayable in this game without a
fraction, and it sits neatly beside round 39's protection cut (one battery every five to
ten days) and well under the lights (one a night).

Other real prices, for scale: **a 5,000-litre cistern delivery costs US$60-120**, which at
our four litres a head is **1,250 person-days of water**; the whole valley drinks
**18,524 L a day, or 3.7 cistern loads.** A single bottle in a barrio ran to **US$3**.

**And the leak is the part nobody builds.** At Caracas's documented **60% loss**, our one
lit pump stops feeding everybody and feeds **two in five**:

    one lit pump lifts       18,524 L   = 100% of the valley's thirst
    after a 60% leak          7,410 L   =  40%

## 3. THE DELIVERABLE

### FOR QUESTS `[light the pump]` — WHAT HAPPENS TO WHOEVER LIT IT

> **He does not get thanked. He gets a queue.** Cape Town's plan for the day the taps closed
> was **200 collection points with officials on them 24 hours a day and the army held in
> reserve** — because a working tap is the most valuable thing in a dry city and **the queue
> is what has to be guarded, not the pump.** So the reward for lighting the valley's only
> pump is that **everybody now knows where you are and what you can switch off**, which is
> a far better second beat than a battery. And the faction whose street it fronts was
> already the owner before he touched it: in every valley with running water, **one faction
> holds all of it**, and lighting it hands that faction a monopoly it did not have to fight
> for. **Whoever lights the pump makes somebody rich, and it is probably not him.**

### FOR WORLD'S WATER OWNERSHIP

> **DO NOT BUILD A FACTION THAT SEIZES THE PUMP. NOBODY IN THE RECORD DOES THAT.** Build the
> **last mile**: the plant stays public and broken, and the money is in the distance between
> it and a mouth — Karachi's landowners charging for the valve on their ground, Caracas's
> cistern trucks selling 5,000 litres for sixty to a hundred and twenty dollars while the
> municipal pipe leaks sixty percent of its volume into the dirt. **Our game already derives
> the ownership and never reads it:** a plant runs only if the street it fronts is live,
> streets are held ground, and across ten seeds **four valleys in ten boot with nobody
> having running water and the other six each have exactly one lit plant held by exactly one
> faction.** Price a household's water at **one battery every four to six days**, which is
> the real 15-25% of income and needs no fraction. **And the leak is free realism:** sixty
> percent loss turns the single lit pump from feeding everybody into feeding two in five,
> which is the difference between a solved valley and a contested one, and it is one number.

## 7. THE PROBE THAT WAS WRONG, AND THE CONTROL THAT CAUGHT IT

**My first ownership probe reported that every water plant stands on ground held by
NOBODY**, on all three seeds. That would have been a finding, and it was a fault in my hand.

`derive(graph, districts, act)` takes **districts from `towns.districtsOf(m, cat)`** and an
act as a **number**; I passed the overmap and the string `'act1'`, got **0 towns**, and a
turf map with nothing in it.

**The control caught it before the record did.** BB-TURF shipped *"all 9,216 cells are
held, by 14 factions"* on 9/5, so I sampled 576 cells and got **0 held**. A hundred percent
disagreement with a shipped measurement is an instrument fault, not a discovery. Corrected:
**14 towns, 576 of 576 held**, and only then did the plants get read.

That is the third round running where the positive control was the thing that saved the
finding. The running kept-mistakes list is DAY 36 §9, DAY 38 §1, DAY 40 §7 and DAY 44 §7.
