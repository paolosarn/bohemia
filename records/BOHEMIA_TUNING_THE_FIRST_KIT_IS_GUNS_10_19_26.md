## The finding that proves us wrong (two of them)

1. **14 OF 15 ORIGINS WALK INTO THEIR FIRST FIGHT WITH NO GUN THAT FIRES.** The map hands the fight each man's background and level and nothing else (slices/BOHEMIA_DEMO.html picks(): company = men.map(background, level)); the fight then dresses him by his PLACE IN THE LINE (ours.crew): the first six places are pipe, pipe, sledge, pipe, pistol, sledge, and the pistol rides the Dagger (a one-tile stab). The first gun that fires is the SEVENTH man (the rifle). Only THE BLOCK WATCH (12 men) fields one. THE HUNTERS start with two pipes and a sledge. And in THE CREW THAT IS LEFT, A NEW CREW and UP FROM HENDERSON the third man is Battle Brothers' own RANGED companion (ranged skill 48 to 52, the best eye of the three), and his place in the line hands him a SLEDGE. This is why he has never started with guns, whatever any row said.
2. **HANDING OUT GUNS ON TODAY'S ROWS MAKES THE FIRST FIGHT HARDER, NOT EASIER.** Three of yours against three brigands (200 fights, our start): the pipes win 87 in 100; the same men holding today's gun rows (the Light Crossbow, the Dagger, the Handgonne) win 56; holding Battle Brothers' first bows and crossbows 55. Six against six: 85, 55, 57. Three reasons, all Battle Brothers' own rules: a crossbow fires once a turn and a Handgonne once every other turn (the reload), where a pipe swings twice; a gun holds no ring (rule 103), so the one man on the line is swarmed; and a gun cannot fire at a man beside him (rule 83), so a shooter reached is a man with nothing to do (13 in 100 of a gunman's turns start with a foe beside him, 3 v 3). Battle Brothers makes bows a support weapon on purpose. A gun start built on its rows is the first fight he already hates, harder.

## Both angles

- **THE BEST GAME (Battle Brothers, the wiki rows in records/target/bb/weapons.json and rules.json):** hit chance = ranged skill minus ranged defence, plus the skill's base, plus a fixed amount per tile past the first (Hit Chance page: "At distance = 1 (adjacent), hit chance is unmodified"); Quick Shot (bows) +0 and -4 a tile, 4 AP; Aimed Shot +10 and -2, 7 AP; Shoot Bolt (crossbows) +15 and -3, 3 AP then Reload 4 AP; Fire Handgonne +10 and -10, 3 AP then Reload 9 AP (a whole turn); throwing +20 and -10 a tile past two, 5 uses. A man caught in melee with a bow swaps to a sidearm from his bag. Ammunition is a STOCK, not a price: a quiver holds 10, refilled after the battle from the company's points (records/BOHEMIA_TUNING_AMMO_A_BULLET_COSTS_ONE_AND_BB_MAKES_IT_A_STOCK_NOT_A_PRICE_10_10_26.md).
- **THE REAL WORLD:** the RAND Corporation's study of New York police shootings, 1998 to 2006 (Rostker et al., 2008): officers hit 18 shots in 100 in gunfights where the other man fired back, 30 in 100 when he did not, 37 in 100 at seven yards or closer and 23 past it. A combat tile is a house (rule 21, 9/4); a house is roughly 10 to 15 m across (draft, no law gives the metre), so two tiles is about 25 m and three about 40 m, three to five times the police's close range. At two houses a real pistol under fire lands about 20 in 100; a rifle is flat out to hundreds of metres; buckshot opens about an inch a yard, a man-sized circle at two houses and wider than a man at three. THE SHAPE is real (pistol short and steep, rifle long and flat, shotgun close and brutal, buckshot weak on plate); THE NUMBERS are not: real pistol accuracy at two houses would be a fifth of Battle Brothers' bow. That trade is his and he made it (rule 117(d)).

## WHAT CANNOT BE TESTED YET, AND WHY
