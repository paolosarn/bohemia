## THE SHORT VERSION
**The first line is 26 words and FOURTEEN NUMBERS. More than half of it is digits.
A regulated cut-off notice has to carry three things: how much, by when, and how to
get help, and our card carries one of the three. And the line that says what
happened is written from the creditor's side, not his: "so they cut 43 of their own
streets off" never mentions him at all. A list and a notice are two different
documents doing two different jobs, and squeezing twelve lines into three merged
them.**

## 1. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG, AND IT IS A COUNT

**Fourteen numbers in one sentence, on a card the row asks a stranger to understand
in one look.** Nothing is wrong with any single number; the line is accurate. It is
a LEDGER ROW, and the card is being asked to be a NOTICE.

**AND A NOTICE HAS A REGULATED SHAPE.** A utility disconnection notice is a formal
communication that service will end unless the past-due balance is paid or
arrangements are made by a stated deadline, and regulators prescribe its content:
**the amount owed, the deadline, and the available assistance or dispute options.**
Put plainly, it must say **how much you owe, by when, and how to get help.**

Checked against our card:

| the three required slots | ours |
|---|---|
| how much you owe | **present** (43, buried at word 3 of a 14-number line) |
| the deadline | **absent** |
| how to get help | **absent** |

**One of three.** And the one we have is the one a person can least act on.

## 2. THE TWO DOCUMENTS, AND WHY MERGING THEM WAS THE COST
A bill does two jobs and real ones separate them physically. The top says what you
owe and when; the breakdown below lets you check the arithmetic. **The breakdown is
for DISPUTING. The top is for UNDERSTANDING.** They are read by different people at
different moments, and a person who wants to know whether the lights go off tonight
does not read the itemisation.

The Lebanese generator bill is the same shape even though nothing about it is
regulated: to understand one, a subscriber has to hold **three figures at once, the
amperage of the subscription, the meter reading, and the official per-kWh rate.**
Even the informal version is a subscription, a usage and a rate, and even there the
comprehension burden is the thing people complain about.

**Our card puts the itemisation FIRST and the consequence SECOND, which is the
opposite order**, and it gives the stranger the dispute view before he has the
notice view. FACTIONS had to, because the card overflows. The fix for that is words,
not pixels: **one sentence that tells him, and the list underneath for the man who
wants to check it.**

## 3. WHO THE SENTENCE IS ABOUT, WHICH IS THE OTHER HALF
Plain-language practice in legal notices is explicit that the reader is addressed as
**you**. Our consequence line is:

    so they cut 43 of their own streets off

**It has no "you" in it.** It is a true sentence about what a faction did to its own
property. A stranger reads it and it is somebody else's news. What it MEANS, that
the streets he was using are dark, is left for him to infer from a possessive.

## 4. AND THE READING LEVEL IS ALREADY A LAW HERE
Oklahoma's eviction summons **read at an advanced college level in a state where 27%
of people have attained one**, which is why the bar association rewrote the forms in
plain language: so that all parties understand what is at stake and what is
required. The reform's own standard is that a reader understands **the first time
they read it**.

This is not a foreign standard borrowed into Bohemia. **Paolo's own 8/24 ruling is
that everything he reads is at an eighth-grade level**, and the night card is a
thing he reads at the end of every single day. A line of fourteen numbers is not
eighth grade in any language.

## 5. WHAT SCHOOL LEAVES ME HOLDING FOR ROUND TWO
1. **THREE SLOTS, IN THIS ORDER: WHAT HAPPENED TO YOU, HOW MUCH, WHAT YOU CAN DO.**
   The regulated shape, reordered so the consequence leads.
2. **THE SENTENCE IS ABOUT HIM, IN THE SECOND PERSON.** If a line has no "you" in
   it, it is news about somebody else.
3. **THE TELLING AND THE CHECKING ARE TWO DIFFERENT TEXTS.** One sentence he reads,
   one list he may read, and they must not be welded into one line.
4. **A DEADLINE, BECAUSE A NOTICE WITHOUT ONE IS A COMPLAINT.** The card knows when
   the next night is.
5. **AND A WAY OUT NAMED**, even a bad one, because every real notice carries one
   and ours carries none.
6. **NOT ONE NUMBER DROPPED.** FACTIONS kept all of them under a hard height limit
   and round two inherits that constraint rather than solving its problem by
   deleting their data.
