## 2. THE REAL AISLE

### 2a. A BILLION PEOPLE, NO CONTRACTS, NO COURTS
The World Bank estimates **over a billion people** take part in some form of
informal savings group. A rotating savings circle is a handful of people who each
put in a fixed amount every period, and each period one member takes the whole
pot. **No credit score, no minimum balance, no fees, no approval process, and no
legal foundation whatsoever.**

**What enforces it:** not law. *"If a member fails to make their payment, there is
no legal recourse. Instead, the failure is met with social disapproval, leading to
a loss of standing within the community and reduced or eliminated access to future
loans within the group."* The named tools are **mutual trust, gossip, social
pressure, isolation and embarrassment**, and the decisive one is **exclusion**.

**And who gets in at all is decided by reputation reaching the organisers through
their social connections** -- which is `whoHears` with a different name.

### 2b. THE STRUCTURAL WEAKNESS IS THE BEST MECHANIC IN THE ROUND
Every source names the same fault line: **members whose turn to collect has
already passed have an incentive to default.** Once you have taken the pot, the
only thing still holding you is what the group can do to you socially.

**So the interesting moment in courtless credit is never the lending. It is the
round AFTER somebody has been helped.** That is day 7's obligations motor with a
shape, and it is a far better scene than any repayment screen: the person who owes
you is the person who has already got what they wanted.

### 2c. HAWALA: A WORD IS THE INSTRUMENT
Value moves between countries with **no promissory notes, no bank statements and
no central entity to be held responsible.** A broker takes your money and gives
you **a code, a token or a password**; you pass the code to the person at the
other end; they walk into a different broker's shop, say it, and are paid in local
currency, often **within hours**. The two brokers settle between themselves later,
by trade invoices, cash couriers, or simply offsetting.

**Enforcement:** *"a hawaladar who cheats and doesn't uphold the trust or implied
contract is often tagged as someone who has lost their honor and will be
excommunicated from the network."*

**A CODE THAT A STRANGER HONOURS is a complete game object**: it is a thing you
carry, it costs nothing to carry, it is worthless to anyone else, it can be
refused, and refusing it is a scene.

### 2d. WHAT ALL THREE HAVE IN COMMON
The tab at a shop, the savings circle and the hawala network are enforced by the
same thing, and it is not punishment:
> **THE THREAT IS NOT THAT SOMEBODY TAKES YOUR STUFF. THE THREAT IS THAT THE GAME
> STOPS DEALING YOU IN.**

Credit without courts needs exactly two conditions, both of which we already have
running: **you will see these people again**, and **what you did will be heard
about.**

## 3. THE GAMES AISLE, IN PLAIN WORDS
(No game he has not named enters this record, the design, or the vocabulary.)
- The reliable shape for an unpaid obligation is **a thing that stays on the
  screen until it is resolved**, not a number in a menu. An open item nags; a
  number is arithmetic.
- **Debt that is owed to a PERSON outperforms debt owed to a system**, because
  the collection scene has a face in it and a system's does not.
- The failure mode to avoid: **a debt that only costs money.** If the only
  consequence of not paying is a smaller number, the player will simply not pay,
  and correctly.

## 4. *** THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG ***

> **WE HAVE BEEN WAITING ON A RULING FOR A DEBT TABLE THAT SHOULD NEVER BE A
> TABLE. A DEBT IN THIS GAME IS A PERSON WHO REMEMBERS, AND WE ALREADY BUILT
> REMEMBERING.**

The purse was right to keep balances non-negative and right that debt is canon.
But what it declined to invent was a **number**, and the real record says the
number is the least important part. A billion people run credit with no number
anybody could sue over. What they run it on is **a face, a repeat encounter, and
gossip** -- `memory`, the day loop, and `whoHears`.

So the obligation Bohemia needs is not a field on the purse. It is **an open item
attached to a PERSON**, which:
- is created by somebody doing something for you that is not settled on the spot,
- is carried by `memory` (they remember you, and they remember you longer because
  you are familiar now),
- is enforced by `whoHears` (three hops, no dice, the shortest path decides),
- and is punished by **exclusion**, which `belonging` and `commitment` already
  model as stages and a wall.

**Every one of those parts is built and live. The only missing piece is the open
item itself, and it needs no number because EVERYTHING COSTS ONE.**

### 4b. AND HIS OWN RULING MAKES THIS THE EASIEST THING IN THE GAME
Courtless credit in the real world is complicated because of principal, interest,
schedules and rollover. **EVERYTHING COSTS ONE deletes all four.** A debt in
Bohemia is always **one thing**: one battery, one favour, one day's work. There is
no interest to compute, no schedule to track, no partial payment to argue about.

**The 8/15 ruling has now solved three separate hard problems in this lane
without anybody noticing**: the small-change problem (round 2), the numberless
price (round 5), and now the entire arithmetic of debt.
