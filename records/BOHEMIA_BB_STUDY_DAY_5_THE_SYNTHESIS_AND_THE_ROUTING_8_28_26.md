## 0. THE QUESTION THE STUDY WAS BUILT TO ANSWER
His: *"Imagine if Rogue Fable 4 and Battle Brothers had a baby... the
world, the caravan system, the city-state system... it was missing a
little bit of this, like, CITY SOUL."* Plus the acceptance test he stated
himself: ***"every time it's ONE FUCKING BATTLE, it's not a 40 MINUTE
LONG CHESS MATCH."***
Mine, from the plan: **how do we get Battle Brothers' DEPTH without
Battle Brothers' PACE?**

## 1. THE FOUR DAYS, ONE LINE EACH
- **DAY 1 — THE CONTRACT ECONOMY.** *Renown gates the OFFER, not the
  difficulty.* The world's opinion of you changes what you are allowed to
  attempt and what it pays, and never how hard the world hits you.
- **DAY 2 — THE MAP AS AN ECONOMY.** *We have prices without geography.*
  A grounded scarcity model where every point on 33 square miles quotes
  the same number. And difference in WANTS, not wealth, is what starts a
  market.
- **DAY 3 — DUMB ORDERS AND THE GUN PROBLEM.** *His "go, slow down" order
  set is mission command, and it is the STRONGER design when things are
  fast, not the dumbed-down one.* And cover-and-hold, the genre's answer
  to guns, produces the exact 40-minute match he named, so **guns must be
  bad up close.**
- **DAY 4 — ATTACHMENT WITHOUT PERMADEATH.** *Permadeath does not create
  attachment, it cashes it in.* His ruling is safe; the bond is still
  unbuilt. And the going-back-for-your-wounded verb is already coded, for
  the enemy only.

## 2. *** THE FINDING THAT CHALLENGES WHAT WE BELIEVE, AND IT IS THE
## WHOLE POINT OF DOING FIVE DAYS INSTEAD OF ONE ***
I expected this study to end with a list of systems to BUILD. It does
not. Four days measured four different corners of the game and **every
one of them found the same shape:**
| day | the engine we already own | where it is pointed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | a faction standing graph with canon constraints, 14 factions, every write wrapped | **at other factions. The player is not a node.** |
| 2 | a real scarcity price model, hyperbolic, sourced to siege data | **at nothing. No price takes a place.** |
| 3 | the whole anti-turtle kit: the 50% shout, the priority target at the back, backline AI that flees your line, the day clock | **at nobody. Never joined into one pressure.** |
| 4 | break cover for a body on the floor, get him up, talk a broken man round | **at their side. Your companion lies there 99 turns.** |
**FOUR INDEPENDENT DAYS, FOUR TIMES, THE SAME FAILURE: WE BUILT THE
ENGINE AND AIMED IT AT THE SCENERY.**
That is the same shape as the seventeen invisible hats, the four bright
garments nobody wore, and the face maker with no door — **THE MATERIAL
EXISTS AND NEVER REACHED THE PLAYER** — except this time it is not art,
it is the systems layer, and it has happened four times without anybody
noticing because each lane could only see its own corner. **THAT IS
EXACTLY THE THING A COORDINATOR EXISTS TO CATCH, AND IT TOOK FIVE DAYS OF
READING ACROSS LANES TO SEE IT.**
**WHAT IT CHANGES:** the honest cost of this study is mostly WIRING, not
invention. Nobody has to write a reputation system, a price model, an
aggro system or a rescue behaviour. They exist. They need an argument, a
node, a join and a side. That is the good news and it is what decides §7.

## 3. WHAT WE TAKE — TEN, WITH THE REASON AND THE COST
Every one is MECHANISM. Every number in every one is HIS.
1. **THE OFFER GATE.** Reputation decides what work you are OFFERED and
   what it pays. Never the difficulty. (Day 1.) *Costs:* the player
   becomes a node in a graph that already exists. *Protects:* NO DAMAGE
   BEFORE THE DIAL, untouched.
2. **TWO AXES, NOT ONE.** Who trusts you (a web, per person and per
   faction) is separate from what you are known to DO. (Day 1.) *Costs:* a
   second scalar and a reason to write to it.
3. **PRICE GETS A PLACE.** The scarcity model takes a district argument.
   (Day 2.) *Costs:* one dimension on an existing function. No new
   economics.
4. **DISTRICTS WANT DIFFERENT THINGS.** Unequal preferences, which is what
   actually starts a market. (Day 2.) *Costs:* a want/produce pair per
   district; the dry stores downtown are already canon.
5. **FRICTION IS PROTECTED.** No fast, safe, universal route, ever. The
   moment goods flow freely the map goes flat. (Day 2.) *Costs:* nothing.
   It is a refusal with a name.
6. **ORDERS ARE INTENTS, A HANDFUL OF WORDS, ONE SYSTEM FOR EVERYBODY** —
   your side, the enemy, the factions off-screen. Changing an intent costs
   one beat. (Day 3, his ruling 4.) *Costs:* the biggest single build in
   the study, and it replaces three systems with one.
7. **GUNS ARE BAD IN CLOSE.** A hard constraint on every weapon, forever.
   (Day 3.) *Costs:* nothing today, everything if we forget it.
8. **NO COVER-AND-HOLD WITHOUT PRESSURE THAT PUNISHES WAITING.** (Day 3.)
   *Costs:* joining four things we already have.
9. **YOU CAN GO BACK FOR HER.** The verb the enemy medic already has.
   (Day 4.) *Costs:* the smallest of the ten, and the highest feeling per
   line in the whole study.
10. **A LOSS THAT IS NOT A DEATH.** A mark that persists — a limp, a
    scar, a hand. It cannot be reloaded away and it does not delete the
    person. (Day 4.) *Costs:* state that survives a fight, which the
    companion currently has none of. **See the collision in §5.1.**

## 4. WHAT WE REFUSE — AND THIS IS THE MORE IMPORTANT HALF
A study that only adds is a shopping list. These are named so no future
session quietly re-imports them.
- **PERMADEATH.** His ruling (7/26), and Day 4 says we lose less than the
  famous argument claims: it amplifies a bond, it does not make one.
- **COIN.** BB runs on money. We have three currencies and nobody in this
  world says the word. EVERYTHING COSTS ONE.
- **COVER-AND-HOLD AS THE ANSWER TO GUNS.** It is the genre's answer and
  it produces his 40-minute match. Refused on his own acceptance test.
- **A GAMBIT EDITOR.** Not FF12's grammar, no condition list, no
  priority table to sort. *"I barely wanted it to be kind of like the
  gambit system."* THE SIMPLICITY IS THE FEATURE.
- **MANUAL CONTROL OF A SECOND BODY. EVER.** His ruling 1, and Day 3's
  doctrine agrees with it: you change the intent, you never take the
  wheel.
- **REPUTATION THAT SCALES DIFFICULTY.** Players read it as being
  punished for succeeding. (Day 1.)
- **A FAST, SAFE, UNIVERSAL TRADE ROUTE.** (Day 2.)
- **SLOW ON PURPOSE.** BB's pace is a design choice we are not copying.
  120 BPM and RF4 are the clock.
- **MORE WRITTEN BACKSTORY AS THE FIX FOR ATTACHMENT.** Every source in
  Day 4 points the other way: *"the narrative is created by the
  gameplay."*
- **A MERCENARY COMPANY OF HIRED STRANGERS.** We are one person, then a
  family, across three generations (8/28). A caravan of paid men is not
  automatically legal here and WHO WALKS WITH YOU IS HIS.

## 5. THE COLLISIONS BETWEEN HIS OWN LAWS THAT THE STUDY SURFACED
Flagged, not decided. A contradiction between two live laws is a bug.
**5.1 THE REWIND CAN UNDO A SCAR.** ~200 moves of take-back (8/15) versus
a mark that is supposed to be permanent (§3.10). Three shapes: (a) the
mark lands AFTER the bell, outside the window the rewind reaches; (b) the
rewind restores position and health but never identity-level state; (c)
rewinding past a mark costs something real. **(a) is the cheapest and the
most honest, and it is still his dial.**
**5.2 "ONE SYSTEM DRIVES EVERYBODY" MEETS "SHE RUNS A FIXED LADDER."**
Day 3's ruling 4 says one order system drives your side, the enemies and
the factions. The 8/31 companion law says she runs a FIXED ladder with no
order menu and no gambits to edit. **THESE RECONCILE CLEANLY AND SOMEBODY
SHOULD WRITE IT DOWN ONCE:** her ladder IS her default intent. An intent
does not add a menu; it SWAPS WHICH LADDER SHE RUNS. One word, one beat,
no list to sort. The control surface does not grow, which is the whole
point of the 8/31 clause.
**5.3 THE CARAVANS FACTION HAS NO CARAVANS.** Named after a thing the
game does not simulate (Day 2). Fixing it is WORLD's row; whether the
player ever runs one is his.
**5.4 THE PLAYER-AS-NODE IS MECHANISM; WHO FORGIVES WHAT IS HIS.** The
graph carries canon constraints he set. Adding a node must not let a
lane invent a standing he never ruled.

## 6. THE ROUTING — A ROW PER LANE
Rows go into BOHEMIA_BACKLOG.md under each lane with these ids. **NONE OF
THEM OUTRANKS THE DEMO PATH** (see §7).
- **COMBAT — BB-PICKUP.** You can go back for her. The enemy medic's
  scoring rule and its two verbs already exist; give the player's side a
  version. `ALLY_DOWN_TURNS=99` and the comment admitting it is not built
  are the exact spot. MUST NOT: become a heal button, or make her
  invulnerable. (Day 4.)
- **COMBAT — BB-ORDERS.** Intents, a handful of plain words, ONE system
  for your side, the enemy and the off-screen factions. Read §5.2 first.
  MUST NOT: grow a menu, a condition list or a take-over button. (Day 3.)
- **COMBAT — BB-GUNS-CLOSE.** Guns are bad in close, as a checked
  invariant over every weapon, not a habit. **A LAW WITHOUT A MACHINE
  GATE IS NOT ENFORCED**, so this row includes its gate. (Day 3.)
- **COMBAT — BB-SCAR.** A mark that persists past the bell. Blocked on
  §5.1 until he sets the dial; the rest is buildable. (Day 4.)
- **WORLD — BB-PRICE-PLACE.** The scarcity model takes a district
  argument. No new economics. (Day 2.)
- **WORLD — BB-WANTS.** Districts produce and want different things.
  MUST NOT: invent what a district holds where he has ruled. (Day 2.)
- **WORLD — BB-CARAVANS.** Goods move, on real roads, slowly, and can be
  interfered with. Friction is protected. (Day 2, §5.3.)
- **PEOPLE — BB-STANDING-PLAYER.** The player becomes a node in the
  standing graph, through the wrapped writer only, with canon constraints
  untouched. (Day 1, §5.4.)
- **PEOPLE — BB-KNOWN-FOR.** The second axis: what you are known to DO,
  separate from who likes you. (Day 1.)
- **QUESTS — BB-OFFER-GATE.** The feed gates offers on who vouches for
  you, not only on who owns a phone. Never on difficulty. (Day 1+2: a job
  is somebody's problem with the map.)
- **WORDS — BB-RESPONSIVE.** The companion's four lines are all about the
  enemy. Closeness is built by responsiveness — understood, validated,
  cared for — so her lines react to what the PLAYER just did, never a
  timer. Voice card applies; nobody in Bohemia is wise. (Day 4.)
- **UI — BB-WHY.** Legibility: the player must be able to see WHY his
  side did what it did, or intents read as broken instead of clever. This
  is the failure mode that kills systems like this. (Day 3.)
- **SHARED — BB-ALPHA-DECODE.** Write the base64 fact into whatever
  audits the build: a plain search of the alpha cannot see the fight.
  (Day 4, §0b of that record.)

## 7. THE RUNNING ORDER, WHICH IS MINE, AND IT IS SHORTER THAN THE LIST
**NOT ONE ROW ABOVE IS DEMO-CRITICAL, AND I AM NOT GOING TO PRETEND
OTHERWISE TO MAKE THE STUDY LOOK URGENT.** The demo still needs a build
at its own link (SHARED -14), a door, an ending and an instrument. A
standing graph does not get a stranger through the front door.
**SO THE ORDER IS:**
1. **THE DEMO PATH KEEPS THE FLEET.** Unchanged.
2. **TWO ROWS JUMP THE QUEUE ANYWAY, AND ONLY TWO.**
   - **BB-GUNS-CLOSE**, because it is a constraint, it costs nothing
     today, and every weapon written before it is written is rework.
   - **BB-PICKUP**, because it is the smallest row in the study, the
     machinery is written and tested on the other side of the board, and
     it is the single best feeling-per-line item we have found in five
     days. A demo where you drag your partner out is a better demo.
3. **EVERYTHING ELSE QUEUES BEHIND THE DEMO** in its lane, in the order
   the lane already runs.
**AND THE ONE THING I WOULD NOT START YET:** BB-ORDERS. It is the biggest
build in the study and it touches the fight, the enemies and the
factions at once. Starting it before the demo ships is how a lane spends
three weeks and the demo slips again.

## 8. WHAT THE STUDY COST AND WHETHER IT WAS WORTH IT
Five days, five records, zero lines of game code, zero art. It produced:
four measured holes nobody had named, one cross-lane failure shape that
only shows from above (§2), two of his own laws found in collision (§5.1,
§5.2), one design constraint that will save real rework (guns in close),
one correction to a famous piece of design wisdom that happens to
vindicate a ruling he made on instinct (Day 4), and thirteen routed rows.
**AND ONE THING IT DID NOT PRODUCE: A REASON TO DELAY THE DEMO.**
