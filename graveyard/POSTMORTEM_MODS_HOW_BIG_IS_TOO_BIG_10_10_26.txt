POST-MORTEM: MODS [range column] / "HOW BIG IS TOO BIG" (voted DOWN 10/9 in the VOTE tab; written 10/10)

HIS WORDS: "This is not the point of the mod chat, bro literally the only point is to help people mod the game if
they want like not even like have laws about that shit like are you fucking insane?"

WHAT I BUILT: a draft min and max for every numeric field (computed from the base rows) and a warning in the reference
merge when a mod's number was outside it ("a knife at minus 5 gets a warning"), plus four proof legs.

WHY IT WAS WRONG: it was a RULE about what a mod may be, wearing a friendly name ("a warning, not a block"). He did not
ask for a mod that is checked; he asked for mods to be possible and easy. A modder who wants a 150-damage hammer should
get a 150-damage hammer and no comment. My own page said so ("observed range punishes exactly the mod a modder most
wants") and I shipped it anyway, which is the tell: when my own finding section argues against the feature, the
feature is wrong.

THE PATTERN, WHICH IS THE REAL LESSON: this is the third time. 9/30 "the march stops" (a game feature, down), 10/1
"mods at boot" (NAH), now ranges (a law). Each time I drifted from "make it easy to mod" to "decide how mods behave".
THE TEST FROM NOW: does this page make something POSSIBLE or EASIER for a modder, or does it tell a modder what they
may do? Only the first ships. No limits, no warnings that guard, no policies written as rules.

WHAT WAS DONE: the ranges tool and the generated draft moved here (graveyard/MODS_ranges_*). The --ranges flag and the
four proof legs that used it were removed from the reference merge and proof (22 of 22 pass without them).
STILL STANDING AND AT RISK OF THE SAME VERDICT, awaiting his word, not decided by me: the id policy page and the
`--namespace` warning (an id is a promise), and the schema-number policy (when the data changes). Both are written as
recommendations and carry no enforcement; if either goes down they follow this one into the graveyard.
