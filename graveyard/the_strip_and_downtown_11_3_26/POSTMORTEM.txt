POSTMORTEM: THE STRIP AND DOWNTOWN FIGHT BOARDS OF RULE 341 (COMBAT MAPS 3, killed 11/3 by rule 346)

What died: plaza.1 (THE PLAZA AND THE SIGN), plaza.2 (THE BRIDGE), fremont.1 (UNDER THE CANOPY), fremont.2 (THE BACK
ALLEY), the four block pictures the Strip and downtown boards were dealt from in rule 341.

His words (VOTE tab, 11/2): on the Strip "The worststtt this needs to be THE BESTTTT"; on downtown "Looks like shit";
on every ground "watch out for straight lines at grid borders and colors that end abruptly and roads that dont connect
and things that dont visually and logically make sense".

Why they failed:
1. The Strip was a marble plaza with a fountain: one flat white field. Nothing on it said Las Vegas Boulevard. The
   Boulevard itself was a strip of asphalt inside one block that ended dead at the block's edges (a road that does not
   connect).
2. Downtown was asphalt with brick boxes. The camera looks north at 45 degrees, so a casino's east and west faces are
   never seen and a tall mass draws its roof over the tile behind it: the fronts were grey roofs and the street was the
   back of a building.
3. Both mixed old blocks (lots, strip, main) with the new ones, so their ground changed colour exactly on the block
   seams.

What replaced them (rule 346): the Boulevard runs down its own column of blocks (blvd.1, blvd.2), kerbs off the grid
lines, its two drives at the same two rows as every casino frontage (stripw.*, stripe.*), the casino towers' faces to
the camera; Fremont Street runs across the middle (fremont.3, fremont.4) with COOK PROPS' canopy bays in a row, the
fronts face the camera from the north (fremont.5, fremont.6), the south side's roofs and the alley at the bottom
(fremont.7). The lesson, for every board: design for the camera (only south faces are seen, height spills north), and
for the seam (the same ground at every edge two pictures share).
