WHY IT DIED, AND IT IS NOT THAT THE LIGHTING WAS WRONG
  The measurements held up. The two halves of a face really did differ by 1.9 points
  out of 255 before the pass, the world really is lit from the right while the faces
  were lit from the left, and the scene data really had been naming its own fixture
  with nothing ever reading it. Every one of those is still true and still written
  down in records/.
  IT DIED BECAUSE IT WAS THE WRONG SHAPE OF WORK. He asked for the SYSTEM to have
  range before anybody hands him a finished face, and I handed him eight finished
  faces in two rounds. Polish on four people was never what was blocking him. That
  is a running-order mistake, which is mine, and the note "before we start trying to
  hand me shit" is him saying so plainly.
  AND THE REGISTER WAS WRONG. Five separate "more analog horror" in one sitting. A
  correctly lit ordinary face is still an ordinary face; rule 20 wants the frame to
  be ordinary with exactly ONE thing wrong, and nothing in those eight cards was
  wrong on purpose.

WHAT SURVIVES, AND ONLY BECAUSE HE NAMED IT
  THE EYES. "the eyes are going pretty good", "less of a frog". The sclera was
  [230,231,228], brighter than every skin tone in the game; it is tied to each face's
  own lightest skin step now and the lid casts on it. NOTES ARE RULINGS: it is in the
  shipped renderer as of 9/22 and it does not go back.
  Nothing else from the two passes was kept. The fixture parameter, the terminator
  pivot, the hair shading: all of it stays in the tool and none of it is in the game.

  THREE ROUNDS OF THIS LANE WENT INTO MAKING FOUR FACES BETTER WHILE THE SYSTEM THAT
