POST-MORTEM: THE SIX NIGHT SONGS OF 10/24 (SOUNDS, buried 10/25/26, rule 166(a)). GRAVEYARD FINAL.

THE DEAD: THE METER READER, THE SUBSTATION HUMS ALONE, THE LOBBY PIANO STILL PLAYS, THE CASHIER'S CAGE,
THE MALL AFTER CLOSING, THE LAST BUS TO HENDERSON. Made 10/24 (records/BOHEMIA_MORE_MUSIC_SOUNDS_10_24_26.md), cards
sounds-*-10-24 in the VOTE tab, never voted, never wired. Their files are in this folder; their cards are out of the tab.

HIS WORDS (10/25): 'it's the same one for like seven songs bro and they use the same instruments... seven of the same songs
with like one slightly thing different... you have like this background creepy vibe, and it's hard to hear really anything
else.'

WHY THEY DIED, MEASURED, NOT GUESSED:
1. EACH WAS ONE OF HIS SONGS WITH TWO OR THREE VALUES CHANGED. The tool's own design: a parent from his OVERWORLD NIGHT
   category and two to four fields (a lead, an accent, a hat, a bass) taken from another of his songs. Everything else, the
   kit, the bass line, the form, the key moves, the late beat, was the parent's. A song made that way is the parent wearing a
   different hat; seven of them in a row is one song seven times.
2. REPO MAN'S MACHINE PLAYED UNDER ALL SIX. The machine party (the coupling on the kick, the clock on the hat, the motor) is
   REPO MAN's identity (his YES 10/19) and it was put under every one, so the loudest, most recognisable layer of each new
   song was the same layer.
3. THE TUNE WAS BURIED. Measured through a phone's speaker the tune sat 12.4 to 21.6 dB under the mix (THE SUBSTATION HUMS
   ALONE -21.6, THE LAST BUS TO HENDERSON -20.5): the creepy bed led and the melody was a rumour under it. His 'it's hard to
   hear really anything else' is that number.
4. THE GATE CHECKED THE WRONG THING. more_music_gate proved each song was built only from values he had said yes to, which is
   exactly what made them clones. It never asked whether a stranger could tell them apart.

THE LESSON (rule 166(b)(c)(g)): a new song has its own lead, its own instrument set, its own key or mode, its own groove on
the 120 grid, a melody you can hum after one listen, and a form that goes somewhere; no two new songs share more than one of
lead, bass, drums, pad, key; the creepy bed is never the song, analog horror lives inside the instruments' sound; the tune and
the beat lead the mix; REPO MAN's machine plays under REPO MAN only. THE TEST: ten seconds of each, back to back, and a
stranger tells them apart. A song made by changing a few values of another song never comes back under a new name.
