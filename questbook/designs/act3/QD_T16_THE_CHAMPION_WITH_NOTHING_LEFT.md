## THE SITUATION
The arena is a parking structure now, clean, with planters. Hector sits on the gate in his old belt. Eleven kills, no losses, and twenty years of nobody to fight. He has heard about the family, and he has waited on this road for the heir to pass.

## THE PERSON AND THE FIRST LINE
Hector, standing up slowly, belt over a pressed Bottega shirt: "Your grandmother never fought me. She was smart. I'm asking you to be stupid, once."

## THE CHOICES
A. FIGHT HIM. A one-against-one on the fight board cut from the arena's block, a routine fight, 2 to 4 minutes; he is old and still good. The dial comes up for the last shot: the heir can take it, or not. If taken, Hector dies smiling. If held, he goes down, alive, beaten, and says thank you.
B. REFUSE. "Not you, not today." He sits back down. On every later visit to the Strip, his chair at the gate is there; after a while it is empty, and the barber's line is that he walked out into the desert.
C. SPAR WITH HIS OWN RULES. Blunt weapons, first blood. A routine fight; nobody dies. He calls it "a game for children" and goes home satisfied, and less.
Each choice costs time and the heir's standing with the Strip crowd (up for A, down a little for B, none for C). No batteries.
