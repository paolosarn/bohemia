## THE SITUATION
Act 3, the healing age, and people have money and fear. A rich tenant pays the company to watch the couple across the courtyard for three days and report: are they working for the Network? Fee: 12 batteries, and 6 more "if it's true". The couple are ordinary. Almost. On the second night, the player sees the wife talking to a dead man on a screen. That is not the Network. It is a grief app, a cheap copy of her father. It is still the one thing the tenant could call proof.

## THE PERSON AND THE FIRST LINE
Julian Arce, seventies, a long pleated Rick Owens-style robe, at a window with a telescope and a cup of cold tea. He speaks when the player stops beside him.
First line: "Watch them tres días. Tell me what they are. If it's what I think, I pay double."

## THE CHOICES
- A. TELL THE TRUTH: "They're a couple. She talks to her dad." Cost: STANDING with Arce (he does not believe it and says the company is soft). Paid 12.
- B. CONFIRM IT. Paid 18. Cost: A PERSON, acts later: Arce reports the couple, and the wife is taken off the tower's roster. The report is written TOLD and HAPPENED.
- C. TELL HIM ABOUT THE SCREEN, AND WHAT IT IS. Arce is quiet, then asks where to buy one. Cost: nothing in the ledger; paid 12. It lands later: Arce buys a copy of his own dead wife and stops going to the window.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
Arce's standing; the couple's presence in the district; in C, one more household on the grief app, which feeds the act's Network-crumbling state (small, but counted). The contract is FINISHED on every branch. If the player proves Arce knowingly asked for a false confirmation (he says so in B's follow-up), the lie voids: the player may leave without the dropped-contract deed. DECLINING LEAVES NOTHING: nobody remembers the no.

## THE SIGN (the world testifies first)
Day one and day two are dull on purpose: the couple cook, argue, fix a lift button. The innocence is watched before any line lets you deny it.

## THE ONE WRONG DETAIL
