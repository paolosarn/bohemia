## THE SITUATION
Act 3, the rocket being built, a bust stretch. At shift change the highway fills with yard workers walking home
under the gantry lights. One man's lunchbox falls open when he trips on the shoulder. Inside, wrapped in a towel,
is a fuel valve assembly with the yard's stamp on it. He has a daughter who needs insulin every week, and the
clinic has raised its price twice this month because the yard's chemists buy up the supply. The crisis made both
the part and the price.

## THE PERSON AND THE FIRST LINE
The worker, forties, in yard coveralls cut like a Rick Owens jumpsuit, kneeling to close the box, not getting up.
First line: "It was going to be in the rocket. It's one valve. They have a hundred."

## THE CHOICES
1. SAY NOTHING. You walk on. He sells it. A week of map time later the feed posts a delay at the yard over a
   missing part. Nobody is named.
2. PAY FOR HER MEDICINE. 4 batteries, to the clinic, for a month. He takes the valve back in the morning, before
   the count. His name is on the rocket's build list later, one line among hundreds, and the family can read it.
3. WALK HIM BACK TO THE YARD GATE. He loses the job; the valve is counted; the yard's standing rises a step. His
   daughter's medicine is his problem. He does not argue. He says: "You're right. That's the worst part."

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER

- A person: the worker and his daughter. In choice 2 his name is on the build list; in choice 3 he is at the yard
  gate every shift after, asking for work, and nobody hires him; in choice 1 he is gone from the road.
