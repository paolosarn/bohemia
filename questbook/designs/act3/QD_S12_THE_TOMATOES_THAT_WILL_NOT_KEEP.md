## THE SITUATION
Act 3, the valley healing. The farm tower put in the new lights and they worked too well. Two tonnes of tomatoes came ripe in the same week. Every market in the valley is full of tomatoes. In five days it is sauce on the floor. This is what a boom problem looks like: too much of a good thing, in the wrong place, on a clock.

## THE SETTLEMENT AND THE OFFER SCREEN
- SETTLEMENT: the farm tower, green behind every window. Buildings: the hall (the packing floor), the market, the barber, SCAVENGE.
- BUILDING: the packing floor. Two portraits.
- CLIENT PORTRAIT: Maru Esteves, grower, green-stained hands, a Bottega knit with the sleeves pushed up.
- THE SCREEN'S LINES: "Two tonnes, all red the same week. We got too good." / "The rocket yard canteen pays. The wash camp can't pay and eats. The co-op dries them and wants a cut." / "Five days, then it's sauce on the floor. You pick where; I pay the same."
- PAY: 8 batteries whichever place, said on the screen. SKULLS: 1.
- NEGOTIATION: ask for more: 9. No second ask. Advance: 3 now, 3 less at the end.
- ACCEPT / DECLINE. Declining writes nothing. The offer leaves in five days.

## THE CHOICES
A. THE ROCKET YARD CANTEEN. The paved solar road, fast. The canteen pays the tower. The yard's shift workers eat well for a week.
B. THE WASH CAMP. The dirt road. The camp eats; nobody pays the tower; the camp's children follow the truck the whole last leg.
C. THE CO-OP'S DRYING RACKS. Two legs, the racks are on the roofs. The co-op keeps a third. The rest hangs until winter.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
A: the tower's next board is fatter (the money came home); the rocket yard's board posts a canteen supply contract. B: the wash camp's next board has two offers instead of one, because fed people have work to offer. C: when winter comes, the valley's food price does not rise at the co-op market, which the player can read as a price. Declining leaves nothing; half the crop rots and the tower's board shows a compost job.

## THE ONE WRONG DETAIL
Every tomato in the two tonnes weighs the same to the gram, and the scale on the packing floor has a sticker saying it always has.
