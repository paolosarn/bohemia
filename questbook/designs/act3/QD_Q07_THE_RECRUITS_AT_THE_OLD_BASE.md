## THE SITUATION
Act 3. The rocket needs a guard, and the guard is kids. The base wants to borrow the company's most experienced member for ten days to train them. It is the late game spending what the early game built: the veteran's twenty fights are the thing being paid for. The company rests a member for ten days (they are off the road). This is the only contract in the library shelf that hires a person rather than the company.

## THE SETTLEMENT AND THE OFFER SCREEN
- SETTLEMENT: the air base. BUILDING: the hall (a hangar with a folding table).
- CLIENT PORTRAIT: SERGEANT LUPE MARQUEZ, fifties, a flight jacket reworked with Bottega leather patches.
- THE SCREEN'S LINES: "I need your oldest one for ten days." / "Not your best shot. Your oldest." / "They teach what keeps you alive."
- PAY: 10 batteries and two trained recruits who can join the company after. NEGOTIATION: ask for more: 12; ask to send a different member: allowed, but she pays 6 for anyone under ten fights.
- ACCEPT / DECLINE. Declining writes nothing.

## THE CHOICES
A. SEND THE VETERAN. Ten map days without them. On day ten, one recruit takes the veteran's road line as their own (the line they say most).
B. SEND A YOUNGER MEMBER. Pay drops; the recruits come out worse in their first fights (fewer formation perks).
C. "I'LL PULL THEM OUT EARLY." Priced: the veteran comes back on day five, pay is 5, one recruit instead of two.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
A: the recruits carry the veteran's name in their portraits' tags ("taught by"); if the veteran later dies (rule 36, or the 10% veteran rule), a recruit comes to the family's base on their own. B and C: plain lines. Declined: nothing.

## THE ONE WRONG DETAIL
The range targets are cut from old Network billboards, and every one of the smiling faces on them has had its eyes sanded off.
