## THE SITUATION
Act 3, a boom, the rocket going up in the open. The yard pays triple for anyone who can fix a circuit, so every
certified mechanic in the valley is under contract there. Everywhere else, ordinary repairs wait. At the co-op the
grow lights' driver boards are failing, and the lettuce and beans that feed three settlements will die in six map
days. The chair has the money. She cannot buy a mechanic's hours at any price, because the rocket has bought them
all. This is a real boom's shape: one project everyone is paid to build crowds out everything else.

- SETTLEMENT: the co-op town. Its screen: the produce stall (market; greens getting dear), the loading dock where
  pickers eat (rumours: "the yard pays a kid more than his grandmother"), and the co-op's front office (the HALL).
  The grow room behind glass dims a step each map day.
- CLIENT PORTRAIT: the co-op chair, a grandmother, a Balenciaga hi-vis vest over a cardigan, green under her nails.

- PAY: 8 batteries, locked at the offer. If half the crop is saved (choice 3), 4, and she says so here.

## THE PERSON AND THE FIRST LINE
The chair, holding a dead lettuce up to the office window: "Look at that. It's leaning the wrong way."

## THE CHOICES (inside the taken contract)
1. BUY A SHIFT FROM THE YARD. You pay the yard foreman 5 of your own batteries for two days of one mechanic. The
   crop lives. The feed posts, the next trip: the launch schedule slipped a day. Nobody blames anybody.
2. BRING THE GRANDSON HOME. He is an apprentice at the yard and can do it. On the yard's fight board he is proud and
   does not want to leave the gantry. Ways through: ask his crew boss for two days, trade him your company's
   best hand for two days of yard work, or tell him plainly what his grandmother said. The crop lives; the rocket
   loses one apprentice's two days; he comes back to the yard after.
3. I WON'T PULL ANYONE OFF THE ROCKET (the priced refusal). You bring the co-op the yard's surplus lamps instead.
   Half the crop lives. She pays 4, as stated. Finished, not dropped.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
- Batteries; the co-op's standing; greens' price in three settlements (up one band for a trip if half is lost).
- The rocket's own ledger: one day slipped (1), two apprentice days (2), none (3). It is a real input, small.
- Declining leaves nothing. The crop's fate is the world's: the settlement screen shows the grow room lit or dark.

## UNDER LIBERATE, RESPECT, BECOME (the ending's one line for this row)
- LIBERATE: the epilogue's first free harvest names the co-op, and whether the family took a day from the rocket
  to save it, said plainly, with no judgment.
- RESPECT: the co-op and the yard shared the hour; the grandson goes back and forth for the rest of his life.
- BECOME: the steward remembers the first time the family weighed a crop against a launch, and now weighs every
  crop in the valley against everything, kindly, for everyone.

## THE ONE WRONG DETAIL
Every dying plant in the grow room leans toward the rocket yard, not toward the lights.
