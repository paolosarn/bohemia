## THE SITUATION
Act 3. The Network is falling apart. Canon names what it kept in its forge: the robotics factory, the data
fortress's compute, "the hoarded chips and knowledge." One warehouse of it is now in the valley's hands: on the
war path it was taken, on the conversion path it was handed over by the un-implanted members who switched sides.
The councils have agreed to share its chips and stored cells by lot, in a school gym, over three days. The price
of cells in the valley is about to crash. The traders who hoarded cells for the lean years are about to be ruined,
and the queue outside the gym is already two blocks long.

## THE SETTLEMENT AND THE OFFER SCREEN
- SETTLEMENT: the council town. Its screen: the market (cell prices falling a band a day), the bus shelter where
  people wait (rumours: "they say there's a crate for every street"), and the school gym, which is the HALL for
  the three days.
- CLIENT PORTRAIT: a council clerk, twenties, round glasses, a Rick Owens cargo vest full of pens.
- THE SCREEN'S LINES:
  1. "Three days. We draw lots for what they kept. Keep the line a line."
  2. "Ten cells. From the hoard, so, you know. Fresh."
  3. "Everyone who's in the valley gets a ticket. That's the rule we wrote."
- PAY: 10 batteries, locked at the offer.
- NEGOTIATION: ask for more (up to 12; the clerk checks with nobody and says yes). Advance: 5.
- ACCEPT / DECLINE.

## THE PERSON AND THE FIRST LINE
The clerk, tearing lottery tickets off a roll: "Do you know how to make a queue fair? Because I don't."

## THE CHOICES (inside the taken contract)
1. KEEP THE LINE A LINE. Three days at the gym: the gym door, the queue, the loading bay (each night's trouble a fight on the fight board, under the 15-minute ceiling). On the second night a
   group tries to take the loading bay. Ways through: close the bay and move the draw inside, hold the bay (rule 36
   applies), or give the group tickets and a place at the back of the line, which some take. Full pay.
2. PUT THE NETWORK'S FAMILIES IN THE DRAW. On day two, a dozen families from the Network's own quarter stand at the
   end of the queue with no tickets. The councils' rule says "everyone who's in the valley"; some councillors say
   they are not. You can argue them in (costs standing with two councils; they get tickets) or leave the rule to
   the councils. Full pay either way.
3. I WON'T GUARD A LINE THAT KEEPS PEOPLE OUT (the priced refusal). You stand down on day two; the clerk pays 5,
   as stated at the offer. Finished, not dropped. The councils keep the line without you.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
- Batteries; standing with the councils; the cell price in the valley (it falls either way; the ledger holds how
  fairly it fell).
- Choice 2: the Network families are in the valley's record as neighbours. On the conversion path, one of them is
  a hand on the launch-eve roll call (QD-H06); on the war path, their tickets are the first peace.
- A trader the family knows (derived from earlier acts) is on the settlement screen selling cells at a loss.
- Declining leaves nothing. The draw happens; someone else keeps the line.
