## THE SITUATION
Act 2, a bust year. The power share reaches the west side on one pole line. At night, crews cut the copper and sell it by weight. Every cut is a brownout for six hundred houses. The line warden has no crew left who will walk it at night. This is the patrol contract: walk the line three nights and keep it whole.

## THE SETTLEMENT AND THE OFFER SCREEN
- SETTLEMENT: RELAY WEST, a substation, a canteen and a few streets. In the settlement view: the relay building, a clinic, a small market, and a lit guard post.
- BUILDING: the relay building, drawn with an intercom speaker by the door that repeats the outage schedule in a flat recorded voice, on a loop (PL07). Tap the door.
- CLIENT PORTRAIT: Reina Oyola, line warden, forties, an oversized reflective rain shell worn like couture, a headlamp on her forehead, a notebook of cut dates.
- THE SCREEN:
  1. "Line's been cut four times since the rain." (FL01, the place stated flat)
  2. "Walk it three nights, dusk to light. Whoever you find cutting, stop them."
  3. "They come in fours and fives, with bolt cutters and one gun between them."
  4. "{fee} baterías a night. You get paid each morning, whatever you found."
- PAY: per NIGHT, not per body: (K x 1 day + R1 + S) x 3, paid each morning. A night with nothing found pays the same as a night with a fight.
- NEGOTIATION: ASK FOR MORE: one step, then "That's the line's budget. It's the line's or nothing." ADVANCE: the first night's pay now; a debt only if you walk off before the first night ends. Third push: "I'll walk it myself." Nothing written.
- ACCEPT / DECLINE: accept, and the line is on the map as a strip of poles. Decline: "Sure." She writes the next cut date. Nothing written.

## THE CHOICES
Each night on the map the party walks the pole line; the clock runs; tracks show. On nights two and three a stripping crew is on the line, and the fight is on the fight board under a pole (the mound is the transformer pad).
1. **Walk all three nights.** DONE; three nights' pay, paid as they came. The line holds.
2. **Stake one pole.** Read the tracks on the map after night one; wait at the pole where they all converge. You meet the crew once, on your ground. DONE; the same three nights' pay (the contract was nights, not kills).
3. **Talk to the crew.** On night two the crew's leader offers to split the copper. Say no and fight, or say no and they leave, taking nothing. There is no yes: the contract said "stop them", and the screen said so. DONE; the same pay.
The "I won't do this part" choice: after night one, go back and tell Reina you won't do nights two and three. She pays night one and closes it. Stated on the screen as "paid each morning, whatever you found": it finishes at the price of the nights you walked, not a drop.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
- Declined: nothing.
- Done: "held the west line three nights". Next visit the intercom loop has a shorter outage schedule; the canteen is lit.
- Closed after one night: "walked the west line one night".

## THE ONE WRONG DETAIL
On the third night the intercom's recorded voice reads the schedule for a date that has not happened yet.

## WHY A PLAIN JOB IS GOOD
A patrol paid by the night, not by the body, makes the player's cleverness (reading tracks, staking one pole) pay in time saved and injuries avoided, never in more batteries. That is the Battle Brothers patrol without the bounty for corpses.
