## THE SITUATION
Act 2, the world clawing back. At three in the morning a woman reads a bulletin on a dead band: weather, water levels, which line is out, who was taken. Half the valley listens. The main quest needs voices that can carry the truth when the whisper broadcast goes out, and a voice that can be found cannot carry it. A client from an earlier contract mentions her in passing ("you ever listen at three?"), and from then on the roof is a place on the map. Nothing forces the climb.

## THE PERSON AND THE FIRST LINE
LUZ, thirties, headphones over a Balenciaga hood, the transmitter under a tarp. First line: "You found me the easy way. The hard way is walking my street with a case."
Her one want, in one line: "I want to still be doing this in a year."

## THE CHOICES
A. TEACH HER TO MOVE. Four minutes a night, a different roof, no pattern. Cost: two map nights of the party carrying the set between roofs. She stays on the air; her bulletin is shorter.
B. ONE LONG NIGHT, THEN BURY THE SET. Everything she has been careful not to say, once, to the whole valley. Cost: one proximity step (the Amalgamation's attention rises a notch, the heat rule of act 2); she goes silent after.
C. WALK THE FINDERS A FALSE BEARING. Only sayable after she has moved (the lie must be arranged first). The finder, a polite technician, writes it down as smoothly as it was said: "We walk what we write." Cost: a face in his notebook; the finder party on the map shadows the company for the rest of Act 2.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
QD-H04 (WHO CARRIES IT) reads this beat. A: Luz is on the list of hands, a voice that can carry the truth to the dark quarters. B: the valley remembers the long night (a feed post in the city view the next morning: "Did anybody else hear that at three?"), and Luz is not on the list. C: Luz is on the list, and the finder party is one of the things the night of the broadcast must get past. If the player never climbs the roof, H04's default: her bulletin is still on the air, and she is a hand nobody has met.

## THE ONE WRONG DETAIL
