## THE SITUATION
The depot pays its drivers in its own paper. In a boom the paper was as good as batteries. In this bust the Strip takes it at two thirds. The drivers sat down six days ago. They are right about the pay. The medicine for the chapel clinic is on the dock, and the cold box's battery is half gone.

## THE SETTLEMENT AND THE OFFER SCREEN
- SETTLEMENT: the depot. Buildings: the hall (the dispatch window), the market, the clinic, SCAVENGE. Drivers sit on crates outside the window; the screen shows them.
- BUILDING: the dispatch window. Two portraits of three are dark (no runs to offer); this one is lit.
- CLIENT PORTRAIT: Rosalind Ocampo, dispatcher, a headset and a Balenciaga hi-vis coat.
- THE SCREEN'S LINES: "Six days, not one truck. The chapel's medicine is going warm on my dock." / "The drivers want batteries, not our paper. I can't give them batteries. I can give YOU batteries." / "One run, there and back. The picket is your business."
- PAY: 10 batteries. SKULLS: 1.
- NEGOTIATION: ask for more: 12. A second ask: "Twelve is the dock's cash box. Empty it and I close." Advance: 3 now, 3 less at the end.
- ACCEPT / DECLINE. Declining writes nothing. The offer leaves when the strike ends.

## THE CHOICES
A. DRIVE THROUGH THE PICKET. Nobody lays a hand on the truck. Every driver watches.
B. CARRY THE DRIVERS' DEMAND. Take their letter to the Caravans' boss at the depot office and wait for an answer. Two map days; the medicine goes out late but goes out with a union driver at the wheel.
C. HIRE TWO STRIKERS AS CREW. Rosalind pays their wages at the drivers' price out of her own cut. The picket splits in the middle.
Same pay on every road.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
A: the strike breaks; six drivers leave for the cartel yards, whose next board gains a haul offer; the depot's board drops to two. B: the scrip is honoured at par; the depot's board is three offers again and the market takes the paper at face. C: half the drivers go back; the strike ends in a grudge that the barber's rumour line repeats. Declining leaves nothing.

## THE ONE WRONG DETAIL
The departure board over the window keeps flipping its letters every hour to show trucks leaving on time.
