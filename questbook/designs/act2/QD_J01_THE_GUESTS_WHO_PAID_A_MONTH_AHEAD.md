## THE SITUATION
Late act 2, the valley clawing back, money moving again. The truck stop at the north edge is doing well: rooms
full, pumps charging. Eight guests from out of state checked into the motel together and paid a month ahead in
full cells, the best money the owner has seen in ten years. They go out only at night, all eight at once, and
come back before dawn. Nobody knows who they are. The canon ladder names "out-of-state" strangers as the thing's
late rungs; this contract never says so, and the guests never explain themselves. This row appears on the board
only once the player's proximity heat has reached the RUNGS stage; a player who never looked never sees it.

## THE SETTLEMENT AND THE OFFER SCREEN
- SETTLEMENT: the truck stop, on the map where the interstate leaves the valley. Buildings on its screen: the
  pumps (market), the diner (tavern, rumours), the motel office (the HALL).
- CLIENT PORTRAIT: the motel owner, sixties, a draped black Rick Owens coat over a housedress, reading glasses on
  a chain, a ledger open in front of her.
- THE SCREEN'S LINES:
  1. "Eight rooms. Paid a month ahead. I should be happy."
  2. "Three nights. Watch where they go. Tell me if I throw them out."
  3. "I'm not asking you to touch them. I'm asking you to look."
- PAY: 7 batteries, locked at the offer.
- NEGOTIATION: ask for more (up to 9; past that she closes the ledger: "Then no." The offer waits; nothing is
  written). Ask for an advance: 3 now.
- ACCEPT / DECLINE. The screen shows, in her words, the one danger: "They don't like being looked at."

## THE PERSON AND THE FIRST LINE
The owner, from behind the office counter, before the screen's lines: "You're the ones with the truck. Sit.
Eat something. I made too much pozole for eight people who don't eat."

## THE CHOICES (inside the taken contract, on the motel block, three nights)
1. WATCH AND REPORT. Three nights at the motel, each a post he picks on its screen (cover behind the ice machine, the roof, the diner window). What you
   see: eight people standing in the dark lot at 3 a.m., facing the dam, not moving, for an hour. You tell her.
   She keeps them (the money is good). Costs three map days. Heat rises one step (you looked).
2. ASK THEM TO LEAVE, FOR HER. On the third night you knock. All eight leave within the minute, without a word,
   without a fight. Pay in full. Heat rises one step. On the next trip north, a different motel on the same
   road has eight guests.
3. I WON'T WATCH THEM (the priced refusal). You tell her to take their money and not look. She pays 4 of the 7,
   as you agreed if you walk; the job is finished, not dropped. Heat does not rise.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
- Batteries; the owner's standing (she offers again only after 1 or 2).
- Heat: +1 on choices 1 and 2. The row "watched the strangers" (1) or "moved the strangers" (2).
- At the STOP stage the motel's lot is empty either way; the feed, later, carries a line about the truck stop.
- Declining leaves nothing, anywhere. The guests are there whether or not the company ever came.

## THE ONE WRONG DETAIL
All eight names in the register are written in the same handwriting, and it is not hers.
