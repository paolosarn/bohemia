## THE SITUATION
Act 2, the valley clawing back, a boom stretch. The faction that holds most of the dam share has started charging on
the paved road, and it prints receipts. The rate is a percentage of what the company carries, so a rich company pays
more. The booth is new; the map drew it the round the faction's share passed a threshold.

## THE PERSON AND THE FIRST LINE
The toll-taker: a young woman in a faction-coloured Rick Owens drape, a receipt printer on a strap, bored.
First line: "Five percent of the load, papi. It's on the paper. Everybody pays the paper."

## THE CHOICES
1. PAY. Costs 5 percent of the batteries carried (scales with the wallet). Standing with the faction up by a
   small step. The receipt goes in the company's ledger; it means the next booth on this faction's roads waves you
   through that day.
2. ARGUE THE RATE. Costs an hour and a small drop in standing with this faction. You pay 2 percent, and she writes
   your company's name in a second book, by hand.
3. TURN BACK AND TAKE THE DIRT. Costs time: the dirt road is slower, arrival is later. Nothing else. No one follows.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
- Batteries (percentage, choice 1 or 2).
- Standing with the faction holding the share.
- Time (choice 3).
- The echo: if the company's name is in the handwritten book, later in act 2 a street contract comes from someone
  who got that book and wants to know why you are in it. On the feed: a post about the booth, from a trader who
  also paid.
