## THE SITUATION
Act 2, money moving again. The tower's old director left for the foothills the night the new one took the top floor, and he took the tower's memory with him: the ledger of who owes what, since when. A boom is when debts are worth collecting. The new director wants the book.

## THE CHOICES
The old director opens his door with a shotgun and then puts it down. He says it aloud, once: "Ask him who kept these books before he was director."
A. BRING THE BOOK. The director tears out one page in front of you, folds it into his pocket, and pays.
B. BRING THE BOOK AND THE OLD MAN. He wants to walk back in and say it in the lobby. Two days slower. The lobby hears it.
C. BURN THE DEBT PAGES. The old man asks it: the co-op families in the book are freed. The director pays the full 9, because he cannot tell until he counts. When he counts, the tower's board is one offer thinner for the family next visit.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
A: collections start; the co-op blocks' board posts a ration-guard job within two stops. B: the director keeps his floor but the barber's rumour line says what the lobby heard, and the tower's next board posts an audit guard offer. C: the co-op's rent price falls one; the tower board thins. Declining leaves nothing; the director sends someone else and the book comes back whole.

## THE ONE WRONG DETAIL
The ledger's last entry is in the old director's hand and dated two weeks after the night he left.
