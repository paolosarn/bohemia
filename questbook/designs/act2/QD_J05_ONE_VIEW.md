## THE SITUATION
Act 2, a bust stretch. The party stops to charge at a crossroads post. A girl of fifteen sits on the post's
concrete plinth with a cracked phone. Three nights ago she filmed men in clean work clothes carrying cases into
the storm drain under the relay mast. She has posted it eleven times. Each time it is gone within a minute. Each
time, before it goes, the view counter says 1. She is not scared. She is annoyed, the way a teenager is annoyed
at a phone. The canon says the thing deletes what is about itself; nobody here knows there is a thing.

## THE PERSON AND THE FIRST LINE
The girl, runway-thrifted: an oversized Balenciaga hoodie, one sneaker unlaced, charging her phone off the post.
First line, not looking up: "Is your feed broken too, or is it just me?"

## THE CHOICES
1. TAKE THE CARD. She pulls the memory card out and asks you to carry it by hand to her uncle, who fixes phones in
   the next town: "He'll put it up right." Costs nothing but the stop; heat rises one step (you looked, and you
   carry it). The uncle becomes a person in a place, and a hand for QD-H03 if the family keeps visiting.
2. TELL HER TO DELETE IT AND GO HOME. She shrugs and does. Nothing happens to her. Costs nothing. Heat stays.
   "Okay, okay. You sound like my mom." (the warm beat: she means it as thanks).
3. KEEP MOVING. She keeps posting. The counter keeps saying 1.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
- Heat (choice 1). The card is an object in the inventory, not a note: it can be delivered, kept, or lost.
- The uncle's standing if delivered; the unrecorded ledger gets one row (a thing carried by hand).
- Choice 2 writes a small row: the girl is safe and on the feed later, posting about something else.
- KEEP MOVING writes nothing. She is at the post the next time the party passes, still posting. After the
  broadcast night she is there once more, and her counter says 4,000.
