## THE SITUATION
Late act 2, a boom. On the northern highway a well-off household has pulled three trucks onto the shoulder and is
selling what will not fit: a charge bank, a stand mixer, a rack of good coats, crates of cells. Everything at half.
The husband is a middle-rank Network man. His superior told him, kindly, to "take the kids to see your mother
up north for a while." He does not know why. Neither does anyone else. This is the rich side of the same crisis
QD-B06 shows from the poor side (thirty people walking south into nothing): the people nearest the top leave
first, quietly, by truck, and sell on the way.

The cause is visible on the map before the stop: for two trips the north road has carried more traffic out than in.

## THE PERSON AND THE FIRST LINE
The wife, forties, in a camel Bottega Veneta coat, sunglasses pushed up, a clipboard of prices she wrote herself.
First line: "Everything's half. Cells too. We can't carry it and I won't leave it for whoever moves in."

## THE CHOICES
1. BUY. Cells at half the valley price, up to 6 for 3 batteries, and one good thing (the charge bank). Prices are
   shown before the pick. No one on the screen judges it.
2. ASK WHY. Costs an hour. She tells you what her husband's boss said, word for word, and the street the boss
   lives on. That is a thread on the map. Heat rises one step (you looked). She adds: "If it's nothing, we'll
   come back and buy it all back at twice. That's fine. That's what money's for."
3. KEEP MOVING. The trucks pull out behind you, heading north.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
- Batteries and goods (choice 1). The price of cells in the two nearest settlements drops one band for a trip,
  because the family sold down the road too.
- Heat and a map thread (choice 2).
- If the nuke is stopped, the family comes back; the feed posts it, and in a settlement north of the valley the
  wife's portrait is in the market, buying. She remembers whether you bought, asked, or drove on, and says so
  once, flat, never as a debt.
