## THE SITUATION
Act 2. The ranch moves horses up the Red Rock road in strings. Every string that goes up comes back one horse short, and it is always the LAST horse. Twice now it was the last rider.

## THE SETTLEMENT AND THE OFFER SCREEN
- SETTLEMENT: the ranch. Buildings: the hall (the tack room), the corral (horses for sale), the barber.
- CLIENT PORTRAIT: Beatriz Salcedo, the ranch's head rider, a Rick Owens duster over riding boots, a rifle with a scope and no rounds.
- THE SCREEN'S LINES: "A lion. Not a show cat. Bigger than anything the Strip had." / "It takes the last one in the string. Always the last." / "I had the shot once. I didn't have a round."
- PAY: 38 batteries, or 30 and 10 ROUNDS now. SKULLS: 3.
- NEGOTIATION: one "more" (42); ceiling: "That's the price of four horses. That's what it eats a month." Advance: 10 ROUNDS, shown as 10 batteries less.
- ACCEPT / DECLINE. Declining writes nothing; the string loses a horse a trip on the world's clock.

## THE PERSON AND THE FIRST LINE
Beatriz, on the offer screen, slotting an empty magazine in and out: "I counted. It waits until the string is long enough."

## THE SIGN AND THE LAIR
Her line is the rule. The map shows one set of big cat tracks that shadows the road on the ridge side, only when a string passes. The lair: the ridge, a flat rock with bones of horses, all hindquarters.

## THE CHOICES
A. BAIT THE STRING. Travel the road in a line with your strongest man LAST. The lion strikes him from the ridge (the tell: the ridge tile darkens one beat before). If he holds, it is on the board: one animal, one tile, hits once and hard. Routine hits barely scratch it; the dial shot is the kill. Pay. The pelt goes on the tack room wall; it is the ranch's (QR-AO, line 30).
B. GO UP TO IT. Climb the ridge by day and fight it at its rock. It takes the mound (accuracy). Harder; no one is bait.
C. MOVE THE STRINGS. Pay the ranch's detour: 2 days more a trip on the dirt road. No fight, a third of the pay from Beatriz for scouting the route. The lion stays on the ridge and takes something else.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
A or B: the Red Rock road is safe; horses at the ranch drop two steps in price. If the lion strikes down a company member and he dies (20%), the lion is NAMED after him in the ranch's mouths, and the ranch hangs his name under the pelt. No price rises for a death (QR-AO, line 30). Declining leaves nothing.

## THE ONE WRONG DETAIL
It has a zoo's microchip scar behind its ear, and it is washed, like it was groomed this week.
