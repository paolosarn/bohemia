## THE SITUATION
Act 2. The gate post keeps a bounty ledger, and there is always another name. This is the hall's standing work, the way a Battle Brothers town always has a patrol or a bounty. Most entries are PLAIN (a raider band that did what the ledger says). About one entry in five is a debtor who ran.

## THE SETTLEMENT AND THE OFFER SCREEN
SETTLEMENT: the gate post. BUILDING: the post's hall; the ledger lies open on a trestle.
CLIENT PORTRAIT LINE: THE QUARTERMASTER, fifties, a surplus field coat recut by somebody who once worked for Rick Owens, a pencil behind each ear.
THE SCREEN (2 to 3 lines per entry, each under 30 words): "Raider band, four of them, took a water line. Eight batteries." or "One man. Owed and ran. Eight batteries. Bring him in breathing." Beneath either: "Not found, not paid."
PAY: 8 batteries per name, the same for a raider and a debtor. NEGOTIATION: ask for more (9; the second ask closes the entry for this visit, nothing written); no advances on bounties. ACCEPT or DECLINE; decline turns the page. Nothing is said.

## THE PERSON AND THE FIRST LINE
A raider entry is a fight against the band on the fight board (a group; rule 36 applies to any company member struck down: 20% dead, else 30 to 40 days injured, which the quartermaster says once, flatly, the first time the player takes a raider entry: "They fight back. You'll lose some. Everybody does."). A debtor entry is a house. The debtor's wife answers the door. First line: "He's in the back. He knows you're coming. He's been packed for a month."

## THE CHOICES (debtor entries)
A. BRING HIM IN. He walks to the post. Full fee. He works the post's labour line until the debt is worked off.
B. REPORT "NOT FOUND". The priced refusal, stated on the screen before the yes: no pay. The name stays in the ledger.
C. PAY HIS DEBT FROM THE COMPANY'S BATTERIES. Costs the company 8. The quartermaster crosses the name out and pays the fee, 8. The company is exactly even and the man is free.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
A: the man is on the post's labour line, hauling at the gate, on every later visit. B: his name is still in the ledger, a pencil tick beside it for every company that reported "not found". C: his name is struck through in ink. After the company's THIRD debtor brought in (A), the quartermaster's screen gains one line, once: "You don't read the reasons any more." That count is the flag, and that line is its reader. Declining any entry writes nothing.

## THE ONE WRONG DETAIL
The top line of the ledger is always the same name, in fresh ink, and nobody at the post will say who it is.
