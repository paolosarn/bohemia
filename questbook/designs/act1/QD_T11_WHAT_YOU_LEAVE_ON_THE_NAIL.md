## THE SITUATION
The base is the family's. On its settlement screen a building nobody built appears: a small house with the door open, a nail by the door. Nothing happens when it appears. Nothing speaks. It waits for a tap. Inside, the first person's things are laid out on a table, and the phone in the corner shows a second face frame, dark, with the first person's jaw already in it.

## THE PERSON AND THE FIRST LINE
The first person, alone in the house, to nobody, after the player taps the nail: "Somebody will live here after me. What do I leave them."

## THE CHOICES
One thing goes on the nail. The heir starts act 2 with it, and the face frame on the phone fills in from the first person, the player's customizing starting there.
A. THE GUN. The first person's weapon, with whatever it has done (the ledger rides on it). The heir starts with its accuracy and its history.
B. THE LOT. A building on this base, chosen from what the family built; it stands in act 2 no matter how the rest of act 1 goes (it cannot be raided away between acts).
C. THE BOOK. What the first person learned: the map's roads already known, the tracks of one party already named. The heir starts knowing.
All three cost the first person something NOW: the gun is gone from act 1 (the heir has it), the lot is locked from being sold, the book takes two map days to write.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
The nail holds one of three for good. After this beat, the phone has two face frames, and the flip is a big transition, not a button (third votes). The heir's generated face shares the first person's jaw, eyes, and one trait; the player changes the rest at the start of act 2, as at the barber. If the player never taps the nail, the house keeps the door open and the heir starts with nothing on it, which is honest: the first person left nothing.

## THE ONE WRONG DETAIL
The second face frame on the phone is warm to the touch before anyone has been born into it.
