## THE SITUATION
Nine days over 45 degrees. The data centre's cooling tower lost a pump and the building is running hot. The Network does not raise its voice. It raises its price. This is the board that pays too well: one tanker run for more than a fortnight of guard work anywhere else. The water it wants belongs to somebody.

## THE SETTLEMENT AND THE OFFER SCREEN
- SETTLEMENT: the data centre, fenced and humming. Buildings: the hall (a reception desk with no one behind it), the market (vending machines that take batteries).
- BUILDING: the reception desk. One portrait of three is lit; the other two are dark.
- CLIENT PORTRAIT: Ms. Adair, facilities, immaculate grey Jil Sander. She does not sweat.
- THE SCREEN'S LINES: "Two tankers of water to our loading dock, before sundown tomorrow." / "Fourteen batteries. We do not negotiate upward; we are already generous." / "Your family's name is on our visitor list. It has been for some time."
- PAY: 14 batteries. SKULLS: 1. ADVANCE: 7, offered before you ask, which is the only time a client on this map does that.
- NEGOTIATION: ask for more: "Fourteen is generous." That is the whole answer.
- ACCEPT / DECLINE. Declining writes nothing. The offer leaves when the heat breaks.

## THE CHOICES
A. THE DAM ROAD STANDPIPE. The pump shed sells two tankers for 4 batteries. The queue with jugs watches you fill them. They go short a day, and the map shows the queue longer next visit.
B. THE WASH CISTERN. The wash camp's old cistern, theirs by use. They sell for 4 batteries and a ride for two of their sick to the chapel. Slower.
C. "ONE TANKER, NOT TWO." Deliver one. Half pay. Ms. Adair says "Understood." and nothing else, ever.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
Done: the racks cool and the brownout on the next block grows by two hours at night (visible from the map as a darker patch). A: the standpipe's queue is longer next visit. B: the wash camp's board shows one more offer (the ride made a friend). C: the data centre's hall shows the family one fewer portrait next visit. Declining leaves nothing.

## THE ONE WRONG DETAIL
The loading dock is cold enough to fog your breath, and the thermometer on its wall reads the temperature outside.
