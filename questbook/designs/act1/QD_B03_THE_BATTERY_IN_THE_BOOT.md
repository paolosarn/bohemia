## THE SITUATION
The company counts batteries at camp and is two short. One brother (the one with a thief's history in his background)
is accused by another (the one who lost a brother to thieves). The accused did take them. He took them to pay a debt
to the family of a man the company killed in its last fight, which nobody else knows. Fires only when both people are
in the company.

## THE PERSON AND THE FIRST LINE
The accuser speaks first, standing; the accused sits with his boots off, one boot still in his hand.
Accuser: "Dos baterias. Count them with me. Right now."
Accused (only if asked): "I paid for what we did on the road. You want it back, go ask the widow."

## THE CHOICES
1. BACK THE ACCUSER. The accused pays the 2 batteries back out of his wages; standing inside the company rises
   with the accuser and falls with the accused. The accused may leave at the next town.
2. HEAR THE ACCUSED OUT. Costs an hour. The truth comes out; both keep their places; the company as a whole loses
   2 batteries for good, and the widow's settlement remembers the company kindly.
3. LET THEM SETTLE IT. Nothing from you. At dawn they either fight (a short fight on the fight board between two of your own,
   nobody dies, one is hurt) or have made peace (a friendship pair, a small bonus when they stand together).

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
- Batteries: 2, from someone's pocket.
- People: a possible departure, a possible friendship, a possible injury.
- Standing with the widow's settlement (choice 2).
- The echo: whichever pair forms, the two speak about this night once, later, in a different event.
