## THE SITUATION
Act 1, the ruin, a bust, the kind of stretch when a cut goes bad and nobody has antibiotics. A trader walks a
handcart on the ranch road. What he carries reads from the nearest faction town's buildings, as QR-B says: the ranch
town has a feed store, so he has what a feed store has. His one good thing is a full course of veterinary
amoxicillin, labelled for horses. It is real medicine and it works on people (that is the realism). The catch is the
dose: the bottle is written for a five-hundred-kilo animal, and splitting it right needs someone in the company
whose background is farm or medic. The roster decides whether the company has that person, and the trader asks.

## THE PERSON AND THE FIRST LINE
The trader, sixties, a long black Rick Owens coat worn to the lining, a straw hat, one earbud with no wire.
First line: "Horse medicine. Works on people. You just have to know how much."

## THE CHOICES (price on the button)
1. BUY IT. Price: 5 batteries (bust price, shown). The company gains one course of antibiotics (1 MEDS, rule 47). If the roster has a
   farm or medic background, the button says so and the course cures one infection. If not, the button says "no one
   here can dose it" before the pick, and the course is half as sure. No surprise either way (`Q107.X1`).
2. TRADE THE GOOD JACKET. Price: one company member's best clothing piece (named on the button), no batteries.
   The member says one line about it. The jacket goes into the trader's cart and can be seen on him on the map.
3. KEEP MOVING. Price: nothing.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
- Batteries or one clothing piece; one course of medicine.
- This trader party offers the pills ONCE. After this stop, whatever the choice, his cart is sold out of them, so the
  stop cannot be farmed (`Q114.X6`).
- If the jacket was traded: in a later act, someone in the ranch town is wearing it. Nobody comments.
- Keep moving writes nothing.

## THE NARRATOR
Read over the event screen in the machine voice (rule 45: too even, tape and room, a little wrong; never in the first minute; people on the screen still speak in the squiggle): "A full course of medicine. The dose is written for someone larger than you."

## THE ONE WRONG DETAIL
