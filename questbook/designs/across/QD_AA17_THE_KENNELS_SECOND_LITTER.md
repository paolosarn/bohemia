## THE SITUATION
Rule 39: the company inherits. Dogs live twelve years; the acts are a generation apart. The dog the first captain kept does not reach the heir. Its line does. In act 1 the company may keep a dog and, if it holds a block, build a KENNEL on a lot (5 batteries, 1 day). A kennel breeds: the dog's pups are raised on the block.

## THE PERSON AND THE FIRST LINE (act 1)
The kennel keeper the company hires to run it, Joaquín, an old man who trained police dogs before the crash, in the squiggle; subtitle: "A good dog is three generations of good dogs. You got the first one. Don't waste him."

## THE CHOICES (act 1)
A. BREED FOR THE FLANK. Joaquín trains the pups to hold a line: act 2's dogs stand beside a pike and do not chase.
B. BREED FOR THE NOSE. The pups track: act 2's hunts find beasts a day faster, and tracks on the map last a day longer for the company.
C. SELL THE LITTER. 4 batteries a pup, now. No line.

## THE LANDING (act 2)
The heir starts act 2 with the dog of the line (the heirs are prepared from the ledger): the first dog's grandson, same markings. On his first hunt, the dog does what the line was bred for, and a company veteran who remembers says so.

## THE NARRATOR (act 2, the first night of the heir's first hunt)
"The dog has found the scent. The dog's grandfather found this same scent, on this same road. The dog does not know that. You do."

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
A: every act 2 fight the dog is in, the company's flank holds a beat longer. B: act 2 hunts are shorter on the map. C: batteries in act 1; in act 2 the heir hears the story from Joaquín's daughter and buys a dog of another line. Default read if no dog was kept: the heir buys a dog at a kennel and has no line; nothing is lost but the story. The kennel is a building on a held lot: if that block is raided and falls (the future goes both ways), the line is gone and a mouth says who took the dogs.
