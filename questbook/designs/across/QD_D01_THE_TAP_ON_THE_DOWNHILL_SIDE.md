## THE SITUATION
Act 1, the anarchy decade. The crew that holds this main by share (their pumps, their pipe) has lost pressure uphill. They know why: somebody downhill has cut a tap into the line and runs it into a hydroponic tent. They offer a contract at their pump yard: cap the tap, 12 batteries. Declining is free and leaves nothing. If taken, the shed is a short walk off the dirt road, and the woman who cut the tap is inside it, rinsing lettuce for about twenty people on her block. Uphill, a half-built clinic needs that pressure for its sterilizer.

## THE PERSON AND THE FIRST LINE
The woman at the tent, in a cut-down Balenciaga work jacket with the sleeves taped, does not stop rinsing. Her first line: "Ya sé who sent you. Sit down, the lettuce doesn't care." She says plainly that nobody knows what happens to either block in ten years.

## THE CHOICES
1. CAP IT. Paid 12. Two hours. Her tent dies within a week. The crew's standing rises. The clinic uphill gets its pressure.
2. LEAVE IT AND SAY IT IS CAPPED. Paid 12, the same (no reward difference). The tent lives. The crew's standing is unchanged until somebody checks; the clinic never gets its pressure.
3. CAP IT AND BUILD HER A RAIN ROOF. Only if the family holds THE CISTERN's verb. (QR-G line 29: the choice is shown on every read; without the verb she says why it is closed, "Somebody who knew cisterns could catch the rain for me", and choices 1 and 2 are full endings, so the best road is never behind a check nobody told him about.) Paid 12, costs a day and 4 batteries of pipe. Both blocks get something, and the crew loses a customer: their standing drops, and their district runs one step darker in the derive.

4. CAP IT AND PAY FOR HER WATER (QR-AC, line 24; `Q123.X2`, `Q120.X1`: the kindest road needs no boss verb). Paid 12; the player hands the crew 8 of it for a season's metered share piped to her tent. Both blocks get something and the crew keeps a customer. Without THE CISTERN this is the kind ending; with it, choice 3 is the cheaper one.
5. REFUSE TO CAP IT, TO THEIR FACE (QR-AC, line 12; `Q139.P2`). Paid nothing; FINISHED, not dropped. The tent lives a week, then the crew sends someone else.
THE LIE HAS A TELL (QR-AC, line 1; `Q128.X2`): if the crew checks choice 2, the player meets it at the pump yard: the boss holds up the cut fitting, still wet, and says nothing about money.

## WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER
Act 1 writes one row: tap_capped, tap_lied or tap_rained. Act 3's derive reads it:
- capped: the uphill block is a working clinic with a lit sign; the downhill block is still the ruin.
- lied: the downhill block is a four-storey grow tower (reclaimed, techy); uphill is a clinic-shaped shell with its sign painted and no roof.
- rained: a small green roof downhill, the clinic uphill, and the crew's old yard dark.
THE LANDING SPEAKS: in the "lied" world, a woman at the tower's door says, past tense, "Your grandmother told them it was capped. Mi abuela watered lettuce off a lie for eleven years." The pointer chain is one hop: the tower's founding plaque carries the shed's address.
THE ANSWERING DEED: having seen act 3, the player may flip back and pay the crew for the water (24 batteries) or turn the lie into an honest deal. The first row is never removed; the record now reads "lied, then paid", and the derive gives the clinic a roof while the tower loses a storey. The cost moves; it is not erased.
