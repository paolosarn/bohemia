## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Our pages already agree with Battle Brothers on the bones: a person with a portrait offers, the fee is stated before the yes and locks at the yes (`Q148.W1`, `Q126.N5`), the danger is shown before the choice (`Q060.W1`, `Q041.W6`), one twist at most and most jobs plain (`Q148.X3`, `Q148.P4`), and the finished job shows up in the world (`Q147.W7`, `Q013.W1`). We differ on purpose in two places. First, THE FREE NO: Battle Brothers writes nothing for a decline [R] but charges standing for every haggle ask and a large drop when the client throws you out [V]; our law keeps the no free, and QR-E already warned that the haggle and the free no collide (`Q126.W3`, `Q126.N13`, `Q084.X5`, `Q103.X1`). Second, THE FACE: Battle Brothers' negotiation is a hidden random walk (annoyance 3 to 6 per ask, fail chance annoyance x 0.1, out at 9) [V], which players solved into math; our pages want a ceiling the face SAYS (`Q107.X1`, `Q130.W2`). What we MISSED is the biggest finding: in Battle Brothers the board is WRITTEN BY THE WORLD. Contracts come from a settlement's situation and the result writes the situation back (a castle freshly supplied, granaries burned) [V]; none of our four pages makes the board a readout of world state, though the library argues for it hard (`Q044.W1`, `Q045.W1`, `Q056.W9`, `Q002.W9`). We also missed ONE ACTIVE CONTRACT [V] (folded 10/1 from rule 51: Battle Brothers' count, which we now differ from on purpose; ours is TWO slots), RENOWN AS THE GATE on who offers [V], and THE PAYMENT MODE as a risk choice (up front, on completion, per head) [V].

## THE FINDINGS

### 1. WHERE WE MATCH

**1a. The offer is a face, a few lines, a fee, a reading of danger.** Battle Brothers' offer screen is a client portrait, a short statement, the fee and one to three skulls (#236 W3, W4). QR-E arrived at the same shape from the library: the person's first line is about them and the place (`Q125.W2`, `Q114.N1`, `Q095.W3`), and the terms are said clean before any yes (`Q126.N5`, `Q131.N1`). QR-L's "danger that was stated" is the skull in other words (`Q060.W1`, `Q041.W6`). Match, and the skull is the cheaper glyph: we should use it.

**1b. The fee locks at the yes, before the truth.** Battle Brothers has no investigation before the price; you haggle at ignorance and the twist comes after (#236 NODE BB-3 NOVERB). That is exactly `Q148.W1`, which QR-A, QR-C (rule 4) and QR-L (the two hard laws) all adopted. Match.

**1c. One twist, mid-job, and a template if overused.** Battle Brothers' twists (contraband cargo, the bigger camp, the rival company, the client who will not pay) [R] map onto QR-C's bank: the client lied (`Q003.W1`, `Q003.N1`), a third party (`Q094.W4`), the payer gone (`Q089.W3`). Players call them expected beats after enough hours (#236 F2), the same decay `Q148.X3` documents; QR-C rule 2 rations them (`Q148.P4`). Match, and #236 is a second source for the ration.

**1d. Accept is the promise; failing costs.** Failing a contract lowers local relations and renown [V]. QR-A's "only a contract taken and dropped is a deed" is the same rule, backed by the library's proportionate exits (`Q040.W10`, `Q087.W9`) and its always-open off-ramps (`Q140.W8`, `Q151.W7`, `Q040.W2`). Match.

**1e. The pay does not pick the moral.** Battle Brothers mostly avoids moral forks in contracts, but where the client refuses to pay, taking it by force pays MORE at the cost of standing [R]. QR-L's rule is stricter: the crueller branch pays the same or less (`Q004.W4`, `Q097.W10`, `Q108.X2`, `Q070.X2`). Near match; ours is the stronger law and should stand.

### 2. WHERE WE DELIBERATELY DIFFER

**2a. The free no, and the haggle that is not free.** Battle Brothers: decline costs nothing [R], each haggle ask costs about 0.5 percent relations [V], being thrown out costs a substantial drop [V]. QR-A's research found the library's best refusals cost nothing and are never mentioned again (`Q126.W3`, `Q134.W3`, `Q122.N1`, `Q113.N1`) and its worst are punished (`Q084.X5`, `Q075.X4`, `Q103.X1`, `Q106.X6`, `Q139.X2`, `Q100.X1`). Paolo's 9/27 law is the free no. The difference we keep: a thrown-out haggle should END THE OFFER (the client walks) but write NO standing loss, because a haggle that went too far is still a no, not a deed. This is our reading; it is [PENDING, Paolo's call] only in the sense that #236 PORT 2 and QR-E OPEN 1 flag it, and our default is: nothing written.

**2b. The face shows a ceiling; it does not roll dice.** Battle Brothers' annoyance is hidden and random [V]; the fail chance on the FIRST ask is 30 to 60 percent [V]. Forum threads reduce it to "only one option is a net gain" and "the advance loses money to rounding" [V] (#236 F1). The library's rule is that options must do what they say (`Q107.X1`) and the game must teach the truth before the fork (`Q130.W2`, `Q032.P4`); a trap that cannot be read is a cheat (`Q149.X2`, `Q121.W7`). QR-L P8 already proposed a visible ceiling. Keep it: one ask lands, the second is answered with the ceiling in a line, a third ends the offer. Deterministic.

**2c. The advance is an honest loan.** Battle Brothers moves 25 percent of completion and per-head pay into the advance and loses to rounding [V]. For us that is a hidden cost told late, the exact shape of `Q121.W7`. Our advance shows the trade plainly: one now, one less at the end. The currency-you-need logic (`Q096.W3`) says an advance is worth it when batteries today matter more, which in a crash they often do.

**2d. The client is a person with a want, not a mood bar.** Battle Brothers' elder has no want beyond the fee (#236 F3). QR-E demands a person doing something in a place (`Q095.W3`, `Q108.W1`, `Q013.W6`) with a want you can see. Keep the difference; the portrait on our offer screen should belong to someone the player can meet again in the same home base.

**2e. The crunch.** Battle Brothers' daily wage burn makes refusal expensive in practice (#236 F4). The library's warning is that time as the only fee is honest (`Q146.W8`, `Q109.W5`, `Q011.W2`) but no-reward loops lose players (`Q113.X4`). Our third votes give SCAVENGE in the settlement screen: a second income so a no is never a slow death. Keep.

### 3. WHAT WE MISSED

**3a. THE BOARD IS WRITTEN BY THE WORLD (the big miss).** In Battle Brothers a settlement's SITUATION (raided, besieged, short of food, freshly supplied) makes its contracts, and the contract's result makes the next situation [V] (#236 W1, W8). Our pages treat an offer as authored per person and place (QR-E's PLACES THAT ASK). The library's system studies say the stronger design is the generator that reads the sim: the game as story generator (`Q044.W1`, `Q044.P8` for the blend with authorship), emergent narrative from interlocking systems (`Q045.W1`, `Q045.P2`), parameterized generation from input variables (`Q056.W9`, `Q056.W3`), emergent fragility as honest cost (`Q002.W9`, `Q007.W8`), and character-driven drama from the sim (`Q046.W9`). And the result must be seen in the place (`Q147.W7`, `Q013.W1`). THE RULE: an offer exists BECAUSE a home base has a situation, and hand-in changes that situation. Authored asks (QR-E) ride on top as the rationed minority, like the twist.

**3b. ONE ACTIVE CONTRACT (folded 10/1 from rule 51: OURS IS TWO).** Paolo 9/30 ruled TWO contract slots and one main quest: Battle Brothers' one slot is "wack" when a caravan job carries you to a city and a hunt waits near it. What follows is kept as the record of what Battle Brothers does and why round two leaned to one; the count for us is two. Battle Brothers allows exactly one [V] (`Q236.W2`, `Q236.N1`). None of our pages stated a count then. The library's clock logic (`Q087.W9`, `Q011.W2`) and the one-session arc (`Q148.W10`) both favour it: one promise, one marker, one clock. It also answers QR-A OPEN 5 (a contract open across the act flip): with one contract, the flip can simply show it paused on the act it belongs to. (With two slots the same answer holds per slot: each taken contract shows paused on the act it belongs to.)

**3c. RENOWN GATES WHO OFFERS.** Noble contracts need renown [V]. For us the equivalent is standing with a home base or faction: bigger clients appear as the family's name grows. The library's progression studies (`Q049.W1`, `Q067.W1`) are dynasty-scale; the board gives a small, legible ladder inside an act without a quest log.

**3d. THE PAYMENT MODE AS A BET.** On completion, part up front, per head for kill jobs [V]. QR-L covered the advance but not per head. Per head suits our group fights (rule: always against a group) and makes formation depth (rule 36) pay directly. TUNING owns the numbers.

**3e. THE GREYED BUTTON TEACHES.** Battle Brothers shows the offers it will not let you take (one at a time) as visible but not takeable (#236 BB-1, `Q236.N1`). (folded 10/1 from rule 51: ours greys accept only when BOTH slots are full.) That teaches a rule without a pop-up, which fits A QUEST IS NEVER A CARD and NOTHING POPS UP better than any tutorial line.

**3f. THE OFFER EXPIRES WHEN THE SITUATION DOES.** QR-E OPEN 3 asked whether a refused offer ever expires; `Q126.W3` says never. Battle Brothers' answer [R]: it goes when the situation that made it goes. That reconciles the free no with a moving world: nobody remembers your no, but the brigands may have moved on.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)

1. SITUATION FIRST. Every generated offer names the home-base situation that made it. Test: no offer record without a `situation:` field.
2. HAND-IN WRITES THE SITUATION. A finished or failed contract changes its base's situation. Test: before/after diff on the base is non-empty for every terminal state except declined.
3. TWO ACTIVE CONTRACTS AT MOST (folded 10/1 from rule 51; was "one active contract", Battle Brothers' count, `Q236.W2`, which we differ from on purpose). Accept is disabled while two are active; the others stay visible and readable, and a third offer waits. Accepting happens only on a settlement screen's offer, never on the phone. Test: three actives never coexist; no phone element writes a contract state.
4. SKULLS ON THE SCREEN. One to three, before the accept exists (`Q060.W1`).
5. DECLINE AND THROWN-OUT WRITE NOTHING. Test: ledgers byte-identical after either (`Q126.W3`, `Q084.X5`).
6. THE CEILING IS SPOKEN. At most two asks; the second answer states the ceiling; no hidden roll (`Q107.X1`, `Q130.W2`).
7. THE ADVANCE IS SHOWN AS A LOAN. Total pay with and without advance differs by zero, or by a number printed on the screen (`Q121.W7`).
8. PER HEAD ONLY FOR GROUP KILL JOBS. And capped by the stated group (`Q041.W6`).
9. AN OFFER LEAVES WHEN ITS SITUATION LEAVES, never because the player said no.
10. AUTHORED ASKS ARE THE MINORITY. Generated offers are the floor; authored people-in-places are rationed like twists (`Q148.P4`, `Q044.P8`).

11. RENOWN BRINGS BIGGER CLIENTS. (folded 10/9 from QR-U) Renown gates the tiers, so the board is its own progression with no quest
log (`Q236.W9`). Compatible with QR-O rule 14: renown enriches a board and never locks a screen.
12. THE CLIENT IS NOT A VENDING MACHINE. (folded 10/9 from QR-U) The elder with a portrait and no want beyond the fee is the flaw
(`Q236.X3`); every client wants something besides the fee (QR-A C20).

## WHAT TO AVOID (the flaws)

- A solvable hidden haggle (#236 F1) is `Q149.X2` in a shop: a trap the player cannot read.
- A template twist (#236 F2) is `Q148.X3`.
- A client with no want (#236 F3) becomes the vending machine the format law names.
- A punished no (`Q084.X5`, `Q103.X1`, `Q106.X6`, `Q100.X1`).
- An outcome that changes nothing on the map (`Q033.X2`).
- Reward that picks the moral (`Q108.X2`, `Q070.X2`).
- A mercy with no visible difference (`Q109.X5`), and a taught tool punished (`Q116.X3`).
