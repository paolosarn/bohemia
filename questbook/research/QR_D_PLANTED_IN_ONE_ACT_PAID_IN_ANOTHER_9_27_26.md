# QR-D: PLANTED IN ONE ACT, PAID IN ANOTHER

QUESTION (rows [across the ages], question (c), rule 31): The delayed payoff in the library (the Bloody Baron's Whispering Hillock choice, Q001/Q004, and its kin across the 152): how long is the gap between plant and landing, what did the player know at the plant, how is the landing shown, is it fair? Then the SHAPE for a quest planted in act 1 and paid in act 3, given that in Bohemia the three acts become open together (swept 9/29, rule 39c: one person at the start, the next generation unlocks later, default the first home base) and the player then FLIPS between them, with the future DERIVED from the past acts' ledgers (so the player can see the landing, flip back, and change the plant). Also: a promise made by the grandparent and paid by the heir; a VERB earned from a boss in one age used in another. What makes this satisfying versus confusing on a phone.

STATUS: research only, nothing built (rule 35). Written for DYNASTY (the flip and the derive), WORLD and LIFE+CITY (the derived cities that must show a landing), PEOPLE (the named people who carry a debt across ages), WORDS (the past-tense lines), ECONOMY (what a plant costs in batteries).
FOLDED 10/1 (QR-AB): rules 4, 5 and 17 as amended in QR-U (and applied to the designs in QR-X). Each edit is marked in place.
FOLDED 10/9 (QR-AK): QR-U rules 19 to 23 and the sharpens of rules 2, 7 and 15 and QR-G lines 5 and 18, at the end of THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS, each marked in place.

## ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

In the library the great delayed payoff is short in hours and long in feeling: the Hillock lands "quests later, in a DIFFERENT quest, so cause and effect are separated by hours" (`Q004.W5`), and the longest tails in the 152 run across whole sequels (`Q137.W8`, `Q042.W7`, `Q080.W9`). What the player knew at the plant was deliberately thin: two unverifiable stories and a bad feeling (`Q004.N1`, `Q004.W1`), with the true stakes hidden and the reward identical either way (`Q004.W4`). The landing is shown on faces and places the player already knows (the consequence arrives as scenery, as testimony, as a roll call: `Q116.W3`, `Q125.W8`, `Q006.W8`), and the library's verdict on fairness is split in one precise place: a hidden plant is fair when the player can find out afterwards and a gotcha when they cannot (`Q116.X2`, `Q118.X1`, `Q086.X7`). Bohemia breaks the Hillock's one assumption, that the past is closed. When the player can flip to act 3, see the landing, and flip back, the hidden load stops being hidden and the choice becomes a save slot, which the library says drains the meaning out of the repair (`Q147.X3`, `Q144.P5`). So the shape we need is not "hide the hinge" but "the past can be ADDED TO, never UNDONE": a finished plant is a deed forever; the player who sees the landing may go back and plant a SECOND, answering deed in the same act, which re-derives the future and costs its own price, so both deeds stand in the family record (`Q126.P35`, `Q126.P36`, `Q004.W8`). Every landing is a person, place or thing in the derived city with a pointer chain back to its plant (`Q147.P2`); a grandparent's promise is carried by a named person or a thing that testifies (`Q145.P5`, `Q128.P5`); a boss's VERB travels forward in the family's hands and backward only as the player's knowledge (`Q144.W2`, `Q011.P5`, `Q147.W10`). On a phone, the whole thing lives or dies on legibility: few plants, each with one visible wound and one person who can name its cause (`Q128.X1`, `Q102.X1`, `Q096.X1`).

## THE FINDINGS

### 1. THE HILLOCK, TAKEN APART

- THE PLANT LOOKS UNRELATED. "A major outcome decided in a quest that looks unrelated, its weight hidden at decision-time" (`Q001.W4`). You think you are deciding orphans versus a monster; you are deciding whether the Baron's wife lives and whether he lives (`Q004.W1`).
- THE GAP IS DISPLACED, NOT JUST DELAYED. The payoff lands in a different quest, Return to Crookback Bog, hours later (`Q004.W5`). The Baron questline itself pauses and threads through other quests so its end "feels discovered across the world rather than delivered in a line" (`Q001.W5`). Distance in PLACE and in QUEST matters as much as distance in time.
- WHAT THE PLAYER KNEW. The spirit "cannot prove" its claim; the node the whole quest stands on is the refusal of proof (`Q004.N1`). The petitioner is morally opaque by design (`Q004.W6`). The study's compile finding: player-facing information at decision time is "two unverifiable stories and a bad feeling", and the format has no mechanism for outcome preview. The player knew it was uncertain. They did not know it was the hinge.
- THE TRAP IS THE OBVIOUS GOOD. "Save the children" is the reflexive choice and it triggers the worst family outcome (`Q004.W2`). The study's own port warns it is potent "and can feel unfair if overused" (`Q004.P2`).
- NO REWARD DIFFERENCE. Identical loot either way, so the choice is decided on ethics alone (`Q004.W4`, `Q004.P3`).
- NO CLEAN OPTION. Every path kills somebody (`Q004.W3`, `Q001.W11`).
- THE CHOICE GALLOPS AWAY. Free the spirit and it possesses a horse and runs off, "consequence leaving your control" (`Q004.W8`, staged per `Q004.P6`). This is the image Bohemia loses first, because our player can chase the horse into the future.
- NO CONFESSION. At the landing, nobody knows it was you and the game offers no "I did this" (`Q004.N3`). Guilt cannot be bought down.
- ORDER MATTERS, HIDDEN. Doing the Hillock before its intended moment changes who gets cursed (`Q004.W7`); the port says scope this carefully so it does not break the fold (`Q004.P5`).
- THE PORTS ALREADY POINT AT US. "Plant Bohemia choices whose TRUE stakes are invisible at decision-time and land a GENERATION later in a different quest" (`Q004.P1`); "quiet at the moment, loud a generation later" (`Q001.P4`).

### 2. THE KIN: HOW LONG THE GAP RUNS IN THE 152

Short (minutes to a few hours, same game):
- A truthful line in a freezer comes back as a Speech check "much later" (`Q002.W10`).
- The quest calls you later, mid-other-content, like a real development (`Q009.W10`).
- A window you did or did not hold changes who stands beside you at the end; you meet the corpse as scenery hours later (`Q116.W3`, `Q116.W10`).
- Many choices in the reactivity engine "only pay off hours later" (`Q024.W8`).

Long (tens of hours, same game):
- A relic chased from act 1 is revealed in act 2, "seeded for dozens of hours" (`Q016.W6`).
- A night meeting under a borrowed name hands you a gift "100 hours early" (`Q071.W3`).
- Mercy in the game-before-the-game returns forty hours later as named individuals with memory (`Q145.W7`, and `Q145.P5`: "Tyranny pays mercy back in forty hours; Bohemia can pay it back in forty years").
- An optional thread seeded all game delivers the medium's most loved moment (`Q062.W1`, `Q062.W4`).

Longest (across acts that are years apart, or across sequels):
- Judgments ripen across 25 in-world years across the acts (`Q026.W10`, `Q026.W6`); the port calls this our generational fold, validated (`Q026.P9`).
- A spared enemy ripens across sequels (`Q042.W7`); the dead stay dead across a sequel, "the corpus's longest consequence tail" (`Q137.W8`).
- A victory's meaning lands "1,000 years later" in a different game (`Q080.W9`).

THE FINDING: the gap the player FEELS is not hours; it is the number of other things that happened in between plus the change of place and people (`Q004.W5`, `Q001.W5`). In Bohemia the in-world gap is fixed by canon (act 1 ~2060 to act 3 a generation or two on), but the PLAY gap can be short, because once act 3 has unlocked the player may flip to it at any moment (three acts law, section 2b; swept 9/29: never in the first minute of the game, since act 1 is played first with one person and the next generation unlocks later, rule 39c). So the play gap is no longer something we fully control, though it now has a floor: the player has lived act 1 long enough to win a home base before any landing can be seen. What we control is displacement: a different place, a different person, a different act.

### 3. WHAT THE PLAYER KNEW AT THE PLANT

The library sorts plants into three knowledge states:
- KNEW IT WAS UNCERTAIN, NOT THAT IT WAS THE HINGE. The Hillock (`Q004.W1`, `Q004.N1`). "Every ending has you consider all the decisions you had the power to influence WITHOUT KNOWING what the repercussions would be" (`Q077.W9`).
- KNEW NOTHING. Mercy in mission two becomes testimony in a courtroom "hours later" and beats the stat check (`Q125.W8`); the same study files it as "glorious in retrospect and unfair in play" because "neither moment signals that it will matter" (`Q125.X6`). Choices made in twenty minutes before the player knows the world read back "as accusations about a stranger's decisions" (`Q145.X2`).
- KNEW, IF THEY WERE LISTENING. The fair-play twist is "clued relentlessly... inevitable-in-hindsight, not cheap" (`Q032.W4`). A line only lands later: he admires the spot where John will be buried (`Q010.W8`). The Baron's doll is solvable only if you remembered an offhand line about a flower (`Q001.W8`).

THE FINDING: the best plants are the third kind wearing the first kind's clothes. The player is told the truth that something is uncertain; the clue exists in the world if they listened; the hinge is not labelled.

### 4. HOW THE LANDING IS SHOWN

- AS SCENERY. The consequence arrives as a corpse in a lab you walk into (`Q116.W3`). Skip the Ikana suite and "the dry river stays, walkably, for the whole game" (`Q147.W7`). The port makes this compile-checkable: every hidden-class quest must declare its visible wound AND a pointer chain toward its cause (`Q147.P2`). For a DERIVED future city this is the most important finding in the file.
- AS A PERSON WHO TESTIFIES. The spared return and testify (`Q125.W8`); the spared one "shows up in Hell and fights the devil with you. He misses a lot" (`Q126.W9`). Inherited NPCs "speak in past tense", the cheapest reactivity carrying the heaviest freight (`Q144.W7`, ported as a compile rule for every fold-crossing NPC in `Q144.P3`).
- AS A COUNT READ ALOUD. The roll call (`Q006.W8`); every death is a receipt with a named cause (`Q137.W1`); "the room counts aloud" so the player HEARS their weeks of choices (`Q136.W10`); the payout table as catharsis, "accounting can be the finale when every row was earned" (`Q141.W9`).
- AS A THING THAT SURVIVED. The mask survives the rewind and only the mask and the player remember (`Q128.W7`); the port wants one artifact class that survives a reset AND testifies (`Q128.P5`) because the original "opens one door" and does almost nothing (`Q128.X9`). Relics bought with people should carry their receipt (`Q131.X10`). The loot for letting her die is an heirloom that "cannot be recycled in any way" (`Q119.W7`).
- AS WATER. The reward for the Ikana repair is water, cashed downstream by strangers (`Q147.W8`); the river is dry because a soul is (`Q147.W2`). Infrastructure as the landing is our native tongue.

### 5. IS IT FAIR

The library is unusually unanimous about where the line is:
- "The difference between a secret and an accident is whether the player can find out" (`Q116.X2`).
- "Unsignposted consequence is powerful and it must be LEARNABLE... our persistent consequence needs a tell" (`Q118.X1`).
- "A consequence the player couldn't have foreseen isn't a choice, it's a gotcha" (`Q086.X7`).
- "If order matters, TELEGRAPH it, or make failure legible/recoverable" (`Q023.X2`).
- The spare trap that "reads as the good option and detonates into the worst outcome" leaves players "feeling tricked rather than taught" (`Q149.X2`).
- The cross-game combination lock: "a fold that carries state across generations must not bury its best scene behind a combination lock nobody knows they're setting" (`Q096.X1`); "the game must SHOW the player they're building keys" (`Q102.X1`); do not silently gate the best endings on missable content (`Q063.X5`, `Q070.X1`).
- The promissory note: "a consequence that only pays out in a sequel is a promissory note... our fold has three dynasties, so we can actually cash it, but each generation must feel the bill" (`Q121.X1`).
- The audit must name its categories in the world, or the wiki is mandatory (`Q137.X1`).

THE HILLOCK'S OWN VERDICT: fair by these rules, just. The uncertainty was stated (`Q004.N1`), the bad outcome lands on faces the player knows, and the port itself limits the obvious-good trap to "sparingly" (`Q004.P2`). Where it fails is traceability: the study records that most players never trace the landing back to the oak. That is the part we must fix, and the flip gives us the tool.

### 6. WHAT THE FLIP CHANGES (the part no study had)

Bohemia's three acts are open together once unlocked (rule 39c: act 1 first, the next act unlocks later) and act 3 is derived from act 1's ledgers (three acts law, section 2a to 2c). The library has partial cousins:
- Time travel as a consequence engine: "actions in one era materially reshape another" (`Q050.W5`), ported as "plant now, forest later; a sin now, a haunted heir later" (`Q050.P5`).
- The loop that carries knowledge: you learn the schedule on one cycle and execute it on the next (`Q011.W4`); the loop "carries knowledge + inventory" (`Q020.W3`); knowing the path collapses the loops (`Q020.W8`). The port for us: "a quest the SECOND dynasty solves cleaner because the first mapped it" (`Q011.P5`).
- Two perspectives converged by the player (`Q011.W6`) and the don't-help-too-early dependency (`Q011.W7`), which the port says must be telegraphed "so it doesn't feel like a gotcha" (`Q011.P8`).
- "Repairs are loop-local, knowledge permanent" (`Q147.W10`).

And the warnings:
- The reset economy "cheapens the repair for some": players who experience resets as futility disengage from exactly the scenes that matter (`Q147.X3`). Repetition fatigue: every retry replays known beats (`Q128.X10`).
- The standing gate for every fold feature: "IF THIS RUN WERE RETRIED, WHAT WOULD STILL MATTER? Anything whose entire meaning evaporates under retry is re-anchored in record... or rejected" (`Q144.P5`). A flip back to act 1 to change a plant IS a retry. This is the single most important law for this page.
- Required replays are a hard sell unless each pass stands alone (`Q022.X2`).
- Branches too similar make the fold "POINTLESS + HOLLOW" (`Q067.W9`, `Q067.X1`): if the landing barely differs, the flip has nothing to show.
- The front-loaded reactivity budget runs out: "the shipped game reads back the big axes loudly and the fine grain thinly" (`Q145.X1`); a solo team must pick "fewer origins, deeper callbacks" (`Q042.X2`).

THE FINDING: the flip turns the Hillock inside out. The hidden load cannot stay hidden once the player can walk into the future and look. What can stay is the Hillock's WEIGHT, if the past is additive. The library's best single port for this came from a forum post: void the devil's contract, walk out, then give him the thing anyway later, freely; "Same outcome. Completely different act" (`Q126.P35`), and "Gen 3 can read that the grandmother voided the deal and then honored it on her own terms, and that record is worth more than the water was" (`Q126.P36`). A second deed on top of the first, both kept in the record, is what survives `Q144.P5`'s retry question.

### 7. THE GRANDPARENT'S PROMISE, THE HEIR'S BILL

- "Adjudication-class fold entries... persist on NAMED NPCs across generations, and at least some repayments must skip a generation: the person the grandmother spared answers the grandchild's need" (`Q145.P5`); mercy planted blind pays off named (`Q145.W7`).
- The will is authored by living, not by a menu; the death screen only READS what the life wrote (`Q144.W1`). "Knowledge is the estate's gold standard": what crosses best is what was learned (`Q144.W2`), and the estate rule makes that a compile gate: heritable classes are knowledge, bond-states and record entries; anything that can be ground is refused (`Q144.P2`).
- "The refusals make the line real": unbanked secrets burn, stranded arcs stay stranded (`Q144.W3`). A generational game earns its weight by what it will NOT carry.
- The inheritance is a list of children: a father's paper with eight names and nobody can say why they are on it (`Q113.W1`, `Q113.W2`), and the flaw: "the list is never explained in time... our fold needs the dynasty" to know why (`Q113.X5`).
- The last goal is someone else's future: he spends his death buying the next generation a chance and hands over his hat (`Q073.W7`).
- You miss the inheritance by hours and the town blames you for it (`Q098.W1`); the inheritance is the only thing that makes him mean anything (`Q098.W2`).
- The heir inherits hatred with no reason given, and it reads as arbitrary; the law is to make the handoff's difficulty legible (`Q046.X5`).
- The devil's offer that stays refused across generations gets BETTER terms each generation, until "the refusal is no longer a moral position, it's an expensive habit somebody's grandmother started" (`Q126.P7`). A flag crossing a generation needs a named test.
- A dead quest-giver wants a delivery, not a deed (`Q147.W1`), ported as the grief relay (`Q147.P1`).

### 8. A VERB EARNED IN ONE AGE, USED IN ANOTHER

- "Remembering is the level-up": progression is the theme (`Q082.W8`).
- Help arrives as OBJECTS, not messages: "Death Stranding leaves you AN ACTUAL LADDER" (`Q089.W7`); roads crowdfunded by strangers, driven by later waves (`Q089.W4`); paths smooth as people walk them (`Q089.W6`).
- The community invented the inheritance: a journal you write before you die, which your next survivor finds (`Q088.W9`).
- Knowledge with provenance outclasses every fetchable object: the song works because of WHO taught it (`Q147.W3`), ported as an item class whose effect gates on its source (`Q147.P5`).
- New Game Plus can trivialize: carrying max power makes the replay easy; decide whether the carry is for STORY or CHALLENGE (`Q050.X4`).
- The boss ladder's own verbs already reach across ages: THE SCHOOL grants "teach, so an heir starts with what you knew"; THE CREDITOR grants "call a debt in: somebody arrives and does it"; THE PRESS grants "print, so a rule becomes a thing that exists" (records/BOHEMIA_THE_BOSS_LADDER_v7_8_7_26.md, rows 50, 51, 37).

THE FINDING: the three acts law already rules the direction. "The past rewrites the future's world, never the future character's hands" (section 2c). So a verb travels FORWARD as a family possession (the grandmother's mould, the father's workshop) and BACKWARD only as the player's knowledge (`Q011.P5`, `Q147.W10`). And the most interesting case in the library for us is not the carry itself but the same verb meeting a different law: a tap drawn in the anarchy decade is survival; the same draw in the reclaimed act is theft from a meter.

### 9. SATISFYING VERSUS CONFUSING ON A PHONE

Confusing, per the library:
- The guide problem: three days of exact hours across a dozen places, "blind completion is near-mythical" (`Q128.X1`); silent failure means players "lose the chain without learning they lost it", and the law is "one diegetic tell per broken link" (`Q128.X2`, ported in `Q128.P3`).
- Missable at scale: cryptic works in small, enfolded spaces; at open-world scale it needs more in-world signposting (`Q023.X1`). The suite hides its own front door (`Q147.X1`).
- The theme of inheritance "almost entirely optional... most players finish never knowing the game is ABOUT birth" (`Q092.X5`). Our fold's theme cannot live in missable side content.
- The invisible pre-count: "political capital needs a LEDGER VIEW" (`Q136.X1`).
- The narrator who pre-solves the choice (`Q126.X3`, and the Bohemia law in `Q126.P39`: the narration never grades a choice).

Satisfying, per the library:
- The payoff of comprehension: the finale lands because you UNDERSTAND (`Q018.W9`).
- The earned scene gated by mastery lands like an achievement and a story beat (`Q011.W10`), with the caveat that timed windows be "GENEROUS + legible" (`Q011.P12`).
- Small choices teach big ones: front-load a low-stakes lesson that the world watches (`Q050.W4`, `Q050.P4`).
- Parameterized generation from input variables (`Q056.W9`): a derived city can show a landing as a rule, not a scene.

For a phone specifically: one thumb, a small screen, short sessions, no hover (blind spot 2). The flip is a city-view act on the cracked phone (three acts law 9b). So the landing must be readable at the SCALE the flip lands on: the Battle Brothers map first (a place visibly different), then the settlement screen (a person who speaks past tense). A landing that only exists as a line in a menu is invisible here.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS

1. ONE PLANT, ONE LANDING, DECLARED. Every across-acts quest declares, in its data: PLANT (act, place, person, thing), LANDING (act, place, visible wound, the person who speaks), and a POINTER CHAIN of at most two hops from the landing back to the plant (`Q147.P2`, `Q116.X2`). No declaration, no ship. Testable: a checker lists every across-acts entry and refuses one without all three fields.
2. DISPLACED, NOT JUST DELAYED. Plant and landing are in different acts AND different places (at least one district apart on the map) AND the landing is spoken by a person who was not the plant's giver (`Q004.W5`, `Q001.W5`). Testable from the declaration.
3. THE LANDING IS A PERSON, A PLACE OR A THING IN THE DERIVED CITY. Never a notice, a card, a pop-up or a stat. The derive rule that draws act 3 carries a row: if ledger row X, then block Y renders Z and person P has line L (`Q147.W7`, `Q144.W7`, `Q056.W9`). Testable by the three acts law's own gate (2d): change the act 1 row, flip, assert the act 3 block and line changed.
4. THE PAST IS ADDED TO, NEVER UNDONE. A finished act 1 deed is never removed from the ledger by a flip. A player who has seen the landing may return to act 1 and do a SECOND, answering deed (a repair, a payment, a confession to a face), which re-derives the future; both deeds stay in the record and a person in act 3 can name both (`Q126.P35`, `Q126.P36`, `Q004.W8`, `Q144.P5`). Testable: after an answering deed, the act 1 ledger has two rows, not one edited row. (folded 10/1 from QR-U and QR-X: the new shelf argues against a second choice, since understanding is not undoing (`Q187.W6`) and the past is a walk, never a rewrite (`Q194.W2`); the three-acts law is Paolo's, so the rule stays, tightened: the first deed stays in the record WITH ITS LANDING, because the deed stays real (`Q186.W5`); the answering deed is the shape of `Q162.W3`, the power to undo arriving later and forcing the choice AGAIN with the first choice's damage in view; and it changes the walker more than the walk (`Q194.W5`). Added test: the act 3 person who names both deeds names the first one first.)
5. THE ANSWER COSTS, AND IT MOVES THE COST, IT DOES NOT ERASE IT. The answering deed costs batteries, time or standing, and the re-derived act 3 shows a different wound somewhere else (`Q004.W3`, `Q001.W11`). If the second deed makes the landing clean, it is a bug. Testable: every answering deed's derive changes at least two blocks, one better and one worse. (folded 10/1 from QR-U and QR-X: the answer MOVES the wound and never erases it (`Q187.W6`, `Q194.W2`); an answering deed that costs nothing is a bug of the same class, so its price is stated before it is done, in batteries, map days, a fight or standing (QR-X fixed QD-D06 on exactly this: two map days and a fight); and the record keeps the first deed's row beside it (`Q186.W5`).)
6. THE CONTRACT RULE HOLDS ACROSS AGES. An act 1 plant that is a CONTRACT follows the contract law: declining is free and leaves nothing in any act; taking it and finishing it is the deed; taking it and dropping it is its own deed. A declined plant has no landing at all (rule of 9/27, and `Q126.W3`: the refusal that costs nothing).
7. TELL THE TRUTH ABOUT UNCERTAINTY, NEVER LABEL THE HINGE. At the plant, a person says plainly that nobody knows how it goes (`Q004.N1`, `Q077.W9`). No preview, no tooltip, no narrator grading (`Q126.X3`). But the world holds one clue a listener can catch (`Q001.W8`, `Q032.W4`).
8. THE OBVIOUS-GOOD TRAP IS RATIONED. At most one in six across-acts quests uses it (`Q004.P2`, `Q149.X2`).
9. NO REWARD DIFFERENCE ON THE MORAL FORK. Batteries paid at the plant are the same on each branch; the branches differ only in who lives and what the city becomes (`Q004.W4`, `Q004.P3`).
10. THE GRANDPARENT'S PROMISE IS A NAMED PERSON OR A THING THAT TESTIFIES. A promise crossing acts is carried by a named NPC with memory who has a past-tense line for every earlier family member they met (`Q145.P5`, `Q144.P3`), or by a thing that is never sold and that people in the later act react to (`Q128.P5`, `Q131.X10`, `Q119.W7`). Batteries themselves never cross as the payoff: only knowledge, bonds and record (`Q144.P2`).
11. SOME REPAYMENTS SKIP A GENERATION. Of the grandparent-to-heir quests, at least one in three lands in act 3 and says nothing in act 2 (`Q145.P5`).
12. A VERB TRAVELS FORWARD IN HANDS, BACKWARD AS KNOWLEDGE. A boss verb earned in act 1 is the family's in acts 2 and 3; a verb earned in act 3 is never usable by the act 1 character, but what the player learned there (where a door is, whose name opens it) is (`Q011.P5`, `Q147.W10`, three acts law 2c). The same verb is judged by the law of the act it is used in (see QD-D03).
13. CARRY IS FOR STORY, NOT POWER. An inherited verb does not make the later act's fights easier; it opens a place or a person (`Q050.X4`, `Q089.W7`).
14. FEW AND DEEP. At most three across-acts plants are live in one district at a time, and each landing differs enough to be seen from the map scale (`Q042.X2`, `Q145.X1`, `Q067.X1`).
15. ONE TELL PER LANDING, READABLE FROM THE MAP. The derived act 3 map shows the wound at map scale (a dark block, a dry channel, a lit tower) before the street shows the person (`Q128.P3`, `Q147.W7`). The phone's feed may carry one rumour post pointing at it, never an instruction.
16. NEVER IN THE FIRST SIXTY SECONDS. No landing speaks in a player's first minute of any act, and the first hop into act 3, once it unlocks, shows the ruin plus what the world did, not a scripted callback (three acts law 9a, second votes).
17. NO CONFESSION VERB AT THE LANDING, BUT A COSTLY SHOULDER VERB. The person in act 3 does not know it was your family unless the record says someone told them (`Q004.N3`); the heir cannot buy relief with a line (`Q126.P24` was the comfort-verb law; this is its other side). (folded 10/1 from QR-U and QR-X, as amended:) the new shelf adds the other side of the ban. The heir is answerable for a self he cannot remember (`Q215.W1`), owning a crime without the memory of it is the truest accountability (`Q215.W9`), and the victim is owed regardless (`Q215.W2`). So the heir MAY choose to SHOULDER an ancestor's deed he learned of, and it must cost MORE than lying or staying silent, in batteries, standing or a person's trust, or it is only a third dialogue option (`Q215.X3`). Nobody makes him; nobody thanks him for free; silence stays a full ending. Test: every shoulder option has a stated price greater than the silence branch's, and no line in the game asks him to shoulder. (QR-X checked QD-T09, QD-B08 and QD-K09 as already passing, and fixed QD-T07, whose confession had paid the same as silence.)
18. THE FLAG ACROSS AN ACT BOUNDARY HAS A NAMED TEST. Any ledger row read by a later act's derive ships with a checker case (`Q126.P7`, the three acts law 2d).

19. THE GRIEVANCE IS WITNESSED IN ONE ERA AND FORGIVEN IN ANOTHER. (folded 10/9 from QR-U) A prior generation's atrocity and its
    refusal to forgive, seen in play, so a later forgiveness carries a history the player lived (`Q155.P3`); a crime
    long dead and still killing (`Q155.W9`); the flaw is grief told, not felt (`Q155.X2`). Test: the forgiving scene in

## WHAT TO AVOID (the flaws)

- THE GOTCHA: a consequence that could not be foreseen (`Q086.X7`), an invisible counter (`Q118.X1`), a spare that detonates without warning (`Q149.X2`).
- THE COMBINATION LOCK ACROSS AGES: the best scene gated on flags set in another act the player never knew were keys (`Q096.X1`, `Q102.X1`, `Q125.X6`, `Q063.X5`, `Q070.X1`).
- THE PROMISSORY NOTE THAT NEVER CASHES: a plant whose landing is a line of dialogue (`Q121.X1`, `Q145.X1`, `Q069.X1`: a choice emotionally huge and mechanically "minor differences").
- THE RESET THAT CHEAPENS: letting a flip unmake a deed (`Q147.X3`, `Q128.X10`, `Q144.P5`).
- THE FREE SHOULDER: owning an ancestor's deed at no more cost than silence (`Q215.X3`; folded 10/1 from QR-U, rule 17).
- THE WORLD WITH NO MEMORY ORGAN: aftermath is a zero, nobody finds out (`Q130.X10`, `Q131.X2`, `Q134.X6`).
- THE BRANCHES TOO SIMILAR: a landing that looks the same either way (`Q067.X1`, `Q152.X2`).
- THE HIDDEN FRONT DOOR: a plant nobody can find (`Q147.X1`, `Q023.X1`, `Q128.X1`).
- THE THEME IN THE OPTIONAL: if the across-ages quests are all side content, most players never learn the game is about a family (`Q092.X5`).
- THE UNEARNED ANCESTRAL GUILT: the founder's ledger starts empty; nothing is inherited that the player did not do (`Q145.X2`, `Q046.X5`).

## OPEN (what the library could not answer)

1. THE PLAY GAP OF ZERO. No studied game lets the player see the landing before planting. The Hillock loophole (`Q004.W7`) is the closest cousin and it is a curiosity, not a structure. Whether seeing a landing FIRST makes the plant feel like a chore (go back and fetch the good ending) is untested. QD-D04 is our attempt; DYNASTY should watch it in play.
2. HOW MANY ANSWERING DEEDS. Rule 4 allows a second deed. Is a third allowed? The library has no model of a past you keep adding to. Default proposed: one answering deed per plant; after that the landing is final.
3. WHO IN ACT 3 KNOWS. The Amalgamation is custodial, records everything recorded, and is blind to the unrecorded. A landing whose cause was recorded may be "known" to the Network in act 3 while unknown to people. The library has resemblance-trust (`Q128.X8`) and the dead worn as a leash (`Q080.W4`) but nothing on a machine that remembers your grandmother better than your family does. QD-D05 is a first attempt.
4. THE DERIVE BUDGET. How many ledger rows the act 3 derive can read on a phone before it is slow is PLUMBER's measure, not the library's.
5. DECLINING ACROSS AGES. Rule 6 says a declined plant leaves nothing. A world that derives the future might still show what the world did without you (the contract went to somebody else). Is that "remembering a no"? Proposed default: the world may show another company did the job; no person may say you refused.
