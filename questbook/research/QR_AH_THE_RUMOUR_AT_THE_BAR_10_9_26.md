# QR-AH: THE RUMOUR AT THE BAR. HOW YOU HEAR WHERE THE GOD GEAR IS

You buy the bartender a drink, and she tells you something she heard: a vault under a drowned casino, a gun nobody came back for, a crew moving tonight. Some of what she says is true, some is old news, and some is a lie, and the map remembers all of it as a pin that slowly fades.

QUESTION: row [the rumour at the bar] (QUESTS, Paolo 10/9 'finding good equipment throughout the settlements'), matched to WORLD [where the god gear is] (the six special places, discovered by rumour at the bar) and PEOPLE [the keepers speak] (every keeper has a mouth and one rumour). RUN TWO [the bar] builds the screen; this page is what the mouth says and why.

STATUS: draft:true, research only, nothing built (rule 35). Every rumour line below is an attempt; place names are WORLD's and Paolo's to change; every number of ours is a SHAPE and TUNING owns the value (rule 36). Folklore monsters are machines of the robotics plant or men, real animals stay lab-made (rule 81).

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Battle Brothers' tavern is a slot machine with a face: one rumour free per visit, up to four more at 20 crowns each times the town's price, and every paid drink buys +0.1 relations; the roll is 20% a contract rumour, 20% a location rumour, 20% a named-item rumour, 40% flavour, and anything unavailable falls back to flavour (source: wiki dump, unverified, PAGE Tavern Rumors). A location rumour reveals a real place but "there is no guarantee of treasure, even if the rumor said so"; a named-item rumour only reveals an item that already exists, within 30 tiles of a 140-tile map. We keep the bones (pay for talk, talk reveals real places, the item exists before you hear of it) and change four things: the rumour comes out of a KEEPER's mouth with a portrait, never a text box (A QUEST IS PEOPLE PLACES AND THINGS, 9/20); a rumour is TRUE, STALE or a LIE, and the face tells you which if you know how to read it (`Q039.P5`, `Q054.P7`); the pin on the map AGES so old talk visibly rots (`Q018.P4`); and a rumour can point down the same road as a contract you already hold (QR-AE), so the treasure hunt rides a paid trip instead of being an errand of its own (`Q079.P9`, `Q071.X3`).

## HOW BATTLE BROTHERS DOES IT

1. THE TAVERN is a building in the settlement screen. Buy a round for the men (morale up, risk of drunk then hangover; price scales with crew size). Rumours: "Paying drinks for patrons can be repeated 4 times a visit." Source: wiki dump, unverified (PAGE Settlement buildings, Tavern); also source: Grok, unverified (GROK_21_WIKI_ROUND_01_2026_09_30.md: 'Tavern. Drinks for mood... Rumors, pay for drinks up to 4 times a visit').
2. THE COST: 1 free per visit; each extra is 20 crowns x the settlement's buying multiplier, +0.1 relations each. At our 10 crowns to a battery (rule 75d) that is 2 batteries a rumour at a fair town. The Minstrel follower makes useful rumours likelier. Source: wiki dump, unverified (PAGE Tavern Rumors).
3. THE ODDS: 20 / 20 / 20 / 40 as above. No rumour is ever a lie in code: the place is real, the reward may be smaller than said. Truth lives in the place, not in the line. Source: wiki dump, unverified (PAGE Tavern Rumors, Location Rumors).
4. NAMED ITEM RUMOURS name the item and point at the party or camp that holds it ("some big green oafs north of here are toting around an incredible shield"), with a direction and a terrain word, never coordinates. The rumour does not create the item. Source: wiki dump, unverified (PAGE Tavern Rumors, Named Item Rumors).
5. THE NAMED-ITEM RULE: a named weapon always rolls durability x0.9 to 1.4 plus two more random stats (damage x1.1 to 1.3, armour damage, head chance, fatigue); named armour durability x1.1 to 1.25 and a lighter fatigue penalty; legendary pieces are fixed, not rolled. Named gear lives in camps and on champions, so the item has a guard. Source: Grok, unverified (GROK_29_WIKI_BULK_5_2026_09_30.md); wiki dump, unverified (PAGE Named Weapons). Legendary camps stay fixed in strength while ordinary camps grow with days (source: Grok, unverified, GROK_111_UNDERBELLY_SPAWN_2026_10_04.md).
6. THE LEGENDARY LOCATIONS (19 on the wiki page, most from the Beasts and Exploration DLC; Abandoned Village and Artifact Reliquary from 1.5.1): Abandoned Village, Ancient Spire, Ancient Statue, Ancient Temple, Artifact Reliquary, Black Monolith, Curious Ship Wreck, Grotesque Tree, Hunting Ground, Icy Cave, Rul'gazhix, Stone Pillars, Sunken Library, The Ancient City, The Fallen Star, The Oracle, Unhold Graveyard, Watermill, Witch Hut. Source: wiki dump, unverified (PAGE Legendary locations). HOW THEY ARE FOUND: walking near them (the fog lifts), a location rumour (reveals with no range limit), an event chain with footprints (the Icy Cave via the Ijirok events), or a visiting hand (the Spire and Statue reveal the map around them). The near ones are safe and give information; the far ones are a hard fight then a fixed reward (the Monolith's ancient legion, the Sunken Library's rappel into the dark, the Reliquary's squatters, the Watermill's kraken). Holy sites (Fallen Star, Oracle) do not count as legendary for ambitions. Source: wiki dump, unverified (PAGES Legendary locations, Black Monolith, Sunken Library, Icy Cave, Artifact Reliquary, The Fallen Star, The Oracle, Watermill).

What the library says about this shape: a rumour web works when it is a known-versus-implied map with always one thread to pull and no waypoint (`Q018.W3`); the notebook should be the thing the player reconstructs (`Q037.P3`); every lead must be fairly findable, clue density is everything (`Q037.P9`, `Q038.P8`, `Q019.P8`). Small spaces make marker-less discovery honest (`Q023.P2`), and places can tell their own story with props (`Q023.P4`, `Q078.W10`). The flaw to avoid: a legend that outruns the content (`Q085.P9`), and a reward you out-level in five levels (`Q070.X2`).

## OUR VERSION

### THE BAR (a building in the settlement screen, RUN TWO [the bar])
Tap the bar. The keeper's head (PORTRAIT) fills the left, her squiggle voice plays (rule 45), her line reads under it. Three buttons: BUY THE ROOM A ROUND (morale, rule 76's BB tavern), BUY HER A DRINK (a rumour), LEAVE. Night bars are loud, day bars are dead; the murmur is SOUNDS [the settlement's sounds]. Other keepers (the smith, the armourer, the board's man, the clinic, the barber) carry ONE rumour each, about their own trade (PEOPLE [the keepers speak]); the bar keeper is the one who sells many.

### WHO SAYS IT
Always a face with a name and a want (`Q022.P4`, `Q013.P2`). Four kinds of mouth, each with its own tell:
- THE KEEPER: her trade is talk. Mostly true, sometimes stale. She never lies to a regular (relations high).
- A DRUNK AT THE END OF THE BAR: cheap, loud, often stale, sometimes the best tip in the valley.
- A STRANGER IN RUNWAY CLOTHES (a faction's runner): never free, sometimes a lie that walks you into his crew's ambush (`Q020.P5`).
- A KEEPER OF ANOTHER TRADE: the smith knows who bought rounds, the clinic knows who came back burned. Narrow and reliable.
The player's build reads the face: a high-resolve heir or a follower like the Minstrel sees the lie tells (`Q039.P5`, `Q026.P7`). A lie is legible on replay, never a gotcha (`Q019.P8`, `Q058.X3`).

### WHAT IT COSTS
- One free line per visit, always flavour or a contract rumour (the room's mood, what a faction is about to do; rule 80c).
- A drink for the keeper: 2 batteries at a fair town x the town's price (BB's 20 crowns, rule 75d), or 1 WATER where the town is thirsty (rule 47). Up to four a visit, as BB. Each one +0.1 with the town.
- A FAVOUR: the best rumour (a god-gear lead) is not for sale. She wants something carried, someone found, a debt called in. The favour is a small contract on the board (QR-AF's CARRY or FIND), and the rumour is the pay (`Q048.P8`).
Never a loot box, never a paid reroll (`Q048.X4`).

### TRUE, STALE OR A LIE (a SHAPE; TUNING owns the numbers)
| | the keeper | a drunk | a stranger | another trade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TRUE (the place is real, the thing is there) | 70% | 40% | 40% | 80% |
| STALE (real, but a crew got there first; the guard or the gear is gone) | 25% | 50% | 20% | 20% |
| A LIE (a real place, the wrong thing: a trap, an ambush, a rival's errand) | 5% | 10% | 40% | 0% |
BB's rule stands under it: the PLACE is always real, only what waits there changes. A lie never sends you to an empty desert; it sends you to a real place with the wrong party in it (`Q054.P7`, `Q054.P9`). Relations raise TRUE; a raided town's bar runs STALE (rule 41, the future goes both ways). God gear is never fully a lie: the ladder of real places is fixed (`Q002.P2`, `Q015.P8`).

### WHAT IT MARKS ON THE MAP (a pin that ages)
A rumour drops a pin in the city view, in the keeper's own words: a direction and a landmark ("east of the dead fuel depot, past the overpass"), never a dot on the exact cell, so the last block is found by eye (`Q017.W9`, `Q023.P2`). The pin has an AGE: crisp the first week, then its colour drains and the edge frays (analog horror: a VHS freeze frame going to snow, rule 59 of DIRECTION), and after about 20 days a STALE pin is just a smudge. A true god-gear pin never vanishes, it only fades; a contract pin dies with the contract. The cracked phone (rule 51) lists your pins with the face that gave each one, so the notebook is a row of mouths, not a quest log (`Q037.P3`, `Q021.P7`).

### THE GOD GEAR AND WHERE IT WAITS (WORLD [where the god gear is])
Named gear in our skin = BB's rule: a real weapon or armour row from weapons.json / armor.json with durability and two stats rolled up, held by a guard party; LEGENDARY gear sits in a legendary place, fixed, guarded by a party sized by the math (records/target/bb/party_math.json) that does not grow with days, as BB's legendary camps. Discovered by: walking near it, a rumour, or an event's tracks. Guards per rule 81: machines of the robotics plant, men, or lab-made animals.

### TWELVE DRAFT RUMOURS (draft:true; the keeper speaks Spanglish where she is that kind; the player never does)
1. THE ARSENAL OFF THE STRIP (keeper, TRUE-weighted): "Mira, the gun shop one block off the Strip, the one with the steel door. Nobody opened it since the dollar died. Two guys tried. Una vez." Points at a fixed fight and a named rifle.
2. THE SUNK CASINO VAULT (drunk): "The cage under the old casino, where they kept the chips. It flooded. There's a cart of something down there, and something pumping the water out at night." The guard is the storm drain's pump beast (rule 81's Kraken); BB's Watermill and Sunken Library in our skin.
3. HOOVER'S TUNNELS (another trade, the clinic): "Three came back from the dam tunnels with burns on their hands like from a wire. One had a helmet I never saw before. He died with it on." A legendary helmet in the inspection galleries, a fixed party of men.
4. THE ROBOTICS PLANT BY HENDERSON AIRPORT (stranger, LIE-weighted): "The loaders at the plant stopped walking. Free parts for whoever gets there first." Some days true; on a lie the loaders (the Unhold) are walking and his crew waits at the gate. BB's Black Monolith: the hard far fight.
5. THE LAB VAULT AT TULE SPRINGS (keeper): "The lab by the fossil beds kept a cold room. The door still has power. Whatever they grew is out, pero the room is not empty." Lab-made animals in the yard, a fixed armour piece inside (rule 42).
6. THE GYPSUM CAVE CACHE (drunk, STALE-weighted): "Somebody stashed rounds in Gypsum Cave, past the sloth bones. Pfff, that was months ago." Usually picked over; the cave stays a place.
7. THE TWO DEAD FUEL DEPOTS (board's man): "One depot still has a tank that sloshes. The Teamsters say it's theirs. Everybody says it's theirs." Reward is a resource stock, guards are a faction (FACTIONS owns who).
8. THE GRANARY (keeper, after a raid nearby): "The granary crew stopped coming to drink. Either they got rich or they got dead." A fight and a food stock; a home-base candidate (rule 43).
9. THE DATA FORTRESS (stranger): "They still pay in batteries at the server farm. You bring them a voice on a tape, they give you a coat you can't cut." The Alp and the Geist as the network's broadcast in a head (rule 81); a legendary coat.
10. THE LIBRARY (another trade, the barber): "A kid swept hair for me, read everything. Said the library basement has a map with the plant's doors on it." Reveals the robotics plant and a second pin (BB's Ancient Spire: a place that reveals places).
11. THE CHAMPION ON THE ROAD (drunk, a named-item rumour, BB's 'biggest man alive'): "Saw a man on the 95 carrying a hammer with a name scratched in it. Big como un refri. He was going south." A roaming party with a named weapon; the pin moves and ages fast.
12. THE WITCH OF THE RAIL YARD (keeper): "The lady in the rail yard runs the cutting rigs like dogs. She'll trade, if you ask nice. If you don't, the trees fall on you." The Hexe as a woman who runs the Schrat machines; BB's Witch Hut, a talk first and a fight if you choose.

### A RUMOUR PAIRS WITH A CONTRACT ON THE SAME ROAD (QR-AE)
The keeper reads your board: if you hold a contract going east, she is likelier to sell a rumour pinned within a day's detour of that road (BB's 30-tile named-item range, applied to the road not the town). The phone draws both pins with the road between them, so the player sees one trip, two pays (QR-AE's 'a caravan quest takes you to a city and near that city you can kill something', rule 51). The detour costs days against the contract's deadline; that trade is the decision (`Q026.P3`, `Q052.W4`). Never forced: a rumour is never REQUIRED to finish a contract (`Q005.P10`). If the contract fails, the pin stays (`Q236.W8`: the world moves, the lead does not care).

### WHAT WE DO DIFFERENTLY FROM BATTLE BROTHERS
1. A face and a mouth, not a text box; four mouths with four reliabilities, read by your heir's build (`Q014.P2`, `Q083.P4`).
2. A rumour can be a LIE that sends you into an ambush; BB's lines are never false in code. Capped low and legible (`Q058.X3`).
3. The pin AGES visibly; BB's revealed places just stay lit.
4. The best lead costs a FAVOUR, not crowns, and the favour is a real small contract.
5. The bar knows your road and sells along it (QR-AE).
6. A rumour about a faction is a rumour about a house you care about (rule 80c): the keeper tells you what a house is about to do before it does it (`Q081.P6`, `Q083.P2`).
7. Legendary gear is placed and fixed, never rolled out of a chest, so a reward is never out-levelled in five levels (`Q070.X2`, `Q086.X7`).

## THE FLAWS WE WILL NOT BUILD
- Rumours that all say 'a treasure' and pay junk: the legend outruns the place (`Q085.P9`, `Q085.X2`).
- A fetch chain hidden as a rumour chain, three bars to learn one thing (`Q071.X3`, `Q079.P9`).
- An AI that magically knows where everything is (`Q061.X4`): the bar knows what was SEEN, with the age it was seen.
- A lie that is a cheap trick (`Q036.P5`, `Q058.X3`): every lie has a tell and a reason (the stranger's crew).
- After a hard hit, the bar is where relief comes from: a cheap true tip (`Q044.W7`, `Q041.P4`).
- A rumour that turns out to be a PERSON, not a thing, is allowed once an act and is the best kind (`Q016.P1`, `Q019.P3`, `Q088.P2`, `Q079.P2`).
