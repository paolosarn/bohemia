# QR-A: HOW DO THE BEST QUESTS OFFER A JOB, TAKE A NO, HOLD A YES, AND PRICE A QUIT?

QUESTION: rows [contracts at your pace] and QUESTS question (h). How do the 152 studied quests handle the OFFER,
the REFUSAL, the ACCEPTANCE and the ABANDONMENT of a job? What do the best ones do when you say NO? Then: the rule
the builders follow for Bohemia's shape (one main quest runs; side quests are contracts a person in a place offers;
take it or leave it; once taken you finish it; DECLINING IS FREE; only a contract taken and dropped is a deed), the
collision in blind spot 8 (a world that remembers everything vs declining is free), and what "once taken you finish
it" means in practice.

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The library is close to unanimous: the strongest "no" in the 152 is a no that costs nothing, closes nothing, and is
never mentioned again (`Q126.W3`, `Q126.N13`, `Q131.N1`, `Q134.W3`, `Q122.N1`), and the most hated refusals are the
ones the game punishes, stubs out, or removes (`Q084.X5`, `Q075.X4`, `Q103.X1`, `Q106.X6`, `Q139.X2`). The best
offers are made by a person who states clean terms, who needs you for a reason you can see, and who lets you say
"No." at every single node of the conversation (`Q126.N3`, `Q126.N5`, `Q126.N7`, `Q108.W1`). The best contracts fix
the fee BEFORE the truth is known (`Q148.W1`), are exactly what they say most of the time (`Q148.X3`, `Q148.P4`),
and run whole from notice to payment in one sitting (`Q148.W10`). Once a job is taken, the best designs keep the
exit OPEN (`Q040.W2`, `Q140.W8`, `Q151.W7`) because an exit that is always there is what makes finishing a choice,
and they make the exit a DEED with a real, proportionate price (`Q040.W10`, `Q087.W9`, `Q094.W4`). The library also
answers blind spot 8 directly: several ports ask the ledger to RECORD refusals (`Q131.P3`, `Q134.P3`, `Q126.P7`),
and Paolo's rule overrides them for contracts. The world remembers what HAPPENED. A declined offer is not a thing
that happened; it is a conversation that did not become a job. So the rule is: the ledgers never write a decline;
they write a taking, a finishing, a failing and a dropping, and nothing else.

## THE FINDINGS

### 1. THE OFFER: A PERSON, CLEAN TERMS, A VISIBLE NEED

- **The job comes through a person who is already there, at work.** `Q108.W1`: the quest-giver in Papers, Please is
  the guard who has stood outside your booth for 24 days; the favor comes from a coworker, not a quest marker.
  `Q013.W6` and `Q114.W2`: Yakuza's substories start by WALKING PAST someone, never from a menu, and are "not on the
  map". This is our rule "a quest is people, places and things, never a card" said by the library. Note the one
  translation we must make: `Q148.W2` and `Q148.P1` praise the notice BOARD as "a dignified front door", "a job
  system that never begs". What we keep from it is the DIGNITY and the NEVER BEGS; the board itself is a card and is
  banned for us. The person is our board.
- **State the terms clean, once, with no subclause.** `Q126.N5`: "He states them clean. No subclause. No trick. The
  contract says what he said." with `"No." [gate: none]` offered on the same node as `"Done."`. `Q126.N3`: even the
  first node of the pitch has a plain "No." as an option. The lesson for a phone: the refusal option is never a
  hidden verb and never needs a stat.
- **Show the need.** `Q126.N7` calls OFFER_NEED "the node the quest is built on": a devil with a private hell is
  asking a mortal for a favor because he cannot do it himself. A client who explains WHY they cannot do the job
  themselves (too old, no gun, no batteries, the wrong faction colour on their coat) makes both answers legible.
- **The fee is agreed before the truth.** `Q148.W1`: "Haggling at maximum ignorance separates money from morality
  forever after." `Q148.P1` makes it a schema rule: no fee renegotiation node after stage 2. For us: the batteries
  are counted on the table at the offer, and they do not change when the job turns ugly. That is also why a decline
  can be free: the player said no to a PRICE, not to a person.
- **The offer must be honest about the danger.** `Q060.W1` (Into the Breach): perfect information makes "every death
  your own fault". A contract whose danger is stated in the client's mouth ("there are six of them and they have a
  truck") lets the player say "too strong for me" with no shame, because the game told them. `Q051.W6` (Gothic): no
  level-scaling, "the world self-gates by power and you grow INTO it", so jobs that are too strong for you EXIST and
  are meant to be walked past. `Q007.W2` and `Q040.W9`: the hardest content is opt-in and signposted.
- **The offer must be good on its own numbers.** `Q126.P6`: "Bohemia's offer must be genuinely the better deal on
  the numbers ... If the player is only refusing because the wiki says the heist has better loot, the quest is a
  difficulty setting." A free no only means something when the yes is worth having.
- **Aid has a grammar.** `Q113.N1`: the apprentice refuses medicine as charity and accepts the SAME vial as pay for
  a day's work. Some clients can only be helped as employers. This is how a contract can carry kindness without the
  game preaching it, and it is why contracts are the right container for "kindness is glimpses".

### 2. THE REFUSAL: WHAT THE BEST ONES DO WHEN YOU SAY NO

- **The best "no" in the library costs nothing and forecloses nothing.** `Q126.W3`: "No ambush, no curse, no closed
  door ... A punished 'no' is a trap you escaped. An unpunished 'no' is a temptation that lives in the save file."
  `Q126.N12`: `WRITES: flag:refused LOCKS: NOTHING.` `Q126.N13`: "A refusal that costs nothing and forecloses
  nothing is rarer than any branch in this file. Most games punish 'no' to make the 'yes' feel weighty. Larian made
  'no' free, and the offer more tempting for it."
- **The quest simply waits.** `Q131.N1`: "And if I refuse?" leads to B_INDIFFERENT: "the quest simply waits,
  forever ... refusal is free". `Q134.W3`: "No timer, no escalation, no punishment for refusal." `Q127.N1`: O'Dimm
  answers "And if I refuse?" with no threat at all.
- **The exit at the door is respected totally.** `Q122.N1`: in Sinnerman, letting the family have him is "EXIT-AT-
  THE-DOOR -> the chain ends before it starts; the quest respects the refusal totally", and the design accepts that
  most players never see the rest.
- **Punished, stubbed or missing refusals are the library's most repeated flaw.** `Q084.X5`: refusing is "punished
  with deletion", it "reads as a losing button rather than a path". `Q075.X4`: the refusal path is "an OFF switch".
  `Q103.X1`: "THERE IS NO REFUSAL." `Q078.P7`: without a real resistance path "the design (not the villain) is what's
  trapping the player". `Q106.X6`: refuse to drink and you lose the priest, the sermon and the best scene in the act:
  "the quest punishes abstinence". `Q139.X2`: never accuse the player of an act the mechanism made.
- **Guilt and nagging are flaws, not flavour.** `Q043.X1`: a benched companion who cries turns management into
  "guilt-laden busywork". `Q043.X2`: reputation hooks that make a subtle character "bitch and moan constantly" break
  character. The same holds for a client who sulks when you pass on low pay: that is the game guilting the player,
  which Paolo banned.
- **The "no" that is NOT free lives elsewhere, and that is correct.** `Q139.P2` (the priced refusal) and `Q040.W10`
  (early exits with real lockouts) are about refusals INSIDE something already taken or already forced on you (an
  overwhelming-force scene, a bail from a chain you started). They are not about turning down an offer. This split
  is exactly Paolo's: declining an offer is free; dropping what you took is a deed.

### 3. THE COLLISION (BLIND SPOT 8): A WORLD THAT REMEMBERS vs A NO THAT IS FORGOTTEN

The library argues BOTH sides, which is why the collision is real:

- **For remembering:** `Q086.W8`: "reputation is a memory, not a bar ... strangers cite YOUR SPECIFIC DEEDS by
  name". `Q131.X7` calls it a flaw that "no NPC, shrine state, or line ever registers that you looked at the price and
  walked", and `Q131.P3` ports the fix: "Refused offers never leave the Fold's open-items list." `Q134.P3`: "the
  Fold logs abandoned demands as FIRST-CLASS ENTRIES". `Q126.P7`: the devil's terms improve each generation the
  offer stays refused. `Q126.N14`: the Emperor's line differs if you "turned him down". `Q150.X3` complains that
  declining a god goes unread by the world.
- **For forgetting:** `Q126.W3` and `Q131.N1` (the no is free), `Q140.P4` ("entries WITHOUT weight ... No mechanical
  cost, ever, or it stops" being a free exit), `Q043.X1` and `Q043.X2` (guilt and nagging are flaws), and `Q051.W2`
  (Gothic): "THE WORLD DOESN'T REVOLVE AROUND YOU", NPCs treat you as the NPC. A client in a crash economy asks five
  people a day; you are one of them.

**THE RESOLUTION FOR BOHEMIA.** Paolo's rule wins and it is also the realistic one. Every port above that records a
refusal is about a DEVIL-CLASS or MAIN-QUEST-CLASS offer (Raphael, Boethiah, Molag Bal, Vaermina, the Emperor): one
standing temptation per act that the story is built around. None of them is about a job. For CONTRACTS:

1. **The world remembers deeds, and a decline is not a deed.** In the real crash economy a worker turns down most
   of the work they hear about; nobody keeps a list. The world's memory is honest precisely because it only stores
   things that happened: a job taken, finished, failed, dropped. "She asked, you said no, she asked the next one" is
   not an event in anyone's life.
2. **What the world DOES keep is world-state, not player-state.** If you decline the cistern guard job, the cistern
   still needs guarding. Someone else takes it, or nobody does and the cistern is robbed. The world moves on its own
   logic (`Q055.W1`, `Q061.W2`: a world that runs without you). That outcome is written as a WORLD event with no
   player name on it. The test: the ledger entry for the robbed cistern must be identical whether the player declined
   the job or never walked down that street.
3. **The main quest and the devil-class offers keep the recording ports.** `Q131.P3`, `Q134.P3`, `Q126.P6`,
   `Q126.P7` stay valid for the ONE main quest and for the rare standing temptations DYNASTY and WORDS write into it.
   Refusing the Amalgamation's offer is a stand. Refusing to guard a cistern is Tuesday.
4. **Refusal dignity without memory.** `Q131.X7`'s complaint (the strongest choice is invisible) is answered, for
   contracts, by the client's behaviour at the moment of the no: one short, in-character line that lets the player
   go ("Bueno. Somebody else, then.") and then the client returns to their routine (`Q051.W4`). Dignity is in the
   delivery, not in the ledger.

### 4. ACCEPTANCE: WHAT "ONCE TAKEN YOU FINISH IT" MEANS IN PRACTICE

- **The exit stays open; that is what makes finishing mean something.** `Q040.W2`: about fifty back-out points in
  Sinnerman mean "reaching the end is a CHOICE you keep making". `Q140.W8`: the off-ramp is real, so "continuing
  means what it means". `Q151.W7`: "Abandoning everything mid-siege stays on the table, always, and its standing
  availability is what makes staying a CHOICE instead of a checkpoint." So "once taken you finish it" must NOT be a
  hard lock. It is the default and the only CLEAN exit. Dropping is always possible, and it is a deed.
- **There is no quit button.** A contract is dropped the way a person drops a job: in the world. You go back and say
  it to the client's face, or you let the stated window pass. Both are deeds. Neither is a menu item, because a quit
  button in a list would make the contract a card.
- **Two contracts at a time, never a pile.** (folded 10/1 from rule 51, which replaces round one's "one contract at a
  time".) `Q037.X2`: hoarding quests "floods the journal into confusion"; a system built on directions "needs FOCUS".
  `Q148.W10`: the contract is the best-scoped quest container because "nothing has time to dilute it". Paolo 9/30
  set the number: one main quest and TWO contract slots, because Battle Brothers' one slot (`Q236.W2`, `Q236.N1`) is
  "wack" when a caravan job carries you to a city and a hunt waits near that city. Two promises still fit in the
  player's head, so the phone still never needs a quest log; the focus finding holds at two and breaks at a pile.
  `Q043.X3` is the warning for the second slot (there about juggling several romances): threads held at once break
  silently unless their state is legible and robust, so each slot shows on the map (its marker, its window). Paolo's own words still point here: "you have to finish a side
  quest as soon as you pick up the contract". A third offer while both slots are full is readable and waits (free);
  it is taken only by finishing one, or by dropping one (a deed).
- **Deadlines: stated, generous, audible, and only when the job is about time.** `Q011.W2` and `Q011.P3`: a real
  deadline has teeth, "use sparingly", and a failed timed quest should "REROUTE, not softlock". `Q038.X1`: the hard
  clock alienates; tune it. `Q018.W8`: "the pressure is generous". `Q119.X3`: a timer nobody feels is not there; make
  it AUDIBLE. `Q095.X1` and `Q095.P7`: a silent expiry tied to an unrelated main-quest step is "a bug wearing a
  design costume"; expiry must be VISIBLE. `Q108.W8` and `Q129.X5` show the same failure: a window nobody tells you
  about. `Q121.X6`: a timer that makes players postpone character content is fighting the writing. `Q142.P5`: our
  beat clock already makes conversation clock-free unless a quest declares otherwise. `Q146.W8`: "TIME IS THE ONLY
  FEE", urgency without a UI timer. For us: the window is days of MAP time, said by the client ("before the water
  truck on Friday"), heard again from the world as it closes (the truck's horn from two streets away), and it never
  runs out because of something the player did on another contract or on the main quest.
- **Failing is not dropping.** `Q114.W3`: "failure costs the good item, not the story". `Q146.W1` and `Q087.W1`:
  defeat continues as a new state rather than a reload. `Q148.P3` and `Q148.X2`: a contract investigation must be
  able to conclude WRONG and still pay and close. So a contract has three ways to end that are not a drop: DONE
  (paid), DONE WRONG (paid, the world keeps the truth for later, `Q148.P2`), and FAILED WHILE TRYING (the fight was
  lost, the goods burned: paid nothing or part, and remembered as an attempt, not as a flake).
- **The world can void a contract, and then it closes clean.** `Q109.N2`: the contract is cancelled by the paymaster
  and the relationship changes shape. `Q109.W6`: "the villain gets laid off". If the client dies, is arrested, or the
  thing is already done by someone else, the contract ends with no deed on the player. `Q128.X2`: every broken link
  needs "one diegetic tell", so the player hears it (a neighbour at the door), never a pop-up.

### 5. ABANDONMENT: WHAT A TAKEN-AND-DROPPED CONTRACT COSTS, REALISTICALLY

- **The job still does not get done, and the world executes on that.** `Q087.W9` and `Q087.P5`: ignore the warning
  and "you'd come back to find it SACKED, ALL THE NPCs GONE". The main cost of a drop is the consequence of the job
  not being done, landing on the people who trusted you.
- **Proportion: one drop is a story, a pattern is a name.** `Q094.W4`: one betrayal "recalibrated an entire
  society"; `Q094.W1`: the biggest war in EVE started with one unpaid bill. Both show how far one broken obligation
  travels in a trust economy. But `Q109.W8` and `Q109.X5` warn the opposite way: if the outcome is identical whether
  you helped or not, kindness means nothing. So the realistic cost is graded: the client stops offering to you; the
  story travels by mouth at the speed of people (face to face, which is also the Amalgamation's blind spot); a
  second and third drop make strangers mention it by name (`Q086.W8`).
- **Money: an advance is a debt.** Batteries are the money. If a client paid anything up front, a drop turns that
  advance into a debt owed to a named person, which the canon's creditor machinery already knows how to carry. No
  advance, no debt: just the client's closed door.
- **The drop is inherited.** Three acts at once: the future is computed from the past. A dropped Act 1 contract is a
  fact in the ledger that derives Act 2 (the family name at that pump house). `Q144.W3` gives the principle: "the meaning lives in the non-heritable remainder", so only DEEDS cross the
  generation line, and a decline is not one.
- **The lie is discoverable.** `Q148.X1`: a white lie to a client with "zero risk, zero later cost" cheapens the
  choice. `Q148.P2`: write TWO entries, what you told the client and what happened. A player who drops a job and
  claims the raiders were too many, when they never went, has told a lie the world can find later.
- **Never make the drop a guilt screen.** `Q139.X2` and `Q143.P4`: forced deeds go in a separate book from player
  deeds, and the player is never accused of what the mechanism made. The cost of a drop is shown in the world (a
  closed door, a sacked cistern, a debt), never in a lecture.

### 6. PAY AND THE MORAL FORK INSIDE A CONTRACT

- `Q148.X3` and `Q148.P4`: the twist must be a MINORITY of contracts on an unpredictable schedule; "most jobs should
  be exactly what they say". Volume is the twist's predator.
- `Q148.W9`: the client's closure is outcome-invariant; the moral weight sits on the professional's side of the
  counter. `Q004.W4`: zero reward difference makes a choice purely ethical. `Q108.X2`: when the moral choice also pays
  more, "you didn't build a dilemma". `Q148.W8`: the temptation needs a real number on it.
- `Q002.W2`: one person as the branch-router keeps a job's fork legible, which fits a phone and a portrait.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)

C1. **A CONTRACT IS OFFERED BY A PERSON STANDING IN A PLACE.** Never a card or a pop-up. The offerer has a
portrait and speaks. Test: every contract definition names a person id and a place id; none names a UI panel.
(`Q108.W1`, `Q013.W6`, `Q114.W2`, `Q148.W2` translated.) (folded 10/1 from rule 51: the cracked phone, in the city
view only, may SHOW a read-only board of which settlement offers what right now, a board that ages, so the player can
plan a road; nothing on the phone can accept. Accepting is a mouth at the place, on the settlement screen's offer.
Test: no phone element writes a contract state; every TAKEN row was written from an offer screen at a place.
`Q236.W1`: the board is a readout of the map, not a quest list; `Q236.W7`: the accept button is the only promise.)

C2. **NOTHING OFFERS ITSELF.** The client may be walked past; the offer opens only when the player stops and talks
(rule 34, the second votes). Walking past is not a decline and writes nothing. (Swept 9/29: there is no walking
through the city any more, rule 38; "walked past" now reads "the building he did not tap", QR-P 2.2.) Test: no contract has an entry that
fires on proximity alone.

C3. **CLEAN TERMS IN ONE BREATH.** The first offer node states: what, where, how many (the danger), the pay in
batteries, and the window if there is one. Test: the offer node carries those five fields; a validator refuses a
contract whose danger or pay appears only after acceptance. (`Q126.N5`, `Q060.W1`, `Q148.W1`.)

C4. **"NO" IS ON EVERY OFFER NODE, UNGATED.** Test: every node before acceptance has an option with `[gate: none]`
that leads to the decline exit. (`Q126.N3`, `Q126.N5`.)

C5. **THE FEE LOCKS AT ACCEPTANCE.** One haggle is allowed at the offer; after acceptance there is no renegotiation
node. Test: no fee-changing node is reachable from the accepted state. (`Q148.W1`, `Q148.P1`.)

C6. **DECLINING WRITES NOTHING.** The decline branch has ZERO ledger writes: no standing, no rumour, no feed post, no
flag, no cooldown on the client. Test: game state after a decline equals game state before the offer, except the
clock. (`Q126.W3`, `Q126.N13`, Paolo 9/27.)

C7. **THE NO IS ANSWERED ONCE, KINDLY, IN CHARACTER, AND NEVER AGAIN.** The client gets one short line that lets the
player go. No line anywhere in the game (client, companion, feed, event) may be conditioned on a contract decline.
Test: no gate reads a decline. Banned registers: sulking, "too good for this?", "coward", "I thought you needed the
work". (`Q043.X1`, `Q043.X2`, `Q131.N1`.)

C8. **THE WORLD KEEPS WORLD-STATE, NOT A NAME.** A declined job may still happen to the world (someone else takes it,
or the harm lands). That outcome is written without the player's id. Test: the outcome entry is byte-identical
whether the player declined or never met the client. (`Q055.W1`, `Q061.W2`, `Q051.W2`.)

C9. **TWO CONTRACT SLOTS, PLUS ONE MAIN QUEST.** (folded 10/1 from rule 51; this replaces "one open contract at a
time".) The company holds at most two TAKEN contracts and one main quest thread. Once taken, each is finished (C10).
A third offer while both slots are full can be declined free, read in full, or left to wait; it is taken only when a
slot opens, by finishing one or by dropping one (a deed, C13). The pair is the design: a haul to a city and a hunt
near that city share one road (rule 51). Test: the contract table allows at most two rows in state TAKEN; while two
are TAKEN, every other offer screen still opens and shows its terms, and only accept is disabled, shown as a greyed
button, never a speech (`Q236.N1`). We differ from Battle Brothers here on purpose: its hall holds ONE (`Q236.W2`,
`Q236.N2`); ours holds two. TUNING holds the slot count as a felt number. (`Q037.X2`, `Q148.W10`, `Q236.N1`,
`Q236.W2`.)

C10. **NO QUIT BUTTON.** A contract ends only in the world: DONE, DONE WRONG, FAILED, VOIDED, or DROPPED. DROPPED
happens by telling the client face to face, or by letting a stated window pass. Test: no UI element changes a
contract's state. (`Q040.W2`, `Q151.W7`, `Q140.W8`.)

C11. **A WINDOW IS SPOKEN, GENEROUS, AUDIBLE AND LOCAL.** Only contracts that are about time carry a window. It is in
map days, stated by the client, heard in the world as it closes, and it can never be ended by an unrelated action.
Test: every window has a spoken statement node and a closing tell; no main-quest step can expire a contract.
(`Q011.P3`, `Q018.W8`, `Q119.X3`, `Q095.P7`, `Q128.X2`, `Q146.W8`.)

C12. **FAILING IS NOT DROPPING.** Losing the fight, being robbed, reaching the wrong answer: the contract closes as
FAILED or DONE WRONG, pays nothing or part, and is an attempt, not a flake. Defeat continues into a state, not a
reload. (`Q114.W3`, `Q146.W1`, `Q087.W1`, `Q148.P3`.)

C13. **DROPPED IS A DEED, PRICED IN FOUR REAL THINGS.** (a) the job's own harm lands on the client; (b) the client
stops offering to you; (c) any advance becomes a named debt in batteries; (d) the story travels by mouth, and at
three drops strangers cite it. One drop is never a blacklist. Test: DROPPED writes exactly these, and the
"strangers cite it" gate reads a count of 3 or more. (`Q087.W9`, `Q094.W4`, `Q086.W8`, `Q040.W10`.)

C14. **THE WORLD CAN VOID A CONTRACT CLEAN.** If the client dies or the job is overtaken, it closes as VOIDED with no
deed, and the player hears it from a person. (`Q109.N2`, `Q128.X2`.)

C15. **MOST CONTRACTS ARE EXACTLY WHAT THEY SAY.** The twist (the job is not what it seemed) goes on a minority, on an
unpredictable schedule. Test: the generator counts twists per act and keeps them under a set share. (`Q148.X3`,
`Q148.P4`.)

C16. **TOLD AND HAPPENED ARE TWO ENTRIES.** What the player tells the client and what happened are both written; any
later system may read the gap. (`Q148.P2`, `Q148.X1`.)

C17. **THE MAIN QUEST IS DIFFERENT.** A refusal INSIDE the main quest, or of a standing devil-class offer, is a stand
and may be recorded (`Q131.P3`, `Q134.P3`, `Q126.P7`). That recording is never applied to a contract.

C18. **A DECLINED OFFER MAY BE MADE AGAIN BY THE WORLD, NEVER BY THE GRUDGE.** If the job is still open next time you
pass, the client may ask again with the same words (or a new price if the world's economy moved, boom or bust).
Never "you again?". (`Q126.W3`, C6.)

C19. **A NO IS NEVER A SLOW DEATH.** (folded 10/1 from QR-U.) The wage treadmill makes every offer weighed
(`Q236.W10`), and the same treadmill can force the yes: the decline is free in the ledger but not on the treasury
(`Q236.X4`). So a second income always exists: SCAVENGE on the settlement screen (QR-O rule 11) covers the company's
keep (FOOD and WATER, rule 47) at a thin margin, so one declined offer never forces the next yes. C6 keeps the ledger
clean; C19 keeps the purse honest. Test: a company that declines every offer for ten map days and scavenges at each
stop does not go bankrupt at default tuning. TUNING owns the numbers (QR-U rule 3). (`Q236.X4`, `Q236.W10`,
`Q236.W7`, `Q126.W3`.)

C20. **THE CLIENT WANTS SOMETHING BESIDES THE FEE.** (folded 10/9 from QR-U) The Battle Brothers elder has a portrait and a mood but no
want beyond the fee and no life on the map (`Q236.X3`); the study's own law is a person in a place with a want the
player can see, whose place changes after the hand-in. QR-G line 25 says this for every item. Test: every offer screen
names one want of the client that is not the fee, and the hand-in changes the client's place.

C21. **A CLIENT WHO REFUSES TO PAY.** (folded 10/9 from QR-U) At hand-in the client may refuse, a twist: take it by force or leave unpaid,
which makes the player price his own honour (`Q236.N5`). QR-C's T11 and T12 remove the payer; this is the payer who is
present and says no. It closes as DONE (the deed is the client's) unless the player takes the pay by force, which is
his own deed. It rides the twist ration (C15). Test: a refused hand-in writes no deed against the player unless he
takes the pay by force.

## OPEN (what the library could not answer)

1. **How much a drop costs in numbers.** The library says graded and real; it does not say how many batteries or
   how many days of a closed door. ECONOMY owns the numbers (the advance, the debt's terms, how long a client's door
   stays closed).
2. **How long rumour of a drop takes to travel.** The library has rumour systems but no speed. PEOPLE [weights shape]
   owns it, and it should move at walking speed because it is face to face.
3. **Whether a client can die of a declined job.** C8 says the world may let the harm land. If a declined guard job
   leaves the client dead, the player will feel responsible even with no ledger entry. The library shows this feeling
   is powerful (`Q030.W5`: guilt arrived unprompted in playtest) but does not say whether to allow it for contracts.
   Default: harm to property yes, death of the client no, for contracts; deaths belong to the main quest and to
   events.
4. **What Battle Brothers itself does.** The blind spots record says its game docks relations for a refused contract.
   None of the 152 studies is Battle Brothers, so the library cannot confirm or refute it; the rule above does not
   depend on it.
5. **Whether the two contract slots survive the three acts at once.** (Swept 9/29: the flip exists only once the next
   act has unlocked, rule 39c, so this only arises mid-game. Folded 10/1 from rule 51: the count is now TWO slots,
   not one.) If the player flips acts with a contract open in Act 1, does the contract's window run while he is in
   Act 3, and does a slot held in one act count against the other? DYNASTY owns the flip; the library is silent.
