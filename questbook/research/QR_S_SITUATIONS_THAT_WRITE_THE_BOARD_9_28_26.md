# QR-S: WHAT SITUATIONS AT A HOME BASE WRITE ITS BOARD, AND WHAT DOES HAND-IN CHANGE?

QUESTION: row [fourth shelf], letter S. QR-N found the big miss (the board is written by the world: an offer exists BECAUSE a home base has a situation, and hand-in changes that situation). QR-O drew the fourteen home bases and their contract kinds. This page is the missing middle: a catalogue of the SITUATIONS a base can be in (short of water, raided last week, a wedding, a plague, a strike, a new boss, a glut and so on), and for each one, the contract kinds it writes, which bases it fits, what a finished contract changes, and the library ids that back it. It closes with rules the building lanes can test.

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

A situation is a short sentence about a place that is true for a while: "the dam is short of a turbine", "the Strip was raided last week", "the co-op votes in three nights". The library's system studies say that is the right unit: the storyteller READS the state and picks the event that makes the best story (`Q044.W2`), stories come out of systems colliding (`Q045.W1`, `Q044.W6`), and a generator works when it is built from input variables (`Q056.W9`). Battle Brothers does it at the settlement: a situation (raided, plague, high demand) moves prices and contracts, and the result writes the next situation (QR-N 3a). We found 36 situations that fit our canon, in seven families: SHORTAGES, GLUTS, VIOLENCE, PEOPLE, POWER, WEATHER AND SICKNESS, and MOVEMENT. Every one writes one to three contract kinds, and every one has a HAND-IN that changes a thing the player can see: a price at the market (`Q151.W10`, `Q107.W4`), a board with more or fewer offers (`Q055.P6`), a face that comes back (`Q031.W6`, `Q119.W8`), or a mark on the map (`Q147.W7`). Three findings matter most. First, a glut writes as well as a shortage, and our shelves were starved of it: before this round every act 1 contract on the shelf was a bust job. Boom situations (a glut, a surplus, a new road, a festival, a tournament) are the missing half of the board (`Q044.W4`, `Q151.W3`, `Q107.X6`). Second, when the world writes the offer, the pay should be for fixing the SITUATION, not for the method, because then the kind road and the cruel road pay the same without any special rule (`Q148.W9`, `Q130.X9`). Third, a situation fixed at one base usually writes a situation at the next one (the fever pushed out of the chapel reaches the wash; the drivers who quit the depot join the cartel yards), which gives us the library's cheapest reactivity for free (`Q055.W5`, `Q078.X2`, `Q092.X6`).

## THE FINDINGS

### 1. Why the situation is the unit

- THE BOARD IS THE SIM, POSTED. `Q148.P1`: boards post contracts "generated from the world sim's actual events". `Q148.W2`: public postings make paid work opt-in and dignified. A situation is the smallest piece of "actual events" a phone screen can hold in one line.
- THE STORYTELLER READS THE STATE. `Q044.W2` and `Q044.W7`: an algorithm reads the colony and picks the event that makes the best story, and after a hard hit it sends relief. For us the base's situation IS the state it reads, and a recovery contract after a raid IS the relief.
- VARIABLES, NOT SCRIPTS. `Q056.W9`: break the generated thing into input variables. Our variables per base are few and readable: the base's job (QR-O's table), the economy (boom or bust), the act's crisis stage (QR-J), the base's own history (built or raided), and ONE situation at a time.
- EMERGENCE NEEDS AN AUTHOR ON TOP. `Q044.X4` and `Q044.P8`: pure emergence has no theme; blend the generator with authorship. So the situation writes the offer's KIND and PAY, and a written person (portrait, three lines, one want) carries it. The situation is the sim; the client is the author.
- THE WORLD LIVES WITHOUT YOU. `Q055.W1`, `Q055.W7`: factions trade, war and patrol on their own. Situations happen whether the player is near or not, which is why a board can change between two visits with no cutscene.

### 2. A situation has a lifetime

- IT ARRIVES SLOWLY. `Q021.W3`: the pressure creeps in. A situation should be visible on the settlement screen (a dark window, a queue, black cloth on a door) one visit before, or at the same time as, the offer it writes. `Q146.P4`: the world warns about itself before the danger applies.
- IT LEAVES ON ITS OWN. QR-N rule 9: an offer leaves when its situation leaves, never because the player said no. The rain comes, the heat breaks, the auction happens, the count is done. `Q126.W3` says a refusal costs nothing and the offer can wait; Battle Brothers says it goes when the situation goes. Both are true together: the NO costs nothing, the WORLD moves.
- ITS CLOCK IS SAID ALOUD. `Q095.X1` and `Q106.X5` are the silent deadline that kills the best content. When a situation has an end, the client says it on the offer screen ("Count is in three nights", "By the next moon they're garbage") and the map shows it (QD-S04, QD-S08, QD-S20).
- IT CAN BE FIXED BY SOMEONE ELSE. A declined offer is not frozen. Someone else takes it, or the base copes alone, and the board shows the cheaper result (QD-S16's inspection failed, QD-S18's collectors win most of the race). `Q128.W6`: failure is ambient; `Q128.X2`: ambient failure needs one tell, and our tell is the next board.

### 3. Hand-in writes the situation back

- THE WORLD RE-PRICES, IT DOES NOT CLAP. `Q151.W10`: stand the siege and the region quietly re-prices. Our hand-in's first mark is a price at the base's market (QR-O rule 10). Power falls one at the dam (QD-S01); tyres go cheap at the co-op (QD-S04); rent falls at the outer blocks (QD-S18).
- THE SECOND MARK IS THE BOARD. `Q055.P6`: the story pivots on system state; a raided base is a different board. After hand-in, the base's offer count and kind change (the depot back to three offers when the scrip is honoured, QD-S05; the yards from gunman jobs to haul jobs, QD-S16).
- THE THIRD MARK IS A FACE. `Q031.W6`: recurring faces make the rules personal. `Q119.W8`: the person you help becomes a supply line. `Q145.W7`: mercy planted blind pays off named. The deck crew hired in QD-S01 comes back as a client; the old gunmen in QD-S16 stand at the gate.
- THE FOURTH MARK IS THE MAP. `Q147.W7`: the failure state is visible geography. A town ruined (QD-S17 choice B), a camp gone from the river bank (QD-S20 failed), a garage with a mural (QD-S13).
- WHAT MUST NEVER HAPPEN. `Q146.X1`: the triumph is met with nothing. `Q151.X3`: the base's interior is silent. `Q130.X10`: aftermath is a zero. Every situation in the catalogue lists its hand-in mark for this reason.

### 4. Situations chain between bases

- THE HARM TRAVELS. `Q092.X6`: a kindness or cruelty whose effect nobody ever sees is trivia. When the chapel's fever caravan is forced out, the fever reaches the wash camp's board (QD-S06). When the depot's strike is broken, six drivers appear at the cartel yards (QD-S05). The chain is the proof.
- THE TRIGGER IS THE NEIGHBOUR. `Q107.W2`: the trigger for the Londoners was another city like yours falling. A base that falls writes a situation at its neighbours (refugees, a price jump, a party with a name).
- PROSPERITY IS A MAGNET. `Q044.W4` and `Q151.W3`: threat scales with wealth. A boom situation at one base writes a raid-expected situation at the same base or on the road to it (QD-S02's stake on the road, QD-S04's rival crew at the fence).
- THE INVENTORY TELLS FIRST. `Q107.W4`: the rebellion arrives as food going missing. A bust at one base often shows first as theft at the next (the rack's steel in QD-S01 is sold somewhere; the market two stops away has cheap steel).
- KEEP THE CHAIN SHORT. `Q071.X1` and `Q128.X2`: a chain the player cannot follow is a silent break. Rule: one link, within two map stops, and the link is a face or a board, never a line of text.

### 5. The pay should buy the fix, not the method

- THE FEE LOCKS BEFORE THE TRUTH. `Q148.W1`: haggle at ignorance, and money is separated from morality after. If the fee is for "the rack cleared" or "the caravan found", the method cannot change the pay (QD-S17).
- OUTCOME-INVARIANT CLOSURE. `Q148.W9`: trophy in, coin out, whichever world. The farm tower pays the same whether the tomatoes feed the rich canteen or the poor camp (QD-S12).
- THE CRUEL BRANCH NEVER PAYS MORE. `Q130.X9`, `Q108.X2`, `Q070.X2`, `Q121.X2`: when the moral choice is also the profitable one, or the cruel one is, the choice becomes math. Writing four of these twenty designs, the first draft broke this rule because the kind road cost the family its own batteries (paying the sweepers, paying the wash for water, paying the strikers). The fix each time was to move that cost to the CLIENT (the field pays the sweepers, the dispatcher pays the strikers), which is also more realistic: the base is the one with the problem.
- THE CRUEL JOB MAY STILL BE ON THE BOARD. `Q031.W5`: the regime pays for the cruel job. A board in a bust may post the collection or the eviction as its best-paid offer. The price tag lives BETWEEN offers, never inside one (QR-O rule 15).
- THE PRICED REFUSAL. `Q139.P2`: inside a taken contract, "I won't do this part" finishes the job at a stated price (QD-S01 C, QD-S19 C). This is round one's [PENDING] default and every design here follows it.

### 6. How many situations at once

- ONE SITUATION PER BASE AT A TIME. `Q037.X2`: many quests at once flood the journal. `Q065.X2`: an overwhelming first hour. A base with three situations at once reads as noise. One situation writes one to three offers (the tier's count); a second situation waits until the first leaves.
- BUT NEVER NONE. `Q107.X6`: the best-run city gets the least story. `Q084.X3`: the empty beautiful town. A base with no bad news has a good-news situation (a glut, a festival, a tournament, a new road). That is why the catalogue's boom half matters.
- AND NEVER THE SAME ONE TWICE IN A ROW. `Q148.X3` and `Q046.X1`: a format repeated loses its people. A base's next situation is drawn from a different family than its last.

### 7. The situation is shown on the settlement screen

- THE SCREEN IS THE PLACE. Third votes s2: places are settlement screens. `Q013.W1`: side content is sense of place. So a situation must be drawn on the settlement view (black cloth for a death, a queue for water, a tarp city in the garage after a flood, bunting for a festival), not only written on the offer.
- TWO CHANNELS. `Q141.X3`: a clue in one sense is lost to some players. A situation shows as a picture AND a sound (the hum of the data centre louder in the heat, the drivers' chant at the depot).
- THE GATE IS THE POSTURE. `Q151.W5`: how a base treats people at its door is its reputation. The situation's first picture is often at the gate: the chapel's sick by the well, the squat's door with a missing name on it.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)

1. EVERY OFFER HAS A SITUATION. An offer record carries `situation:` naming one entry of this catalogue (or a new one added to it) and the base it is at. Test: no offer without it. (`Q148.P1`, `Q044.W2`)
2. ONE SITUATION PER BASE AT A TIME. It writes the tier's count of offers (camp 1, town 2, fortress 3). A second situation waits. Test: never two live situations on one base. (`Q037.X2`, `Q065.X2`)
3. NEVER NO SITUATION. A base with nothing wrong gets a boom situation (8 to 13). Test: every base has one live situation on every visit. (`Q107.X6`, `Q084.X3`)
4. NEVER THE SAME FAMILY TWICE IN A ROW at one base. Test: consecutive situations at a base are from different families. (`Q148.X3`, `Q046.X1`)
5. THE SITUATION IS DRAWN ON THE SETTLEMENT SCREEN, in picture and sound, on the visit the offer appears or earlier. Test: every situation id has a screen prop and a sound id. (`Q146.P4`, `Q141.X3`, `Q013.W1`)
6. A SITUATION WITH AN END SAYS IT. The client speaks the day, the map shows it. Test: every timed situation has a spoken line with the count and a map marker. (`Q095.X1`, `Q106.X5`)
7. THE OFFER LEAVES WHEN THE SITUATION LEAVES, and for no other reason. A declined offer stays until then. Test: an offer's removal is always caused by its situation's end. (`Q126.W3`, QR-N rule 9)
8. THE FEE BUYS THE FIX. The screen states what is being paid for (the rack cleared, the caravan found), and every road through the contract pays that fee. A cost that only the kind road carries is paid by the client, not the family. Test: pay is identical across all non-refusal choices. (`Q148.W1`, `Q148.W9`, `Q130.X9`, `Q108.X2`)
9. HAND-IN LEAVES AT LEAST TWO OF FOUR MARKS: a price at the base's market, a change in its board, a returning face, a mark on the map. Test: the before/after diff of a finished contract touches two of the four. (`Q151.W10`, `Q055.P6`, `Q031.W6`, `Q147.W7`)
10. ONE LINK OF CHAIN, WITHIN TWO MAP STOPS. When a hand-in pushes harm or people out of a base, the nearest base within two stops gets a matching situation. Test: every "pushed" outcome names its receiving base. (`Q092.X6`, `Q107.W2`, `Q071.X1`)
11. PROSPERITY PULLS PRESSURE. A base in a boom situation gets a raid-expected situation (15) or a road danger in its next draw at a rate TUNING sets. (`Q044.W4`, `Q151.W3`)
12. THE RESULT OF A VOTE, A TABLE OR A TRIAL IS NOT THE PLAYER'S PICK. The contract gets the thing there; the world decides. Test: no contract choice sets a vote's outcome directly. (`Q136.W2`, `Q107.X1`)
13. A DECLINE WRITES NOTHING, EVER, whatever the situation. The base copes alone and the board shows the cheaper result, which is the world, not a punishment. Test: ledgers byte-identical after a decline; only the situation's own default resolution runs. (`Q084.X5`, `Q131.X7`)
14. ACROSS THE ACTS, a situation resolved in one act may become a BASE FACT in a later act (the garage the camp moved to, the grave in the yard), with a full default read if the earlier act was never played. (`Q049.P3`, `Q096.X1`)

SHARPENED (folded 10/9 from QR-U):
- Rules 1 and 9 (every offer has a situation; hand-in leaves marks): `Q236.W1`, `Q236.W8`, `Q236.P4`, and each base's
  offers generated from its current situation (`Q236.P1`).
- Rule 12 (a vote, a table or a trial is not the player's pick): the verdict predates the work (`Q182.W2`); being right
  is not power (`Q182.W3`); the powers are plural (`Q182.W9`); the denial must be legibly the system's (`Q182.P2`).
- Rule 12, the one exception: a name must be given (`Q234.W6`), decided without verifying (`Q234.W1`), carried for
  generations (`Q234.W3`). When a base HIRES the company to judge, the naming is the player's, on the offer screen

## THE [bb situations] LINE

Battle Brothers teaches that a town's troubles are its job board; our twist is that the trouble fixed in one town is usually the next town's trouble.

## THE ANALOG HORROR LINE

Every situation carries its one wrong detail on the settlement screen, not in the offer: the bar put back on the stripped rack, the thermometer that reads outside in a cold room, the box heavier on the way back. The situation is ordinary; one thing in its picture is not.
