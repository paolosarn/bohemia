# QR-V: WHEN THE LIBRARY STUDIED THE SAME QUEST TWICE, WHAT DID THE SECOND READING ADD?

QUESTION: row [duplicate studies], letter V. Four pairs of studies look like the same quest read twice: #190 and #123 (Come Fly With Me), #193 and #122 (Sinnerman), #192 and #154 (the Forsworn Conspiracy in Markarth), #181 and #112 (the Hanged Man case in Disco Elysium). For each pair: is it the same quest, how do the two readings differ (angle, depth, findings), and which findings does the later study ADD. Then: what does each quest teach the wider library, so a building lane citing one reading knows the others exist.

STATUS: draft:true, research only, nothing built, nothing deleted, nothing renumbered. Every id in the library keeps its meaning. Each later study now carries one line at its top ("SECOND READING of #N") so nobody mistakes it for a new quest. For RUN, WORLD, PEOPLE, WORDS and FACTIONS.

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Three of the four pairs are the same quest, and two of those are really TRIPLES: Come Fly With Me is read three times (#79, #123, #190) and Sinnerman three times (#40, #122, #193), with #123 and #122 already marked as superseding the first readings. The Markarth pair (#154, #192) is the same quest read twice at the same depth from two different angles, and both readings earn their keep. The Disco Elysium pair (#112, #181) is NOT the same quest: it is the same murder case, but #112 reads the body (getting it down, the autopsy form) and #181 reads a different thread, Klaasje's interrogation, which no other study covers. The pattern across the three true duplicates is the same: the earlier reading is a long, sourced teardown built on quotes (6,000 to 7,000 words), and the later reading is a short v2 study (about 2,900 words) that re-states the quest as a clean moral machine. The later reading adds real LENSES (how to frame an unverifiable heaven, whether a chooser is competent, whether the exploiter is too cartoonish) but it also DRIFTS from the facts the earlier reading sourced: #190 invents a "ground them" fork and moves the truth option from Chris to Jason, #193 has the victim's widow forgiving Joshua when #122 sourced a family that wants him dead. The rule for builders: cite the sourced reading for WHAT HAPPENS, cite the later reading only for the lens it adds, and never build a beat from a later reading's plot claim that the earlier one contradicts.

## THE FINDINGS

### PAIR 1: #190 and #123, COME FLY WITH ME. VERDICT: SAME QUEST (and a third reading, #79, exists)

Same quest, same game, same place: the ghoul cult at the REPCONN test site, Jason Bright, the rockets. #123 says of itself that it supersedes #79, so this quest has been read three times.

HOW THEY DIFFER.
- Depth. #123 is a 6,300-word sourced teardown with eleven stages and quotes on every claim; #190 is a 2,900-word v2 study with five stages and no quotes. #79 sits between them as the first pass.
- Angle. #123 reads the quest as a FAITH WITH A PARTS LIST: the heaven has a fuel requirement (`Q123.W1`), the flock is leaving for a real reason, bigotry against ghouls (`Q123.W2`), and the holy fuel is inside toy rockets from a gift shop (`Q123.W7`). #190 reads it as an UNVERIFIABLE HEAVEN: the Far Beyond cannot be checked (`Q190.W1`), the removed verb is "check the destination first" (`Q190.W6`), and the game refuses to resolve it (`Q190.W9`).
- The truth option moved. In #123 the knife is Chris Haversam, a bald human who believes he is a ghoul (`Q123.W3`), whom the prophet used knowing he would be left behind (`Q123.W4`); telling Chris the truth unlocks only once you know he is being used (`Q123.W8`), and the kind answer creates the bomber (`Q123.W9`), which is the node `Q123.N1` calls a trap made of kindness. #79 already had this: the cruelty is inside the punchline (`Q079.W2`). #190 drops Chris entirely and puts the truth option on Jason ("tell Jason the truth", `Q190.N2`).
- The forks drifted. #123's real endings are the launch true, the launch nudged 12.5% closer (`Q123.W10`, `Q123.P5`), the rockets crashed into each other (`Q123.P5`), sabotage through Chris (`Q123.W9`), or murdering the whole cast for Manny Vargas (`Q123.X3`). #190's forks are "fuel, ground, or expose" (`Q190.N2`, `Q190.N3`). Neither #123 nor #79 records a "ground them" ending, and #190's own header calls it "a rocket that will never fly" while its `Q190.W5` says the launch is real. Treat #190's fork list as a lens, not as the quest.
- The enemy drifted. #123 names the demons as Davison's nightkin, who leave if you solve their supply problem (`Q123.W6`); #190 calls them "hostile ghouls". #79 records the same talkable obstacles (`Q079.W3`).
- The flaw #190 banks is answered by #123. `Q190.X3` says the flock is undercharacterized and the stakes abstract; #123 already has Chris (`Q123.W3`), the man in the basement waiting on news of his friend (`Q123.P5`), and Jason's one mercy line about sparing ferals (`Q123.W5`, `Q079.W4`). The flaw belongs to #190's summary, not to the quest.

WHAT #190 ADDS (the lens, cite these for it).
- The unverifiable destination as the ENGINE, not a gap: `Q190.W1`, `Q190.W6`, `Q190.W9`. #79 banked the same fact as a flaw, the payoff offscreen and unresolved (`Q079.X2`); #190 turns it into a rule: own the ambiguity so it reads as the condition, not as the writers dodging (`Q190.X2`, `Q190.P3`).
- Every option as love and cruelty at once (`Q190.W3`) and agency against safety (`Q190.W7`): the flock WANTS to go.
- The completion reward nudges toward one ending (`Q190.X1`) with the law that all endings be equally supported (`Q190.P2`).
- One believer must be known as a person (`Q190.P4`), which is exactly what #123's Chris already is.

WHAT THE QUEST TEACHES THE WIDER LIBRARY. It is the promised-afterlife machine that #190 ties to the Amalgamation (`Q190.W10`). Its neighbours: the false salvation that makes successors, not survivors (`Q034.W7`), and the comforting lie people cling to (`Q034.W2`); the cult whose harm IS its theology, where naming the harm saves no one (`Q196.W1`, `Q196.W4`) and there is a con inside the faith (`Q196.W3`), which is Jason using Chris in another key; the master who wants a death because the paradise is barred while he lives (`Q216.W8`). For Bohemia this is Act 3's rocket seen from outside: a community whose whole meaning rides on a launch. The honest numbers come from #123, not #190: the best a helper can do is 12.5% (`Q123.W10`).

### PAIR 2: #193 and #122, SINNERMAN. VERDICT: SAME QUEST (and a third reading, #40, exists)

Same quest chain: Joshua Stephenson, the murderer who sells his crucifixion to a media company. #122 says of itself that it supersedes #40.

HOW THEY DIFFER.
- Depth. #122 is a 5,800-word sourced teardown; #193 is a 2,900-word v2 study. #40 is the first pass and still carries the chain's best structural findings.
- Angle. #122 reads it as THE CLIPBOARD: a corporation bought a man's death as a product (`Q122.W1`), the product is the dying experience itself, a braindance they plan to call Passion (`Q122.W2`), and the production is BORED (`Q122.W3`), with a dressing room (`Q122.W4`). #193 reads it as ATONEMENT VERSUS EXPLOITATION: the penance and the product are one event (`Q193.W1`), he means it completely (`Q193.W2`), and the question is who owns a punishment (`Q193.W5`).
- The facts drifted. #193 has the victim's widow forgiving Joshua and taking part (`Q193.W3`). #122 sourced the opposite: the chain opens with a client, Bill Jablonsky, who wants Joshua dead, and you must betray that grief to continue (`Q122.X4`, `Q122.N1`). #40 records a murderer begging the bereaved for forgiveness, with no clean feeling available (`Q040.W5`), not a forgiveness granted. #193 calls the product a livestream; #122 sourced a braindance recording (`Q122.W2`). #193 speaks of death row; #122 sourced a day of parole granted by a corporation (`Q122.W1`). Build from #122.
- The flaw #193 banks is answered by #122. `Q193.X2` says the player's degree of participation is under-specified; #122 sourced four explicit levels, leave, watch, start and quit, finish (`Q122.W5`), with "stay with Joshua until he dies" as a literal objective (`Q122.W6`) and watching as a mechanic (`Q122.W7`). #40 counts about fifty back-out points (`Q040.W2`).

WHAT #193 ADDS (the lens, cite these for it).
- Stopping him is also a wrong: protection as paternalism (`Q193.W7`), the pull goes both ways (`Q193.W9`).
- The exploiter must not be so cartoonish that it decides the question (`Q193.X1`, `Q193.P2`). This is new; neither #40 nor #122 names it.
- The chooser's soundness must be established or the player dismisses him as deluded (`Q193.X3`, `Q193.P4`). #122 holds the other half: the game never resolves whether he meant it (`Q122.W10`).

WHAT THE QUEST TEACHES THE WIDER LIBRARY. It is the corpus's sharpest complicity-by-consent quest: the content requires sustained agreement (`Q122.W9`, `Q122.X1`), and quitting early locks later quests (`Q040.W10`). Its neighbours on penance: repentance that is unfinished and unprovable (`Q208.W1`, `Q208.W7`); atonement aimed at the son, not the self (`Q219.W1`); the reform that was real against an order that will not explain (`Q225.W3`, `Q225.W4`); and the verdict engine that grades conduct, not speech (`Q143.W3`). For Bohemia it is the Amalgamation's own business model seen small: a recorded death sold as an experience (`Q122.P2`). The performance bonus (`Q122.W8`) is the detail a contract-giver in our valley would actually say.

### PAIR 3: #192 and #154, THE FORSWORN CONSPIRACY. VERDICT: SAME QUEST, TWO GOOD ANGLES

Same quest, same city, same depth (2,600 and 2,900 words, both v2). This is the one pair where the second reading is a genuine second angle rather than a thinner copy.

HOW THEY DIFFER.
- #154 reads it as THE CITY THAT FRAMES YOU: the city itself is the antagonist (`Q154.W1`), investigation is the offense (`Q154.W2`), the frame escalates in readable steps, note, guards, prison (`Q154.W9`, `Q154.N1`), and the real power is untouchable (`Q154.W5`).
- #192 reads it as THE LID ON A GRAVE: the clean surface is a lid (`Q192.W1`), the enemy is the robbed (`Q192.W2`), the powerful already know (`Q192.W4`), and the foundation is only a generation deep, with living survivors (`Q192.W8`).
- Where they overlap, they agree: prosperity built on suppression (`Q154.W3` and `Q192.W1`), no clean exit (`Q154.W6` and `Q192.W7`), the truth lives in the mine (`Q154.W7` and `Q192.W5`), the sympathetic-and-terrible fork (`Q154.W8`).
- Where they disagree on the Forsworn, they are both right: #154 says the Forsworn are flattened into a lever (`Q154.X3`); #192 says their violence elsewhere in the game muddies the reframe (`Q192.X1`). Together: the wronged need a face AND their violence must be reconciled with the wrong (`Q192.P2`).
- A small fact drift: #154 has a woman stabbed in the market; #192 has a woman killing a man. #154 matches the game. Low stakes, but it marks #192 as written from summary.

WHAT #192 ADDS.
- The dispossession lens: the "terrorists" are the robbed (`Q192.W2`), and the prison puts truth-teller and wronged in one hole (`Q192.W5`, `Q192.N3`).
- The powerful already know, so the only threat is saying it aloud (`Q192.W4`, `Q192.N2`).
- Preserving the lid must cost something visible (`Q192.X2`, `Q192.P3`), and either exit must implicate the player (`Q192.X3`, `Q192.P4`).
#154 keeps what #192 lacks: a thread a later generation can pull against the untouchable (`Q154.X1`, `Q154.P2`) and the right to stop looking, which the quest denies (`Q154.X2`, `Q154.P3`).

WHAT THE QUEST TEACHES THE WIDER LIBRARY. It is the institution-is-the-crime quest. Its neighbours: the city that has abdicated and leaves justice to a stranger (`Q160.W1`, `Q160.W10`); the verdict that predates the work, where being right is not power (`Q182.W2`, `Q182.W3`); the mystery about how official truth gets authored by the strong (`Q026.W9`); the oppressed who owe the player nothing (`Q205.W1`, `Q205.W2`); and the suppressed record whose truth we never see win (`Q081.W2`, `Q081.X1`). For Bohemia this is a faction town whose share of the dam's power rests on a buried taking, and a contract that turns into the thread (`Q192.W9`).

### PAIR 4: #181 and #112, THE HANGED MAN CASE. VERDICT: SAME CASE, DIFFERENT QUEST (not a duplicate)

Both studies are the Martinaise murder in Disco Elysium, but they read different threads of it and share almost no findings.

HOW THEY DIFFER.
- #112 (7,000 words, sourced) reads THE BODY: the first obstacle is the smell (`Q112.W1`), a child throwing rocks at the corpse (`Q112.W2`), every route to getting him down costs something ugly (`Q112.W5`, `Q112.N2`), the autopsy is a form and most of it is N/A (`Q112.W6`, `Q112.N3`), the key detail is buried in a joke (`Q112.W7`), and the evidence is not proof until someone says it (`Q112.W10`). The name Klaasje does not appear in it.
- #181 (2,900 words, v2) reads KLAASJE: the truth-teller is guilty of the wrong thing (`Q181.W1`), honesty is fatal, not redemptive (`Q181.W2`), the wrong charge would be true (`Q181.W9`), and the game records, it does not judge (`Q181.W6`).
- They touch at one point: #112 finds the body was carried and staged (`Q112.W7`), and #181's node is the woman who found it and tampered (`Q181.N1`). One reading is the scene; the other is the witness to it.

WHAT #181 ADDS (all of it is new to the library).
- The witness you cannot keep: every response is a distortion, arrest is the wrong charge, release is unauthorized mercy (`Q181.W3`, `Q181.N3`), and she asks to be let go (`Q181.W5`).
- Her running is a whole life (`Q181.W7`), so the weight is a person, not a puzzle.
- Three flaws with laws: her candor can read as one more manipulation (`Q181.X1`) so give the confession an anchor (`Q181.P2`); the outcomes have uneven weight (`Q181.X2`, `Q181.P3`); the consequences arrive late and faint (`Q181.X3`, `Q181.P4`).
- One claim to verify before building from it: #181's third option, turning her over to the people hunting her (`Q181.W8`), has no support anywhere else in the library.

WHAT THE CASE TEACHES THE WIDER LIBRARY. The Hanged Man case is now read from five sides: the whole-game model (`Q005.W1`, the dialogue IS the gameplay; `Q005.W2`, the skills as arguing voices, which #181 uses as the chorus that settles nothing, `Q181.W4`), the body (#112), the witness (#181), the killer, a lonely deserter whose faith outlived its cause (`Q195.W1`, `Q195.W2`), and the child at the body (`Q217.W1`, the armor is the wound, which is #112's rock-throwing kid seen from inside). The Phasmid thread pays the case off with a belief validated against the evidence (`Q062.W3`, `Q062.W8`). For Bohemia this is how one death in a street becomes several contracts from several mouths, each a different person with a different stake, which is what our "a quest is people, places and things" rule asks for.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)

1. For WHAT HAPPENS in a studied quest, cite the sourced reading: `Q123` for Come Fly With Me, `Q122` for Sinnerman, `Q154` for Markarth, `Q112` for the Hanged Man's body. Test: no Bohemia beat is built from `Q190.N2`'s "ground them" fork or `Q193.W3`'s forgiving widow.
2. Cite a later reading only for the lens it adds, by the ids listed under "WHAT IT ADDS" above. Test: every citation of `Q190`, `Q193` names a W, X or P from those lists, not a node's plot.
3. A flaw that one reading banks and another reading answers is a flaw of the summary, not of the quest. Test: before citing `Q190.X3` or `Q193.X2`, check `Q123.W3` and `Q122.W5`.
4. When a builder cites any reading of these quests, they also check its siblings: #79 with #123 and #190; #40 with #122 and #193; #154 with #192; #5, #112, #181, #195 and #217 together. Test: a design citing one Come Fly id cites the ids it relies on from the sourced reading.
5. #181 is a new quest, not a duplicate. Its findings stand on their own and it can be cited freely, except `Q181.W8` until verified.
6. Nothing is deleted or renumbered. Every existing id keeps its meaning; the "SECOND READING" line at the top of each later study is the only change to them.

## WHAT TO AVOID (the flaws)

- A second reading written from a summary drifts on facts and then banks the drift as a flaw of the game: `Q190.X3` (the flock is faceless) and `Q193.X2` (participation is fuzzy) both describe the study, not the quest.
- The library's own warning about reading the wrong surface: players grade the dialogue when the game grades conduct (`Q143.X2`). A reader who grades the short study instead of the sourced one makes the same mistake.
- Double counting. Three readings of Come Fly With Me and three of Sinnerman inflate how often "the doomed faith" and "the filmed death" appear in the library. When a research page counts confirmations, count a quest once.
- The equal-weight flaw appears in three of these readings (`Q190.X1`, `Q181.X2`, `Q193.X1`): the second readings keep finding that one ending is quietly favoured. That repetition is real and is worth carrying: every ending we ship needs its own full resolution.

## OPEN (what the library could not answer)

- Whether #181's "turn her over" option (`Q181.W8`) exists in the game. No other study records it.
- Whether the Sinnerman victim's family ever forgives Joshua. #193 says yes, #122 and #40 record grief and a contract to kill him. A sourced pull on the family scene would settle it.
- Whether any other studies in the 153 to 236 batch re-read an older quest. This page checked the four pairs it was given; a sweep of every study title against every other would find the rest.
- Whether the three Come Fly readings should be folded into one. That is a library-keeping call, not a research one, and nothing here depends on it.
