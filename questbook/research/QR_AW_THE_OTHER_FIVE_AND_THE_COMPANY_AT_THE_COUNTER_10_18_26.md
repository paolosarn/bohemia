## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The five were picked by a word, not by a reading: every one of them contains "beside", and four of the five have no
company member anywhere near a hall card. Reading the whole library found what the word missed. Thirteen designs put
the company at the counter: one member's family on a hall card that nobody wrote (QD-M06, the cousin), two members'
own things sold or redeemed through a stranger's card while the member said nothing first (QD-M02, Teo's father's tool
roll; QD-T07, Tavo's father's rifle), one member's grief said at the counter (QD-AE12, on the ambition shelf), and the
other nine of that shelf, where "the senior member, beside the player at the window" speaks on the offer screen. That shelf is our
own pages disagreeing: QR-M rule 3 (9/28) says no company member speaks on the offer screen before the yes, QR-Z rule
15 (9/30) says ambitions never appear in the hall, and QR-AE rule 16 (10/1) put the company's line on the offer screen
anyway; three of the shelf's ten lines even said the fee aloud. The real world sides with the older rules: a bargaining team
settles its own business in a caucus away from the table, and a person with a family stake in a contract discloses it
to his own side and stays out of the deal. So all thirteen now tell at the fire and leave the stranger alone on the
card. Line 33, run over every camp door (49 bound designs), held on 32 of the 33 that last round stamped, failed on
the one money line still in a veteran's mouth (QD-AU14), and failed on all three of the oldest camp asks (QD-M01,
M05, M07), which no round had ever read against it. Everything is fixed in place.

## 1. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG (measured against the repo)

What the real world says about the second finding (search-result text only; the pages did not open here):
- A BARGAINING TEAM DOES ITS OWN TALKING AWAY FROM THE TABLE. The University of Missouri Extension's collective
  bargaining guide: "Whenever it appears that committee members are advocating conflicting positions, the committees'
  leadership should call for a caucus. Internal conflict should be resolved away from the bargaining table, not in the
  presence of the opposing party." Practitioner guides in the same search say the same in plainer words: one
  spokesperson, and a team's differences "have to be concealed from the other side". Our hall card has a haggle with a
  patience bar (`Q236.N3`). A sergeant who says "that's the one we want" at the window, in front of the client, is the
  team showing its want across the table; the company's fire is its caucus.
- A PERSON WITH A FAMILY STAKE DISCLOSES TO HIS OWN SIDE AND STEPS OUT. Utah Administrative Code R33-24-105, as the
  search returned it: an executive branch employee "may not take part in any procurement process, contracting or
  contract administration decision relating to the employee or a family member of the employee", and "must advise
  their supervisor of the relationship, and must be recused from any discussions or decisions" on it. Two steps,
  disclose then recuse, are exactly the camp door's two halves: the member TELLS his lead in private (the fire), and he
  is NOT on the card (the counter). M06's cousin and T09's widow's son are the textbook case.
- And the fire is where people tell stories (Wiessner 2014, about four in five firelit talks, as QR-AV read it), which
  is why the moved lines open on one.

The library already had a name for the failure: four admirals at a tribunal, "one of them presiding without recusing"
(`Q125.W6`). A company member at the counter for his own family or his own goal is that admiral.

## 4. THE RULES (numbered, testable; the camp door's, extended)

1. NO ONE OF THE COMPANY ON THE OFFER SCREEN, FOR ANY REASON. Not for a member's own stake (QR-AV rule 1) and not for the
   company's goal (QR-M rule 3, kept; QR-AE rule 16 amended). The card is the stranger's: one portrait, the client's
   (`Q236.N2`, `Q108.P3`). Test: inside every offer-screen block, no line names a company member as a speaker (10 on
   main before this round, 0 after).
2. DISCLOSE, THEN RECUSE. A member whose family, thing or past is the subject of a stranger's job tells the player at the
   fire (disclose to his own side) and is not on the card (recuse from the counter), the two steps of a real conflict of
   interest rule. If the hall comes first, he tells at the first fire after the yes, and there must be a fire before the
   job's choice is made (T07's "tomorrow night"). Test: a design whose stranger's job touches a member names a camp
   telling, and the choice comes after it.
3. THE AMBITION LIVES AT THE FIRE. The company's voice (QR-Z 3.1, the most senior living member) tells it the night the
   job is within a day's road, three lines, one [SLEEP]; the town's pin carries the ambition's thing beside the one line
   in the top bar (QR-Z 3.2 item 5); the contract pays the same with or without it (QR-AE rule 16's surviving half). If
   the company reaches the hall first, nothing marks the card, and the contract still counts when done. Test: no
   ambition text on any offer screen; every ambition-advancing design has a camp telling.
4. A TELLING THAT CANNOT CHANGE ANYTHING NEEDS A ROAD THAT CAN. When the member's telling comes after the yes, at least
   one road after the yes lets the player act on it at a stated price (T07's D: the cord, not the rifle). Otherwise the
   member speaks into a decision already made (`Q125.X1`: an argument that could have been made before boarding).
5. AN HEIR'S STAKE READS THE HEIR LAW. A cross-act design that gives a member's descendant a part checks s12 (a man dead
   in act 1 has descendants only if he already had a child) and s11(c) (the act starts with the last act's heirs), and
   writes the read for both a line that carried and one that ended (`Q121.X1`, `Q096.X1`, `Q022.X2`).
6. LINE 33'S READING. "Asks" means an ASK (a member wants something of the player) or a TELLING (a member points at a
   stranger's job). A QUARREL the player walks into (QR-M rule 6, `Q121.W9`) and a RECKONING where the player answers for
   a fight or a death (QD-M04, M08; `Q137.W1`, `Q041.W2`) read n/a, and so does a stranger at the fire (DOOR road) and a
   device (QR-AV rule 12). A price of nothing is still a price: "I'm not paying" goes on the button, not in the mouth.
   Folded into QR-G line 33 this round as its READING.
7. A WORD IS NOT A READING. A list that defines a row is made by reading the designs, never by a search for a word;
   the search may find candidates, and the page says how many it found and how many the reading kept.
