## THE READINGS

RULE 84, AN ACT IS A YEAR. A player spends 300 to 450 days in one act. No mechanic may need more than a year inside one
act: no multi-year clock, no child growing up, no visible aging, no death of old age mid-act. Aging shows BETWEEN acts,
at the generation turn. Injuries of 30 to 40 days, a pregnancy of weeks, map days for a build: all fine. Backstory that
names a span ("ten years since the dollar died", "the anarchy decade" as the era's name, "kin carry it for decades"
across acts) is not a mechanic inside an act and stays. DYNASTY's old-body trade is retired; no quest page leaned on it.

RULE 85, THE FLIP UNLOCKS AT THE FIRST CRISIS. Our pages defaulted (from rule 39c, 9/29) to "the next generation unlocks
at the first home base". That default is dead. The flip opens when the act's FIRST ENDGAME CRISIS starts, around day 80
to 100. Nothing flips before. Designs that land "once that act unlocks" still work; only their trigger text changed.
A home base is still worth taking; it just no longer opens a generation.

RULE 86, THE MAP IS FOR LOOKS. Visible parties, tracks, pins and settlements on the map are fine; a place appearing on
the map when a contract is accepted is fine (it is a pin). Not fine: a map-only gauge, a hidden map stat, a build that
changes what the map can show, or a build drawn on the map or the fight board. What you BUILD lives in the settlement
screen of your base and nowhere else ([built on the board] and [built on the map] are retired). Also from 86: a respec
is learned through dialogue, never listed; stealing exists. The sweep found no design that lists a respec or forbids
stealing, so neither needed an edit.

## WHY THE LIBRARY AGREES

RULE 85, the first crisis as the door:
- A first generation that is a slow stroll before the magic opens is the named failure (`Q067.X4`); the crisis puts
  the door where the act is hottest, not at a quiet capture.
- Too many systems up front nearly lose players (`Q065.X2`, `Q081.X4`); a door at day 80 to 100 leaves the first stretch
  to one person.
- The climb from the real floor (`Q235.W7`) and the start that fades without callbacks (`Q042.X1`) both want the unlock
  earned by surviving something, then calling back.
- The heir is generated from a person you made (`Q049.X1`), never against him without reason (`Q046.X5`); the
  library's unlocks come at a birth, a death or a crisis (`Q069.W3`, `Q144.W9`), which the crisis is closer to than a
  captured base.
- Once open, one era reshapes another (`Q050.W5`), and a payoff lands elsewhere and later (`Q004.W5`, `Q001.W5`,
  `Q024.W8`); the prologue shapes the starting world (`Q025.W6`). The trigger changes; none of this does.
- Show the keys before the door (`Q102.X1`): the crisis is loud and visible, a far better key than a quiet rule.
- Clocks must be seen, never silent (`Q095.P7`, `Q109.W4`, `Q142.P5`, `Q128.P2`).

RULE 84, aging between acts:
- The heir is raised visibly and cheaply (`Q144.N2`), the household outlives officeholders (`Q152.P4`, `Q152.W8`), and
  officers who cross remember the last captain (`Q144.W7`): all of it reads at the TURN, which is where aging now lives.
- A line that ends is felt (`Q144.W3`), and kin carry a choice for decades ACROSS acts (`Q026.W6`, `Q234.W3`,
  `Q234.W8`): decades are fine between acts, never inside one.
- Heir-only ambitions (`Q144.N1`) and a voice that says what the heir never saw (`Q084.W2`) need the gap between acts,
  not a clock inside one. A heir who refuses because the parent was treated badly (`Q081.W4`) and the heirloom
  (`Q119.W7`) cross at the same turn.
- Roster churn as busywork (`Q041.X2`, `Q043.X1`) is what an in-act aging clock would become; injuries stay respectful
  and short (`Q049.X4`).

RULE 86, the map is for looks, the build lives in the settlement screen:
- The ruling chore is a management screen nobody wants (`Q061.X3`); the rocket is still a picture, now on the base's
  screen (`Q079.X2`), and parts stay scarce (`Q065.X1`).
- The outcome changes the world the player meets later (`Q236.W8`) through boards and prices, which live on the
  settlement screen.
- Battle Brothers keeps its hall a list of faces and greys what you cannot take (`Q236.N1`): readable, not a hidden stat.
- Everything carried must be heard and seen (`Q141.X3`): a watcher's spoken report beats a silent range bonus.
- The best branch gated on hidden bookkeeping is the named failure (`Q096.X1`); a lookout that silently stretches the
  map would be that.
- A truth withheld must be a character's choice (`Q034.W8`), and owning what you did matters (`Q215.W9`), which is
  why the watcher tells you to your face. A victory read far away (`Q080.W9`) still works through people.
