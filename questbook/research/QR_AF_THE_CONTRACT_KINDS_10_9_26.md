# QR-AF: EVERY KIND OF JOB BATTLE BROTHERS HANDS OUT, AND WHAT EACH ONE IS IN OUR VALLEY

Battle Brothers has about forty kinds of paid job, and they all boil down to a few verbs: walk somebody somewhere, carry something, kill a group, clear a camp, find a thing, hunt an animal, or hold a place. This page turns each one into a real person in a real part of the valley, says how dangerous it is (one to three skulls), what it pays in, what can go wrong, and which two jobs fit on one trip.

QUESTION: row [the contract kinds] (QUESTS, Paolo 10/9 'taking contracts', rule 51 two contracts, RUN TWO's board with skulls and pay). Every Battle Brothers contract kind as people, places and things in our valley, with skulls, a pay band, who gives it and what can go wrong; a page RUN TWO's board cites.

STATUS: draft:true, research only, nothing built (rule 35). Names of places and people are attempts; Paolo's to change. Every number Battle Brothers gives is quoted with its source; every number of ours is a SHAPE and TUNING owns the value (rule 36, the numbers table).

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The wiki's Contracts page lists 18 civilian kinds, 21 noble kinds and 10 southern kinds (source: wiki dump, CORE_WIKITEXT.md, PAGE Contracts, lines 466 to 594), but they collapse into EIGHT VERBS that QR-AE already uses for pairing: HAUL (escort), CARRY (deliver), CLEAR (drive off, destroy a camp), HUNT (a beast), PATROL (a road), HOLD (defend, last stand, siege), RECOVER (return, obtain, retrieve) and FIND (discover, investigate, a missing person). Battle Brothers' own skeleton is sound and we keep it: the job comes from a place's current trouble (`Q236.W1`), the danger is a glyph read before the yes (`Q236.W3`), the payment mode is a bet on your own company (`Q236.W6`), and the result changes the place (`Q236.W8`); the wiki confirms that last one per kind (an escort leaves the town well supplied, a patrol gives safe roads 33% of the time, a cleared camp lifts ambushed trade routes). We differ in four places on purpose: TWO slots not one (rule 51), DECLINING writes nothing (`Q236.P3`, `Q126.W3`) where Battle Brothers docks relations even for haggling, the fee LOCKS at the offer (`Q148.W1`) so no twist renegotiates it, and the haggle is a face with a spoken ceiling instead of a hidden dice roll players solved on a forum (`Q236.X1`, `Q236.P2`). Battle Brothers' twists fire per kind at 5 to 40% each, so a veteran sees them coming (`Q236.X2`, `Q148.X3`); ours are capped at about one offer in four from QR-C's bank. The noble war and crisis kinds (siege, decisive battle, privateering, holy site) are not daily board work: they belong to the act's crisis and the biggest home bases, behind clout, exactly as Battle Brothers gates them behind renown (`Q236.W9`).

## WHAT BATTLE BROTHERS SAYS, THE NUMBERS (what the shelf actually holds)

1. ONE ACTIVE CONTRACT, pay rises with renown, noble jobs need renown; finishing pays crowns, renown and relations; failing lowers both. Contract targets show a gold coin on the map. Source: wiki dump, CORE_WIKITEXT.md, PAGE Contracts, lines 401 to 406. Also source: Grok, unverified (GROK_10_JOBS_PAY_2026_09_30.md).
2. THE PAY LINE, a real example: nothing up front, 610 on completion, 10 a head for up to 15 heads, 760 total; "more pay" gives 640 + 11 a head = 805; "per head" gives 410 + 20 a head = 710; "advance" gives 150 + 510 + 5 a head = 735. Source: CORE_WIKITEXT.md, PAGE Contracts, the haggling table; also source: Grok, unverified (GROK_10_JOBS_PAY_2026_09_30.md). At our 10 crowns to 1 battery (rule 75d) the plain line is 61 completion + 1 a head, 76 total.
3. THE HAGGLE: each ask adds 3 to 6 annoyance, failure chance is annoyance x 0.1, thrown out at 9; a success raises all pay 4 to 11%; an advance moves 25% of completion and per-head into the advance; each haggle costs 0.5 relations without the Negotiator. Source: CORE_WIKITEXT.md, PAGE Contracts, lines 410 to 425. Already folded into `Q236.N3` and `Q236.W5`.
4. DIFFICULTY: "Contract difficulty scales with the strength of your roster, the difficulty rating (skulls), the type of contract" (quoted by records/target/bb/party_math.json from the wiki dump's Game Mechanics page). Party strength is the 12 best men, each worth 10 plus 2 a level past 1; a contract uses that score, clout does not change party size but unlocks harder twists. Source: Grok, unverified (GROK_111_UNDERBELLY_SPAWN_2026_10_04.md).
5. RELATIONS: betraying a contract minus 100, failing a civilian contract minus 20, cancelling minus 10, a finished noble contract plus 5. Source: Grok, unverified (GROK_118_UNDERBELLY_RELATIONS_2026_10_04.md).
6. THE BOARD IS A BUILDING in the settlement menu ("The elder, the house, the block. One job at a time"). Source: Grok, unverified (GROK_20_SETTLEMENT_MENU_2026_09_30.md). The market refreshes every 3 days "and after some jobs" (CORE_WIKITEXT.md line 251).
7. AMBITIONS AND CLOUT: most ambitions pay 100 renown, a named item or a win against 12 pays 250; "Make Nobles Aware" needs 2 done, 800 renown and the banner, and only then do noble houses offer contracts. Source: Grok, unverified (GROK_123_UNDERBELLY_AMBITIONS_2026_10_08.md).
8. CONTRACT EVENTS are their own event bucket. Source: Grok, unverified (GROK_27_WIKI_BULK_3_2026_09_30.md).

What the shelf does NOT hold: a skull-to-pay formula, duration by kind (only Patrol states 5 days and Privateering 5 days), or offer counts per town. Grok ask 22 (rule 71) is the open request. Until it lands, our pay bands are shapes keyed to RUN TWO's current 30 / 60 / 90 a skull.

## THE SIX RESOURCES AND WHO PAYS IN WHAT (rule 47)

A contract names its pay in one of BATTERIES, FOOD, MEDS, ROUNDS, TAPE, WATER. Batteries are the default. A base pays IN KIND when it is raided or bust (QR-O rule 6; `Q236.P4`), and in kind is drawn from what that base makes: the dam pays water and hours of power as batteries, the farm tower pays food, the chapel clinic pays meds, the cartel yards pay rounds, the depot and the solar field pay tape. QR-L: pay in kind is worth more to the receiver than its battery price when the player is short of that thing, and that is the whole reason to offer it (`Q021.P5` triage, `Q088.W4` the apocalypse as a process with a clock).

## THE ROWS (every kind, grouped by verb)

Each row: BB (the mechanic, numbers quoted), OURS (who, where, what), SKULLS and PAY (shape), WRONG (the one twist and how often), PAIRS (QR-AE), DIFFERENT (what we change).

Home base numbers are QR-O's table: 1 dam, 2 Strip block, 3 data centre, 4 cartel yards, 5 rail and truck depot, 6 chapel town and clinic, 7 tower offices, 8 co-op blocks, 9 squat, 10 solar field, 11 custom, 12 farm tower, 13 wash camp, 14 colourful market. Beasts are rule 42's lab animals only: dire wolves, hyenas, feral dogs, feral cattle, horses and Camelops as mounts; bosses the American lion, the short-faced bear, the sabre-tooth, one mammoth.

### HAUL: walk something alive or valuable to another place

**H1. ESCORT A CARAVAN** (civilian, also southern).
- BB: protect a caravan to its destination; you lose control of movement and must follow it; the caravan feeds you but you still pay wages; losing all donkeys fails it, losing some halves crowns and relations (full renown); finishing makes the destination well supplied. Twists: 5% your men want the cargo, 10% noble troops stop it as stolen goods, 5% a caged prisoner asks to be freed, 10% a monster attack. Source: wiki dump, PAGES_ALL.tar.gz, page "Escort Caravan".
- OURS: the depot's dispatcher (5) or the farm tower's seed boss (12) needs a truck or a Camelops string walked to another home base. The client's face is on the hall wall at the depot; the cargo is a real thing on the map (a box truck with one working headlight). Meets: road crews, the cartel's tax stop, dire wolves on the dirt legs.
- SKULLS 1 to 2. PAY: batteries, plus FOOD covered on the road (Battle Brothers' own rule: the caravan feeds you). Per-cargo pay: each crate that arrives pays its share, so a partial arrival is a partial pay, the wiki's 50% rule made visible.
- WRONG: T13 THE LOOT IS THE BAIT (your own crew eyes the cargo) or T18 A THIRD PARTY SHOWS UP (a faction says the cargo is stolen). One escort in five or fewer.
- PAIRS: HAUL + CLEAR or HUNT near the destination is Paolo's own example, the strongest pair (QR-AE 2.3, rule 51). HAUL + HAUL never.
- DIFFERENT: the march is not stolen. We keep the convoy's speed (slow) but the player still taps where to stop for the night, because a phone game that takes the controls for three minutes reads as broken. The cargo's provenance is real: `Q148.P2` the discoverable lie, a stolen cargo can be found out later.

**H2. ESCORT AN ENVOY** (noble).
- BB: take an envoy to a destination, wait hours while he talks, bring him back; he dies, it fails; 10% a shady man offers a bribe to give him up, taking it flags you a betrayer with that house. Source: wiki dump, PAGES_ALL.tar.gz, "Escort Envoy".
- OURS: a negotiator from the tower offices (7) or the Strip block (2) going to a meeting at a rival base. The envoy is a person with a face and a portrait who walks in the party; he can be struck down in a fight (rule 36's 20% / injury for him too).
- SKULLS 2. PAY: batteries, only on return.
- WRONG: T14 THE BRIBE IS REAL (QR-C, boom), and it is the only escort twist that is a fork rather than a fight. One in five.
- PAIRS: HAUL + FIND at the meeting place is GOOD when the meeting has someone you are looking for.
- DIFFERENT: the betrayal is a deed and the bribe is real money; taking it is minus 100 in Battle Brothers (GROK_118). Ours: the house that hired you stops posting to you, shown on its hall as an empty board, never as a number.

**H3. FREE PRISONERS / RESCUE** (noble crisis, "Free Greenskin Prisoners"; southern "Slave Uprising" inverted).
- BB: search a battle site for clues and free prisoners; 5% one joins you, 5% a powerful weapon, 5% an accident injury, 15% scouts come back. Source: wiki dump, "Free Greenskin Prisoners".
- OURS: the squat (9) asks you to get people out of a cartel holding yard; walk them home. People, not a counter: each freed person is a face who can be hired at the squat's post wall afterward (the 5% "joins you" made a standing door).
- SKULLS 2 to 3. PAY: favours (QR-O: the squat pays in favours) plus a hire.
- WRONG: T21 THE RESCUED DO NOT WANT RESCUE, or T26 THE RESCUE FRAME DELIVERS THE VICTIM (rare, a-person tier). One in six.
- PAIRS: FIND first, then HAUL home.
- DIFFERENT: the rescued remember who walked them out (`Q151.W9` attachment manufactured by labour).

### CARRY: take a thing, not a person

**C1. DELIVER ITEM** (civilian, southern).
- BB: safely deliver cargo to a settlement. 5% the cargo is an evil artifact that starts moving; 10% mercenaries stop you, surrender it or fight; 5% brigands steal it and leave tracks to their lair. Source: wiki dump, "Deliver Item".
- OURS: a sealed cold box from the chapel clinic (6) to the wash camp (13); a drive of data from the data centre (3); a ledger from the tower (7). The thing sits in the bag (rule 76) as an icon you cannot sell.
- SKULLS 1. PAY: batteries; meds when the clinic is the client.
- WRONG: T12 THE CLIENT BECOMES THE CARGO is too heavy here; use T07 THE TARGET KEEPS A SCHEDULE (the receiver is only there at night) or the wiki's own "stolen, tracks to the lair" which turns it into a CLEAR. The data centre's drive is where the ONE WRONG DETAIL lives: the box is warm when it should be cold. One in five.
- PAIRS: CARRY + CLEAR or HUNT near the destination, GOOD. CARRY + CARRY never (QR-AE: "soup").
- DIFFERENT: opening the box is allowed and is a deed; the client may learn (`Q148.P2`).

### CLEAR: a group of people in a place

**K1. DRIVE AWAY BANDITS / BARBARIANS / NOMADS** (civilian, southern "Drive Off Nomads").
- BB: destroy a camp. Twists: 20% the leader visits, extra pay for his head, then 50% bounty hunters demand it; 10% a prisoner joins you; 10% slit their throats or chase them away. Clearing lifts ambushed trade routes (nomads: 50%). Source: wiki dump, "Drive Away Bandits", "Drive Off Nomads". A camp already on the map scales with days, tripling by day 267 (GROK_111).
- OURS: a road crew camped in a dead strip mall off the 95. Given by whichever base sits on the road it chokes: the co-op (8), the depot (5), the farm tower (12). Meets the road-crew faction of rule 75b's tiers.
- SKULLS 1 to 3 by camp size. PAY: batteries, with PER HEAD offered only here (QR-N rule 8, capped by the stated group).
- WRONG: T18 A THIRD PARTY SHOWS UP (another crew wants the leader's head) or T08 THE CLUE IS MISREAD ONCE (the camp is not the crew that hit the road). The 10% throat-or-chase choice is not a twist, it is the end screen, and we keep it every time (`Q008.W8` the approach is a moral stance).
- PAIRS: CLEAR + HAUL or CARRY to the same base, GOOD. CLEAR + CLEAR never (two tough fights, rule 40 fight length).
- DIFFERENT: the road the camp sat on gets safer for everyone, visibly: fewer roaming parties on that leg for a refresh (`Q089.W6` paths smooth as people walk them).

**K2. DESTROY A CAMP** (noble: "Destroy Orc Camp", "Destroy Goblin Camp", "Root Out Undead").
- BB: a bigger camp for a house. 10% two factions already fighting there; 5% a prisoner; 75% a betrayer gets a noble force sent after him. Root Out Undead reveals the 2 closest locations, 10% chase two necromancers. Source: wiki dump pages of those names.
- OURS: the cartel yards (4) want a rival's chop shop burned; the dam (1) wants the intake squatters out. Root Out maps to rule 42's necromancer translation: a man reviving his robots in a dead server farm; the data centre (3) never posts that one, its rival does.
- SKULLS 2 to 3. PAY: batteries, or ROUNDS from the cartel.
- WRONG: T17 THE CLIENT LIED (the camp is a family, not a crew; `Q148.P3`-style wrong conclusion). One in four.
- PAIRS: GOOD after a HAUL to the same quarter.
- DIFFERENT: a camp destroyed is a PART you can take (rule 43). The offer screen says so in one line when it is true.

**K3. MARAUDING / INTERCEPT RAIDING PARTIES** (noble, crisis).
- BB: kill raiders around the settlement, whoever's camp is nearest; 5% a champion with them; 5% a scared merchant pays to be walked home. Intercept: 2 to 3 waves raiding the attached locations, burned all fails, some burned halves pay; 50% the villagers give trade goods or food after. Source: wiki dump, "Marauding Greenskins", "Intercept Raiding Parties".
- OURS: the co-op (8) or the farm tower (12) during a bust, raiders hitting the greenhouses at the edge. Rule 68: you SEE the raiding party on the map before it lands; this contract is the base paying you to catch it.
- SKULLS 2. PAY: batteries, plus FOOD from the grateful (the wiki's 50% made a line on the hand-in).
- WRONG: T29 SUCCESS AT A COST (one greenhouse burns whatever you do). One in four.
- PAIRS: fine with a PATROL; never with HOLD.
- DIFFERENT: the burned lot stays burned on the settlement screen until rebuilt (rule 43, the future goes both ways).

**K4. RAZE A LOCATION / RAID A CARAVAN / PRIVATEERING** (noble, war).
- BB: raze location A near settlement B; each escapee is a 10% chance to fail with no pay; you choose to sweep or encircle; the place gets "raided" for 3 days. Privateering: 5 days, raze 5 points, troops 2, 10 to 14 completes, 15+ doubles reputation; 15% a rival offers a named weapon to switch sides. Source: wiki dump, "Raze Attached Location", "Privateering".
- OURS: the dirty work. Only the cartel (4) and the Strip block (2) post it, and only once your clout is high. The target is another home base's outlying lot: real people, so the twist is built in.
- SKULLS 2 to 3. PAY: batteries, high.
- WRONG: every raze carries T25 THE OBVIOUS GOOD IS THE TRAP's mirror, the faces of who lived there, on the end screen. Not a twist, the cost.
- PAIRS: never with a job for the base you are raiding (QR-AE 2.5: opposite clients are a fork, not a pair).
- DIFFERENT: Battle Brothers lets a raze be routine. Ours writes a deed at the raided base that its board remembers for the act (QR-A: deeds are remembered, declines are not).

### HUNT: an animal

**B1. ROAMING BEASTS** (civilian; southern desert version).
- BB: the base's people are disappearing; 35% direwolves, 30% nachzehrers, 30% webknechts, 5% bandits dressed as wolves. Clears "disappearing villagers". Source: wiki dump, "Roaming Beasts".
- OURS: the wash camp (13) or the farm tower (12): something is taking people at night. Rule 42 only: dire wolves, hyenas, feral dogs. The 5% "bandits dressed as wolves" is ours word for word, a crew using a dog pack as cover.
- SKULLS 1 to 2. PAY: batteries or water (the wash camp has nothing else).
- WRONG: the wiki's own 5% disguise, as T03 THE PRETEXT DROPS. One in twenty, deliberately rare.
- PAIRS: HUNT + HAUL GOOD (QR-AE). HUNT + HUNT never.
- DIFFERENT: the animals are from one lab (rule 42); the client's last line can point at the lab, never explain it.

**B2. HUNTING [A BEAST KIND]** (civilian: Alps, Hexen, Lindwurms, Schrats, Unholds, Webknechts; southern: Ifrits, Serpents).
- BB: a named beast type near the base; Unholds: 40% you can scare them off if you have a wildman, beast slayer, barbarian or a dumb brother; clears the matching "attacks" situation. Source: wiki dump, "Hunting Unholds" and siblings. The skin page maps beast jobs to lab animals with mechanics unchanged: source: Grok, unverified (GROK_08_BEAST_SKINS_2026_09_30.md).
- OURS: one row per rule-42 animal: feral cattle trampling the farm tower's beds (12), a hyena clan in the depot yard (5), a sabre-tooth at the solar field's pylons (10). The background that can scare it off is ours: a ranch hand with cattle, an animal-lab tech with the big cats.
- SKULLS 1 (cattle, dogs) to 3 (a boss animal). PAY: batteries, plus the hide or the bone for the armourer (rule 76's bag: an attachment item).
- WRONG: T23 THE THING YOU WERE SENT FOR IS A PERSON's animal form: the beast is a mother with young (`Q148.W6`, `Q148.W7`). Rarest, one in ten hunts at most (`Q148.X3`).
- PAIRS: GOOD after a HAUL to the same base.
- DIFFERENT: the scare-off path is a real verb from preparation (`Q148.W4`, `Q148.P5`), not a dice roll.

**B3. BIG GAME HUNT** (noble).
- BB: a house pays for the dangerous trophy, three tiers of beast. Source: wiki dump, "Big Game Hunt".
- OURS: the Strip block (2) wants a mammoth tusk or a lion pelt for a table. The one mammoth (rule 42) is never a contract, it is a boss verb; this posts the lion, the bear, the sabre-tooth.
- SKULLS 3. PAY: batteries, the highest band of any hunt.
- WRONG: none by default. A trophy job is honest (QR-C rule 1).
- PAIRS: alone; it is a tough fight (8 to 15 minutes, rule 40).
- DIFFERENT: we say plainly that the client wants a trophy, and the bear's den is on the map before the yes.

### PATROL: walk a route and come back

**P1. PATROL** (noble).
- BB: patrol two settlements and return; paid for up to 20 to 35 heads of any enemy; 5 days; the closest camps spawn roaming parties while you are on it; 10% a scouting report about a crucified man; 33% the patrolled place gets safe roads. Source: wiki dump, "Patrol".
- OURS: the dam (1) pays you to walk the line from the dam road to two pump stations and back. The heads are counted as crews stopped, not body parts (runway valley, not grimdark trophies).
- SKULLS 2. PAY: per head capped, the only kind besides CLEAR where per head is right. DURATION: Battle Brothers' 5 days is the shape; TUNING sets ours.
- WRONG: the wiki's 10% crucified man is a perfect ANALOG HORROR line, ordinary road, one wrong thing, never explained: kept as the patrol's road event, not a twist.
- PAIRS: PATROL + anything fine; PATROL + PATROL never.
- DIFFERENT: rule 68, the parties the patrol spawns are drawn and visible, never a popup.

### HOLD: be at a place at a time

**D1. DEFEND SETTLEMENT** (civilian: Bandits, Greenskins).
- BB: up to three parties attack; you face whoever is nearest; 20% a kidnapping you must chase; 20% the militia takes your orders; burned attached locations cut pay; fail and the place is "raided". Source: wiki dump, "Defend Settlement Bandits".
- OURS: any base you are at when rule 68's raiding party is closing. The client is the base's elder on the hall, the militia are faces from the post wall. Meets the nearest faction crew.
- SKULLS 2 to 3. PAY: batteries; in kind if the base is already bust.
- WRONG: the wiki's 20% kidnapping as T10 THE CLOCK ARRIVES. One in five.
- PAIRS: HOLD + anything is risky (QR-AE: an appointment), allowed only when the phone shows the days and the other job ends first.
- DIFFERENT: a HOLD is the one kind that can be declined and still happen. Declining writes nothing, but the raid lands anyway and the base may fall (third votes, the future goes both ways). That is world-state, not a punishment (QR-A finding 3).

**D2. LAST STAND / HOLD FORTRESS / BREAK SIEGE** (noble and southern, crisis only).
- BB: Last Stand: 3 waves, militia help, the fewer militia die the more join the next wave; 40% the dead are already at the walls; 30% an extra wave. Hold Fortress: 2 to 3 waves, stray and you fail, 20% they retreat after wave 2, 30% an ultimatum to switch sides. Break Siege: 2 to 3 raiding parties and 1 to 2 siege engine camps, resupply in the besieged town freely. Source: wiki dump, "Last Stand", "Hold Fortress", "Break Greenskin Siege".
- OURS: the act's crisis, at a home base you OWN (rule 43). QR-R holds the siege design. `Q151.W6`: the siege happens where your hours live, so the best hold is your own built lots.
- SKULLS 3. PAY: batteries and standing; the real pay is the base survives.
- WRONG: the ultimatum (T22 THE BETRAYAL THAT ARMS YOU) on Hold Fortress only.
- PAIRS: never paired.
- DIFFERENT: we cap it at three waves and a tough fight's 15 minutes total (rule 40).

**D3. SIEGE FORTIFICATION / DECISIVE BATTLE / HOLY SITE** (noble war, southern holy war).
- BB: Siege: 50% assault the castle, 50% hold the siege line, with a secret-passage assassination branch. Decisive Battle: two preliminary missions on two mornings (scouts, requisition a farm where farmers 33% fight, 33% want pay, 33% cooperate), then the battle. Source: wiki dump, "Siege Fortification", "Decisive Battle".
- OURS: the earth-side crisis lines of each act, never board filler. The requisition step is the realest economic-crash scene Battle Brothers has: an army taking a farm's food, and the farmers' three answers. Kept as an event.
- SKULLS 3. PAY: per QR-H, the main line's.
- DIFFERENT: these are MAIN-LINE BEATS in our game (one main quest, rule 51), not contracts.

### RECOVER: take back a stolen thing

**R1. RETURN ITEM** (civilian).
- BB: get back stolen property; 70% brigands, 15% a necromancer with brigands, 15% a shady man offers more for it, taking it fails the contract. Source: wiki dump, "Return Item".
- OURS: the co-op's (8) ration ledger, the chapel's (6) generator coil, a family's photo drive. A thing with a history.
- SKULLS 1 to 2. PAY: batteries.
- WRONG: the wiki's 15% better offer is T14 THE BRIBE IS REAL; the lie voids the contract (QR-C finding 9). One in six.
- PAIRS: RECOVER + CLEAR GOOD (the thieves are the camp), RECOVER + FIND GOOD.
- DIFFERENT: provenance is a discoverable fact (`Q148.P2`).

**R2. OBTAIN ITEM / FIND ARTIFACT** (civilian, noble crisis).
- BB: Obtain: get a relic from an ancient-dead site; you hold it before the fight and can retreat; 10% an ancient weapon on the floor, 50% you skip the fight. Find Artifact: 54% fight an army then get it, 20% wrong place, 16% a map elsewhere, 5% a trap injury, 5% someone took it first. Source: wiki dump, "Obtain Item", "Find Artifact".
- OURS: the lab (rule 42) and the Amalgamation's old server rooms are our "ancient sites". A drive, a culture sample, a key card. The data centre (3) posts it; the offer pays too well (QR-O's bust shape for 3).
- SKULLS 2 to 3. PAY: batteries, high.
- WRONG: T23's machine form, `Q016.W1` the MacGuffin is a person: the drive holds somebody. Rarest; it brushes the shadow secret, and the Amalgamation's threat scales with proximity to it.
- PAIRS: RECOVER + HAUL out, fine.
- DIFFERENT: walking out with the thing before the fight is kept (Battle Brothers' own mercy for a weak company).

**R3. RESTORE LOCATION** (civilian).
- BB: clear a ruin near the town, go back, escort the workers out. 15% no fight, 15% refugees, 30% beasts, 40% brigands. Source: wiki dump, "Restore Location".
- OURS: the best fit in the whole list for "the future gets better": the solar field (10) wants a dead pylon row cleared and its crew walked out to it; the farm tower (12) a flooded greenhouse. Done, the lot becomes a buildable lot (rule 43).
- SKULLS 1 to 2. PAY: batteries, plus the lot.
- WRONG: the wiki's refugees as T21 (the squatters live there). One in six.
- PAIRS: CLEAR then HAUL in one trip, GOOD.
- DIFFERENT: the restored place shows on the settlement screen the next visit (`Q236.P4`).

### FIND: a place, a person, the truth

**F1. DISCOVER LOCATION** (civilian).
- BB: find a place in a region in a direction; you get directions if lost; 15% a man asks you to keep it secret; 20% the altruist's directions lead into an ambush. Source: wiki dump, "Discover Location".
- OURS: a wash-camp (13) elder wants the old cistern under a casino found; the depot (5) a lost truck (QR-O's "find a lost one").
- SKULLS 1. PAY: batteries or water.
- WRONG: the 15% "keep it secret" is T14; the 20% trap is T22. One in five combined.
- PAIRS: FIND + HAUL GOOD.
- DIFFERENT: no gold coin on the map; the client describes it in words with landmarks (QR-E: the ask in a place).

**F2. INVESTIGATE CEMETERY** (civilian).
- BB: secure a graveyard; 10% graverobbers trade a treasure map for their freedom (50% you still get half pay); 30% nachzehrers, 30% brigands, 30% zombies; about 25% a follow-up contract to hunt the necromancer; clears "terrified villagers". Source: wiki dump, "Investigate Cemetery".
- OURS: the chapel (6) asks why the dead in its lot are dug up at night. Our answer is realism: scrap thieves after gold teeth and pacemaker batteries (batteries are the money). The follow-up is a real chain into a HUNT or a CLEAR.
- SKULLS 1 to 2. PAY: meds or batteries.
- WRONG: `Q148.W3` the evidence built to be misread once (T08). One in four.
- PAIRS: FIND + RECOVER GOOD.
- DIFFERENT: an investigation can conclude wrong (`Q148.P3`, against `Q148.X2`).

**F3. FIND A MISSING PERSON** (not a Battle Brothers kind by name; its "kidnapping" twist and our RUN TWO offer 'Find the Ortiz kid' make it one).
- BB: only as a twist (Defend Settlement 20% kidnapping).
- OURS: QR-O gives it to the co-op, the wash camp and the tower. `Q002.W1` one node, many quests: the missing son fans out.
- SKULLS 2. PAY: batteries.

## THE FINDINGS (what the rows teach, with the library)

### 1. Battle Brothers' kinds are verbs plus a place, and the place's trouble writes them
Every kind on the wiki clears a settlement situation when done (Escort: well supplied; Patrol: safe roads; Drive Away: ambushed trade routes; Roaming Beasts: disappearing villagers; Investigate: terrified villagers; Last Stand: besieged). That is `Q236.W1` and `Q236.W8` confirmed kind by kind, and QR-S's catalogue is the right parent: the situation makes the offer (`Q044.P2` the storyteller reads the district, `Q044.W7` relief after a hard hit). `Q085.P7` and `Q151.W3`: prosperity draws raids, so a rich base posts HOLD and a poor base posts FIND.

### 2. Their twist rates are fixed per kind, and that is why veterans see them coming
The wiki prints them: 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 30%, 40%. Fixed percentages per kind make the twist a template (`Q236.X2`, `Q148.X3`, `Q030.X3` repetition over a long loop). QR-C's answer: draw the twist from the base's history and the boom or bust (`Q148.P4` ration the somebody), not from a per-kind dice table.

### 3. The best twists in their list are ours already
Bandits dressed as wolves, a crucified man on a patrol, a graverobber trading a map, the leader of the indebted bargaining for freedom, an army requisitioning a farm and the farmers' three answers. Each is an ordinary thing with one wrong detail. They are kept as text, re-skinned (`Q040.W3` the escort that isn't; `Q009.W2` the mundane hook).

### 4. Battle Brothers punishes the haggle and the no; we do not
Each haggle costs 0.5 relations and a thrown-out offer is a "substantial" penalty (CORE_WIKITEXT.md). `Q236.X4` the crunch forces the yes. Our law: decline and thrown-out write nothing (`Q126.W3`, against `Q084.X5` and `Q131.X7`), the ceiling is spoken (`Q236.P2`, `Q107.X1`, `Q130.W2`), an advance is shown as a loan with no rounding loss (`Q236.P5`, `Q121.W7`).

### 5. Losing control of the march is their worst escort feature
The wiki says it outright: "The player will lose control of movement". `Q084.X2` names escort as the middle-game graveyard. `Q147.P3` delivery under fire is the version worth keeping: the escort's drama is in the fight, not the walk.

### 6. Partial success is a real outcome
Escort, Intercept, Defend and Raze all have a "poorly complete" line (50% crowns and relations, full renown). The clean version is `Q041.W6` the greed lever and QR-C T29 success at a cost: the hand-in shows what arrived, and pays for that.

### 7. Time is a resource, but HOLD jobs are appointments
Patrol and Privateering run 5 days; Decisive Battle runs on mornings. `Q011.P3` failable by time, `Q021.W1` time as something to lose, `Q119.W3` the medicine clock: deadlines belong to HOLD and to bust CARRY jobs only, and QR-AE's "risky" cell is why.

### 8. Their crisis kinds are our main line
Siege, Decisive Battle, Holy Site, Last Stand only exist during a late-game crisis. With one main quest (rule 51), those beats are the act's crisis, not slot jobs (`Q151.W6`, `Q055.P6` the story pivots on system state).

### 9. A job's end screen is where the character is
Throat or chase, freedom or contract, sell the cargo or deliver it: `Q008.W8` the approach is a moral stance, `Q008.W7` force is valid with a cost, `Q148.W1` the fee settled before the truth keeps the coin out of it.

### 10. Repeat foes give the board a memory
A crew you drove off three times and spared can return with a name (`Q048.P2`), and the difficulty curve follows the company (`Q044.P3`, `Q049.P6`) the way Battle Brothers' party math uses the 12 best men (GROK_111).
