## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

A Battle Brothers ambition is the company deciding, out loud, what it wants next: every so often, when no ambition
is open, the game offers three or four goals [V] (hire a dozen men, save 2,000 crowns for a battle standard, ally a
noble house, win a three-skull contract, own a named weapon), the player picks one or picks nothing, and finishing it
gives at least 100 renown and lifts the mood of every man [V], sometimes with a real object (a cart for more carrying
room, a banner, noble armour) [V]. There is no timer [R]; cancelling costs a morale hit to about half the men [V]; "no
ambition" becomes a choice only later and buys about three days of quiet [V]. It is the lightest piece of direction in
the game: it tells a sandbox player what a good next week looks like without a quest log. The library agrees that an
open world needs exactly this (`Q055.X3`, `Q045.X2`, `Q061.W3`), and the one study whose opening node IS an ambition
pick (`Q144.N1`) adds the lesson Battle Brothers never needed, because Battle Brothers has one generation: in a
three-generation game, AN AMBITION IS THE FIRST CLAUSE OF A WILL. What it banks is what the heir inherits, so money
ambitions build the worst estate and ambitions about people, places and knowledge build the best (`Q144.N1`,
`Q144.W2`, `Q144.X2`). For Bohemia: the company's most senior voice (a face and a mouth, never a pop-up) offers three
goals at camp or at a home base, declining is free and says nothing, finishing gives standing, mood and ONE THING YOU
CAN SEE (a banner on the base, a named coat, a held block), and an ambition open when the generation turns is offered
to the heir as the grandparent's unfinished business, which the heir may take or leave. PEOPLE gets 26 drafts below
across the seven kinds the brief names.

## 1. WHAT BATTLE BROTHERS' AMBITIONS ARE (THE SOURCE, TAGGED)

### 1.1 The machine
- WHEN: whenever the company has no open ambition, after a wait, the company offers new ones [V]. After a completed or
  cancelled ambition the wait is about one to two days [V]; after "no ambition" about three days [V] (one Steam thread
  says roughly a week, and players report 7 to 20 days in some saves [V]: the number is not stable across versions).
- HOW MANY: three or four at once [V]. The list is drawn from a pool whose entries have preconditions (campaign age,
  a prior ambition done, relations with a house) [V].
- HOW IT LOOKS: an event-style screen with a line in the company's voice, one per option ("We shall get the company
  strength to a dozen men! It will make us a formidable force and will allow us to take on more profitable work." [V]).
  The open ambition then sits in a small slot on the world map's top bar [R].
- THE NO: "No Ambition" appears as an option only after a handful of ambitions (after "Make Nobles Aware") [V]. Before
  that, the player must pick one. This is the single most criticised detail (see 1.4).
- TIME: no deadline [R]. An ambition waits as long as the player needs.
- CANCEL: allowed any time; the "Ambition Failed" event gives about 50% of the men a bad mood [V], and the leader's word
  is doubted [V].
- REWARD: every ambition gives at least 100 renown and lifts the mood of the whole company [V]. Some add an object: the
  cart and the wagon (inventory room, 7,500 and 15,000 crowns) [V], the battle standard (a banner carried in the fight)
  [V, the 2,000-crown goal], trophies after beast and greenskin ambitions [V], and the noble alliance's armour (a
  decorated helm, a heraldic hauberk, shields) [V].

### 1.2 The pool (named on the wiki list, [V] unless marked)
Roster of 12, 16, 20; Have 2,750 renown ("Glorious") and 8,000 renown ("Invincible"); Make Nobles Aware (a noble
contract); Allied with a Noble House (30 days in, Make Nobles Aware done, relations "Open" or better); Named Item;
Have Armor Upgrades; Hire Follower; More Contracts; Fulfill Southern Contracts; Win Arena Fights; Visit Settlements;
Discover All Unique Locations; Allied Civilians; Defeat Kraken; Taxidermist Crafting; the crown-savings goals (battle
standard, cart, wagon). Recalled, not re-verified: defeat a named champion, win a three-skull contract, have a
sergeant (a man with the rally perk) [R].

### 1.3 What the pool is made of (our reading)
Sort the list and it is six kinds: THE COMPANY'S SIZE (roster 12/16/20), ITS NAME (renown tiers, make nobles aware),
ITS KIT (named item, armour upgrades, standard, cart), ITS FRIENDS (noble ally, civilians, followers), ITS REACH
(visit settlements, discover locations, southern contracts) and ITS TROPHIES (kraken, beasts, arena). Every one is
checkable by the game from state it already keeps, which is why the system is cheap: no ambition needs a script.

### 1.4 What players say is wrong with it [V, from the Steam threads]
- FORCED PLAYSTYLE: "whenever the ambition window pops up, players must drop what they're doing to complete it, or
  face a morale hit." The goal meant as light guidance feels like an order.
- NO REAL NO early: you cannot say "nothing" until several are done.
- SOLVED: after a few, players know the pool and pre-stage the conditions; the choice becomes a checklist.
- SOME ARE A WALL: "amass 10,000 crowns" is hard with wages running, so the player grinds.
- THE LEADER DOUBTED: failing makes the men "question your leadership", which some players hate as a guilt device.

## 2. THE FINDINGS FROM THE LIBRARY (GROUPED, EACH WITH ITS IDS)

### 2.1 An open world needs a light goal, and the player should author it
- Total openness leaves many players aimless: "no victory conditions... more fun to watch than play"; the fix is to
  OFFER optional goals as a spine the free play hangs on (`Q055.X3`). The same lesson from deep simulation: pure
  emergence without legible goals drowns people (`Q045.X2`).
- The strongest version lets the player pick the dream (merchant, mercenary, vassal, conqueror) and the freedom makes
  the story (`Q061.W3`); the port is that the player authors his own ambition UNDER the authored spine (`Q061.P3`).
  In Bohemia the spine is the main quest and the Amalgamation; ambitions are the player's own sentences beside it.
- Reputation as the ladder, not XP (`Q061.W4`), is exactly Battle Brothers' renown gating the hall (`Q236.W9`): an
  ambition that raises standing is a key to bigger clients, so the goal feeds the contract loop without a quest log.
- Side content is there to make a place feel alive, not to fill a checklist (`Q013.W1`); a goal the world can see
  being pursued (the banner going up, the new hires at the fire) is sense-of-place, not a tick box.
- A no-marker world keeps its goals in people's mouths, item text and places (`Q023.W1`); an ambition is the company
  saying what it wants, which fits that exactly: it is a line from a person, not a log entry.

### 2.2 In a generational game, the ambition is the will's first clause
- The one ambition pick in the library (`Q144.N1`): three life-goals, each a different will-shape. "Wealth: retire rich"
  banks money, "the least heritable substance in the game", the trap-shaped ambition; "your father's bones: a search"
  banks geography and story, the best estate; "make something" banks fragments and can END a life well. Later
  ambitions unlock only from a prior life's discoveries: "some life-goals can only be CONCEIVED by a dynasty, not a
  founder". This is the page's central finding.
- The will is written by living, not by a menu; the death screen only reads what the life already wrote (`Q144.W1`).
- Knowledge is the estate's gold standard (`Q144.W2`); the heritable estate must be experiences, never grindable labour
  (`Q144.X2`, `Q144.P2`). An ambition whose reward is a pile of batteries fails this; one whose reward is a held block,
  a bond, a named object or a thing learned passes.
- Open ambitions die with the captain and that refusal is what makes the line real (`Q144.W3`). OUR BEND: our main
  character never dies (third votes) and the generation turns by a transition, so an open ambition does not have to
  die; it can be OFFERED to the heir, which keeps the weight (the grandparent did not finish) without the waste.
- Completing an ambition can end a line alive, retirement as the rare alternative to legacy (`Q144.W8`). For us: a
  finished founder ambition can be the thing that makes the next generation's start better, the future getting better
  than the ruin by the family's own hand.
- The heir is a person, not a respawn (`Q144.W4`), paid for with present strength (`Q144.W5`, `Q144.P4`), and the
  raising of the scion is its own recurring cost (`Q144.N2`). A family ambition ("raise the heir at the base") is
  already designed in the library as a costed line.
- An officer's arc can freeze mid-way when its captain goes; bonded officers carry resolutions across (`Q144.N3`), and
  inherited people speak of the last captain in past tense (`Q144.W7`). The reading of the estate is a scene
  (`Q144.N4`, `Q144.W10`, the chronicle frame). An inherited ambition should come from such a mouth, in past tense.
- The second life proves the first happened (`Q144.W9`): an heir finishing a grandparent's ambition is the player
  feeling his own inheritance without a UI claim.
- The family levels up across generations above any single ruler (`Q046.W7`, `Q049.W2`), and the meta is best as a
  HOME the family visibly builds (`Q049.W8`). An ambition's reward belongs on the home base where the heir will see it.
- Death is expected and passes a legacy (`Q066.W2`, `Q066.W7`); the legacy move is spending a life on someone else's
  future (`Q073.W7`); the player-character may be the parent who enables the child, not the hero (`Q069.W6`). The best
  third-generation ambitions are ones the founder could never have finished.
- The personal arc inside the strategy (`Q046.W10`): rulers carry their own agendas; a named ambition per generation
  is how the player attaches to a person, not a spreadsheet. The chronicle should be retellable (`Q044.P7`).

### 2.3 The dark side of an inherited ambition (the counterfeit family's hook)
- An ancestor's ambition that outlives her body and lies in wait for the living (`Q199.W3`) is "every bloodline's buried
  fear made flesh" (`Q199.W9`). The worst legacy is the template the parent leaves (`Q219.W7`).
- On the far side of an ambition is emptiness: what a life is for once it's won (`Q216.W10`). A finished ambition must
  hand the player a new question, not a trophy shelf.
- The quest's answer to the perfect lie: freedom is choosing the worse true thing (`Q174.W8`). THIS IS WHERE THE
  AMALGAMATION ENTERS: a sim that offers to "finish grandmother's ambition for her", in her voice, is the counterfeit
  family's purest move. The player declining it and finishing it by hand is the core theme in one choice.

### 2.4 What finishing should give
- A title without verbs is a hat (`Q152.X1`): never grant a status without granting what it lets you DO. Renown in
  Battle Brothers passes because it opens the noble contracts (`Q236.W9`, `Q061.W4`).
- A max-effort reward that is a lecture turns the thesis into a trophy (`Q113.X6`). Rewards are objects and doors.
- The world's first acknowledgment of you can arrive as a bill, not a fanfare (`Q151.W2`): recognition in Bohemia is a
  thing the world does back (a collector at the gate, a rival company pricing you), not a banner saying "achieved".
- Reputation titles that change how the world treats you are a consequence ledger (`Q038.W7`). "Glorious" in Battle
  Brothers is this. Our version is how clients in the hall greet the company.
- A heirloom can be pointless and unrecyclable and still carry the most weight (`Q119.W7`): a named coat or a family
  ring as an ambition's reward is right, and it must never be sellable scrap.
- Arrival you figured out yourself is an achievement a dot destroys (`Q037.W5`): an ambition names a goal, never a
  route.

### 2.5 The clocks, the grind and the checklist (the traps)
- A failable hard clock alienates (`Q038.X1`); pressure that only punishes makes despair, not commitment (`Q206.X2`); no
  hard fail and no timer respects the player's pace (`Q019.W8`). Battle Brothers' ambitions have no timer [R]; ours
  must not either.
- Clocks that track you AND the world make a living map (`Q065.W6`); an ambition is the player's own clock on the
  company's projects, the one clock that belongs to him.
- Grind as the price of a goal turns legacy into a spreadsheet (`Q144.X2`), attrition needs pacing (`Q041.X2`), and an
  endlessly scaling goal with no destination discourages (`Q049.X2`). Humour and heart soften a grind (`Q049.W10`).
- Checklist morality fails when intent is laundered through bookkeeping (`Q140.X2`); a completion incentive must never
  side with a moral fork's dark branch (`Q150.X1`, `Q150.P4`: "the checklist never votes"). An ambition list is a
  checklist by nature, so no ambition may reward a cruelty (kill N of a people, betray a client) that a fork elsewhere
  asks the player to weigh.
- Unmarked content turns players into guide-readers who experience your writing in a list (`Q114.X3`). Battle
  Brothers' pool being solved [V] is the same failure; rotate lines and hide half the pool behind prior deeds.
- A collectible in a grave room destroys the room (`Q125.X7`): an ambition reward must not fire during a death scene.
- Overreach is its own story: chasing a new project while the basics decay (`Q045.W7`). An ambition to hold three new
  blocks while food runs out should be allowed and should hurt, which is the realism-first answer.
- The top of a rise must stay fun; success must not curdle into admin (`Q061.X3`). Late ambitions ask for deeds, not
  upkeep.

### 2.6 Ambitions are small, human and specific
- A man's life ambition is to cross a bridge without getting beaten up, and he pays you in the ability to see danger
  (`Q114.W10`). The best ambitions are small enough to say in one breath.
- A wish is a wording problem: ambition is the trap when the words are loose (`Q127.W8`). Our lines must say exactly
  what counts.
- The first task that pulls a life back into motion must carry purpose, never busywork (`Q235.X3`); the founder's first
  ambition should be tied to the ruin he starts in.
- A verdict carried for generations must be shown and felt (`Q234.X3`); an inherited ambition must be visible on the
  map or the base (the half-built wall, the empty flagpole), unfinished business as geography (`Q147.W7`).

### 2.7 How it meets the contract loop (study 236)
- The hall is the world's current problems (`Q236.W1`); ambitions are the company's own wants. Keep them apart: an
  ambition never appears in the hall.
- One contract at a time (`Q236.W2`); one ambition at a time, the same legibility.
- The skulls are a reading before the yes (`Q236.W3`): each ambition shows its size (a small, a long, a lifetime mark).
- The accept button is the only promise (`Q236.W7`), declining writes nothing (`Q236.P3`).
- The wage treadmill makes every offer matter (`Q236.W10`) and can force the yes (`Q236.X4`). An ambition must never be
  a second treadmill: it is the thing the player does BECAUSE he chose to.
- The client as a vending machine (`Q236.X3`) has its twin here: an ambition read as a menu item. The fix is the same:
  a person with a want says it.

## 3. THE SHAPE FOR PEOPLE

### 3.1 Who offers it (a mouth, never a pop-up)
The COMPANY'S VOICE: the most senior living member of the company (not the player, who does not speak Spanglish), at a
fire on the map when the party rests, or on the home base's settlement screen when the company is home. It is a Battle
Brothers event screen (a face, a line, the choices), which rule 33 s2 already allows on the road; never in the first
minute, never while a contract's fight is live. The narrator (rule 45) may read the one framing line above the face
("The fire is low. Somebody has been thinking.") but the ambition itself comes from the veteran's mouth, in the
Animalese-style squiggle with the text under the portrait. If the senior member dies (10% veteran rule, or 20% struck
down), the next most senior takes the fire; the ambition they offer can mention the dead one (past tense, `Q144.W7`).

### 3.2 How the list is offered
1. Three offers at once (Battle Brothers' three or four [V], cut to three for a phone screen), plus a fourth line that
   is always there from the very first offer: "Not now." Declining is free from the start, which fixes the worst
   Battle Brothers complaint [V].
2. Each offer shows its SIZE as one glyph (one tick: a week or two of map time; two: a season; three: a lifetime or
   longer), the way skulls read a contract (`Q236.W3`).
3. Draws: one COMPANY or RESOURCE ambition, one PLACE or BEAST ambition, one FAMILY, RUNWAY or SECRET ambition, so every
   offer spans the three kinds of want. Offers whose conditions are already met are never drawn (no free tick).
4. After "Not now" nothing happens for about three in-game days [V, BB's own number] and nothing is written. After a
   completed ambition, one or two days [V].
5. One open ambition at a time. It sits as a single short line in the top bar on the map and the base (UI owns the
   look), and the company's voice mentions it now and then at rest (never a nag).
6. No timer. Ever.

### 3.3 Dropping an open ambition
Battle Brothers gives half the men a bad mood [V] and players read it as guilt [V]. Our contract law says only a
contract taken and dropped is a deed, and an ambition is a promise to your own people, so dropping it is a small deed:
the veteran says one line ("Bueno. Next time we mean it."), two or three members get a short low mood (TUNING's
number, far below Battle Brothers' half), and nothing else. No lost standing outside the company, no ledger for the
world. The honest cost is inside the family, which is the theme.

### 3.4 What finishing gives (three layers, always all three)
1. STANDING: renown (our standing number, which opens bigger clients in the hall, `Q236.W9`), sized to the ambition.
2. MOOD: the whole company gets a good mood (Battle Brothers' own reward [V]), shown as faces at the fire, not a number.
3. ONE THING YOU CAN SEE: a banner that flies over the base and on the fight board, a named coat, a cart that carries
   more, a held block that goes on the map in the family's colour, a painted wall, a stuffed beast head in the base.
   Placed on the home base where possible (`Q049.W8`). Never batteries alone (`Q144.N1`, `Q144.X2`). Every thing
   carries a verb or a door (`Q152.X1`): the banner steadies the line in a fight, the cart carries two more of a
   resource, the held block is a lot to build on (rule 43).
Plus a line in the phone's feed (city view only): "@[company] finally got their flag up. Took them long enough."
drafted by WORDS, draft:true.

### 3.5 Across three generations
1. Each generation has its own ambitions; the founder's are about surviving the ruin, the second's about holding and
   rebuilding, the third's about leaving and healing (the three eras' tones).
2. WHEN THE GENERATION TURNS (the transition, unlocked when the first endgame crisis starts, rule 85, around day 80 to 100) with an ambition open, it is NOT carried
   as a task. At the heir's first fire, the company's voice offers it as one of the three: "Your abuela wanted a wall
   around the pump house. She never got it." The heir may take it or say "Not now" (nothing written, as always).
3. INHERITED AMBITIONS KEEP THEIR PROGRESS (the wall half-built stays half-built on the base, `Q147.W7`), so the heir
   finishes it rather than restarting it, and the reward names both: "the wall your grandmother started".
4. SOME AMBITIONS EXIST ONLY FOR HEIRS (`Q144.N1` later unlocks): they are drawn only if a prior generation did a named
   deed (killed the lion, held the dam road, found the lab's door). A founder cannot conceive of them.
5. THE THREE ACTS ARE OPEN AT ONCE after the flip unlocks (rule 85, the first endgame crisis): an ambition the founder finished changes what act 2 offers
   (a held block in act 1 is a held block in act 2, the future derived from the past); an ambition dropped in act 1
   can be offered in act 2 as "the one they gave up on". The ledger holds the fact; the offer is always optional.
6. THE AMALGAMATION'S OFFER: in act 2 or 3, once an inherited ambition exists, the Network may offer (through its
   ordinary face, a voice on a speaker, a message on the cracked phone in the city view) to "finish it for her. She
   would want that. We have her voice." Accepting gives the reward instantly and it is WRONG in one detail forever
   (the banner is in her colour but the stitching is perfect). Declining costs nothing. This is the counterfeit family
   in one small choice (`Q174.W8`, `Q199.W9`), never explained.

## 5. THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)

1. An ambition is offered ONLY by a person with a portrait (the company's most senior living member) at rest on the map
   or on a home base's screen. Test: no ambition text appears without a speaker id and a portrait.
2. Three offers plus "Not now", and "Not now" exists from the FIRST offer. Test: the offer screen has four lines on
   every offer in a new save.
3. Declining writes nothing to any ledger. Test: the save diff after "Not now" is only the cooldown counter.
4. No ambition has a timer or a fail state except the player dropping it. Test: no ambition data row carries a
   deadline field.
5. One open ambition at a time. Test: accepting while one is open is impossible (the button reads like the greyed hall
   button, `Q236.W2`).
6. Dropping costs a small mood hit inside the company only. Test: no faction standing or world ledger changes on drop.
7. Finishing gives all three layers: standing, mood, and one thing you can see that carries a verb. Test: every reward
   row names a visible object or map change AND what it does.
8. No reward is batteries alone or any grindable pile (`Q144.X2`). Test: a reward row whose only field is a resource
   amount fails.
9. No ambition rewards a cruelty that a fork elsewhere weighs (`Q150.P4`). Test: cross-check the ambition table against
   the fork list; none counts killings of a people, betrayals or broken contracts.
10. Offers never include one already satisfied. Test: every draw checks its condition false first.
11. At the generation turn an open ambition is re-offered to the heir with its progress kept, never auto-carried.
    Test: after the transition the open slot is empty and the heir's first offer includes the inherited one.
12. At least a quarter of the pool is heir-only, unlocked by a named prior deed (`Q144.N1`). Test: count rows with a
    prior-generation condition.
13. Ambitions near the Amalgamation's secret show their risk on the offer (the size glyph carries the mark). Test:
    every secret-row shows the mark.
14. Every ambition line is draft:true, attempts from WORDS; every number is in TUNING's one table.
15. Ambitions never appear in the hall and contracts never appear at the fire. Test: the two tables share no ids.
16. The whole table is a data file a modder can edit (rule, chat 22 MODS). Test: the pool loads from one file.

## 6. WHAT TO AVOID (the flaws, by id)

- THE FORCED PICK: Battle Brothers makes you choose before "no ambition" unlocks [V]. The free "Not now" from the first
  offer answers it, and matches the contract law (`Q236.P3`, `Q236.X4`).
- THE GUILT: "your men question your leadership" [V]. Keep the drop cost small and inside the family.
- THE CLOCK: a deadline turns a wish into an order (`Q038.X1`, `Q206.X2`).
- THE GRIND WALL: "amass 10,000 crowns" [V]; a money goal is also the worst estate (`Q144.N1`, `Q144.X2`). Resource
  ambitions ask to KEEP a reserve, not to pile a sum.
- THE ENDLESS LADDER: renown tiers that never stop (`Q049.X2`); cap the named tiers, then the ambitions turn to places,
  people and the secret.
- THE SOLVED LIST: players learn the pool and pre-stage it [V], a checklist read in a list (`Q114.X3`). Rotate lines,
  gate a quarter behind prior deeds, draw across kinds.
- THE CHECKLIST THAT VOTES: a completion reward that pays the dark branch (`Q150.X1`, `Q140.X2`).
- THE TITLE WITHOUT VERBS (`Q152.X1`) and THE LECTURE AS PRIZE (`Q113.X6`).
- THE REWARD IN THE GRAVE ROOM (`Q125.X7`): hold a completion line while a death scene or funeral is on screen.
- THE REPLAYED PROSE: an heir must never be handed the parent's scenes again (`Q144.X1`); the inherited ambition gets a
  new line in past tense, not the old one.
- THE ADMIN TOP: late ambitions that are upkeep (`Q061.X3`).
- THE HOLLOW FIRST GOAL: the founder's first offer must be tied to the ruin he starts in (`Q235.X3`), a wall, water,
  the cart, never "visit five settlements".
- THE MERCIFUL QUESTION: if the fold's meaning is only loss, it collapses (`Q144.X3`); ambitions give the fold meaning
  that is not death: the family's own finished sentences.
- THE UNSEEN LEGACY: an inherited ambition that nobody can see on the map or the base loses its weight (`Q234.X3`).

## 7. OPEN (what the library could not answer)

1. Whether the player (the leader) ever PROPOSES his own ambition in free text or from a longer list. Mount and Blade's
   free dream (`Q061.W3`) says yes; Battle Brothers says no. Default here: no, the three from the veteran; PEOPLE may
   add a "you pick" line later. A creative fork for Paolo only if he raises it.
2. The exact cooldowns and mood sizes: Battle Brothers' own numbers are unstable across versions [V]. TUNING.
3. Whether the Amalgamation's "let me finish it" offer is a one-time act-3 beat or a standing temptation on every
   inherited ambition. Default: once per generation, on the first inherited ambition only. DYNASTY owns the flip.
4. Whether a finished ambition in act 1 can be LOST in act 2 (the future goes both ways: a raided base loses its wall).
   Default: yes for places and objects, never for knowledge (`Q144.W2`).
5. Whether heirs-only ambitions should show as greyed "you do not know this yet" slots. Default: no, they simply never
   appear until earned (discovery over direction, `Q037.W5`).
6. How many ambitions a full game offers. Battle Brothers players finish about 15 to 25 in a long campaign [R]; at ~100
   hours over three generations a pool of 60 to 80 with 26 drafted here is a start.
