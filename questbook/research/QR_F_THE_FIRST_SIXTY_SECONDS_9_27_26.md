# QR-F: WHAT DO THE BEST OPENINGS DO IN THE FIRST SIXTY SECONDS, AND WHAT DO THEY REFUSE TO DO?

QUESTION: row [first sixty], question (e) of laws/BOHEMIA_ADDENDUM_QUESTS_IS_RESEARCH_ONLY_9_27_26.md s3.
What do the best-opened quests and games in the library do in the first sixty seconds, and what do they
refuse to do? Then the SHAPE for our first minute on the honest grid: a tiny character, a close street,

and still it must be a GAME (something to do with the thumb, a choice, a discovery). A second-by-second

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The best openings in the library do three things in the first minute and refuse three. They DO: let the
PLACE act before any person does (a floor gives way in ten seconds, `Q099.W4`; a starting kit states the
whole thesis without a word, `Q093.W4`); show a world that is already running on its own clock and
does not turn to look at you (`Q051.W2`, `Q128.W2`); and put one small thing in reach that the player
finds by walking past it, not by being told (`Q013.W6`, `Q023.W2`). They REFUSE: to explain (the best
opening withholds exactly the WHY, `Q091.W7`; the collapse stays ambient, `Q083.W7`); to stop the player
(the house stays calm and "nobody stops you", `Q126.W4`; the off-ramp is real, `Q140.W8`); and to pour
names and systems on a newcomer (the firehose prologue, `Q081.X4`; the tutorial that defines jargon with
jargon, `Q046.X2`). The worst openings in the library fail on the OTHER side of the same line: silence
without anything to do reads as empty (`Q055.X1`), unsignposted helplessness is a brick wall (`Q051.X1`),
and a slow first act loses people before the hook (`Q057.X2`, `Q067.X4`). So our first minute must be
silent AND busy: the thumb has a job from the first tap (the map answers at once, the settlement screen
opens at once, swept 9/29), the first settlement screen has at least one thing to find in its one view, one silent choice
is on the table before second 60 and is filed without comment (`Q143.W8`), and speech, the rumour, the
person by name, the contract and the map's first event all wait on the far side of second 60, each one
never forced (not tapping is free). Silence is the frame; the place is the game.

## THE FINDINGS

### A. WHAT THE BEST OPENINGS DO IN THE FIRST MINUTE

**A1. The place acts first; people act later.** The single cleanest first-minute beat in the library is
architectural, not verbal: in the Ocean House the building "takes your floor in the first ten seconds",
and the other staircase is always intact (`Q099.W4`). The same study states its rule plainly: dread, not
jumpscares, by not showing the monster often (`Q099.W3`), and the terror is "less of there being an enemy"
and more the atmosphere (`Q099.W1`). Subnautica's story is a burning ship on the horizon that is both plot
and compass (`Q090.W5`), and its music exists to make the silence worse (`Q090.W9`). The space testifies
without a speaker (`Q023.W4`); the Strange Man's shack tells his story with skulls and an unfinished
painting and no exposition (`Q010.W9`). FOR US (swept 9/29): in the first seconds the thing that reacts to the player
must be the PLACE (the map answering his first tap, a building on the settlement screen that answers
when tapped, a door that swings, a lamp that browns out). The settlement screen IS our Ocean House floor:
the first thing the world teaches is that a place answers a touch. (The old line here, about the honest
grid and one cell, died with the walk, rule 38.)

**A2. The thesis arrives in the first thirty seconds, and it arrives as a thing.** Dead Money puts its
thesis in the first thirty seconds (`Q105.W2`); Rust puts its whole game in the starting inventory, "a
rock, a torch, and bandages that only stop bleeding" (`Q093.W4`); Tali's trial outcome is announced in the
first thirty seconds by how someone says hello, "the most economical piece of writing in the mission"
(`Q125.W2`); Project Zomboid tells you the ending before it loads you in (`Q088.W1`). Two of those four
deliver the thesis with a voice or a line of text, which rule 32a forbids for us in the first minute
(and A QUEST IS PEOPLE, PLACES AND THINGS forbids the loading-screen card at any time). The other two
deliver it with an OBJECT. FOR US: the thesis in the first thirty seconds is a thing in the picture: a dead
battery, a cut cable, a meter reading zero, a charge post with one working socket. Batteries are the
money; the first object he can touch should say so without a word.

**A3. The world is already running, and it does not revolve around him.** Gothic's world treats the hero
as the NPC and runs its schedules "whether you're there or not" (`Q051.W2`, `Q051.W4`); in Majora's town
everyone keeps their schedule into the apocalypse (`Q128.W2`, `Q011.W1`); Elden Ring's NPCs progress on
world-time whether you watch or not (`Q023.W6`); Kenshi's factions war and trade without you (`Q055.W1`).
Ultima VII's collapse is a mood nobody names (`Q083.W7`). FOR US: the first minute shows people at
distance DOING their routine (queuing at a pump, hauling, sitting at a charge post), none of them turning
toward him. That is the exact visual form of 32a's "the world arrives with time, on the beat
of its own clock", and it is cheap because the routines already exist as a system (rule 33a: parties
roam and leave tracks).

**A4. The first discovery is found by walking past it.** Yakuza's substories start "by WALKING PAST
something, not from a menu" (`Q013.W6`); Elden Ring's "quiet thrill of stumbling on the next step" is
destroyed by a waypoint (`Q023.W2`); the world is the quest log (`Q023.W1`); Outer Wilds makes
everything available from minute one and gates only comprehension (`Q018.W2`), then keeps a rumour web
that "always gives a thread to pull" (`Q018.W3`) under a trust contract that there is always a way
(`Q018.W4`). Fallout 1 trusts the player to reason the next step from naturalistic hints (`Q038.W3`).
FOR US (swept 9/29): the first settlement screen holds, in its one view, something that rewards a tap:
an object, a door, a building drawn wrong. Direction is diegetic (drag marks, a cable, a queue), never an
arrow (`Q023.W10`: the fix is better in-world clues, not markers-or-nothing).

**A5. People are introduced by what they are doing, not by what they say.** Yennefer is introduced
already winning a bar fight she did not need help with, no dialogue required (`Q095.W3`). Glarthir's
paranoia is taught by WATCHING before any node lets you deny it (`Q130.W2`). Baldur's Gate 2 brings its
cast alive through overheard relationships (`Q043.W4`), and Dishonored's party is investigated through
gossip and diaries (`Q014.W3`), exactly as Whodunit's guests each hold optional, unmarked hearsay about
every other guest (`Q007.N1`). FOR US: in 0-60 s a person is a figure in the settlement picture doing a
thing; the portrait and the mouth come only after 60 s and only on his tap.

**A6. One small choice early teaches that the world watches, and the game files it without comment.**
Chrono Trigger front-loads "a low-stakes reactivity lesson" so the player takes later choices seriously
(`Q050.W4`); Silent Hill 2 files the player's silence as a position taken (`Q143.W8`); in Skyrim you can
delete an entire faction "in the first five minutes" (`Q075.W3`); the KCD debt-collection opener gives the
FIRST quest several approaches with lasting consequence (laws/BOHEMIA_ADDENDUM_BEST_QUESTS_COLLECTION_7_13_26.md
s18). FOR US: before second 60 there is exactly one silent choice with the thumb (take or leave a thing,
open or pass a door, tap toward a group or not), it writes one ledger row, and nothing on screen says
it did.

**A7. The verbs come before the fight.** Tyranny's Conquest teaches the role's grammar ("attach, counsel,
adjudicate, proclaim") before it teaches combat (`Q145.W2`). Celeste's "tiny moveset + crystal visual
clarity" is both accessibility and challenge (`Q059.W5`); Into the Breach's small grid holds the whole
puzzle in the head (`Q060.W4`) with "elementary-but-clear graphics + minimal text" (`Q060.W8`). Chrono
Trigger put enemies on the field and fought them in place, "a small-but-huge design courtesy" (`Q050.W9`).
FOR US (swept 9/29): the first minute teaches TRAVEL (the marker moves on his tap), OPEN (a building on
the settlement screen), TAKE or LEAVE (a thing, SCAVENGE). The fight is visible (a group drawn in the
settlement picture, `Q050.W9`), but it starts only if he taps into it, and then it is on the fight board,
house-sized tiles cut from that block.

**A8. The opening is an aftermath.** "Starting after the ending" is the sharpest premise inversion in the
genre, and it is personal when the aftermath is yours (`Q145.W10`). Kenshi makes the failure state a
starting position (`Q110.W2`). Outward's first quest is rent, a debt with a deadline (`Q087.W6`), and
Kenshi's world first acknowledges that you exist "as a bill" (`Q151.W2`). FOR US: this is how the 7/19
raid and the 7/24 stolen power station survive rule 32a: the worst night already happened BEFORE the first
second. The player does not live the raid in the first minute; he arrives in its aftermath, and the recovery
hunt begins only when he chooses to follow the trail. The bill (the creditor who remembers his father)
arrives later, "never in the first minute" (32a), which is exactly where Kenshi puts its first
acknowledgment: late, and as an invoice.

**A9. A seductive opening buys the rest of the game, and a dull one loses it.** Inscryption's cabin hooks
instantly and "earns the weird later turns" (`Q053.W9`). The failures are the mirror: Hades' story "seems
very sparse from the beginning" and some bounce before it hooks (`Q047.X3`); DDLC's mundane first act is a
"slog" for some (`Q057.X2`); Phantasy Star III's first generation is "a boring stroll through monotony"
and the generational magic opens only after it (`Q067.X4`); Death Stranding's first hours are an
"Amazon delivery simulator" because its social system is empty at the start (`Q089.X1`). FOR US: rule 32a
asks for silence, and these five findings are the warning that silence is not the same as nothing. The
first minute must be dense with things to do and find (A4, A6, A7) precisely BECAUSE nobody speaks.

### B. WHAT THE BEST OPENINGS REFUSE TO DO

**B1. They refuse to explain.** Dark Souls' opening "spells it all out" except "WHY the Age of Fire is
ending or what is to be done about it" (`Q091.W7`). The Strange Man is never explained and every reading
is supported (`Q010.W7`); Namira's hunger has no origin node, and "unexplained appetite is the scarier
species" (`Q133.W9`). FOR US: the analog horror rule ("ordinary things with ONE wrong detail, said plainly,
never explained") is the same refusal. In the first minute the wrong detail is shown, never named, and
no one is present to name it.

**B2. They refuse to stop the player.** The House of Hope is the library's best statement: "You can walk
it, loot it, insult the staff... Nobody stops you. It stays calm until you touch the one thing you came
for" (`Q126.W4`). Sans's last corridor lets you walk away at the threshold and "genuinely lets you"
(`Q140.W8`); Kenshi's flee option never closes, "and its standing availability is what makes staying a
CHOICE" (`Q151.W7`); Divinity's Fort Joy makes combat one tool among several (`Q015.W9`). FOR US (swept 9/29):
no person's portrait opens a screen he did not tap; no group moves toward the party marker before second
60; on the fight board the way-out edge is always open.

**B3. They refuse to punish a no.** Raphael's offer: "No ambush, no curse, no closed door... The offer
never expires" (`Q126.W3`), and the conversation file calls a refusal that "costs nothing and forecloses
nothing" the craft itself (`Q126.N13`). The flaw on the other side is Boethiah's: "refusal has no dignity
path... the strongest choice most players make is invisible" (`Q131.X7`). FOR US: Paolo's ruling settles
the tension (DECLINING IS FREE, nobody remembers a no). The dignity path is the person STILL STANDING
THERE, in the same building, the next time he opens the settlement: the offer is a door that never closes, not a line
that thanks him for his restraint.

**B4. They refuse the firehose.** Final Fantasy Tactics' prologue is "a firehose" of houses and orders,
and the lesson is that a faction web must be introduced "through PEOPLE, not org-charts" (`Q081.X4`).
Crusader Kings III's tutorial drowns newcomers (`Q046.X2`); Citizen Sleeper's first hour is "a barrage of
new mechanics" (`Q065.X2`); Bannerlord's interlocking systems intimidate (`Q061.X2`). FOR US: before second
60, zero faction names, zero numbers above one on screen, zero currency counters on screen, one verb
learned at a time. The first NAME he hears is a person's (rule 34f: "the first person by name"), after 60 s.

**B5. They refuse to spend the best thing in the first contact.** The House of Horrors spends its most
interesting casualty "as a tutorial fight" (`Q134.X3`), and its furniture assault, "first contact's whole
personality, never recurs" (`Q134.X9`). FOR US: the first minute must not be the loudest minute. The old
cold open (a raid as the tutorial) was this flaw at game scale; the recovery hunt it set up is better as
the first thing he CHOOSES to start.

### C. WHAT THE WORST OPENINGS DO (THE FLAW SIDE)

**C1. Silence with nothing in it.** Kenshi's start "can feel like nothing to engage with" and the story
stays "in my head, not the game" (`Q055.X1`); its onboarding "does next to nothing" (`Q055.X2`); "no goals
= aimless for many" (`Q055.X3`). Outward's world is "relatively sparse leading to prolonged sections of
nothing happening" (`Q087.X3`). This is the single biggest risk of a literal reading of 32a.

**C2. Opacity mistaken for depth.** Gothic's "brick wall" onboarding: "Friction good; opacity bad"
(`Q051.X1`). Rust: "like merging with heavy traffic" (`Q093.X3`). Zomboid's systems "punish rather than
instruct" (`Q088.X2`). Morrowind needs to "TEACH its own literacy early" or it "reads as broken, not deep"
(`Q037.X3`). FOR US: the map and the settlement screen must teach their own literacy in the first ten
seconds (a tap that moves the marker, a building that opens), because a silent start with an illegible
screen is the brick wall.

**C3. The UX friction of the grid itself.** Fallout 1's "tile-by-tile movement" is named as the dated
friction that should be modernized while keeping the depth (`Q038.X4`), and Into the Breach's phone port
drew complaints about imprecise touch until patched: "a precise grid/positioning game needs TOUCH controls
designed for mobile from the start" (`Q060.X4`). FOR US (swept 9/29): these two findings are part of why the walk
died. Our first minute has NO tile-by-tile movement: a tap on the map, a tap on a building. Every tap must
feel instant and certain on glass. This is RUN's and PLUMBER's line, and it is the first-minute finding
with the most direct bite.

**C4. The thesis left optional.** Vault 11 is New Vegas's thesis and "no quest sends you" (`Q104.X3`);
Theo on the stairs is the quest's thesis and "the thesis cannot be optional" (`Q101.X5`); Tali's meaning
lives in a plaza "you can sprint through" (`Q125.X5`). FOR US: tension with 32a. The resolution: the THESIS
object sits IN THE FIRST PICTURE (the first settlement screen's one view), while every PERSON is
avoidable. Things may fill the eye; people may never stop the thumb.

**C5. Silent consequences with no tell.** Majora's silent failure "cuts both ways... LAW: ambience-failure
needs one diegetic tell per broken link" (`Q128.X2`); Mass Effect's "accept it (or stay silent)" was
under-signposted and locked players into complicity (`Q096.X5`). FOR US: the silent choice of the first
minute (A6) is filed without comment in the moment, and must surface later as a THING in the world
(the battery cell he took is missing from a relative's hand; the door he left open is still open), never as text.

**C6. Heavy choices made blind.** Tyranny's Conquest choices were made "before the player knows the world"
so its read-backs land "as accusations about a stranger's decisions" (`Q145.X2`). FOR US: the first
minute's choice must be SMALL (one battery, one door), because it is made before he knows anybody. It can
matter later (a plant for the delayed payoff), but never heavily in the moment.

**C7. Fetch as the first quest.** Outward's early quests are "a lot of fetch quests" (`Q087.X4`), and the
Ocean House under its atmosphere "is, mechanically, a key hunt" (`Q099.X1`). FOR US: the first contract
offered after 60 s is a situation with a person in it, not "bring me three things".

### D. THE TENSION AND HOW THE LIBRARY RESOLVES IT

Rule 32a (nothing speaks) and rule 34f (it must be a game) look opposed. The library says they are not.
The games whose first minutes are both silent and gripping all put the game in the SPACE: the Ocean
House's floor (`Q099.W4`), Outer Wilds' open solar system gated only by comprehension (`Q018.W2`),
Into the Breach's eight-by-eight board you hold in your head (`Q060.W4`), Kenshi's escape whose central
gate "is stored nowhere but the player's head" (`Q146.W7`). Our settlement screen is that kind of space
(swept 9/29: this used to be the honest grid, which died with the walk): one view of a place where every
drawn building is a door you can tap. A first minute on it is a small puzzle with no instructions and no
wrong answer: what is in that house, what is wrong in this picture, what do I take. The discoveries are things (A2, A4); the choice is silent (A6); the people are
scenery doing jobs (A3, A5). When the first minute ends, the world gets its voice back in the order the
library recommends: overheard first (`Q043.W4`), a person in a place second (`Q013.W6`), a debt last
(`Q151.W2`), and relief after strain (RimWorld's storyteller sends relief after a hard hit, `Q044.W7`;
Pathologic's cruelty is absent early and creeps in, `Q021.W3`).

## THE SECOND-BY-SECOND BUDGET (re-cut 9/29: the map, then the first settlement screen)

The clock starts when loading ends. There is no three-names screen (rule 39c: dead as built); the one
person is customized BEFORE this clock. The walked budget this section held (one cell per press, six
cells, door cells) died with the walk (rule 38); QR-P section 4 re-cut it and this is that re-cut, with
the same citations.

### 0-10 s: THE MAP AND THE THUMB

ALLOWED:
- The map, the family's block under the party marker, already in the world (no fade-in sequence that eats
  input). The first tap answers at once: the marker moves, or the block opens. (minimal, legible moveset
  `Q059.W5`; clarity over text `Q060.W8`; touch designed first `Q060.X4`)
- Sound of material only: wind on sheet metal, a generator somewhere, a dog. No voice, no music sting.
  (silence made worse by sound `Q090.W9`; dread, not jump `Q099.W3`)
- The place may ACT once, small: a lamp on the map browns out and comes back. (the building acts first
  `Q099.W4`; teach literacy early `Q037.X3`; friction good, opacity bad `Q051.X1`)

FORBIDDEN: any line of text, any portrait, any party moving toward the marker, any HUD number that
changes, any fight, any event, any tutorial prompt. (no firehose `Q081.X4`, `Q046.X2`; 32a)

### 10-30 s: THE SETTLEMENT SCREEN AND THE FIRST THING

ALLOWED:
- He taps the block: the settlement screen draws it in one view, three or four buildings he can tap, and
  SCAVENGE. The thesis OBJECT is in that picture (a dead battery, a cut cable, a meter at zero); tapping
  it lets him look (the object's picture, no caption) or take it. (thesis in the first thirty seconds
  `Q105.W2`; the starting kit is the thesis `Q093.W4`; the thesis cannot be optional `Q101.X5`)
- People drawn in the picture, each doing a routine that loops, none turning to him. (world runs without
  you `Q051.W2`, `Q051.W4`; schedules into the apocalypse `Q128.W2`)
- Diegetic direction: drag marks, a cable, a queue, a smoke line. (navigate by deduction `Q038.W3`; the
  fix is better in-world clues `Q023.W10`; environmental storytelling `Q023.W4`)
- The one WRONG DETAIL (analog horror) sits here, shown plainly, not framed, not named. (withhold the why
  `Q091.W7`; never explained `Q010.W7`)

FORBIDDEN: speech of any kind, including overheard; a portrait; a screen he did not open (`Q126.W4`:
nobody stops you); a named faction; a map event.

### 30-60 s: THE SILENT CHOICE AND THE VISIBLE FIGHT

ALLOWED:
- ONE silent choice with the thumb is possible: take or leave a thing (SCAVENGE is its best home, QR-P);
  open a building or not. It writes one ledger row and shows nothing. (small choices teach big ones
  `Q050.W4`; silence writes flags `Q143.W8`; keep it small because it is made blind `Q145.X2`)
- A group may be visible in the picture, doing its own thing (scavenging, arguing, beating on a door). If
  he taps into it, the fight opens on the fight board, house-sized tiles cut from this block, with his
  companion if he has one, a routine fight of 2 to 4 minutes (rule 40a); the way-out edge is always open.
  (visible enemies, fought in place `Q050.W9`; the off-ramp is real `Q140.W8`; combat is one tool
  `Q015.W9`) Nothing hostile initiates in the first 60 s.
- The person who wants something is drawn in a place, doing something that shows the want (sitting by
  a dead cart, standing at a charge post with an empty jug). Not speaking. Untapped is free. (introduced
  by staging `Q095.W3`; discovery by passing `Q013.W6`, re-read as not tapping; 32a)
- The map is one tap away at every second, including this one; no event fires on it. (rule 33c never in
  the first minute)

FORBIDDEN: speech; any text box; the creditor (32a: "never in the first minute"); a contract offered; a
number above one on screen.

### 60-180 s: THE WORLD GETS ITS VOICE BACK, IN ORDER

ALLOWED, in this order of preference:
1. THE OVERHEARD RUMOUR: two people in the picture talk to EACH OTHER, in Spanglish; the line has a delay;
   it is never addressed to "you"; it names a PLACE he can travel to. (ambient banter as characterization
   `Q043.W4`; gossip as diegetic investigation `Q014.W3`; the rumour web as the quest log `Q018.W3`;
   direction by description `Q037.W2`)
2. THE PERSON BY NAME: the person who wants something speaks ONE first line only when he taps them or
   their building; the portrait comes with the mouth. The first proper noun of the game is this person's
   name. (the first person by name, rule 34f; people, not org-charts `Q081.X4`)
3. THE CONTRACT, if any: the offer screen in that person's building, with a situation in it, not a
   fetch. Declining, or closing the screen, writes nothing; the person stays in the same building.
   (refusal costs nothing `Q126.W3`, `Q126.N13`; not a fetch `Q087.X4`)
4. THE BILL, at the earliest here and preferably later: the creditor who remembers his father, one
   line, once, on a screen he opened. (the world's first acknowledgment as a bill `Q151.W2`; 32a)
5. THE FIRST ROAD EVENT, only after the first arrival somewhere, never before second 60. (rule 33c;
   slow-onset pressure `Q021.W3`)

FORBIDDEN: more than one named person; more than one contract; any line that tells him what his silent
choice meant (it surfaces later as a thing, `Q128.X2`); a second person addressing him.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable; re-cut 9/29, numbers kept so QR-P's cross-references hold)

1. SILENCE GATE. From the end of loading to t = 60 s, the count of spoken lines, text boxes, portraits
   and captions rendered is 0. (32a gate: "nothing speaks in the first 60 s of play")
2. THE FIRST TAP ANSWERS. The first tap after loading moves the party marker or opens the block within
   the same beat. (Was ONE CELL, FIRST PRESS; dead with the walk, rule 38. `Q060.X4`, `Q038.X4`)
3. SOMETHING WITHIN ONE TAP. The first settlement screen shows at least one tappable building and the
   SCAVENGE button. (anti-empty start `Q055.X1`, `Q087.X3`)
4. THE THESIS IN THE PICTURE. The thesis object is drawn in the first settlement screen's one view, not
   behind a tap. Every person is avoidable. (`Q101.X5`, `Q104.X3`; `Q126.W4`)
5. NOBODY IN THE DOOR. No portrait, line or screen appears in the first 60 s that he did not open.
   (`Q151.W7`; 32a)
6. NOBODY COMES. No party moves toward the marker and no hostile acquires him in the first 60 s.
   (32a; `Q140.W8`)
7. ONE SILENT CHOICE. At least one take/leave choice is reachable before t = 60 s (SCAVENGE is its
   home); it writes one ledger row; nothing on screen acknowledges it. (`Q050.W4`, `Q143.W8`)
8. THE TELL COMES LATER. Every silent-choice ledger row has at least one later WORLD tell (an object, a
   state, a person's line after 60 s), authored with it. (`Q128.X2`, `Q096.X5`)
9. NO NUMBERS, NO NAMES. Before t = 60 s: no faction name, no currency counter on screen, no number above
   one on screen; the first settlement screen hides its prices until 60 s and his first tap. A battery
