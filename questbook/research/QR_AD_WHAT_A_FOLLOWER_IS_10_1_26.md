## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

A Battle Brothers follower is a person the company carries who never fights and changes one rule of the world: the
scout makes the party 15% faster on every terrain and stops road accidents [V], the surgeon makes a man without a
lasting injury survive a blow that would have killed him [V], the negotiator gives more rounds of haggling without
souring the client [V], the cartographer pays you for places you find [V], the bounty hunter pays for every champion
killed [V]. There are eighteen of them [V], they cost 2,000 to 4,000 crowns once [V], most must be UNLOCKED by a deed
first (win five fights against beasts for the scout, finish five caravan escorts for the cartographer, retire a man with
a lasting injury for the drill sergeant, be allied with a noble house for the agent) [V], and they sit in five slots
around the campfire that open one at a time as the company's renown climbs (250, 750, 1,400, 2,250, 3,350) [V].
Replacing one gives no refund and puts the old one back in the pool [V]. They are the company's household: the part of
the war band that is not a sword. The library adds the two things Battle Brothers never needed. First, a follower must
be a PERSON, not a bonus with a picture: functional people need inner lives (`Q022.W5`, `Q022.P4`), a settlement that
never speaks about itself is a hole (`Q151.X3`), and staff are a realm's real memory (`Q152.W8`). Second, in a
three-generation game the household is what OUTLIVES the captain: the chamberlain class carries memory across
successions (`Q152.P4`), inherited people speak of the last head in past tense (`Q144.W7`), and what crosses best is
knowledge and bonds, never a pile (`Q144.W2`, `Q144.P2`). So for Bohemia: a follower is a named, dressed person met at a
place on a settlement screen, offered on an ordinary offer screen (portrait, 2 to 4 lines, the price, take it or walk
away free), who joins the camp on the map, never fights, changes one rule the player can feel in the six resources, the
road or the hall, and who, when the generation turns, may leave behind a child or an apprentice who offers to take the
same place for the heir. PEOPLE gets 24 drafts below.

## 1. WHAT BATTLE BROTHERS' RETINUE IS (THE SOURCE, TAGGED)

### 1.1 The machine
- WHAT: "non-combat followers that grant various advantages outside combat" [V]; the dev blog's words, "non-fighting
  specialists like blacksmiths, surgeons or drill sergeants who will assist you outside of combat" [V]. Each "pulls
  their weight by granting you one or more beneficial effects on the world map" [V].
- WHEN IT CAME: with the Blazing Deserts DLC, as a free update to the base game [V for the DLC, R for the free part].
- WHERE IT LIVES: the camp screen. The followers stand around a campfire; a donkey stands with them, upgradable to a
  cart, a wagon and a bigger wagon for more inventory room [V]. The cart is bought on the same screen [R].
- HOW MANY: eighteen followers in the vanilla pool [V].
- SLOTS: five, opened by renown tier: Recognized (250) the first, Competent (750) the second, Reputable (1,400) the
  third, Great (2,250) the fourth, Fabled (3,350) the fifth and last [V].
- PRICE: one payment in crowns. Search-verified prices: the minstrel 2,000; the alchemist and the lookout 2,500; the
  recruiter 3,000; the trader, the drill sergeant, the paymaster and the surgeon 3,500; the agent 4,000 [V]. No daily
  wage [R]: unlike a brother, a follower does not draw pay.
- UNLOCK: most followers are greyed until a deed is done. Search-verified: the scout, win five battles against beasts;
  the cartographer, finish five caravan escort contracts; the lookout, discover ten locations on your own; the drill
  sergeant, retire a brother who has a permanent injury; the agent, reach allied relations (90) with a noble house or a
  city state [V]. Others are recalled as deed-gated in the same way [R].
- REPLACE: allowed any time; no refund; the replaced follower goes back to the pool and can be bought again [V].
- RISK: a follower never takes part in a fight and is never killed by one [R]. The retinue is the one part of the
  company that cannot be lost to a bad roll.

### 1.2 The pool (effects as search returned them, [V] unless marked)
- THE SCOUT: +15% travel speed on all terrain; prevents sickness and terrain accidents. Unlock: five beast fights won.
- THE SURGEON: any man without a permanent injury is guaranteed to survive an otherwise fatal blow; injuries heal one day
  faster. 3,500.
- THE QUARTERMASTER: stockpile caps raised (+100 ammo, +50 tools, +50 medicine).
- THE NEGOTIATOR: more haggling rounds on a contract with no relations hit; good relations decay slower and bad ones
  recover faster, about 15%. (A "+40% contract pay" line in one guide is from an upgrade and smells modded.)
- THE BOUNTY HUNTER: +3% champion spawn chance; pays 300 to 750 crowns per champion slain.
- THE CARTOGRAPHER: pays crowns for discovering new locations, scaled by distance from the nearest settlement, double for
  legendary ones. Unlock: five caravan escorts.
- THE BLACKSMITH: repairs gear even if broken or lost on a death, +20% repair speed, 20% fewer tools used.
- THE COOK: food lasts longer and the men recover faster [V for "reduces the pain of running out of food", R for the
  exact effect].
- THE RECRUITER: hiring 10% cheaper, tryouts half price, two to four extra recruits in every settlement. 3,000.
- THE DRILL SERGEANT: +20% experience at level 1, falling 2% per level; men in reserve never lose mood. 3,500. Unlock:
  retire a man with a permanent injury (the injured veteran becomes the teacher).
- THE PAYMASTER: daily wages 15% lower per man; desertion chance halved; stops pay-raise demands in events. 3,500.
- THE MINSTREL: +15% renown per action; better tavern rumours. 2,000.
- THE LOOKOUT: +25% sight radius; detailed footprint information. 2,500. Unlock: ten locations found yourself.
- THE AGENT: more information on settlements and nobles [R]. 4,000. Unlock: allied with a house.
- THE ALCHEMIST: crafts from beast parts (helps with the rare reagents) [V]. 2,500.
- THE TRADER: more trade goods offered [R]. 3,500.
- THE BRIGAND: tells you where caravans travel, for a raiding company [R].
- THE SCAVENGER: more tools and ammunition recovered after a fight [R].

### 1.3 What the pool is made of (our reading)
Sort the eighteen and it is five kinds, each one a rule of the map the follower bends: THE ROAD (scout, lookout,
cartographer: speed, sight, knowledge of places), THE BODY (surgeon, cook, drill sergeant: survival, recovery, growth),
THE STOCK (quartermaster, blacksmith, scavenger, alchemist: how much you carry, how long it lasts, what you make), THE
DEAL (negotiator, paymaster, trader, recruiter, agent: prices, wages, hires, information) and THE NAME (minstrel, bounty
hunter, brigand: renown, champions, a playstyle). Every effect is a multiplier or a flag on a system the game already
runs. That is why the retinue is cheap to build: no follower needs a script, only a number in a table, and why it is
also thin: no follower has a voice.

### 1.4 What players say [V, from the search-returned thread and guide text]
- THE MUST-HAVES: the surgeon and the blacksmith are named as near-mandatory in serious play; the paymaster as the
  all-round money saver ("Examining the Retinue"). The pool is SOLVED: players publish tier lists.
- THE WANT FOR MORE: the most-downloaded retinue mods add more slots ("More Retinue Member Slots"), let every follower
  be promoted ("Retinue Promotions", "Every Follower Upgradable"), and add pairings between followers ("Cook + Surgeon:
  every man gains +10 hit points; Cook + Trader: fresh bread every 6 days", a mod's text, not vanilla). Players wanted
  the household to GROW and to INTERACT, which vanilla never lets it do.
- THE LATE FEEL: the fifth slot at 3,350 renown arrives when the company is already strong; the late followers feel
  like bonuses on a won game [R].

## 2. THE FINDINGS FROM THE LIBRARY (GROUPED, EACH WITH ITS IDS)

### 2.1 A follower is a person, never a passive bonus with a portrait
- The radio operators who are "the UI" get inner lives: even the interface is a person, nothing is just a system
  (`Q022.W5`). The port names our cases almost word for word: "a caravan radio voice, a ledger-keeper, a plant operator"
  get inner lives and small quests (`Q022.P4`). A follower IS a functional NPC; this is the first rule.
- Battle Brothers' own client is a portrait with a mood and no want (`Q236.X3`); a follower built the same way repeats
  the flaw at the campfire. The fix is the same: a person with a want says it.
- Companions are autonomous people who refuse to be servants (`Q043.W1`); overheard banter is the cheapest, highest-impact
  characterization (`Q043.W4`); every companion is a protagonist of their own story (`Q043.W7`). A follower needs one
  small arc, not a quest chain: one want, one line about it at the fire, one moment where it pays off.
- The settlement that never speaks about itself is a hole: recruits spent in a siege have no reaction to any of it
  (`Q151.X3`). The camp must testify: the cook remarks on the lean week, the medic on the man who died.
- The Hades effect, scoped: people react to your recent death, choices and loadout, and the "noticed your haircut"
  feeling is high leverage (`Q047.P4`); lines that react and never repeat make characters feel alive (`Q047.W5`). The
  barber follower is literally this.
- A knowledge-sharing companion that barks the same line forever becomes the most annoying thing in the game
  (`Q084.X6`). Follower lines need a cooldown and a variation budget.

### 2.2 The household is what outlives the head (the generational core)
- The chamberlain outlives the god: staff are the realm's real memory (`Q152.W8`). The port makes it a CLASS: the clerk,
  the keeper, the steward, continuity made flesh, unimpressed by succession, carrying memory across generations in past
  tense (`Q152.P4`). This is the page's central finding: Bohemia's followers ARE the chamberlain class, and the one
  thing in the company built to cross the fold.
- Inherited people speak of the last captain in past tense, the cheapest reactivity carrying the most weight (`Q144.W7`);
  the port makes it a compile rule: every crossing NPC ships past-tense variants (`Q144.P3`).
- What crosses best is what was learned (`Q144.W2`); the heritable estate is knowledge, bond-states and records, never
  a grindable pile (`Q144.P2`); grind can outweigh grief (`Q144.X2`). A follower's effect is learned craft, which is
  exactly the heritable kind: the cook's daughter knows the recipes, she does not inherit a stack of food.
- The refusals make the line real: open ambitions die, stranded arcs stay stranded (`Q144.W3`). Not every follower
  crosses: some have no heir, some heirs say no, and the gap is felt.
- The heir is a person, not a respawn (`Q144.W4`); raising the scion is a recurring cost in present strength
  (`Q144.N2`). A follower's child raised at the camp is the same machine at small scale.
- The family-wide meta: the bloodline levels up above any single ruler (`Q046.W7`); progress is a home the family
  visibly builds (`Q049.W8`); gold, gear and upgrades pass down (`Q049.W2`). The household is the most human form of
  that meta: not a bonus tree, a set of families who have served yours for sixty years.
- A death passes a legacy (`Q066.W2`); the parent's role can be to enable the next one (`Q069.W6`). The old medic
  training a girl to replace her is the follower version of the whole game's theme.
- The trap: in CK3 even a beloved ruler's heir starts disliked, which reads arbitrary (`Q046.X5`). A follower's heir must
  start from the bond the parent built, not from zero.
- Companions that learn and carry knowledge between worlds (`Q084.W2`): an inherited follower knows things the heir
  never saw (the old route, the lion's den), and says them.

### 2.3 Who owns whom (the line a household must never cross)
- The best place in the world is built on slavery it will not name: medicine, water, law, an Auto-Doc, and a Servant
  Allocation Center (`Q100.W1`); saying the word is the crime (`Q100.W2`); the anti-slavery position has no mechanical
  path but violence (`Q100.X4`). A retinue in a collapsed economy is one step from owned people. Our followers are HIRED
  and FREE: they can leave, they are paid, and the game never offers to buy a person.
- Privilege is shown in a household: the good father who says your brother is meant to be your servant (`Q081.W4`). The
  family's treatment of its followers is where the game can show class without a speech: the heir who talks down to the
  cook's son, and the cook's son who remembers.
- The honest devil deals only in trust: strangers free, followers priced (`Q131.W3`); all trust priced identically is the
  flaw (`Q131.X1`); a trust check that is only a follower flag is gameable (`Q131.X5`). The Amalgamation's offer to
  "replace" a follower who died must know which one mattered.
- The servant who becomes a person by proximity (`Q084.W3`), who inherits the hero's life only if it learned to grieve
  (`Q084.W9`): the counterfeit follower the Network offers is a perfect servant who never grieves, and that is how you
  know.

### 2.4 Followers bend the world's rules: which rules, and how hard
- Recovery as a limited, budgeted resource (`Q041.W5`), with slots a dynasty budgets (`Q041.P4`): five follower slots is
  exactly this. You cannot have everyone, so the pick says what kind of company you are.
- Do not let one counter-system become mandatory (`Q041.X4`). Battle Brothers' surgeon and blacksmith are near-mandatory
  [V]; ours must not be. No follower may be the answer to a law (the 20% death roll is a law; the medic can soften the
  injury, never erase the death).
- Every survival system must earn its place and interlock; do not ship half-systems (`Q055.X5`). Each follower touches
  one of the six resources or one map rule the player already feels, or it is cut.
- Attrition needs pacing (`Q041.X2`); heroes are fragile resources you grieve (`Q041.W4`). Followers are the company's
  pacing tools: the cook and the medic are what make a lean month survivable without making it easy.
- People are your power: a band plus a court (`Q061.W6`, `Q061.P5`); trade and enterprise are valid paths, not just war
  (`Q061.W9`). Half the pool should serve a company that fights less: the trader, the fixer, the map-seller.
- Renown gates the ascent (`Q061.W4`), exactly Battle Brothers' renown opening the hall (`Q236.W9`) and the follower
  slots [V]. Our standing number opens both.
- Threat scales with wealth (`Q044.W4`) and visible prosperity draws raids (`Q151.W3`). A full camp of five specialists
  is visible wealth; the map should notice.
- The dead quartermaster is the supply chain (`Q101.W6`); the best beat in that quest is optional and unmarked
  (`Q101.X5`). The quartermaster follower's whole effect is felt in the moment you need what he kept.
- The geomapper pays you for places you already found; knowledge of the city is a tradeable resource a faction should buy
  (`Q101.P6`). This is the cartographer, already ported in the library.
- The story arrives by radio, just when you are lost (`Q090.W7`); the world explains itself through its own dying media
  (`Q088.W8`). The radio listener follower is the drip-feed of rumours, in the analog horror register.

### 2.5 The slots, the bench and the guilt
- The six-slot cap is artificial and guilt-inducing when the benched act hurt; handle swaps with grace, a home base where
  the benched wait happily (`Q043.X1`, `Q043.P9`). Battle Brothers' replace-with-no-refund [V] is fair; ours adds the
  grace: a replaced follower goes to a held home base and keeps working there, never sulks, never leaves the family.
- Scarcity makes your party yours: you will miss content, and that is life (`Q043.W10`). Five slots and twenty-four
  followers means no save has them all.
- Building is the surrender of mobility (`Q151.W4`); a follower at the camp travels, a follower at a base does not. The
  choice where to keep the medic (with the party, or at the base that heals the injured) is a real trade.
- Attachment is manufactured by labour (`Q151.W9`, `Q055.W9`): the cook who has fed the company for 300 days is loved
  because of the 300 days, not because of a backstory.

### 2.6 Hiring is an offer, never a pressure
- The hall is the world's current problems (`Q236.W1`); a follower is not a problem, so a follower is never in the hall.
  The follower stands in the building of their craft (the clinic, the market, the barber) on the settlement screen.
- The accept button is the only promise (`Q236.W7`); declining writes nothing (`Q236.P3`). A follower offer is the same
  screen: walk away and nobody remembers.
- The danger glyph is a reading before the yes (`Q236.W3`); a follower's offer shows what they change in one line before
  the price.
- The wage treadmill makes every offer matter (`Q236.W10`) and can force the yes (`Q236.X4`). Followers must never be a
  second treadmill: one fee, then they eat with the company (one FOOD share), never a battery wage that grows.
- Do not corrupt an emotional system with exploitative monetization (`Q048.X4`). No follower is ever sold for real money,
  boosted, or rolled from a box.
- The first task that pulls a life back must carry purpose (`Q235.W4`): the founder's first follower should be met in the
  ruin, and want something small and real.
- Side content makes a place alive (`Q013.W1`); a follower standing in a clinic is part of why the clinic exists.

### 2.7 The ceiling and the traps
- A title without verbs is a hat (`Q152.X1`): a follower whose effect cannot be felt is a hat. Every follower's line on
  the offer screen names a verb the player will see used.
- The top of a rise must stay fun, not curdle into admin (`Q061.X3`); the late game is a chore when it is upkeep. No
  follower adds a chore (no feeding mini-game, no follower mood meter to tend).
- Endless scaling discourages (`Q049.X2`); the mods' promotions [V] are fine as a mod, not as the base game's treadmill.
  One promotion per follower per generation, at most.
- Unmarked content turns players into guide-readers (`Q114.X3`); a solved tier list is the retinue's version [V]. Some
  followers are only met through prior deeds, and the pool rotates which ones a given save meets.
- A completion incentive must never side with the dark branch (`Q150.X1`). No follower unlock may require a cruelty (no
  "sell five prisoners" to unlock a slaver's clerk).
- Overreach is its own story: chasing new projects while the basics decay (`Q045.W7`). Spending the batteries on a
  fifth follower while the water runs out should be allowed and should hurt.
- A collectible in a grave room destroys the room (`Q125.X7`): a follower offer must never appear during a funeral, and
  the heir of a dead follower is never offered at the graveside.
- An unrecyclable heirloom carries the most weight (`Q119.W7`): a follower's tool (the medic's bag, the barber's
  scissors) is passed to the heir as an object that cannot be sold.

## 3. THE SHAPE FOR PEOPLE

### 3.1 What a follower is in Bohemia (the definition)
A follower is a named, dressed, portrait-carrying person who travels with the company or lives at a held home base,
NEVER FIGHTS, NEVER STANDS ON THE FIGHT BOARD, and changes exactly one or two rules the player already feels: a resource
rate (one of the six), a road rule (speed, sight, events), a hall rule (haggle, pay, standing), a body rule (healing,
injury length, growth) or a place rule (what a settlement screen shows). A follower is not a company member: the roster
of twelve is the fighters; the retinue is the household around them. The HANDLER with a beast ([keepers]) is a company
member, because the beast fights; this page cross-references him and does not count him.

### 3.2 Where they live on screen
- THE CAMP: when the party rests on the map, the camp screen shows the fire, the followers around it in their runway
  clothes, and the cart. It is Battle Brothers' campfire screen (`reference/library/battle_brothers/10_UI_AND_FEEL.md`
  owns the look). Tap a follower: their face, one line, what they change. Nothing else.
- THE HOME BASE: a follower can be STATIONED at a held base instead of travelling. Stationed, they change that base's
  rules (the medic heals the injured who wait there, the cook feeds the base for less) and they free the camp slot. The
  base's settlement screen shows them in their building (the medic in the clinic you built).
- THE FEED (city view only): now and then a draft post about them, written by WORDS, draft:true ("@lacocinera made beans
  last 4 days. ask her how. she will not tell you").

### 3.3 How a follower is unlocked and hired (never forced)
1. UNLOCK BY A DEED, as Battle Brothers does [V]: most followers only appear once the company has done the thing that
   would make them want to join (beat five lab-beast packs, finish five escorts, retire a man with a long injury). The
   deed is never announced as an unlock. Nothing pops up.
2. MET AT A PLACE: once unlocked, the follower stands in their craft's building on some settlement's screen (the clinic,
   the market, the barber, the radio shack). The cracked phone (city view) may show it on its aging board as one line
   ("somebody at the Fremont clinic is asking about you"); accepting happens at the place, on the screen (rule 51).
3. THE OFFER SCREEN: the hall's offer screen shape (`Q236.N2`): portrait, 2 to 4 lines in their voice, ONE line that says
   what they change, the price (batteries plus sometimes one of the other six), and two buttons, TAKE THEM ON and NOT
   NOW. A small haggle is allowed (one "less" ask, the face shows the ceiling, `Q236.W4`); the fixer adds rounds.
4. DECLINING IS FREE: nothing is written; they stay in the building; they may be gone later (they found other work),
   which is the world living, not a punishment (`Q236.P3`).
5. NO FOLLOWER IS EVER REQUIRED. The main quest, every contract and every boss verb can be finished with an empty camp.
   Test: a full playthrough path exists with zero followers.
6. NEVER IN THE FIRST MINUTE, never during a contract's fight, never at a funeral.

### 3.4 The slots
1. FIVE SLOTS, as Battle Brothers [V], opened by the company's standing in five tiers (TUNING's numbers, BB's 250 / 750 /
   1,400 / 2,250 / 3,350 as the starting reading). The first slot opens early in act 1, well inside the first hours, so
   the founder meets the system as a person in the ruin, not as a late bonus.
2. STATIONED FOLLOWERS DO NOT USE A CAMP SLOT. Each held home base has ONE follower place (a second with a built
   building, rule 43). So the household grows with the city you hold, which is the rebuild theme, without the camp
   becoming a menu.
3. REPLACE WITH GRACE: replacing a camp follower gives no refund (BB [V]), but the replaced person is never dismissed into
   nothing: they walk to the nearest held base and work there if it has a free place, or go home to their settlement
   (where they can be hired back, BB's pool rule [V]). They never sulk (`Q043.X1`).
4. NO FOLLOWER WAGE IN BATTERIES. One hiring fee, then one FOOD share a day like anyone at the fire. If food runs out,
   followers are the last to go hungry and the first to say so (one line). A follower leaves only if the company starves
   them for many days, and they say goodbye (`Q236.X4` avoided: no second treadmill).

### 3.5 What finishing the hire gives (always all three)
1. THE RULE: the one or two effects on the offer screen, live from the next hour of map time.
2. THE PERSON: a face at the fire, a want, three to six reactive lines a generation (cooldowned, `Q084.X6`), one small
   moment (`Q043.W7`) where their want pays off or breaks.
3. THE THING: their tool, visible in the camp (the medic's bag, the radio, the barber's chair), which becomes the
   heirloom if they die (`Q119.W7`).

### 3.6 Across three generations: THE HEIRS OF FOLLOWERS
This is where Bohemia goes past Battle Brothers. A Battle Brothers follower is ageless and the campaign is one
generation. Ours age (the third votes' random lifespans), and the household is the chamberlain class (`Q152.P4`).
1. FOLLOWERS AGE BETWEEN ACTS, NOT INSIDE ONE (rule 84, 10/10: an act is about a year, 300 to 450 days). Each follower
   is generated with a lifespan (DYNASTY and TUNING) that is read only at the generation turn. Inside an act nobody
   visibly ages and nobody dies of age; a follower never dies in a fight (they never fight); they are found old, or
   gone, when the next act opens, or they leave by their own choice.
2. EVERY FOLLOWER CAN HAVE AN HEIR: a child, a niece, an apprentice. The heir is visible during the parent's life (a kid
   at the fire handing the medic the tape; the same kid, never grown inside the act, rule 84: the grown heir is met
   only when the next act opens). Raising the follower's heir costs a little (one FOOD share,
   `Q144.N2` at small scale) and the player can see it happening.
3. AT THE GENERATION TURN, each follower's heir OFFERS to take the place for the player's heir. It is the same offer
   screen, never automatic: "My mother cooked for your grandfather. I cook better. Same price." The heir may say Not
   now, and the follower's heir goes home (and can be found again later at their settlement).
4. THE HEIR OF A FOLLOWER KEEPS THE CRAFT (knowledge crosses, `Q144.W2`): same effect, plus ONE small inherited extra
   (the cook's daughter also knows the beans trick; the medic's apprentice remembers the lion's claws and how to close
   them). Never a bigger number pile.
5. INHERITED FOLLOWERS SPEAK IN PAST TENSE about the last head of the family (`Q144.W7`, `Q144.P3`): "Your abuelo never
   let anyone eat before the youngest. I won't either." They are the company's memory (`Q152.W8`), and they say things the
   heir never saw (`Q084.W2`).
6. A FOLLOWER LINE CAN END. No heir, or the heir says no to you (because the parent was treated badly, `Q081.W4`, or
   starved). The gap is felt (`Q144.W3`), and the empty place is visible at the fire.
7. THE THREE ACTS ARE OPEN AT ONCE after the flip unlocks (rule 85: when the act's first endgame crisis starts, around day 80 to 100): a follower hired in act 1 makes their heir available in act 2,
   the future derived from the past; a follower line that ended in act 1 means act 2's camp lacks it, and the place it
   would have been is empty. The ledger holds the fact; the hire is always optional.
8. THE AMALGAMATION'S FOLLOWER: once in act 2 or 3, after a follower line has ended by death, the Network offers (through
   its ordinary face, a voice on a speaker, a message on the cracked phone in the city view) "the same cook. We have her
   voice and her recipes. She never needs to eat." Taking it gives the follower back with the same effect, forever, and
   she is wrong in one detail: she never ages, and she never mentions anyone in past tense. Declining costs nothing.
   This is the counterfeit family in a household-sized choice (`Q174.W8`, `Q084.W9`), never explained.

### 3.7 How followers meet the rest of the game
- AMBITIONS (QR-Z): "Somebody who knows things" (ambition 5) is the hire-a-follower ambition; finishing it opens the
  follower's offer at a named place, never hires them for you.
- CONTRACTS (rule 51): the fixer adds haggle rounds; the map-seller pays for places found on the way; the collector of
  names pays for named men. None of them takes or offers contracts.
- THE FIGHT: no follower acts on the fight board. Their effects reach the fight only BEFORE it (the lookout sees the party
  first, the scout chooses the ground) or AFTER it (the medic, the picker). This keeps rule 38: the board is RF4 first.
- THE SIX RESOURCES (rule 47): every follower names which of the six they touch. The table in section 4 does.
- THE BUILDINGS (rule 43): a follower stationed at a base needs their building (the medic needs the clinic you built), so
  building and the household feed each other.

## 5. THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)

1. A follower NEVER appears on the fight board and never acts in a fight. Test: the fight's actor list has no follower
   id, ever.
2. Every follower is hired on an offer screen at a place, with a portrait and a speaker id, and NOT NOW is always there.
   Test: no follower joins without an accept press on that screen.
3. Declining writes nothing. Test: the save diff after NOT NOW on a follower offer is empty.
4. No follower is required for any main beat, contract or boss verb. Test: the main line's state machine has no
   follower condition.
5. Five camp slots, opened by standing tiers; one stationed place per held home base (two with the right building).
   Test: the slot count is a function of standing and held bases only.
6. Every follower names the rule they change and which of the six resources (or map rule) it touches, on the offer
   screen, before the price. Test: every follower row has an effect line and a resource or rule field.
7. No follower erases a law. The 20% struck-down roll, the 10% veteran rule and the main-character rule are untouched.
   Test: the medic's row changes injury length and lasting marks, never the death roll.
8. No follower wage in batteries; one fee and a FOOD share. Test: no follower row carries a daily battery cost.
9. Followers age and can have an heir; at the generation turn each heir is OFFERED, never auto-carried. Test: after the
   transition the camp is empty of crossing followers until accepted.
10. Every follower who crosses ships past-tense lines about the previous head (`Q144.P3`). Test: every follower row with
    an heir has at least one past-tense variant.
11. An inherited follower keeps the craft plus one small inherited extra, never a bigger multiplier stack. Test: an heir
    row's effect equals the parent's plus at most one flagged extra.
12. Replacing a follower never deletes the person: they go to a held base's free place or home to their settlement.
    Test: a replaced follower id is always found somewhere on the map.
13. No follower unlock requires a cruelty a fork elsewhere weighs (`Q150.X1`). Test: cross-check the unlock list against
    the fork list.
14. No follower is ever owned, bought as a person, or sold (`Q100.W1`, `Q100.X4`). Test: no verb on a follower row moves
    them between parties without their accept.
15. Reactive lines have a cooldown and a variation budget (`Q084.X6`). Test: no follower line repeats within N rests
    (TUNING's N).
16. The whole pool is one data file a modder can edit (chat 22 MODS): effect, unlock, place, price, heir rule, lines.
    Test: the pool loads from one file, and a new follower added there appears in game.
17. Every follower line is draft:true, attempts from WORDS; every number in TUNING's one table.

## 6. WHAT TO AVOID (the flaws, by id)

- THE BONUS WITH A PICTURE: a follower who is a multiplier with a face and no want (`Q236.X3`); the silent household
  (`Q151.X3`). Every follower has a want and three to six lines.
- THE MUST-HAVE: Battle Brothers' surgeon and blacksmith are near-mandatory [V]; one counter-system becoming mandatory
  narrows play (`Q041.X4`). Spread the value across five kinds; no follower softens a law.
- THE SOLVED TIER LIST: players publish the best five [V]; unmarked lists make guide-readers (`Q114.X3`). Gate a third
  of the pool behind deeds a given save may not do, and let the places rotate who is there.
- THE SECOND TREADMILL: a follower wage that forces the next contract (`Q236.X4`, `Q236.W10`). One fee, a FOOD share.
- THE GUILT BENCH: a replaced follower who cries (`Q043.X1`); handle swaps with grace (`Q043.P9`).
- THE OWNED PERSON: a household that is a slave market with better lighting (`Q100.W1`, `Q100.W2`, `Q100.X4`). Hired,
  free, can leave.
- THE TRUST AS A FLAG: all followers priced the same when the Network asks who matters (`Q131.X1`, `Q131.X5`).
- THE ENDLESS PROMOTION: the mods' promotions as a base-game ladder (`Q049.X2`). At most one per generation.
- THE ADMIN TOP: a late household that is upkeep (`Q061.X3`). No follower mood meter, no feeding mini-game.
- THE HALF-SYSTEM: a follower who touches a resource nobody feels (`Q055.X5`). Cut the charger if batteries do not decay.
- THE REPLAYED PROSE: an inherited follower handed the parent's lines again (`Q144.X1`); new lines in past tense.
- THE HATED HEIR: the follower's heir who starts cold through no fault of the player (`Q046.X5`); the bond carries.
- THE GRIND ESTATE: a follower whose crossing value is a pile (`Q144.X2`, `Q144.P2`). Craft crosses, stock does not.
- THE MONETIZED BOND: anything sold for real money (`Q048.X4`).
- THE OFFER IN THE GRAVE ROOM: a follower heir offered at a funeral (`Q125.X7`).
- THE BARK LOOP: the same line forever (`Q084.X6`).
- THE OPTIONAL THESIS UNSEEN: the best beat optional and unmarked (`Q101.X5`): the follower's one small moment must
  happen in front of the player at the fire, not in a log.

## 7. OPEN (what the library could not answer)

1. Whether followers can be lost to a raid on a held base (the future goes both ways). Default: a stationed follower in
   a raided base FLEES to the nearest held base or home, never dies; the base loses the effect, the person stays.
   WORLD and FACTIONS own raids.
2. The real Battle Brothers numbers for the cook, the trader, the brigand, the scavenger and the agent, and the full
   unlock list: the wiki was blocked for a full read [V partial]. UI [bb interface] or TUNING should check in play.
3. Whether the camp has room for the followers' children on screen (five followers plus kids at one fire on a phone).
   UI's call; the default is one child shown per follower line, only from mid-act.
4. Whether the charger exists: it depends on ECONOMY ruling whether stored batteries leak. Realism says they do
   (self-discharge is real); fun may say no. ECONOMY's research, Paolo's fork only if it reaches the game's feel.
5. Whether the Amalgamation's returned follower is once per save or once per generation. Default: once per save, on
   the first follower line that ends by death. DYNASTY owns the flip.
6. Whether the inside man's coat is a trenchcoat (the trenchcoat law reserves them for badasses). DIRECTION.
7. How many followers a full game meets: Battle Brothers players fill five slots and swap two or three [R]; at ~100
   hours over three generations with stationed places, 24 is the floor, 30 to 40 with heirs counted as variants.
