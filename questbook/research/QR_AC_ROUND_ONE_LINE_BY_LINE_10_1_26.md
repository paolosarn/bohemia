# QR-AC: ROUND ONE, RUN LINE BY LINE AGAINST ALL 30 CHECKLIST LINES

QUESTION: row [round one line by line]. The 48 round-one designs (QD-A01 to QD-H06, the across-acts D series included) carried a CHECKLIST line saying lines 29 and 30 passed (QR-X, 9/30) but lines 1 to 28 of QR-G's checklist were never run on them one line at a time. Run each one against all 30 lines honestly. Fix in place what can be fixed without changing the premise; where a line is bent on purpose, say which and why. Replace each CHECKLIST line with the real result. Apply rule 51 (one main quest, TWO contract slots) to any design that assumed one.

STATUS: draft:true, research only, nothing built (rule 35). Author: QUESTS lane, round 10/1. The checklist is QR-G's THE CHECKLIST (lines 1 to 28) plus lines 29 and 30 added by QR-U and applied by QR-X.

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

All 48 now pass. 39 pass all 30 lines; 9 pass 29 of 30 with one bend each, stated on the design's own CHECKLIST line with the reason. Nothing was left failing. Getting there took 176 line-fixes, written into the designs in place and tagged "QR-AC, line N" so a builder can find each one. The single commonest hole was line 28: not one of the 48 named the persistent flags it writes, the play-forward test that covers them, or where the game may save (`Q123.X1`, `Q116.X1`, `Q040.X3`). After that the round-one shelf failed the same five ways, and they are all the same mistake underneath: the designs were written as a good scene and not yet as a contract that can go wrong. They had no priced way out once taken (line 12, 18 designs, `Q139.P2`), no result for a lost fight (lines 10 and 23, 17 designs, `Q095.X3`, `Q146.W1`), one way through instead of three (line 9, 14 designs, `Q087.X4`, `Q084.X2`), and no floor for an act the player has not played yet (lines 5 and 18, 14 designs, `Q022.X2`, `Q121.X1`). Smaller clusters: a lie written TOLD and HAPPENED that surfaces later with no tell (line 1, 6 designs, `Q128.X2`), weekday clocks ("Thursday") where the law wants map days (line 3, 5 designs, `Q095.X1`), four across-acts contracts with no offer screen (line 13), and three moral forks where QR-X's pass was too kind (lines 21 and 30: QD-A04, QD-D04, QD-D06). Rule 51 touched three designs (QD-B02, QD-E04, QD-G02); none had hard-coded one slot, but three were worded as if only one contract could be held. The nine bends are all of two kinds: a stated temptation with its number on the button (QR-X reading 3; five designs bend line 21 on purpose), or a design whose whole point is the thing the line forbids (QD-A04 shows the drop, QD-C04 the test twist, QD-D06 the climax fight, QD-H02 the roll call).

## HOW THE RUN WAS MADE

1. Every design was read in full, top to bottom, and asked all 30 lines in order. A line was marked PASS only if the text said so, not if a builder could guess it. "Nothing silently breaks" in a FLAWS line is not a tell; a person, place or thing on a screen is (`Q128.X2`'s own law, `Q146.P4`).
2. READINGS, held the same across all 48 so the next sweep reads the same way:
   - ROAD EVENTS (KIND: event): lines 3, 9 and 12 are read as not applicable (no deadline, a 2 to 3 choice screen, nothing taken to refuse), and line 11 is met by the free KEEP MOVING. This is the reading the round-two shelf already used (QR-I).
   - MAIN BEATS and ASKS THAT ARE NOT CONTRACTS: lines 3, 9 and 12 likewise; line 11 is met when the person speaks only when tapped and not tapping is a full answer.
   - QR-X's three readings of line 30 are kept: a price the player chooses to pay is not a reward (reading 1); a priced refusal pays less for less work if some branch at least as kind pays the full fee (reading 2, `Q139.P2`); a stated temptation on a road event or an honest-devil beat may pay more if the number is on the button (reading 3, `Q166.W4`, `Q148.W8`). Reading 3 is a pass on line 30 and a BEND on line 21, because line 21 says the crueller branch pays the same or less with no number shown, and reading 3 is exactly a number shown. Five designs carry that bend, said plainly.
   - LINE 28 at design level: research cannot run a test, so the line is met when the design NAMES its persistent flags, names every place that reads them (the play-forward test's route), and names the clean save points. Every design now carries a FLAGS AND SAVES paragraph at the end of WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER.
   - LINE 16 (a thumb-sized target): every round-one design was moved onto settlement screens (buildings, building-slots and offer buttons) by QR-P and QR-W; no design asks for a precise tap on a small thing, so 16 passes on all 48 (`Q060.X4`).
3. A FIX may add a way through, a priced exit, a result for a lost fight, a tell, a floor, a prop, or a line; it may change a weekday into map days or correct a number. It may NOT change who asks, what is asked, the twist, or the wrong detail's idea. Where a fix leans on a flaw or a craft finding, the id is in the fix.
4. Where QR-X had passed line 29 or 30 and the run found otherwise, the design was fixed and this page says so (QD-A04, QD-D04, QD-D06).

## THE COMMONEST FAILURES

### 1. Nobody named a flag or a save point (line 28, all 48)
The flaw families behind the line are the library's most expensive: a quest that corrupts saves at many stages (`Q123.X1`), a flag bugged for 25 years so the man you saved is found dead (`Q116.X1`), a fragile chain that breaks on a wrong input (`Q040.X3`). Round one wrote rich ledgers ("Act 1 writes one row... Act 3's derive reads it") and never listed the values, the readers, or when the game may save. The fix is the same paragraph in every design: the flag and its values, every place that reads it, and the clean states (for a contract: the offer screen closed, the hand-in, the map between legs; for an event: before and after the screen; for a beat: before the beat and after its choice). A builder can now turn each paragraph into a play-forward test without reading the scene twice.

### 2. No priced way out once taken (line 12, 18 designs)
DECLINING IS FREE was honoured everywhere: every offer screen writes nothing on a no. But once taken, most round-one contracts had two endings, finish or drop, and the drop is a deed. The library's lesson is that the refusal inside a taken job must be a path with a stated price that FINISHES the contract (`Q139.P2`), because the invisible refusal (`Q131.X7`), the empty stall (`Q134.X2`), refusal only by quitting (`Q134.X10`), the punished no (`Q075.X4`) and no refusal at all (`Q103.X1`) all read the same to a player: the game did not imagine you saying no. The fixes are small and concrete: one night of two (QD-A01), the halfway well (QD-A02), the half book (QD-A03), the waystation (QD-C01), stop after night one (QD-C05), tell Rafa the truth (QD-C06), refuse to cap it to their face (QD-D01), as far as the culvert (QD-D04), tell her where the truck is (QD-E01), name who is on the wheel (QD-E02), look through the window only (QD-E03), hold nine for him (QD-E04), stop at the car (QD-F02), the first minute only (QD-F03), two nights of three (QD-A05), hand the two back (QD-A06), bring the drive back (QD-E05), stop looking (QD-E06). Each pays less for less work and closes FINISHED, never as a drop, so QR-X reading 2 holds. The model the library offers is an off-ramp that is real and banks nothing false (`Q140.W8`) and a smaller door that is always open (`Q142.W8`); QD-G01 and QD-G04, written against QR-G, already had it.

### 3. A lost fight with no result (lines 10 and 23, 17 designs)
Round one put fights in contracts ("a group is standing on it, and the fight is there") and wrote only the win. A phone player who loses a fight and is offered a reload has been handed the console's worst habit (`Q095.X3`: the fight is the weakest part of its own climax; `Q098.X2`; `Q017.X1`). The library's answer is that defeat continues as a state, not a load screen (`Q146.W1`), and that botching costs the reward, not the story (`Q114.W3`). Every fix is a result sized to the leg (`Q093.X2`, `Q111.X2`, `Q128.X10`): the crew takes one truck's salt and Yesenia pays half (QD-A02), the crate stays with the group (QD-C01, QD-E01), the box stays at the squat (QD-C02), Cano's crew is paid instead (QD-C03), Teodora goes the next night alone and the act 3 street is the do-nothing street (QD-D04), the message goes by relay after all (QD-D05), the sibling still wins and you are hurt (QD-F04). Line 23's other half, the emotional scene reachable without a fight, already held on 47 of 48; QD-D06 bends it because the scene lives inside the act 1 climax.

### 4. One way through (line 9, 14 designs)
"A group is on the valve; it is a fight" is a fetch with a fight bolted on (`Q087.X4`, `Q084.X2`). The fix is the library's own errand rule: every leg has three or more solutions (`Q079.W6`), and the three are almost always the same three in our world: fight it, talk or pay it in one of the six resources, or come at another hour or by another road (night, noon, the wash, the culvert, the tunnels). The talk options lean on our canon, not on a skill check: the men on the wall are the dry block two streets up (QD-A01), the people on the wheel lose pressure when it runs (QD-E02), the scrappers want copper and you want titanium (QD-A06). No new way pays more than the fight way (line 30), and none needs a stat (line 29, `Q155.P2`).

### 5. No floor for an act not yet played (lines 5 and 18, 14 designs)
Since 9/23 the three acts are open at once and act 2 unlocks mid-act 1 (rule 39c), so every plant has two readers: the act that set it, and an act opened before it was set. Round one wrote the landing and forgot the floor, the exact flaw of the payoff gated on bookkeeping (`Q096.X1`), the choice cosmetic in its own game (`Q121.X1`), the meaning that needs a replay (`Q022.X2`) and the hidden order (`Q023.X2`, `Q104.X6`, `Q112.X5`). QD-G03 is the model and was already right: the C read is "the floor, full weight", and the street re-derives when act 1 is played (`Q144.P3`, `Q140.P2`, `Q114.P3`). Every fix now says two things: where the landing is drawn on arrival (a settlement screen, never a street, since the walk is dead), and what the act reads when the row is unset.

### 6. The lie that surfaces with no tell (line 1, 6 designs)
Round one used the told/happened pair well (`Q148.P2`, `Q148.X1`: the lie must be discoverable) and then wrote "the gap can surface later" and stopped. A gap that surfaces off screen is the silent fail the phone punishes hardest (`Q071.X1`, `Q092.X6`: a consequence the player never discovers is trivia). Each fix is one diegetic tell at a place he will tap: the clerk with the book open at that page (QD-A03), Toño with his sunglasses off (QD-A04), Vela turning one page back (QD-C02), Rafa not looking up (QD-C06), the cut fitting still wet (QD-D01), the man holding his unplugged cord (QD-F02). That is `Q071.P9`'s feedback and `Q146.P4`'s world that warns about itself.

## RULE 51: TWO CONTRACT SLOTS

No round-one design hard-coded one slot. Three were worded as if only one contract could be held, and each now says two: QD-B02 (the tracks at the fork can make both taken contracts late, and the companion says so before the choice), QD-E04 (`Q037.X2`'s "nothing stacks" now means nothing stacks inside it; it sits beside one other contract), QD-G02 (the map shows every held contract's lamp beside the clinic's, up to two). The library supports the second slot as long as the clocks stay visible: the player who sees both deadlines before choosing is the fix for the hidden timer that punishes thoroughness (`Q137.X3`), and focus (`Q037.X2`) is kept by the cap of two, not one.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS

1. Every contract names its priced exit: one choice that finishes the taken job for less, at a stated price, written FINISHED (line 12, `Q139.P2`). A design with only finish and drop is unfinished.
2. Every fight in a contract or event names its lost result in the same paragraph as the fight: who takes what, what is paid, and that it closes FAILED WHILE TRYING (lines 10 and 23, `Q146.W1`).
3. Every leg names three ways through, and at least one of them is not a fight (line 9, `Q079.W6`).
4. Every told/happened lie names the tell: who the player meets, where, and what they hold or say (line 1, `Q128.X2`).
5. Every plant across acts names its landing (drawn on arrival on a settlement screen) and its floor (the read when the row is unset) (lines 5 and 18, `Q022.X2`).
6. Every clock is in map days or map nights, said in a screen line and drawn at the place; no weekday names (line 3, `Q095.X1`).
7. Every contract has THE SETTLEMENT AND THE OFFER SCREEN with no more than three lines (line 13).
8. Every design ends WHAT THE LEDGERS REMEMBER with FLAGS AND SAVES: values, readers, clean saves (line 28, `Q123.X1`).
9. Every bend is written on the CHECKLIST line with the line number and the reason; a bend with no reason is a fail.
10. Any wording that implies one contract slot is wrong (rule 51); a design that shows held deadlines shows up to two.
