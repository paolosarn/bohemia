# QR-AA: HOW A HUNT CONTRACT WORKS, AND WHAT EACH OF OUR LAB-MADE BEASTS TEACHES

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

A hunt is the cleanest contract the library holds, and it has five parts that every good one keeps: an OFFER at a
dignified front door where the fee is settled before the truth is known (`Q148.W1`, `Q148.W2`, `Q236.W7`); SIGNS on the
way that a careful player reads and a careless one misreads ONCE (`Q148.W3`, `Q019.W3`); a LAIR whose look tells its own
story before anything moves (`Q003.W2`, `Q003.W3`); a fight that is won by KNOWING THE BEAST'S ONE RULE rather than by
out-damaging it (`Q018.W5`, `Q060.W1`, `Q148.P5`); and a TROPHY whose worth is honest, in the same currency as the
risk (`Q148.W8`, `Q103.X5`). Battle Brothers adds the two things the library lacked: the beast's one rule is a real
mechanic on the board (a pack that flanks, a bite that shreds armour, a thing that grows on the dead, a giant that
heals unless you burst it), and the tracks are on the MAP, so the hunt is also travel (recall). For Bohemia this
lands cleanly because our beasts are real animals with real habits: a dire wolf pack really does flank, a hyena
really does crack bone and armour, a short-faced bear really did live on carcasses, a sabre-tooth really could not
chase. So the SIGN is the habit, the LAIR is where the habit lives in a dead city, the ONE RULE is the habit on the
fight board, and the TROPHY is what a broken economy pays for that animal's body: meat and hide as FOOD and TAPE,
ivory and pelts as BATTERIES, a live capture as a season's pay. Two warnings run through all of it: the contract
formula dies of repetition when every hunt has the same twist (`Q148.X3`, `Q236.X2`), and a hunt that can only be
solved from a wiki is a failed hunt on a phone (`Q019.X1`, `Q037.X1`, `Q023.X3`). The fix is the same for both:
every beast teaches ONE thing, the signs for it are always on the map, and the rare twist (it was never the animal's
fault) is rationed and usually points at the LAB.

## THE FINDINGS

### 1. THE HUNT IS A CONTRACT FIRST: THE FRONT DOOR, THE FEE, THE FREE NO

- The notice board works because it is a DIGNIFIED front door: public, repeatable, opt-in, and it never begs
  (`Q148.W2`). Battle Brothers' hall is the same door with a face on it: the board is a readout of the world's
  current problems, not a quest list (`Q236.W1`), and the hall at a Bohemia home base generates its asks from that
  base's situation (`Q236.P1`). A hunt contract, then, exists only because a beast is REALLY on the map near that
  settlement: a dire wolf pack on the I-15 posts a hunt at the truck stop, not anywhere else.
- THE FEE LOCKS BEFORE THE TRUTH (`Q148.W1`). The player haggles while knowing nothing, so no later choice at the
  lair is about the money. For a hunt this matters because the lair is where the twist lives: the keeper who loves
  the animal, the pup, the lab tag. If the fee is already settled, sparing the animal costs only what the player
  decides it costs.
- ONE CONTRACT AT A TIME (`Q236.W2`), and THE SKULLS ARE A READING (`Q236.W3`): the danger is stated before the yes,
  so "too strong for me" is an informed no. The hunt offer screen shows the beast's kind and its skulls; a lion is
  three skulls in act 2 and says so.
- THE ACCEPT BUTTON IS THE ONLY PROMISE (`Q236.W7`); DECLINE WRITES NOTHING (`Q236.P3`). The feral dogs keep
  killing chickens whether you take the job or not; the world moves, the ledger does not.
- THE FACE IS THE NEGOTIATION UI (`Q236.W4`) and GREED HAS A CLOCK (`Q236.W5`), but the haggle must stay a person,
  not solved math (`Q236.X1`): the rancher's ceiling is a line he says ("That is the whole herd's worth."), never a
  hidden number.
- THE PAYMENT MODE IS A BET (`Q236.W6`). For hunts this is the most useful BB lever: pay ON COMPLETION, pay PER
  HEAD (per wolf), or TROPHY AS PAY (you keep the pelt and the client pays less). Each is a bet on your company.
- ONE HOUR, WHOLE ARC (`Q148.W10`): a hunt goes from notice to payment inside one sitting. On a phone, one sitting is
  shorter, which is why our hunt is one leg of travel, one lair, one fight (QR-G's size rule).

### 2. THE SIGNS BEFORE THE FIGHT: TRACKING THAT CAN BE MISREAD ONCE, BUT NEVER UNFAIRLY

- THE EVIDENCE IS BUILT TO BE MISREAD ONCE (`Q148.W3`): the hoofprints that are really two legs. The scene plants a
  reflex answer and pays the player for looking again. OUR version is free, because the animals are real: a dire
  wolf's print looks like a big dog's until you see the stride; a hyena kill looks like a dog kill until you see
  that the bones are CRACKED, not gnawed; a short-faced bear kill looks like wolves until you see the carcass was
  DRAGGED a long way.
- THE HOLISTIC SCENE READ (`Q019.W3`): the clue is rarely on the corpse; it is in the whole tableau (what is near,
  what is missing). A cattle carcass with no wolves' tracks but a clean water trough gone dry says "something big
  drinks here at night."
- BUT THE ART MUST BE READABLE (`Q019.X1`): the Obra Dinn flaw is literally "is the beast biting, eating, poisoning?"
  in art too small to tell. On a phone this is fatal. Every sign we use is ONE big readable thing: a print, a
  dragged line, a cracked bone, a tag, a smell told by a mouth. Never a detail smaller than a thumb.
- THE FIX FOR NO-MARKER DESIGN IS BETTER IN-WORLD CLUES, not markers-or-nothing (`Q023.W10`); and EVERY quest must be
  fairly clueable, or one bad quest poisons the rest (`Q023.X3`, `Q037.X1`, "find that guy in the desert"). The hunt
  therefore always has TWO pointers: the client's own line on the offer screen ("They come up the wash after
  dark.") and the tracks on the map (Battle Brothers' paw prints that fade with time, recall, 01_WORLDMAP.md).
- THE FIRST OBSTACLE IS THE SMELL (`Q112.W1`), and LOOKING WHERE THE TRUTH IS CAN HURT (`Q112.W8`). A lair is
  approached through its smell and its leftovers; reading a bear's cache up close can cost a company member a day
  (sick) or MEDS. The sign has a price, and the price is small and stated.
- A SIGN IS ALSO A PERSON: the witness who has SEEN the beast is often worth more than the tracks (`Q003.W8`): the
  herder who lost two calves knows it comes from the east, at dusk, and only for the young.

### 3. KNOWING THE BEAST'S ONE MECHANIC IS THE PREPARATION

- KNOWLEDGE-GATES DISGUISED AS SKILL-GATES (`Q018.W5`): the hard zone yields to understanding, not to gear. "I figured
  it out" beats "I levelled up." A hunt's best reward is the moment the player realizes the lion always takes the
  LAST man in the line, and puts his strongest man last.
- THERE IS ALWAYS A WAY (`Q018.W4`): every beast has a knowledge key, and the world never cheats. This is the trust
  contract a bestiary must keep; a beast whose rule cannot be learned is a stat block, not a lesson.
- PREPARATION CASHES AS VERBS (`Q148.W4`, `Q148.P5`): a long-ago build choice turns into minutes saved and doors
  opened. For us: a company with a HANDLER (a dog man, rule 42's keepers) reads tracks a day faster; a man with a
  pike holds the wolves' flank; a crate of TAPE bought before the hyena hunt is the difference between a bad fight
  and a ruined company. PREP SHAPES OPTIONS (`Q024.W9`).
- TELEGRAPH THE BEAST'S NEXT MOVE (`Q052.W5`, `Q060.W1`): when intent is shown, you fail from misplay, not surprise.
  On the fight board the beast's rule is VISIBLE as a tell: the pack's outer wolves circle before they close; the
  bull paws the ground a beat before the charge; the bear's wounds visibly close. NO RANDOM CHANCE IN YOUR ACTIONS
  (`Q060.W2`) is ours only for the dial shot; the routine hits stay Battle Brothers-rough (rule 22 amended), so the
  tell carries the fairness.
- THE BOARD IS THE WEAPON (`Q060.W5`): push, position, terrain. For beasts this is the whole game: the cattle are
  turned, not killed; the sabre-tooth is fought AWAY from the water tile; the mammoth's two tiles are split by a wall.
- BEATABLE BY UNDERSTANDING, NOT GRINDING (`Q006.W9`): a company that knows the rule can win a two-skull hunt
  under-geared; a company that does not can lose a veteran at the 20% death roll (rule 36) to a one-skull pack.
- AND ON A PHONE, THE INPUT MUST BE FIRST-CLASS (`Q060.X4`): a positioning lesson that needs a precise drag on a
  small screen is a touch bug, not a lesson. The beast's rule must be answerable with a tap per man.

### 4. THE LAIR: THE PLACE TELLS THE STORY BEFORE ANYTHING MOVES

- ENVIRONMENTAL WARNING (`Q003.W2`): the space is the exposition. The dire wolves' den in a dead elementary school
  says it with the bones in the sandbox; nobody explains.
- QUIET-HORROR PACING (`Q003.W3`): empty first, then the thing. On the fight board this is the first beat or two
  with nothing on the tiles but signs, before the pack shows. It must stay short (routine fights are 2 to 4 minutes,
  rule 40), so the quiet is two beats, not two levels.
- THE DESCENT (`Q004.W9`): a physical going-down to a trapped thing. The Gypsum Cave annex of the lab is the one
  lair that earns this staging.
- SILENCE IS AN INSTRUMENT (`Q090.W9`): the music exists to make the silence worse. A lair's sound is the absence of
  the city hum; the analog horror law's "one wrong detail" is often a sound (a feeder's motor still running).
- THE LOCATION BIRTHS THE CHARACTER (`Q001.W13`): each beast belongs to a kind of place in the valley (wolves on the
  freeway shoulders, hyenas at the landfill, the sabre-tooth at the water, the mammoth at Lake Mead's shore), and
  the fight board is cut from that place (rule 46a), so the lair is always recognizably the block he tapped.

### 5. THE TROPHY AND WHAT IT IS WORTH

- THE KILL IS HONESTLY PRICED IN POWER (`Q148.W8`): the mutagen sits in the corpse, scarce and useful, and the game
  lets it whisper. Temptation with a real number on it. Our trophies are real goods with real numbers: a dire wolf
  pelt is TAPE-grade hide and a mantle (an armour attachment, rule 36 law's attachments), a hyena clan's bounty is
  batteries, a bull is 300 kilos of FOOD that spoils (`Q088.W4`, food spoils on a schedule), a mammoth tusk is ivory,
  a real trade today, and the biggest single BATTERIES payout on the shelf.
- THE CLIENT'S CLOSURE IS OUTCOME-INVARIANT (`Q148.W9`): trophy in, coin out, both worlds. A hunt should pay the same
  whether the player killed the beast or proved it gone, which is what lets mercy be a choice and not a loss.
- BUT THE TROPHY MUST NOT LAUNDER TOO CLEAN (`Q148.X1`): the spare route's white lie had no risk and no later cost.
  Our fix: a spared beast is on the map later (the pack still roams, the tracks still show), and the client can see
  it. Mercy has a guaranteed chance of being discovered.
- PAY IN THE SAME CURRENCY AS THE RISK (`Q103.X5`, "the reward is a good horse"): if the fight cost TAPE (hyenas
  shred armour), pay TAPE; if it cost MEDS (the bear), pay MEDS or batteries that buy them. A payout in an unrelated
  currency makes the risk feel unpaid.
- REVENGE EQUALS LOOT (`Q048.W10`): align the feeling and the reward. The lion that took your veteran is worth more
  to kill, and the game should let the pelt say so (a named trophy).
- DO NOT LET EARLY BEASTS OUT-DAMAGE THEIR REWARD (`Q067.X5`): an act 1 feral dog hunt that costs more MEDS than it
  pays is a trap before the story hooks. Act 1 hunts pay more than they cost unless the player chose badly.
- THE CRUNCH MUST NOT FORCE THE YES (`Q236.X4`): hunts are income, but never the only income; SCAVENGE in the
  settlement screen stays open (third votes), so a no is never a slow death.

### 6. "THE MONSTER IS SOMEBODY", RATIONED, AND POINTED AT THE LAB

- The contract formula's famous turn is that the monster is a person (`Q148.W6`, the monster gives the trophy), and
  its famous flaw is that the turn becomes expected (`Q148.X3`); Battle Brothers' twist becomes a template too
  (`Q236.X2`). RATION THE SOMEBODY (`Q148.P4`): most hunts are exactly what they say.
- THE MONSTER HUNT IS A TRUTH COMMISSION (`Q155.W1`) and THE CURSE IS A GRIEVANCE IN ANIMAL FORM (`Q155.W2`). In our
  world the animals did nothing wrong; they are what a lab made and a crash let out. So our "somebody" is almost
  never the beast. It is the lab (a place), the keeper who stayed (a person), the buyer who wants the animal alive
  (`Q200.W4`, property or person, applied to a made animal). The rationed twist points there.
- THE NON-HOSTILE MONSTER (`Q039.W10`) and THE CORRECT VERB IS A LULLABY (`Q138.W5`): some beasts only become a fight
  if you start one. The camel bull in rut, the mammoth herd, the grazing horses: the misread (reach for the gun) is
  allowed, priced and never scolded.
- WHAT YOU CANNOT KILL IS INFINITE (`Q090.W1`): the swarm and the pigeon sky are not fights, and are more frightening
  for it.
- THE HUMANIZED SIDE MUST CARRY INTO THE END (`Q155.X3`, `Q207.X1`): if a keeper's love for the wolves is shown, she
  is at the lair when the choice is made, not a line on the offer screen.

### 7. THE LAB IS A PLACE THAT MADE THE PROBLEM AND WILL SELL YOU THE ANSWER

- THEY SELL YOU THE PLAGUE AND THE CURE (`Q092.W6`): the Church created the beasts, then established the hunt and
  armed the hunters. The purest shape for our lab: whoever holds the lab's freezer holds the next generation of
  beasts AND the market for killing them.
- THE APOCALYPSE IS A LAB-ETHICS DISPUTE WITH A CUSTOMER BASE (`Q092.W4`): de-extinction was sold (a zoo, a hunt
  reserve, a tech demo), and the customers are gone. The lab is the ruin of a business, which is exactly our crash.
- THE TWO-SIDED KNOWLEDGE PROBLEM (`Q003.W5`) and THE INSTITUTION WILL MISUSE IT (`Q003.W6`): burn the tissue bank
  (no more beasts, no more knowledge) or sell it (to whoever will make more). Both have teeth.
- A CONSEQUENCE NEVER DISCOVERED IS TRIVIA (`Q092.X6`): whatever the player does at the lab must SHOW later: the next
  act's beasts are fewer, or more, or different, and a mouth says why.
- THE WORLD RUNS WITHOUT YOU (`Q085.W1`) and HAS AN IMMUNE SYSTEM (`Q085.W8`): beasts roam, breed and arrive along
  real corridors whether or not the player hunts (the law's s3, amended: labs a state or two over, the I-15, the
  Colorado, the US-95). The lab is one source of many; closing it does not close the valley.
- NEVER EXPLAIN TOO MUCH: the analog horror law and `Q092.X1` agree from opposite ends: Bloodborne buried its story
  so deep it needed a fan document; the horror bible wants ONE wrong detail said plainly. The lab shows a whiteboard,
  a feeder that still runs, a door cut from the inside. It never shows a villain's diary.

### 8. BEASTS AND WHAT YOU HOLD: RULES 40 AND 43

- RAIDS SCALE WITH VISIBLE PROSPERITY (`Q151.W3`) and threat scales with what you have (`Q044.W4`); a dragon comes
  for your gold (`Q045.W1`). Beasts are the purest form: a coop draws lizards, a smokehouse draws the bear, a corral
  draws the lion. Building on a held lot (rule 40b) ADDS beast pressure to that block.
- OWNERSHIP IS AN ADDRESS (`Q151.W1`): a part you hold is where the world's events land. A contract that hands you a
  PART (rule 43) hands you its beasts too.
- THE SIEGE HAPPENS WHERE THE HOURS LIVE (`Q151.W6`): the fight board cut from your own held block, with your own
  building on it, is the most personal board in the game.
- BUT THE BASE MUST NOT BECOME A FARM THAT HARVESTS ITS ATTACKERS (`Q151.X1`): if a wall makes every beast raid a free
  pelt, beasts stop being a threat. Beasts must LEARN the wall (the pack tests a new side) and must not respawn on a
  schedule.
- BUILDING IS THE SURRENDER OF MOBILITY (`Q151.W4`): a roaming company flees a lion; a company with a smokehouse
  cannot.

### 9. BEASTS AND HEIRS: RULE 39

- KNOWLEDGE IS THE ESTATE'S GOLD STANDARD (`Q144.W2`) and THE COMMUNITY INVENTED THE INHERITANCE (`Q088.W9`): what
  crosses generations best is what was learned. The hunt's inheritance is the bestiary knowledge (where the lion
  drinks) and the trophy (the pelt the heir wears).
- THE SECOND LIFE PROVES THE FIRST HAPPENED (`Q144.W9`): the act 2 heir meets the grown pack his father spared, or
  the empty den his father cleared.
- INHERITED TRAITS (`Q046.W3`): the kennel's dogs have lines as the family does; the company's dog is the grandson of
  the pup the first generation kept.
- NAMED ENEMIES WITH A HISTORY WITH YOU (`Q048.W1`), WHO GROW FROM YOUR LOSSES (`Q048.W3`) and wear the scar you gave
  them (`Q048.W6`): a beast that killed a company member becomes a NAMED champion (the translation table's champions
  row) and carries your man's torn cloth in its lair. Heirs can inherit a grudge against an animal.
- DO NOT REPLAY THE PROSE (`Q144.X1`): the heir's version of a hunt is a new situation derived from the old ledger,
  never the same offer screen again.

THE SIX RESOURCES, AS A HUNT PAYS AND COSTS (rule 47; TUNING owns the numbers, these are the shape):
- BATTERIES: the fee (the client's coin), the per-head bounty, the trophy buyer's price.
- FOOD: a beast's meat (cattle, camel, hog, horse, sloth); spoils on BB's clock unless smoked (a building).
- MEDS: what a bear or a lion costs you; also a client's pay in kind (a clinic pays in MEDS).
- ROUNDS: what a hunt costs if you shoot it; the melee weak tier (rule 47c) is the cheap answer to dogs.
- TAPE: armour repair after hyenas and bulls; hides pay it back.
- WATER: the sabre-tooth's pond and the mammoth's shore are WATER places; clearing one frees a pump.

FIGHT LENGTH (rule 40a): a road pack (dogs, wolves, one tile of hyenas) is routine, 2 to 4 minutes. A boss beast
(lion, bear, sabre-tooth, mammoth) is tough, 8 to 15 minutes, never past 15. Tough only for the three reasons:
enemy level, enemy gear (the lab's plated bear), or the player's mistake (fighting the sabre-tooth at night).

## THE HUNT CONTRACT, STAGE BY STAGE (our formula; each stage cites where it came from)

1. THE HALL (the settlement screen): a face, the beast named plainly, the skulls, 2 to 4 lines, one of which is a
   SIGN ("It only takes the calves."). Pay, the payment mode, one or two haggles, an advance as an honest loan.
   Decline writes nothing. (`Q148.W2`, `Q236.W3`, `Q236.W6`, `Q236.P5`)
2. THE MAP: the hunt names an AREA, not a pin; tracks of that kind appear in it and fade with time; a road event may
   add a witness. The clock runs; food is eaten. (`Q023.W10`, `Q090.W4`, 01_WORLDMAP.md recall)
3. THE SIGN: one readable thing that tells the rule, misreadable once (the print that is a wolf, not a dog).
   (`Q148.W3`, `Q019.W3`, `Q019.X1`)
4. THE LAIR: the fight board cut from that block; two quiet beats; the one wrong detail. (`Q003.W2`, `Q003.W3`)
5. THE FIGHT: the beast's one rule, with its tell; routine 2 to 4 minutes, a boss 8 to 15. (`Q060.W1`, `Q018.W5`)
6. THE TROPHY: a real good in one of the six resources; the named trophy if it killed one of yours. (`Q148.W8`,
   `Q048.W10`)
7. THE HAND-IN and THE LEDGER: same pay if spared and proven gone; the spared beast stays on the map to be seen.
   (`Q148.W9`, `Q148.X1`)

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)

1. A hunt contract is posted ONLY at a settlement whose map area actually holds that beast's party (test: every hunt
   offer names a beast party that exists within N map tiles of the hall). (`Q236.W1`, `Q148.P1`)
2. Every hunt offer screen shows the beast's KIND and SKULLS before accept. (`Q236.W3`)
3. Every hunt has at least two in-world pointers: one line on the offer screen and one kind of track on the map.
   (`Q023.X3`, `Q037.X1`)
4. Every beast kind has exactly ONE rule, written in the bestiary data file, and a visible TELL on the fight board
   one beat before the rule fires. (`Q052.W5`, `Q060.W1`)
5. Every sign is readable at phone size: one object per sign, never a detail under a thumb's width. (`Q019.X1`)
6. The rule can be answered with one tap per company member; no drag precision. (`Q060.X4`)
7. Every trophy pays in the resource the fight cost, or in batteries that clearly buy it. (`Q103.X5`, `Q148.W8`)
8. An act 1 hunt, fought sensibly, pays more than it costs in MEDS and TAPE. (`Q067.X5`)
9. The fee is locked at accept; nothing at the lair changes it except a stated per-head clause. (`Q148.W1`)
10. A spared or relocated beast stays on the map as a visible party; the client can see it. (`Q148.X1`, `Q092.X6`)
11. At most one hunt in five carries a twist; the twist points at people (the lab, a keeper, a buyer), not at the
    animal's morals. (`Q148.P4`, `Q236.X2`)
12. A beast that kills a company member is promoted to a NAMED beast with a trophy that says whose it was. (`Q048.W1`,
    `Q048.W3`)
13. A building on a held lot raises beast pressure on that block by kind (coop: lizards and dogs; smokehouse: bear;
    corral: lion and wolves), and beasts do not respawn on a fixed schedule. (`Q151.W3`, `Q151.X1`)
14. Hunts finish inside one sitting: one area, one lair, one fight. (`Q148.W10`)
15. Declining a hunt writes nothing; the beast's effect on the settlement runs on the world's clock anyway.
    (`Q236.P3`, `Q085.W1`)
16. The lab is never explained by a document; it is explained by one object per room. (`Q003.W2`, `Q092.X1`)
