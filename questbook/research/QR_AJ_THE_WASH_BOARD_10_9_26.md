# QR-AJ: WHAT ELSE IS ON THE WASH'S BOARD WHILE LUPE'S THREE JOBS RUN

Lupe's three jobs are the spine of the demo, but a real job board always has a few other small jobs pinned next to them. This page gives the demo eight of those small jobs in the Wash and the two camps a day's walk away, plus two bar rumours and the road events you can meet, so the board is never empty and never crowded.

QUESTION: row [the wash board] (QUESTS, release lines 2 and 13). Everything else on the demo town's board: 6 to 8 small contracts from QR-AF's kinds at THE WASH and the one or two places within a day's travel, while QR-AI's chain runs; each with face, skulls, pay in the six resources, fight or no fight, what hand-in changes, which chain job it pairs with on which road (QR-AE); plus the keeper's rumours (QR-AH) and the road events in reach (QR-AG). ## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The demo's map (slices/BOHEMIA_LOOP.html, the N and E tables, HPU road 30 and dirt 55 hours per valley unit) puts three places inside one in-game day of THE WASH: COPPER SHEDS about 17 hours east by road, PUMP CAMP about 20 hours southwest (dirt to the junction, road after), THE FORT about 22 hours south. THE TOWER is 28 hours out and stays outside the demo board. So the side board lives at three spots: THE WASH (a town, two offers showing), PUMP CAMP and COPPER SHEDS (camps, one offer each), which is QR-O's count (camp 1, town 2, never more than three on a phone; `Q037.X2`) and the screen's own TIER_OFFERS. THE FORT is a fortress and is the full game's: in the demo it is a place to sell and recruit, its hall closed with a line ("Nothing for strangers this month"), so the stranger's demo stays one town wide (`Q084.X3` warns against empty towns, so the two camps carry real offers instead). Eight small jobs ride beside the chain: four at THE WASH (the Ortiz kid already on the screen, a clinic carry, the smith's stolen wire, Sarge's road walk), two at PUMP CAMP (coyotes at the intake, greens for Nena) and two at COPPER SHEDS (the copper cart, the dog trail). Every one of them pairs on a road with one of Lupe's three jobs, so taking it is a detour of hours, not a second trip (`Q236.W1`, `Q148.P1`, QR-AE). Only one has a twist (the Ortiz kid, T08 THE CLUE IS MISREAD ONCE); the other seven are exactly what they say (`Q148.P4`, ration the somebody; `Q236.X2`, the twist as a template). Two rumours from Manny point inside the day's reach, and four of QR-AG's ten events can fire on these roads, never in the first minute and never on the water cart's walk.

## THE FINDINGS (with the library)

### 1. The board is the town's problems, and side jobs are the town's smaller problems
`Q236.W1` and `Q148.P1`: the board is generated from what is wrong in the place. `Q236.N1` gives the hall shape (faces, one-line problem, skulls). `Q148.W2`: a board is a dignified front door that never begs, which is why expiries write nothing. `Q055.W1` and `Q051.W2`: the world lives without you, so offers expire on their own clock. `Q055.W7`: the road crew is a faction with its own reasons, which is why it shows up in four jobs as the same group.

### 2. Small jobs must pay off on screens the player sees
`Q236.W8` and `Q236.P4`: the outcome changes prices and boards. `Q024.W10`: acknowledge what the player did. `Q002.W7`: the payout is a moment with a line. `Q236.W9`: bigger jobs appear as you grow, which is why three-skull work stays Lupe's.

### 3. Pairing on a road is what makes small jobs worth the trip
QR-AE's table, plus `Q001.W5` (a job threads through others), `Q002.W1` (one incident fans into several jobs: the road crew), `Q009.W10` (the delayed organic trigger: S2 appears because chain 2 ended). `Q037.X2`: too many held jobs flood the log, hence two slots and the cap. `Q051.X3`: backtracking tedium, the reason every offer lives on a chain road.

### 4. Twists are rationed; plain jobs are the default
`Q148.P4`, `Q148.X3`, `Q236.X2`, `Q236.N4` say the twist must be rare or it becomes the expected shape. `Q148.W3` and `Q148.X2` justify the single T08 twist. `Q016.W1` and `Q016.P1` (the MacGuffin is a person) were rejected for the demo because chain 3's tag is the demo's one wrong note and W1 already has a person. `Q003.W1` (the dirty errand) is banked for the full game.

### 5. Saying no is free, haggling is a face
`Q126.W3`, `Q236.P3`, `Q236.W7`: declining writes nothing. `Q236.W4`, `Q236.P2`: the face is the haggle. `Q236.X4`: the wage burn must not force a yes, so all eight are small enough to skip. `Q148.W1`: the fee locks at accept. `Q236.W6` and `Q236.P5`: an advance only on two-skull jobs.

### 6. Pay in kind teaches the economy
`Q021.P5`, `Q088.W4`, `Q088.P2`: water, food and meds as pay make the three legs felt. `Q031.W3`: scarcity makes the small job tempting. `Q044.W4`: threat scales with what you have, so the board never jumps past two skulls while the party is poor. `Q044.W7`: ease off after a hard hit, the no-fight jobs between chain 2 and 3.

### 7. Choices and faces
`Q031.W6` and `Q031.P6`: the same face returns (Teo twice, Sarge if you lose). `Q042.W3`: one line per offer may change by origin. `Q010.W8` and `Q010.W7`: the rumour's note is foreshadowing that never resolves. `Q044.W2`: the storyteller picks which side offer shows, by what the party just did. `Q107.W4`: the stolen wire is the rebellion as inventory shrinkage, a small mirror of the town's tension.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)

1. Exactly these eight side offers exist in the demo; each carries draft:true.
2. THE WASH never shows more than 2 offers, a camp never more than 1; THE FORT and THE TOWER show 0 in the demo.
3. From new game to chain 3 taken, THE WASH shows at least 1 offer at every tick.
4. Every side offer names a chain id it pairs with and a road leg from the loop's E table.
5. No side offer has 3 skulls; no side fight is cut bigger than 8 by 11; every side fight ends inside 4 minutes on a phone.
6. Only W1 has a twist id (T08).
7. Expiry and decline write nothing; take, finish, fail and drop write deeds.
8. At most one road event per trip; none in the first minute; none on chain 1.
9. Every hand-in changes one visible thing on a settlement screen.

- `Q066.X1` and `Q236.X3`: the client as a vending machine; each client has one want beyond the fee.
- `Q126.X1` and `Q108.X2`: a dominant choice; W1's in-kind pay is worth more only when food is low.
- `Q107.X1`: options that do not match their outcomes; the patrol's pay changes with the corner, and Sarge says why.
- `Q044.X1`: unfair stacked events; one event per trip.
- `Q045.X1`, `Q051.X1`, `Q055.X2`: a flooded first hour; the side board only fills after chain 1 is taken.
- `Q021.W9` and `Q126.X4`: nothing grades the player for skipping work.
