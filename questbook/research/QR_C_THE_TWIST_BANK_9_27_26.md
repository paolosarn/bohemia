# QR-C: WHAT ONE TWIST CAN A CONTRACT CARRY, AND WHAT DOES EACH ONE COST YOU?

QUESTION (row [twist bank], question (b)): Every twist the studied quests use (the client lied, the target moved, a third party shows up, the thing you were sent for is a person, the job was a test, the reward is gone, and the rest). Build THE TWIST BANK: each twist with a name, the ids that show it, what it costs the player (nothing / time / batteries / standing / a person), how late it lands (at the offer, mid-job, at hand-in, acts later), and which of our contracts it fits (boom or bust, which act). Sort by cost. This is the bank a Battle Brothers-style contract draws its ONE twist from. Also the rule: when a twist is fair versus a cheat, and how a twist respects DECLINING IS FREE and ONCE TAKEN YOU FINISH IT.

STATUS: draft:true, research only, nothing built (rule 35). For RUN, WORLD, PEOPLE, WORDS when the map and the fight board exist.
FOLDED 10/9 (QR-AK): QR-U rule 18, four new bank entries and the sharpens of rules 2, 6 and 8, at the end of THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS, each marked in place.

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The 152 studied quests use about thirty distinct twists, and they sort cleanly by what they take from the player: some cost nothing but a changed meaning (the haunting was a bankruptcy, the job was a test), some cost time (the target keeps a schedule, the clue was built to be misread once), some cost batteries (the client went broke, the loot was the bait), some cost standing (the client lied, a rival crew was hired for the same job), and the heaviest cost a person (the cargo is a person, the rescue delivers the victim to his torturer, the obvious good dooms somebody). The library is loud on three points. First, MOST CONTRACTS SHOULD HAVE NO TWIST AT ALL: the contract formula that did this best went stale because its one twist became the expected shape (`Q148.X3`), and the fix is to ration the twist to a strict minority on an unpredictable schedule (`Q148.P4`). Second, a twist is FAIR only when the world testified before the fork (`Q149.X2`, `Q130.W2`, `Q032.P4`) and the options do what they say (`Q107.X1`); it is a CHEAT when the cost is told after you commit and could not have been read (`Q121.W7`, `Q139.W7` is the fair version: the information was there). Third, the fee is set before the truth is knowable (`Q148.W1`), which is what lets a twist land without turning into a haggle, and it is what lets DECLINING stay free: no twist may ever reach back and price the offer. For our two contract rules the page proposes one clean mechanism the library supports: a twist can change WHAT finishing means but never WHETHER you can finish (`Q148.W9`, `Q114.W3`), and a twist that proves the client LIED voids the contract, so walking away from a liar is not a dropped contract and writes no deed (`Q003.W1`, `Q148.P2`).

## HOW TO READ THE BANK

Each twist has five fields.
- NAME: the words a builder writes in the contract file (`twist: THE REWARD IS GONE`).
- IDS: the findings that show it working, or failing.
- COSTS: the HEAVIEST thing it can take. NOTHING (meaning only), TIME (days on the map, the clock of blind spot 3), BATTERIES (the money), STANDING (with a person or a faction), A PERSON (someone dies, is lost, or is handed over).
- LANDS: AT THE OFFER (it is information, not a twist, and it must never trap a yes), MID-JOB, AT HAND-IN, ACTS LATER (planted in one act, landing in another; the three-acts-at-once flip).
- FITS: BOOM or BUST (or EITHER), and which ACT (1 Animal, 2 Human, 3 Angel).

The bank is sorted by cost, lightest first. Inside a cost tier it is sorted by how late it lands.

## THE TWIST BANK

### TIER 0: COSTS NOTHING (the ledger does not move; the meaning does)

**T01. THE HAUNTING IS THE ECONOMY.** The client hires you for something uncanny (a cursed shop, a dead block, a thing that eats tenants) and the cause is plain money: the location cannot hold a business. The "entity" is a tired woman making dice. `Q097.W1` (the curse is a real repeating bankruptcy pattern), `Q097.W2` (the supernatural investigation resolves into real estate), `Q097.W7` (the malignant entity asks if you are here for your dice). Costs NOTHING. Lands MID-JOB. Fits BUST, act 1 and act 2. This is our most on-theme light twist: the most realistic economic crash simulator explains its ghosts with ledgers. Warning attached: the whole payoff of the source rides on one optional skill check (`Q097.X1`), so ours must be said out loud by a mouth, never gated.

**T02. THE WAR IS A BROKEN PART.** A standoff that looks like it needs a fight is really a maintenance problem: one failed pump, one missing fuse, and nobody in charge wants to own it. `Q100.W8` (the whole crisis is a broken part, and the official wants nothing to do with it), `Q094.W1` (the biggest war in its game started with an unpaid autopay bill). Costs NOTHING (or a little TIME to fetch the part). Lands MID-JOB. Fits EITHER, best in act 2 when the world is clawing back and things can be fixed. On-theme with power and water held by SHARE: many fights are one fuse away from not happening.

**T03. THE PRETEXT DROPS.** The stated job was never the real one; the client wanted something smaller and more human (to know what became of someone, to be seen, to have company on the road). `Q095.W4` (the quest is nominally about a missing mage; the pretext drops on the mountain), `Q040.W3` (a standard revenge setup is demolished in seconds to launch something stranger). Costs NOTHING. Lands MID-JOB. Fits EITHER, any act. Good first twist a new player meets: it teaches that people have reasons without punishing him.

**T04. THE JOB WAS A TEST, AND THE PASS WAS NO.** The client asked you to do something ugly and the real contract was whether you would refuse. `Q104.W1` (the door was never locked: if the residents had refused, a message would have commended them), `Q104.W10` (the reward for finally refusing is a congratulations tape), `Q075.W2` (the induction test is the job; it does not matter who you pick, the test is whether you kill on command), `Q010.W3` (moral tests with no mechanical stake). Costs NOTHING. Lands AT HAND-IN. Fits EITHER, act 2 (institutions testing recruits). This is the twist that most honours DECLINING IS FREE, because it rewards a no. The flaw to avoid: the test where nothing you do matters reads as a fake puzzle (`Q075.X2`).

**T05. THE ERRAND WAS ONBOARDING.** Every small job this person gave you was training for something bigger they needed you for. `Q152.W2` (the recruitment is onboarding: every errand was a rehearsal), `Q152.W10` (the whole script replays itself), `Q023.W5` (a character met on an unrelated early errand turns out central). Costs NOTHING at the time. Lands ACTS LATER. Fits ACROSS. Belongs to a recurring client, never a one-off.

**T06. THE WITNESS YOU DID NOT KNOW YOU HAD.** Someone watched the job and you learn it only later: a kid in a stairwell, a dog, a camera the Amalgamation owns. `Q078.W4` (your father is the dog: he watched every atrocity), `Q001.W9` (the space testifies: the room verifies what happened after). Costs NOTHING when it lands (the cost, if any, comes from the deed it reveals). Lands ACTS LATER. Fits ACROSS, and is native to the Amalgamation's blind spot: what was said face to face is unrecorded, what was done in front of a lens is not.

### TIER 1: COSTS TIME (days on the map, a missed window)

**T07. THE TARGET KEEPS A SCHEDULE.** The person or thing you were sent to reach is not waiting for you; they live a timetable, and if you arrive at the wrong hour they are gone for a day, or gone for good. `Q011.W2` (the quest can be failed by time: miss a window and she flees), `Q128.W1` (the clock is the dungeon; walls are other people's schedules), `Q146.W8` (time is the only fee; the world's other lives visibly spend theirs meanwhile). Costs TIME. Lands MID-JOB. Fits EITHER, any act; it is the natural twist for our map, where travel spends days. Two laws ride with it: a missed window must leave a diegetic tell (`Q128.X2`), and a hard fail must never be unsignposted (`Q095.X1`).

**T08. THE CLUE IS BUILT TO BE MISREAD ONCE.** The first reading of the trail is wrong on purpose (hoofprints that are two legs), and looking again pays. `Q148.W3` (the evidence is built to be misread once), `Q148.X2` (the formula's investigations cannot fail: a corridor dressed as deduction), `Q148.P3` (investigations that can conclude wrong, and pay anyway, and the world records it). Costs TIME (the wrong lead is a day on the road). Lands MID-JOB. Fits EITHER, act 1 and 2.

**T09. DO NOT STOP THE THIEF.** Helping too early breaks the job: the thing you would naturally interrupt is a link the outcome needs. `Q011.W7` (you must NOT stop the robbery early, or a later beat breaks). Costs TIME (you wait, you watch). Lands MID-JOB. Fits EITHER, act 2. Dangerous: the same shape becomes the hidden-dependency flaw when it is not signposted (`Q023.X2`).

**T10. THE CLOCK ARRIVES.** Midway, a deadline appears that was not in the offer: a bounty hunter is coming, everyone on the list gets sick on the same day, the brownout schedule moves. `Q109.W4` (the antagonist arrives as a clock), `Q109.W5` (the threat eats your calendar, not your health), `Q113.W5` (they all get sick on the same day). Costs TIME, and can escalate to a person if ignored. Lands MID-JOB. Fits BUST, act 1 (the anarchy decade has no schedules but the grid's).

### TIER 2: COSTS BATTERIES (the money)

**T11. THE REWARD IS GONE.** You finish the job and the client cannot pay: his credit died, his cells were stolen, the debt ate the fee. `Q094.W1` (a missed bill payment started a war), `Q109.W3` (the death threat is a bar tab: the price of living is a recurring payment), `Q148.W1` (the fee locks before the truth, so no later choice can be about the coin). Costs BATTERIES. Lands AT HAND-IN. Fits BUST, act 1. The most Battle Brothers twist in the bank and the most realistic one in a crash: people who hire mercenaries in a collapse are people who are running out of money. The finding that saves it from being a cheat: the job still FINISHES (you delivered, the ledger records it), and the client pays in something else, a name, a door, a place to sleep (`Q089.W3`: the only reward is a like, and people build anyway).

**T12. THE CLIENT BECOMES THE CARGO.** The person who hired you is dead, or is the thing the job ends in. There is nobody left to pay. `Q133.W4` (the man who hired the investigation is the feast it ends in), `Q103.W10` (it was all for nothing and the traitor kills your boss). Costs BATTERIES (the fee), and the grief is free. Lands AT HAND-IN. Fits BUST, act 1.

**T13. THE LOOT IS THE BAIT.** A trail of valuables leads upstairs; it was laid for people like you. `Q110.W3` (a suspicious trail of food, medicine and expensive items leading up to the second floor), `Q134.W1` (the trap runs on genre training: a door slightly open is bait enough). Costs BATTERIES (medicine and ammunition spent getting out), rarely a person. Lands MID-JOB. Fits BUST, act 1. Fair only if the trail looks too good in a way the eye can read; our analog horror rule gives it free: ONE wrong detail (the medicine is in date).

**T14. THE BRIBE IS REAL.** Someone on the other side offers more than the client to walk away or switch sides, and the offer is genuine. `Q150.W2` (the bribe is real and only the accusation is false; lies wrapped around true payment are the only lies worth designing), `Q096.W3` (the deal pays in the exact currency you need), `Q148.W8` (the kill is honestly priced in power). Costs BATTERIES (the money you turn down) or STANDING (if you take it). Lands MID-JOB. Fits BOOM, act 2 and 3: bribes need a surplus.

**T15. MERCY COSTS YOUR OWN COIN.** The kind way through is available, but you pay for it: you cover the debt, you hand over your medicine. `Q149.W6` (the clean exit costs the player's own coin), `Q148.W6` (the monster gives the trophy: mercy made practical by its beneficiary), `Q148.W7` (no reform is on sale). Costs BATTERIES. Lands MID-JOB. Fits BUST, any act. The library's rule on this one is that the cost must be real and visible, or mercy is not a choice (`Q148.X1`).

**T16. THE PRIZE IS SMALLER THAN THE PROMISE.** You win and the prize is less than it sounded: six months, one door, a job instead of a payout, or the wish granted exactly as worded. `Q102.W4` (the prize for winning is six months), `Q152.W9` (the reward is the job), `Q127.W2` (the immortality is the punishment: the cost delivered inside the benefit). Costs BATTERIES (the difference). Lands AT HAND-IN. Fits BOOM, act 3 (the age of promises).

### TIER 3: COSTS STANDING (with a person, a family, a faction)

**T17. THE CLIENT LIED.** The job is dirty before you leave: he sent others to die, hid it, and needs you to finish what they could not. `Q003.W1` (the dirty errand: the moral weight is seeded in the hire), `Q034.W8` (the lie is motivated: the deception is character-driven), `Q150.W1` (the liar is the highest authority in the room), `Q148.P2` (client-facing fictions write two entries: told and happened). Costs STANDING (with the client, if you call it; with his enemies, if you do not). Lands MID-JOB. Fits EITHER, any act. THIS IS THE ONE THAT OPENS THE DOOR: a proven lie voids the contract (see the rules below).

**T18. A THIRD PARTY SHOWS UP.** Somebody else wants the same thing, or was hired for the same job. `Q016.W7` (the rival claimant: a third party raises the stakes and offers an alternative), `Q113.W7` (the rival doctors have their own lists), `Q109.W6` (the villain gets laid off: the contract was handed to a worse mercenary). Costs STANDING (whoever you side with, the other side remembers). Lands MID-JOB. Fits BOOM, act 2: rival crews exist where there is money to compete for.

**T19. THE STATED CULPRIT IS A RED HERRING.** The person you were pointed at is not the problem; the premise itself serves somebody hidden. `Q020.W5` (the stated task is a deliberate misdirection that serves a hidden antagonist), `Q009.W2` (a routine break-in escalates to a mind-editing conspiracy). Costs STANDING (you have to tell the client his enemy is the wrong one). Lands MID-JOB. Fits EITHER, act 2 and 3. At act 3 scale it brushes the Amalgamation, and there the threat must scale with proximity to the secret: a contract that gets too close stops being a contract.

**T20. THE CLIENT'S FEAR IS THE JOB.** The client wants proof of a threat, and your report writes his reality: confirm his fear and he pays, and acts on it, with names. `Q130.W1` (the client's reality is player-writable), `Q130.W4` (the lie is frictionless at point of sale; the invoice arrives days later with names on it), `Q130.W3` (truth costs the truth-teller), `Q135.W3` (the paranoia was real, only mis-aimed). Costs STANDING (the truth costs you his favour) or A PERSON (the lie costs a neighbour). Lands AT HAND-IN, pays out ACTS LATER. Fits BOOM, act 3. The library's own flaw attached: the targets must not be certified innocent, or the lie is pure villainy instead of judgment (`Q130.X1`).

**T21. THE RESCUED DO NOT WANT RESCUE.** You reach the person you were sent to bring home and they refuse: they are staying, or they want to walk the other way. `Q128.W3` (the midpoint is a woman declining rescue), `Q107.W1` (the antagonist is the people you saved), `Q107.W3` (they are right about the risk and wrong about the answer), `Q138.W1` (the lock is a child's protective lie). Costs STANDING (with the client, if you respect the no; with the person, if you drag them). Lands MID-JOB. Fits EITHER, any act. This twist is our DECLINING IS FREE rule turned around to face an NPC: people in our world may also say no.

**T22. THE BETRAYAL THAT ARMS YOU.** Your contact or partner sells you out, but what they gave you before the turn stays yours. `Q015.W5` (the betrayal that arms you), `Q075.W7` (the betrayer's motive is love of the family). Costs STANDING (the relationship). Lands MID-JOB. Fits BUST, act 1 and 2.

### TIER 4: COSTS A PERSON (someone dies, is lost, or is handed over)

**T23. THE THING YOU WERE SENT FOR IS A PERSON.** The cargo, the "asset", the stolen goods turn out to be somebody. `Q016.W1` (the MacGuffin is a person: you came for loot, you found a soul), `Q148.W6` (the monster gives the trophy), `Q099.W7` (the fetch item is the murder motive). Costs A PERSON (or the fee, if you let them go). Lands MID-JOB. Fits BUST, act 1 and 2. The library's warning is the loudest here: this is the twist the contract formula overused until players expected it (`Q148.X3`); ration it hardest (`Q148.P4`).

**T24. THE ESCAPE ROUTE IS THE BETRAYAL.** The smart shortcut (the window, the back tunnel) costs somebody who was counting on you going the long way. `Q116.W1` (escape out the window and your brother dies; stay through the front door and he lives). Costs A PERSON. Lands MID-JOB. Fits EITHER, act 2. Fair once; the second time the player stops trusting the level (`Q116.X3`).

**T25. THE OBVIOUS GOOD IS THE TRAP.** The kind, intuitive choice inside the job is the one that dooms someone, and the weight is hidden at decision time. `Q004.W2` (save the children triggers the worst family outcome), `Q001.W4` (the isolated load-bearing choice), `Q149.W7` (false mercy is the worst branch). Costs A PERSON. Lands ACTS LATER. Fits ACROSS. The flaw that follows it everywhere: without warning in the scene, players feel tricked rather than taught (`Q149.X2`).

**T26. THE RESCUE FRAME DELIVERS THE VICTIM.** You find, free and escort a person, playing the job completely straight, and deliver them to the one who hurts them. `Q134.W5` (the rescue frame delivers the victim), `Q133.W5` (the lie costs one sentence), `Q133.P4` (lures need ledgers: the betrayal's price scales with the victim's trust). Costs A PERSON. Lands AT HAND-IN, or ACTS LATER when you learn it. Fits BUST, act 1 into act 2.

**T27. THE WISH WORKED EXACTLY.** The client got precisely what he paid for, and the getting is the ruin. `Q127.W1` (the devil is a literalist, not a liar), `Q127.P3` (the wish that worked: the request, honoured), `Q085.W6` (the dream is the bait). Costs A PERSON (the client himself, changed). Lands AT HAND-IN, fully ACTS LATER. Fits BOOM, act 3 (the healing age sells what people ask for, including the dead back as data).

**T28. THE TRAITOR IS A VICTIM.** The person who turns on you mid-job was forced: a debt, a hostage, an edit. `Q043.W8` (a beloved companion forced to betray you, then lost), `Q094.W2` (ten months of real friendship to earn one word). Costs A PERSON. Lands MID-JOB. Fits EITHER, act 2 and 3. Contract use only with a recurring face; with a stranger it has no weight.

**T29. SUCCESS AT A COST.** The job succeeds and something breaks on the way: a companion hurt, a building lost, a battery bank cracked. `Q065.W7` (success at a cost: dice as a story engine). Costs anywhere from BATTERIES to A PERSON, set by the fight, not the writer. Lands MID-JOB. Fits EITHER. This is the one twist that belongs to the COMBAT lane and the dial, not to WORDS; it is also the only one that may appear on more than a minority of contracts, because the fight writes it, not the formula.

### MAIN LINE ONLY (NOT FOR CONTRACTS)

**T30. THE QUEST-GIVER IS THE THREAT.** The employer and the enemy are one being, revealed in sequence. `Q152.W1` (the quest-giver is the apocalypse on a timer), `Q036.W2` (the guide who turns), `Q033.W5` (the false guide). This twist is a one-shot for a whole game (`Q032.W9`, `Q057.X3`), belongs to the Amalgamation and the main quest, and must never be spent on a side contract. Listed here so nobody draws it.

## THE FINDINGS

### 1. Most contracts must have no twist

The single most important finding for a Battle Brothers-style board comes from the library's own contract study. The formula that made contract quests famous ran its one twist (the monster is somebody) so often that "late-game contracts telegraph their sympathy beats, and the class's power dilutes with volume" (`Q148.X3`). The port is explicit: generated contracts default to EXACTLY WHAT THEY SAY, and the twist attaches to a strict minority on an unpredictable schedule (`Q148.P4`). The substory model says the same thing from the other side: a hundred side stories work because most are light and a few land (`Q013.W7`, the blindside works because the setup's lightness makes the drop land). And the identity twist study warns that one great twist beats three cheap ones (`Q032.P9`). For us: a contract carries AT MOST one twist, and most carry none. The count is ours to set; this page proposes about one contract in four, drawn by the sim, never by a fixed rotation, so that the player cannot count to the twist.

### 2. The fee is set before the truth

The cleanest design decision in any job system the library studied: the client and the player agree the fee at maximum ignorance, before anything about the job's truth is knowable (`Q148.W1`). This "separates money from morality forever after": no choice after the twist can be about the coin, because the coin was settled by people who did not know yet. It is also why the twist in a contract is allowed at all. The board is a dignified front door that never begs (`Q148.W2`), and the whole arc fits one sitting (`Q148.W10`), which is why the fork lands before anything dilutes it. For our contracts: the fee is fixed at the offer, in batteries; a twist can make the fee disappear (T11, T12) or make a bigger fee appear from somebody else (T14), but it can never renegotiate the fee the player said yes to.

### 3. The world testifies before the fork, or the twist is a cheat

The library states the fairness law several ways and they agree. The world must testify BEFORE the fork about what the untreated condition does (`Q149.X2`, the spare trap that punishes without telegraphing: players feel tricked rather than taught). The gameplay should teach the truth before the fork asks (`Q130.W2`: you watch the innocence for three days before any node lets you deny it). Seed the twist relentlessly so it is inevitable in hindsight; a twist without clues is a betrayal (`Q032.P4`, `Q032.P9`). And the fair version of "the cost is revealed after you commit" is one where the information WAS there, readable in hindsight (`Q139.W7`); the unfair version is the one where the helper mentions the radiation after you have chosen, and the player's line is "that would have been nice to know earlier" (`Q121.W7`). The same beat is profound or cheap depending entirely on information design (`Q137.X4`).

### 4. The options must do what they say

A twist cannot work by lying about what an action IS. "Not sure why telling people to go home has to result in physical violence" (`Q107.X1`): if the moral system has to misdescribe a verb to make it costly, the dilemma is fake. The escape route twist is fair once and then poisons trust in the whole level (`Q116.X3`). A gut-punch must carry an authorial fingerprint (one line, one note, one witness) or it reads as a bug (`Q129.X1`). And threatened consequences must be honoured: the permadeath bluff that never happens left players feeling lied to (`Q058.X3`).

### 5. Lies must be discoverable, kindness must show

The contract formula's white lie to the client was flawless: no risk, no later cost, no discovery, and so the mercy it bought was barely a choice (`Q148.X1`). The port: a fiction told to a client writes two entries, TOLD and HAPPENED, and some later system may surface the gap (`Q148.P2`). The inverse law: when a betrayal is unconditional, the player who did every favour gets the same outcome as one who did none, and some players read that as the writer sneering; give kindness ONE visible effect (`Q109.X5`). Where the source deliberately paid truth and lie the same (`Q097.W10`, `Q004.W4`, zero reward difference), it did so to make the choice purely ethical; that is a tool for a rare contract, not the default.

### 6. A twist should change what you do, not only how you feel

The agency twist study's honest limit: a reveal that does not change the gameplay sags (`Q033.X2`, `Q033.W10`). For a contract this is small and concrete: after the twist, the player has a new verb or a new destination on the map (take the person somewhere else, fetch the part, go to the rival's camp), not just a new line of dialogue. The library's strongest contract twists all hand the player a physical fork: spare or kill (`Q148.W6`), stay or leave by the window (`Q116.W1`), the truth or the confirmation (`Q130.W4`).

### 7. Refusing costs nothing, and that is why it works

The library contains the clearest statement of DECLINING IS FREE anywhere in games: "No ambush, no curse, no closed door... the offer never expires. A punished no is a trap you escaped. An unpunished no is a temptation that lives in the save file" (`Q126.W3`). The patient god works the same way: no timer, no escalation, no punishment for refusal (`Q134.W3`). The failures are just as clear: a quest that punishes abstinence loses the scene for the player who said no (`Q106.X6`); an ending that punishes refusal with deletion makes refusal feel wrong (`Q084.X5`); a quest where "regardless of any reluctance on your part, there is no way around it" (`Q103.X1`) removes the no entirely; and the one quest whose only real refusal is abandoning it in the journal leaks the choice out of the fiction (`Q134.X10`). The worst cheat of all for us: the first payment precedes the terms, so you are in debt before the negotiation opens (`Q134.W2`). That is a brilliant horror beat and it is illegal in a Bohemia contract, because it prices the offer.

### 8. Once taken, you finish: the twist changes what finishing means

Our rule says a contract taken must be finished, and a contract dropped is a deed. The library shows how a twist lives inside that without trapping anybody. The client's closure is outcome-invariant: trophy in, coin out, both worlds (`Q148.W9`). Side stories cannot be missed in terms of completion; failure costs the good item, not the story (`Q114.W3`). Kill the quest-giver and the game gives you his ghost to question, refusing a fail state (`Q086.W6`). Losing a talk reroutes you to the vents (`Q017.W8`); failure is content (`Q005.W3`). So every twist in the bank must leave a way to FINISH, where finishing means going back to the person in the place and closing it with them, even if what you bring back is bad news, the wrong culprit (`Q148.P3`), or nobody.

### 9. The lie voids the contract

When a twist proves the client LIED about the job (T17, and T20 when the client knowingly asked for a false confirmation), the library points one way: the moral weight of a dirty errand sits in the hire, not in the player (`Q003.W1`), and the game in that same study moralizes the lie, not the act (`Q003.W9`). A contract is an agreement made on stated terms (`Q148.W1`); a client who lied about the terms broke it first. So: walking away from a proven liar is not a dropped contract and writes no deed. The player can still finish it if he wants the fee; either way, the TOLD and HAPPENED entries (`Q148.P2`) are written against the client, not the player. This is our design choice, grounded in those findings; the library has no study of a formal void rule, which is listed in OPEN.

### 10. A twist lands at one of four times, and each time has a job

- AT THE OFFER is not a twist. Anything true at the offer must be sayable at the offer, or it traps the yes. The fee locks here (`Q148.W1`). The only "twist" allowed at the offer is the dirty errand being visible to a careful reader (`Q003.W1`), which is information.
- MID-JOB is the default and the Battle Brothers shape: something on the road or on the fight board changes the job, and the player gets a new fork on the map. Most of the bank lives here (T01 to T03, T07 to T10, T13 to T15, T17 to T19, T21 to T24).
- AT HAND-IN is the conversation twist: the reward is gone, the test is revealed, the fear is fed. It must still let the player close the contract (`Q148.W9`).
- ACTS LATER is the three-acts-at-once twist: the job planted in act 1 lands in act 2 or 3. The library's delayed, displaced consequence (`Q004.W5`) and the delayed organic trigger (`Q009.W10`) are the model; the silent-failure law applies (`Q128.X2`: one diegetic tell per broken link). Because the future is derived from the past acts' ledgers, an acts-later twist is not scripted, it is READ from the ledger; the only authoring is the tell.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)

1. ONE TWIST AT MOST. A contract file names zero or one entry from this bank (`twist:` field, the T-number). Zero is the default. Test: no contract file names two.
2. A MINORITY, DRAWN BY THE SIM. Across any window of contracts offered, the twisted share stays a minority (proposed: about one in four) and its order is not a fixed rotation (`Q148.P4`, `Q148.X3`). Test: count the board's offers over a play window.
3. THE HEAVY TWISTS ARE RARER. A-PERSON twists (T23 to T28) are rarer than the rest together, and T23 (THE THING IS A PERSON) is the rarest, because it is the one that wore out the formula (`Q148.X3`).
4. THE FEE LOCKS AT THE OFFER. The fee in batteries is fixed when the player says yes; no twist edits it. A twist may remove the payer (T11, T12) or add a second offer (T14) (`Q148.W1`).
5. NOTHING AT THE OFFER TRAPS THE YES. No debt, no body, no obligation exists until the player accepts (`Q134.W2` is banned). Test: an offer declined leaves every ledger byte-identical.
6. THE WORLD TESTIFIES FIRST. Every twist has at least one readable sign BEFORE its fork, on the settlement screen, the fight board or in a mouth: an object, a line, a wrong detail (`Q149.X2`, `Q130.W2`, `Q032.P4`). The contract file lists the sign. Test: a twist with no `sign:` line is refused.
7. THE VERBS TELL THE TRUTH. Every choice's label describes what it does; the twist can change the situation, never the meaning of a verb (`Q107.X1`, `Q121.W7`).
8. ONE FINGERPRINT. Every twist carries one line from a mouth that says, plainly, that it happened (`Q129.X1`), in the analog horror register: said plainly, never explained.
9. A TWIST CHANGES THE MAP. After the twist the player has a new destination, a new person to talk to, or a new verb, not only new text (`Q033.X2`).
10. FINISHING IS ALWAYS POSSIBLE. After any twist there is a way to close the contract with the person in the place, even with bad news or nobody (`Q148.W9`, `Q114.W3`, `Q086.W6`). Test: every twist branch reaches a hand-in node.
11. THE LIE VOIDS. A twist that proves the client lied about the terms releases the player: leaving writes no dropped-contract deed; the lie is recorded against the client (`Q003.W1`, `Q148.P2`).
12. TOLD AND HAPPENED. Any fiction the player tells the client is written twice and is discoverable later with a small real chance (`Q148.X1`, `Q148.P2`).
13. KINDNESS SHOWS ONCE. If the twist makes the outcome the same no matter what the player did, one visible thing still differs for the kind player (`Q109.X5`).
14. NO TAUGHT TOOL TURNED TWICE. A twist that punishes a tool the game taught (the shortcut, the window) is used once per game, then retired (`Q116.X3`).
15. ACTS-LATER TWISTS ARE READ, NOT WRITTEN. A twist landing in another act reads the ledger of the act it was planted in; it needs a `tell:` line for the act it lands in (`Q004.W5`, `Q128.X2`).
16. NEVER THE MAIN LINE'S TWIST. T30 is reserved for the Amalgamation and the main quest (`Q152.W1`, `Q032.W9`).
17. NOTHING IN THE FIRST MINUTE. No twist can land before the player's first hour of play is under way; the first contracts offered are plain jobs, so the player learns the board before the board surprises him (rule 32a).

18. AN INVESTIGATION CAN BE ANSWERED WRONG. (folded 10/9 from QR-U) A reconstruction the player cannot conclude wrong is a corridor
    (`Q153.X1`); two suspects and a real failure state (`Q160.W2`); the fold records the believed version and the true
    one (`Q153.P2`). Test: an investigation contract can be handed in with the wrong name; it closes as DONE WRONG (QR-A
    C12), pays what the screen said, and the truth is written beside the telling (QR-A C16, `Q148.P2`).

ADDED TO THE BANK (folded 10/9 from QR-U) (each rationed by rules 2 and 3):
- THE HELPER IS THE CULPRIT: the killer hides in civic concern (`Q160.W4`); the obvious suspect is innocent
  (`Q160.W3`). A contract to find who drains the pumps, where the man who guided you is the one.

  grievance in animal form (`Q155.W2`). A cousin of T23, sharing its ration.

SHARPENED (folded 10/9 from QR-U):
- Rule 2 (a minority): the twist as template is the flaw, a second study (`Q236.X2`, with `Q148.X3`).

## WHAT TO AVOID (the flaws)

- THE OVERUSED TWIST. `Q148.X3`: the formula's one twist became the expected shape. Ration it.
- THE UNTELEGRAPHED TRAP. `Q149.X2`: the good-looking option detonates with no warning; players feel tricked, not taught.
- THE COST TOLD TOO LATE. `Q121.W7`: the helper mentions the danger after you commit.
- THE SUBVERSION THAT LOOKS LIKE A BUG. `Q129.X1`: seventeen years of "is this intended?".
- THE VERB THAT LIES. `Q107.X1`: "disperse the crowd" becomes a beating.
- THE TAUGHT TOOL TURNED TRAITOR TWICE. `Q116.X3`.
- THE BLUFF. `Q058.X3`: threatened consequences never honoured.
- THE LAUNDERED LIE. `Q148.X1`: a white lie with no possible discovery makes mercy free.
- THE UNCONDITIONAL BETRAYAL. `Q109.X5`: kindness changes nothing, and it reads as a sneer.
- THE SILENT FAILURE. `Q128.X2`: the chain breaks and the player never learns it did.
- THE HIDDEN ORDER. `Q023.X2`: doing one job silently breaks another. And its big brother, side content that silently fails main content, `Q104.X6`.
- THE THESIS BEHIND A CHECK. `Q097.X1`, `Q112.X1`: the twist's payoff hidden behind a roll most players fail.
- THE INVISIBLE BEST OUTCOME. `Q070.X1`: the good ending gated behind unsignposted curiosity.
- THE PUNISHED NO. `Q106.X6`, `Q084.X5`, `Q103.X1`, `Q134.X10`: refusal costs the scene, deletes the friend, or does not exist.
- THE DEBT BEFORE THE TERMS. `Q134.W2`: a great horror beat and illegal in a contract.
- THE TWIST THAT CHANGES NOTHING TO PLAY. `Q033.X2`.
- THE SPOILER-FRAGILE DESIGN. `Q057.X3`: value built on a one-time surprise.
- THE CERTIFIED INNOCENT. `Q130.X1`: if the targets are provably innocent, the lie branch is villainy, not judgment.

## THE [bb contracts] LINE

Battle Brothers is the reference for the board, the contract, and the road. Its contracts are mostly exactly what they say, and a complication is occasional, which is the same rationing the library arrived at independently (`Q148.P4`). Where we go further than Battle Brothers, by the library's evidence: the twist comes from a mouth with a portrait, not from a pop-up; the fee is set before the truth; and refusing is free where Battle Brothers docks relations for a refused contract (blind spot 8; `Q126.W3` is the evidence for Paolo's side).

## OPEN (what the library could not answer)

1. A FORMAL VOID RULE. No studied quest has a written rule that a client's lie releases the player from a contract. Rule 11 is ours, built from `Q003.W1`, `Q003.W9` and `Q148.W1`. PEOPLE should test it against the standing weights.
2. THE TARGET MOVES, TRULY. The library's "target moved" cases are all schedules (`Q011.W2`, `Q128.W1`). No study shows a target that relocates across a map because it heard you coming. Our map with roaming parties and tracks can do this natively; it has no library proof yet.
3. THE RIGHT RATIO. `Q148.P4` says "strict minority"; nothing in the library gives a number. One in four is a proposal for RUN to tune on a real board.
4. PAYMENT IN KIND. T11's fix (the broke client pays in a name, a door, a bed) rests on `Q089.W3` and on the Kenshi and Pathologic evidence that unpaid work can still matter (`Q113.X4` is the warning that no reward at all loses players). The exchange rate between batteries and favours is ECONOMY's.
5. TWISTS ON THE FIGHT BOARD. The library's twists are almost all told in dialogue or found in rooms. What a twist looks like as a change on the fight board (a group that switches sides on the beat, a cover tile that is a person) is for COMBAT, with T29 as the only bridge.
6. HOW A TWIST READS ON A PHONE. None of the 152 are phone games. Whether the "sign" of rule 6 is readable on a settlement screen and on a house-sized fight tile at phone size is DIRECTION's and UI's question (swept 9/29: the one-cell character at close scale died with the walk, rule 38).
