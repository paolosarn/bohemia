# QR-P: THE FRONT DOOR MOVED. WHAT OF ROUND ONE SURVIVES THE SETTLEMENT SCREEN?

QUESTION: row [settlement doors]. Round one (QR-A to QR-H, and the designs QD-A to QD-H) was written when a contract
was a person standing on the close street who could be walked past and tapped. On 9/28 Paolo ruled that contracts are
the Battle Brothers settlement contract screen ("quest aren't gonna be like traditional quest like it's gonna be
based off like this settlement menu contract pop-up screen", records/BOHEMIA_PAOLO_QUESTS_ARE_THE_SETTLEMENT_CONTRACT_SCREEN_9_28_26.md),
and the third votes made every PLACE a settlement screen (laws/BOHEMIA_ADDENDUM_THE_THIRD_VOTES_9_28_26.md s2: tap a
building; on foot only in the fight and special places). Which round-one findings survive the move, which are
superseded, and what do the first sixty seconds look like when places are settlement screens?

STATUS: research only (rule 35, laws/BOHEMIA_ADDENDUM_QUESTS_IS_RESEARCH_ONLY_9_27_26.md). Nothing here is built.
Companion edit this round: all 22 round-one contract designs now carry a "## THE SETTLEMENT AND THE OFFER SCREEN"

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Almost everything round one said about WHAT a contract is survives; almost everything it said about WHERE the offer
opens is superseded. The library's strongest contract findings were never about the street: the fee locked before
the truth (`Q148.W1`), clean terms with "No." on the same node as "Done." (`Q126.N5`, `Q126.N3`), a refusal that costs
nothing and forecloses nothing (`Q126.W3`, `Q126.N13`, `Q126.N12`), the world that runs without you (`Q055.W1`,
`Q061.W2`), the exit that never closes once you take the job (`Q151.W7`, `Q140.W8`, `Q040.W2`) and the drop priced as
a deed (`Q087.W9`, `Q086.W8`). All of that now lives inside the offer screen and on the map. What dies is the
walk-up: QR-A rule C1's "never a board, list, card or pop-up", QR-E's whole "place that asks on the close street"
apparatus, and QR-F's walked first minute on the close street (the walk itself died 9/28, rule 38). The library itself already held the other door:
`Q148.W2`, THE BOARD IS A DIGNIFIED FRONT DOOR ("a job system that never begs"), and `Q148.P1`, a settlement board
port. Round one read those as the thing to avoid; Paolo's ruling makes them the thing to build, and they are the
better fit for a phone. The one real loss is DISCOVERY BY WALKING (`Q013.W6`, `Q114.W2`, `Q114.N1`): the city no
longer snags him on the pavement. The compensation is that the settlement screen is itself a place drawn with one
wrong thing in it, so the oddity that used to be on a street corner moves into the settlement's picture, where the
building he taps is the building that looks wrong. The first sixty seconds become: a map with nothing moving toward
him, one settlement he can tap into, a settlement screen that draws a place and speaks nothing, one silent take-or-
leave (SCAVENGE is the natural home for it), and no offer screen before he has chosen to open a building.

## THE FINDINGS

### 1. What survives whole (the contract's substance)

1.1 THE FEE LOCKS BEFORE THE TRUTH. `Q148.W1` (the fee is haggled at maximum ignorance, which separates money from
morality forever after) is stronger on an offer screen than it was in a street conversation, because the screen
shows the number as a number. QR-A C5, QR-E rule 9. Every re-cut design keeps its fee and states it on the screen
("PAY, SHOWN BEFORE ACCEPT"). `Q148.P1`'s job loop (board, haggle, job, payment) is almost Battle Brothers' own screen
line for line.

1.2 CLEAN TERMS. `Q126.N5` (he states them clean, no subclause, the contract says what he said) and `Q126.N6` (the
node exists to remove your excuse) are the design brief for the 2 to 4 lines on the offer screen. QR-A C3's five
fields (what, where, how many, pay, window) map directly: the lines carry what and where and the danger; the pay row
carries the pay; a window is a line of its own. `Q060.W1` (perfect information, every death your own fault) is the
reason the danger belongs on the screen and not after the yes: QD-A02's screen shows "ocho hombres" beside "doce".

1.3 THE FREE NO. `Q126.W3` (refusal costs nothing and that is why it works), `Q126.N13` (a refusal that costs nothing
and forecloses nothing is rarer than any branch), `Q126.N12` (he is unbothered, the offer stands), `Q131.N1` and
`Q131.W4` (refusal is free and the entry is immortal). Battle Brothers' own screen already works like this: the
decline button closes the screen and the job sits in the hall. QR-A C6, C7, C18 and QR-E rules 11 and 12 all survive
as written. The only change is the verb: "walked past" becomes "did not tap the building" or "closed the screen".
Both write nothing. The flaws still bite: `Q043.X1` and `Q043.X2` (guilt and nagging), `Q106.X6` (the quest punishes
abstinence), `Q084.X5` (refusing is punished with deletion). A decline button that dims the client, marks the
building or posts to the feed would reintroduce all four.

1.4 THE WORLD RUNS WITHOUT HIM. `Q055.W1`, `Q061.W2`, `Q051.W2`, `Q051.W4` and `Q085.W3` (an NPC could finish the
job). QR-A C8 survives: the declined job still happens to the world, written without his id. On the settlement screen
this has a better stage than the street had: the settlement's picture changes on the next visit (the shelf filled by
somebody else, the cistern lid off), which is `Q149.W9` (the verdict arrives as world-state) and `Q147.W7` (the
failure state is visible geography).

1.5 ONCE TAKEN, FINISH IT, AND THE DROP IS A DEED. `Q040.W2` (complicity through sustained consent), `Q151.W7` (the
flee option never closes), `Q140.W8` (the off-ramp before the fight is real), `Q133.W3` (the exit is first), priced
by `Q087.W9` (the world executes on the clock), `Q094.W4` (one betrayal recalibrates a society) and `Q086.W8`
(reputation is a memory: strangers cite your deeds). QR-A C10 and C13 survive untouched. Battle Brothers' own model
agrees: an accepted contract is on the map until done or failed, and there is no abandon button. The advance, which
BB offers on the screen, is exactly QR-A C13(c): an advance turns a drop into a named debt. The re-cut designs use it
deliberately (QD-A02, QD-A04, QD-A06, QD-G01) and refuse it deliberately where the client has nothing (QD-A01).

1.6 MOST CONTRACTS ARE EXACTLY WHAT THEY SAY. `Q148.X3` (the repeatable format erodes personhood) and `Q148.P4`
(ration the somebody). A screen format makes this more urgent, not less: a settlement hall showing the same shape of
twist every visit is `Q148.X3` at phone speed. QR-A C15 and QR-E rule 15 survive and should be read as a hard cap.

1.7 TOLD AND HAPPENED. `Q148.P2` (the discoverable lie). Survives whole; it is about the hand-in, which is also a
screen now (the client's portrait again, in the same building).

1.8 TWO OPEN CONTRACTS AT A TIME (folded 10/1 from rule 51; was "one open contract at a time"). `Q037.X2` (hoarding
floods the journal) and `Q148.W10` (notice to payment in one sitting). QR-A C9 survives as TWO slots. Battle Brothers
holds one (`Q236.W2`); Paolo 9/30 called that wack and set two, so a haul to a city and a hunt near it share one road.
On the screen it stays a visible rule: while BOTH slots are TAKEN, the hall shows other offers readable with accept
greyed (`Q236.N1`), and taking a third is only possible by finishing one or by the drop. The phone may show which
settlement offers what (a read-only board); the offer is accepted only here, on the settlement screen.

1.9 THE PERSON IS DOING SOMETHING. `Q095.W3` (character established in staging, not dialogue), `Q083.W1` (every NPC
has a life), against `Q149.X3` (shallow schedules). QR-E rule 3 survives in a smaller frame: the client portrait on
the offer screen is caught mid-action (Omar cleaning a clean lamp, Vela with the ledger on her knee, Abel at the
grinder). It is the same finding at portrait scale.

1.10 THE FIRST LINE IS ABOUT THEM. `Q001.W1` (transaction carries emotion) and `Q068.X3` (the lore dump). QR-E rule 7
and its FL tags survive as the first of the 2 to 4 screen lines. The screen makes the budget stricter: four lines,
so the emotion has to ride on the transaction, exactly `Q001.W1`'s craft.

1.11 THE REWARD IS A THING THAT MATTERS. `Q147.W8` (the reward is water) and `Q065.W4` (the job for medicine?). The pay
row can show batteries, resources, electricity or clout; round one's mixed currencies (QD-E02's one resources,
QD-E03's one electricity, QD-E05's one clout) survive as screen rows.

### 2. What is superseded (the walk-up door)

2.1 QR-A C1 "NEVER A BOARD, LIST, CARD OR POP-UP" is SUPERSEDED FOR CONTRACTS. The ruling names the pop-up screen as
the contract. What survives of C1 is its first half: the offer comes from a PERSON with a portrait who speaks, and
the job is done in a place. `Q108.W1` (the quest-giver is a coworker) survives as the client portrait. `Q148.W2` (the
board is a dignified front door) replaces `Q013.W6` as the governing citation for the door.

2.2 QR-A C2 "THE CLIENT MAY BE WALKED PAST" survives in meaning, dies in mechanism. The equivalent now is: the
settlement screen opens only because he tapped into the settlement on the map; the hall opens only because he tapped
it; the offer screen opens only because he tapped the offer. Three taps, all his. Nothing offers itself.

2.3 QR-E rule 1 THE PLACE FIRST (a set of cells that differs at close zoom) moves from cells to the settlement
picture. `Q114.N1` (every substory entry is a visible oddity, never a UI element) and `Q023.W4` (the space testifies)
now apply to the settlement screen's drawing: the building that holds the ask looks different (the pump house with a
woman on a bucket, the charge post whose six sockets all read full). QD-F02 now puts its wrong detail on the menu.

2.4 QR-E rule 4 NO STOP, and rule 5 THE ASK OPENS ON HIS TAP, NEVER ON PROXIMITY: rule 4 is moot (there is no path to
step into on the settlement screen); rule 5 survives as "on his tap". The one ambient line QR-E allowed on proximity
becomes one overheard line under the settlement screen's sound (QD-E03's "Tres días, the light"), which is `Q043.W4`
(ambient banter) kept at the smallest dose.

2.5 QR-E rule 14 THE RESULT IS VISIBLE IN THE PLACE survives and gets easier: `Q149.W9` (the verdict as world-state).
The settlement screen is redrawn after the job; the building that asked shows the result.

2.6 The DISCOVERY-BY-WALKING findings are the real loss: `Q013.W6` (the chance encounter, the city snags you),
`Q114.W2` (they are not on the map; found just by walking around) and `Q114.N1`. These lived on the close street,
which is dead except in fights and special places. They survive in two narrower homes: (a) ROAD EVENTS on the map
(rule 33, unchanged: a face, 2 or 3 choices), which are the Battle Brothers form of the chance encounter; (b)
SPECIAL PLACES and the job's own ground after the yes, where on-foot play stays legal and a person can still be
come upon (QD-E03's neighbour, QD-G04's welder, QD-A03's man at the door who wants his meter read low). The
re-cut designs cite `Q013.W6` only in its new sense: the walk-past is the building he does not tap.

2.7 QR-A C11's audible window survives but changes where it is heard. `Q011.P3`, `Q018.W8`, `Q128.W2` (everyone keeps
their schedule), `Q146.W8`. QD-A02's horn and QD-F03's tone used to be heard on the street; now the horn is a sound on
the map clock and the tone plays on the settlement screen at its time.

### 3. What changes shape: QR-F, the first sixty seconds

QR-F's fifteen rules were written for a figure standing on a close grid at second zero. Under the third votes the
game's normal surface is the map and the settlement screen; the fight is on the fight board (house-sized tiles, a
piece of the city; swept 9/29, rule 38: the close grid and the walk are dead, and a special place on foot uses the
112 walk art, never a cell grid). Rule
by rule:

- QR-F 1 SILENCE GATE: survives exactly. `Q134.X3` and `Q134.X9` (spending the best beat in the first minute).
- QR-F 2 ONE CELL, FIRST PRESS: dead with the walk (rule 38); its spirit, the first input answers at once, moves to the first tap. On the map, the first tap moves the party
  marker; `Q060.X4` (clunky touch controls, direct for a phone) and `Q038.X4` (dated tile-by-tile friction) now argue
  FOR the map and the settlement screen as the first surface.
- QR-F 3 SOMETHING WITHIN SIX: becomes "something within one tap". `Q055.X1` (the empty start) and `Q087.X3` (the
  sparse world) are about the first surface: the first settlement screen must show at least one tappable building and
  the SCAVENGE button.
- QR-F 4 THE THESIS ON THE PATH: becomes THE THESIS IN THE PICTURE: the settlement screen's drawing carries the thesis
  object. `Q101.X5` and `Q104.X3` (the best beat unmarked and optional) still argue it must be in view, not hidden
  behind a tap.
- QR-F 5 and 6 NOBODY IN THE DOOR, NOBODY COMES: survive as "no portrait on screen and no road event before 60 s".
- QR-F 7 ONE SILENT CHOICE: survives, and SCAVENGE is its best home. `Q050.W4` (small choices teach big choices) and
  `Q143.W8` (silence writes flags): the first scavenge on the family's own settlement turns up the family's last cell
  (QD-F01's take-or-leave) and the choice writes one ledger row with nothing on screen acknowledging it.
- QR-F 8 THE TELL COMES LATER: survives whole. `Q128.X2` and `Q096.X5`: silent failure must be told by the world.
- QR-F 9 NO NUMBERS, NO NAMES: survives, and is the hardest rule for a Battle Brothers screen, because BB's
  settlement screen is full of numbers. `Q081.X4`, `Q046.X2`, `Q065.X2` (the overwhelming first hour) argue that the
  FIRST settlement screen hides its prices until 60 s have passed and he has tapped something.
- QR-F 10 THE MAP IS ONE SQUEEZE AWAY (renamed ONE TAP AWAY in the 9/29 sweep): survives as the map being the first screen, quiet.
- QR-F 11 OVERHEARD MEANS NOT TO HIM: survives (`Q043.W4`, `Q037.W2`), now as the settlement's sound.
- QR-F 12 ONE NAME, ONE CONTRACT IN THREE MINUTES: survives; the one contract is an offer screen he opened.
- QR-F 13 ONE WRONG DETAIL: survives and moves into the settlement picture. `Q091.W7`, `Q010.W7`, `Q133.W9`.
- QR-F 14 THE FIRST MINUTE IS NOT THE LOUDEST: survives.
- QR-F 15 AFTER A FLIP, THE CLOCK RESTARTS: survives; the first thing after a flip is the same settlement screen in
  another act, derived. `Q144.W9` (the second life proves the first happened): the family home base's picture is the
  proof.

### 4. What the first sixty seconds look like now

A sketch, for RUN and WORLD to test against, built only from surviving rules:

- 0 s: the map, the family's home base under the party marker, no sound but the valley. Nothing moves toward the
  marker (QR-F 6). No numbers on screen (QR-F 9).
- 0 to 20 s: he taps the home base (or taps the map and travels; both are his). The settlement screen opens: the
  family's block drawn in one view, three or four buildings he can tap, one of them the family house (a special
  place), and SCAVENGE. Nothing speaks (QR-F 1). The wrong detail is in the picture: a charge post whose sockets all
  read full with nothing plugged in (QD-F02's detail, moved).
- 20 to 60 s: one silent choice. SCAVENGE turns up the family's last battery cell in the back room; take it or leave
  it; nothing acknowledges it (QR-F 7, `Q143.W8`). Or he taps the family house and the kitchen opens in one view, things to tap
  (QD-F01, re-cut 9/29; nothing is walked).
- After 60 s: the first portrait may appear, only on his tap. The first contract is in the hall (or the charge post
  building on the first settlement): Nina's four lines, 1 battery, accept or decline, and decline writes nothing.
- After the first arrival elsewhere, and never before 60 s, road events may fire on the map.

This keeps the promise QR-F was protecting (`Q134.X3`, `Q134.X9`): the first minute is a place, not a pitch.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)

P1. THE CONTRACT'S DOOR IS THE OFFER SCREEN IN A SETTLEMENT BUILDING. Test: every contract definition names a
settlement id, a building id and a client portrait id; none fires from a proximity trigger (there is no walk to be near anything in, rule 38).
(Paolo 9/28; `Q148.W2`, `Q148.P1`, `Q108.W1`)

P2. THREE TAPS, ALL HIS. Settlement, building, offer. Test: no offer screen opens without the tap on its building in
the same visit. (QR-A C2 re-cut; rule 32a)

P3. THE SCREEN HAS 2 TO 4 LINES, THE FIRST ABOUT THEM OR THE PLACE. Test: line count 2 to 4; line one has no "you"
subject and no quest word. (QR-E rule 7; `Q001.W1`, `Q068.X3`)

P4. PAY AND DANGER ARE ON THE SCREEN BEFORE ACCEPT. Test: accept is disabled until the pay row and any danger line
have rendered. (`Q126.N5`, `Q126.N6`, `Q060.W1`)

P5. NEGOTIATION IS ASK FOR MORE OR ASK FOR AN ADVANCE, ONCE EACH, THEN LOCK. Test: no fee-writing node after
accept. (`Q148.W1`, `Q126.N8`: the push that tells him you are a seller)

P6. AN ADVANCE MAKES A DROP A DEBT; NO ADVANCE, NO DEBT. Test: DROPPED writes a battery debt only if an advance was
paid. (QR-A C13(c); `Q087.W9`, `Q086.W8`)

P7. DECLINE WRITES NOTHING, AND NEITHER DOES NOT TAPPING. Test: state after decline or screen close equals state
before, except the clock; the building's drawing does not change because of a decline. (`Q126.W3`, `Q126.N13`,
`Q131.W4`; flaws `Q043.X2`, `Q106.X6`, `Q084.X5`)

P8. THE WORLD REDRAWS THE SETTLEMENT. A job done, failed or done by someone else changes the settlement picture on
the next visit, written without his id when he was not involved. (`Q149.W9`, `Q147.W7`, `Q055.W1`, `Q085.W3`)

P9. THE JOB IS STILL PEOPLE, PLACES AND THINGS. After accept, the job is on the map, at the place's screen, and on the fight board if it comes to a fight,
with people met there. (rule 20's surviving half; `Q095.W3`, `Q083.W1`, `Q023.W4`)

P10. DISCOVERY LIVES ON THE ROAD AND IN SPECIAL PLACES. Chance encounters are road events (a face, 2 or 3 choices) or
people on a job's ground, never an offer screen that opens itself. (`Q013.W6` re-read, `Q114.W2`, `Q050.W9`)

P11. THE FIRST SETTLEMENT SCREEN HIDES ITS NUMBERS FOR 60 S. Test: no price, fee or counter renders before t = 60 s
and the first tap. (QR-F 9; `Q081.X4`, `Q046.X2`, `Q065.X2`)

P12. SCAVENGE CARRIES THE FIRST SILENT CHOICE. Test: the first scavenge on the family home base yields one take-or-
leave with one ledger row and a later world tell. (QR-F 7, 8; `Q050.W4`, `Q143.W8`, `Q128.X2`, `Q096.X5`)

P13. RATION THE TWIST HARDER ON A SCREEN. Test: per settlement hall, per act, the twist share stays under the cap
QR-A C15 sets. (`Q148.X3`, `Q148.P4`)

## WHAT TO AVOID (the flaws)

- `Q148.X3`: the format that erodes personhood; a hall that always has the same shape of twist.
- `Q037.X2`: a hall that lets him stack jobs.
- `Q043.X1`, `Q043.X2`: a client portrait that sulks on the next visit.
- `Q106.X6`, `Q084.X5`: a decline that locks the best scene or deletes the client.
- `Q128.X2`, `Q096.X5`: silent consequences with no world tell.
- `Q046.X2`, `Q065.X2`, `Q081.X4`: a first settlement screen that opens as a spreadsheet of names and prices.
- `Q149.X3`: portraits that idle the same way in every building.
- `Q055.X1`, `Q087.X3`: a first settlement with nothing to tap.
- `Q060.X4`, `Q038.X4`: putting fiddly cell-walking back on any surface of a phone game (rule 38 killed it everywhere).

## OPEN

- Whether the first settlement's hall shows ONE offer or several. Battle Brothers shows several; QR-F 12 says one in
  the first three minutes. Default: one, until 180 s.
- Whether the client portrait on the offer screen is the same portrait that talks on the job's ground (rule 19 and
  ONE ID, ONE WHOLE PERSON say yes; PORTRAIT owns it).
- How the settlement screen shows a declined job being done by somebody else without implying he was asked. The
  library has `Q085.W3` (an NPC could finish it) but no screen-scale example.
- Whether "ask for more" can make a client withdraw the offer, as Battle Brothers sometimes does. The free-no rule
  says a withdrawal must write nothing; the library has no case of a withdrawn offer that stayed free.
