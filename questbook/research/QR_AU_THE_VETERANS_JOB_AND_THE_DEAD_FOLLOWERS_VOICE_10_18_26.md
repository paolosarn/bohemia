## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

A veteran's job comes from the veteran's own mouth at the night camp, with the veteran's own THING set down in the sand
(the spurs, the dog tags, the coffee can, the cut card), and a PLACE on the map the player can see; it is paid from the
veteran's own share, so the fee means something, and it never touches the hall board, which is the card he keeps
saying no to. A dead follower's unfinished job is used by the Amalgamation only after act 1's reveal, always through a
mouth that is visibly NOT the follower (a CB radio, a ration kiosk, a booking screen, a billboard, a child with an
implant), saying only words the follower really said, with exactly one thing the follower could not have known; every
such job has a living person with a real claim on the same job (the widow, the daughter, the son, the grandson), and
the player can always finish it through that person instead. Nothing in either half is a loyalty gate, nothing brings
the dead back, and every design names its ASSET NEEDED and SOUND NEEDED so the art and sound lanes can make the quest.

## 1. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG (measured against the repo)

Before this shelf the design library held 260 designs. Measured this round with a word count over questbook/designs/:
- 199 of 260 (77%) open on the hall's OFFER SCREEN: a client portrait, three lines, a fee, accept or decline. That is
  Battle Brothers' contract screen (`Q236.W4`, `Q236.W1`), it was ruled the front door on 9/28, and it was the right port.
- 7 of 260 open at the camp, on the road, or in the place.
- 0 of 260 are a veteran's own trouble. 0 of 260 are the Amalgamation using a dead follower's job.
- All 20 of QR-AQ's follower jobs open on the hall's offer screen with the follower "standing beside the client".

The law says a quest is people, places and things, never a card, and text comes from a mouth with a portrait. The offer
screen passes the second half (a face speaks) and he has now said no to the first half three times ("no more cards with
quests", 9/20, 10/17, this round). Our own library says the offer screen reads like a vending machine when the client
has no want of their own (`Q236.X3`) and the twist becomes a template (`Q236.X2`). And Battle Brothers itself never puts
a man's personal story in the hall: his background fires on the map and at camp [R]. So QR-AQ's answer to "how does a
member bring a job" was wrong for the member's OWN trouble: it took the stranger's door for a friend's ask. Correction,
binding on this shelf and routed to WORLD and RUN TWO: **a company member's own job is offered at the night camp, by
their mouth, with their thing on the ground; the hall offer screen is for strangers.** QR-AQ's 20 designs keep the hall
for the client who is a stranger and need one line each moving the member's part to camp (section 8, ROUTED).

## 2. THE VETERAN'S JOB: HOW IT COMES, WHAT IT PAYS

1. WHO. A fighter with about 20 fights (TUNING's number; the third votes use the same 20 for the 10% death rule) and a
   background. Not every man: Battle Brothers men are meant to be spent (`Q041.W4`, `Q041.W9`) and a job for every
   recruit would make every recruit a chore (`Q041.X2`, `Q061.X5`). The job is what tells a veteran from a number.
2. WHERE. At the night camp, after the fire is lit (QR-AS made the camp the stop's door). The veteran sets a THING down
   and speaks three lines (QR-G line 13; `Q074.X5`, `Q068.X3`). The thing is the scar made visible (`Q048.W6`), and the
   portrait is the one talking.
3. THE THING IS ALWAYS ON THE BODY FIRST. The spurs on the belt, the gloves on the pack, the tags in a pocket, for fights
   before the ask. A design that has no thing on the rig body before the ask is not a veteran's job; it is a stranger's
   job with a familiar face. Identity first, then the story (`Q048.W5`, `Q048.W7`).
4. THE PLACE IS ON THE MAP. One map stop, one block, one leg (QR-G line 8; `Q095.X2`, `Q079.X1`). The real region gives
   every veteran a real place (Sloan, Jean, Charleston, Nellis' fence, Boulder City, Henderson, Railroad Pass, Summerlin,
   Apex, Creech), and the deadline, if any, is said aloud and shown on the place's picture (line 3; `Q095.X1`).
5. THE FEE COMES FROM THE VETERAN'S OWN SHARE. A friend paying you out of his own pocket is a different sentence from a
   clerk paying from a faction's chest; it makes the client a person with a want (`Q133.X2`, `Q108.X5`) and kills the
   vending machine (`Q236.X3`). Some ask with no fee at all ("I'm not paying, I'm asking", QD-AU14); the yes is still free
   and the no is still free.
6. THE PAY THE PLAYER FEELS IS THE CHANGE ON THE VETERAN. Not loot (`Q125.X8`, `Q127.X7`): the tag nailed to the cart,
   the scar staying and the jaw unclenching, the pay book tied shut. The Nemesis study's best lesson, align emotion with
   reward (`Q048.W10`), done with a picture instead of a better sword. Rule 117c: a visible and audible win.
7. THE ENEMY REMEMBERS. Some veterans' jobs are the man who gave them the scar (QD-AU05), the brother on the other side
   (QD-AU07). The rival is generated from the company's own history, and then given one written scene, because a
   procedural rival without handcraft is shallow (`Q048.W2`, `Q048.W3`, `Q048.X2`).
8. NEVER A LOYALTY GATE. Carried from QR-AQ section 3: a refused or failed job never makes the veteran leave, sulk or
   fight worse. Declining writes nothing (`Q236.W7`, `Q084.X5`). The fight-free road is always real (`Q030.W10`).
9. THE REFUSAL INSIDE, PRICED. Every veteran's job has a D road (QR-G line 12; `Q131.X7`, `Q139.P2`) and a written fate
   for a lost fight (line 23; `Q095.X3`, `Q098.X2`, `Q093.X2`). QR-AT measured that 40 of 50 designs forgot the second one;
   this shelf writes both at birth.
10. THE JOB CROSSES THE ACTS AS THE HEIR'S. A veteran who dies or ages out with the job unfinished leaves it to a living
    person, who brings it to camp holding the thing (QD-AU20). The heir is a person, not a respawn (`Q144.W4`), and a
    doomed life becomes a testament (`Q144.W6`). The veteran's portrait is not shown; the thing is.

## 3. THE DEAD FOLLOWER'S JOB AND THE AMALGAMATION

What the canon gives: the Amalgamation is a distributed machine grown from merged data-portraits of real people; in
act 1 it is a ghost and never named (the 7/24 lock); its threat scales with nearness to its secret; act 3 is the
Network crumbling. What QR-AQ gave: an unfinished follower job crosses the acts as an offer from the heir. This page
adds the machine's version of the same crossing, and the library is unusually clear on how to make it horror and not cheap.

1. NEVER IN ACT 1. The word and the machine stay hidden in act 1 (the 7/24 lock), so its use of the dead starts in act 2,
   after the reveal has landed. In act 1 a dead follower's job crosses only as the heir's (QR-AQ). No design on this shelf
   with the Amalgamation sits on the act-1 shelf.
2. THE MOUTH IS VISIBLY NOT THE FOLLOWER. A CB radio, a ration kiosk, a clinic booking screen, a billboard, a payment
   kiosk, a seven-year-old with an old implant. The portrait shown is the follower's own, one row of pixels late, or too
   clean. The player is never fooled about WHAT is speaking; the horror is that it sounds right (`Q034.W2`, `Q034.W1`).
   SOMA's honest lesson: the comforting lie must be motivated, and the game must not cheat the player by hiding the truth
   (`Q034.W8`, `Q034.X2`).
3. ONLY WORDS THE FOLLOWER REALLY SAID. The voice is cut from the follower's own lines, breaths in the wrong places. The
   devil is a literalist, not a liar (`Q127.W1`): every word is true, and that is what makes it a bargain and not a trap.
   SOUNDS builds the voice from the follower's recorded lines only; it is never smooth.
4. EXACTLY ONE THING THE FOLLOWER COULD NOT HAVE KNOWN. The keys moved after he died; the daughter's name as an
   ingredient; the son's life after the father died. That is the analog horror (the bible's rule one: an ordinary thing,
   one detail wrong, said plainly, never explained) and the only tell the player needs that this is a recording that
   kept learning (`Q127.W4`, `Q141.W6`).
5. A LIVING PERSON WITH A REAL CLAIM, ALWAYS. The widow two blocks from the creditor, the daughter at the next stall, the
   apprentice in the next town, the son at the shop door, the grandson with the paper ledger, the mothers of the boys.
   The job can always be finished through the living person instead of the machine, and that road pays the same
   (line 30; `Q190.X1`, `Q130.X9`). Three goods that will not align (`Q200.W1`): the dead's wish, the living's claim, and
   what the machine records.
6. OBEYING THE MACHINE IS ALLOWED AND HONEST, AND IT COSTS A RECORD. Taking the voice's job pays what it says (the
   literalist), and the cost is that the Network keeps the recipe, the voice, the bookings, the call: an act-3 ledger
   the crisis can read. Nothing is hidden: the design says at the offer what the machine is (`Q125.X6`, `Q127.X8`).
7. NEVER A RESURRECTION. No road brings the follower back, and no road lets the player keep the voice as a companion.
   The structurally honest ending (`Q034.W5`, `Q034.W7`): the ARK was a false salvation; so is the kiosk.
8. THE HARM COMES BACK WEARING THE FACE THE PLAYER KNEW. That is the whole effect, and the library warns it only works
   if the face was a person first (`Q073.W5`, `Q200.X2`). So these designs fire only if the follower lived long enough
   to have lines, and only on the follower's own unfinished job (the AQ pairs).
9. THE CHILD, CAREFULLY. QD-AU13 puts the words in a child's mouth. The library's duty-of-care study is the floor: the
   implant is a device and the medic can switch it off, never a curse, an infection or a superpower (`Q058.X2`, `Q058.X1`,
   `Q058.W2`); she wants water, which is the line the scene is built round (`Q138.W2`).
10. IN ACT 3 THE VOICES GET LOUDER AS THE NETWORK DIES. The bookings, the billboard and the kiosks are the Network's
    last habits running without supervision (`Q195.W2`: the faith outlived its cause). The madrina's kiosks collect
    kindly in act 2 and all at once in act 3 (QD-AU19). That is the crisis made local, a block at a time.

1. A company member's OWN job is offered at the night camp by their own mouth; the hall offer screen is for strangers.
   Test: no design whose client is a company member opens on the offer screen.
2. A veteran's job has a THING that is on the rig body for at least five fights before the ask. Test: the thing has an
   asset id on the body sheet.
3. The fee comes from the veteran's share or is said to be nothing. Test: the spec's payer field is the member, never the faction.
4. A veteran's job is never a loyalty gate. Test: no branch writes leave, morale down or refuse-to-fight on the member.
5. The Amalgamation's use of a dead follower's job never fires in act 1. Test: act field 2 or 3; the gate reads the 7/24 lock.
6. Its mouth is a device or an implant, never the follower's body. Test: the speaker field is a prop or a living person.
7. Its voice uses only lines the follower said. Test: every line id in its script resolves in the follower's own line bank.
8. It has exactly one detail the follower could not have known. Test: one WRONG line, flagged.
9. A living person can finish the same job and the living road pays the same. Test: two roads, equal pay.
10. No road returns the follower or keeps the voice. Test: no branch adds the follower to the roster.
11. Every design names ASSET NEEDED and SOUND NEEDED. Test: both headings present and non-empty (proposed as a gate
    leg for quests_library_gate.js; PLUMBER's call).
12. Every design writes the job's fate on a lost fight and a priced refusal at birth, and is stamped CHECKLIST owed.
