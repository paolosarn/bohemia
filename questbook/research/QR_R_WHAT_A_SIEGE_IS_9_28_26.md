# QR-R: WHAT IS A SIEGE OF ONE OF OUR HOME BASES, AS PLAY?

STATUS: draft:true, research only, nothing built (rule 35). Letter R, row [the siege], round 9/28.
FOLDED 10/9 (QR-AK): QR-U rule 21 and the sharpens of rules 6, 12 and 13, at the end of THE RULE FOR THE BUILDING LANES, each marked in place.
Written for RUN, WORLD, FACTIONS, COMBAT, PEOPLE, WORDS, ECONOMY, TUNING and DYNASTY. Every name and identity choice is Paolo's.
Governing rulings: laws/BOHEMIA_ADDENDUM_THE_THIRD_VOTES_9_28_26.md s5 (FOURTEEN HOME BASES AND ROAMING PARTIES, NOT
TERRITORY: "a home base can be attacked: taken or ruined; the hard ones late in an act; a SIEGE is a research

NEVER DIES; a veteran of about 20 fights dies at 10%; HIGH GROUND IS A ROOF); rule 36 (struck down = 20% dead, else
30 to 40 days out with a mark); records/BOHEMIA_PAOLO_QUESTS_ARE_THE_SETTLEMENT_CONTRACT_SCREEN_9_28_26.md (contracts
are the Battle Brothers offer screen); QR-O (the board reads the base; its OPEN 2 left the siege open); QR-J (a
crisis is stages that rewrite the board and never expire a taken contract); QR-G THE CHECKLIST.

QUESTION: a home base can be taken or ruined, and a raided base falls in later acts. The library has no siege study.
From the nearest studies and the Battle Brothers reference, what IS a siege of one of our home bases as play: the
warning, the build-up on the map, the contract to defend or to take, the fight on the fight board, the outcome written
into the ledgers and the later acts? How do defending and attacking differ? How does a player who was elsewhere learn
of a fall? How does the loss stay content (retake, rescue, witness), never a dead end?

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

A siege in Bohemia is not a mode and not a set piece. It is a SHORT CHAIN OF ORDINARY PIECES the game already has,
strung on one base: a build-up the player can SEE on the map (tracks converging, a camp that grows, prices that jump,
people walking the other way), an OFFER in a hall (the defender's hall posts "hold us", the attacker's hall posts
"take it"; either can be declined for free), a few MAP DAYS of preparation that the fight remembers, ONE fight on the
base's own fight board (roofs are the high ground, the civilians are on it, a way out always stays open), and an
OUTCOME with three states, HELD, TAKEN or RUINED, written into the base's board, its prices, the feed and the derive.
Battle Brothers, Paolo's named reference for this layer, already has every piece: "protect a village from a raid
(defend at night)", the noble war's "defend a stronghold" and "assault a fortified camp", and the settlement
SITUATION "raided" that moves prices and boards (reference/library/battle_brothers/08_CONTRACTS_EVENTS.md,
01_WORLDMAP.md). The nearest library study, the Kenshi outpost file, supplies the soul: the siege happens WHERE THE
HOURS LIVE (`Q151.W6`), the flee option never closes (`Q151.W7`), raids scale with visible prosperity (`Q151.W3`), and
the world never applauds, it just re-prices (`Q151.W10`). The library is equally loud about what kills sieges: they
collapse into farmable patterns (`Q151.X1`), they have one verb (`Q151.X2`), the people inside are silent (`Q151.X3`),
and a loss measured in days drives players out (`Q093.X2`). So the rule for us: the world may take a base whether the
player engages or not (`Q087.W9`), but only after a warning in two channels and in stated map days (`Q011.P12`);
the player never loses the main character, and every company loss is a legible receipt under rule 36 (`Q137.W1`);
and every fall opens content instead of closing it: a WITNESS event at the ruin, a RESCUE contract for the carried
off, a RETAKE contract in the exiles' new hall, and a later act that SHOWS it (`Q146.W1`, `Q110.W1`, `Q147.W7`).

## THE FINDINGS

### 1. What a siege is made of: the base is a hostage the player already loves

- THE BASE IS A HOSTAGE, NOT A MECHANIC. `Q093.W6`: days of work, and "your heart will pound as you return to your
  base... knowing that everything could be lost in an instant." Its port `Q093.P4` aims it straight at a settlement
  game: the buildings are the thing you love BECAUSE they can be taken. Our fourteen home bases are not the player's
  property, but the family's contracts are written into them (QR-O rule 16), so a siege threatens hours the player
  spent there.
- THE SIEGE HAPPENS WHERE THE HOURS LIVE. `Q151.W6`: "the fight's arena is the player's own labor, so every breach
  costs biography." `Q116.W9` gives the mechanism: "the level remembers your preparation" (players rigged the hotel
  on an earlier visit). For us: the fight board of a besieged base is the SAME drawn block the player has visited,
  with the pump he escorted parts for and the wall he was paid to raise. A siege at a base the player never touched
  is weaker content, and the build-up should prefer bases with a family deed in them.
- ATTACHMENT IS MANUFACTURED BY LABOR. `Q151.W9` and `Q055.W9`: unwritten people become devastating losses through
  invested hours. `Q049.P3`: the settlement is "the persistent structure the family visibly BUILDS across generations".
  The siege is where that investment is put at risk.
- BUILDING IS THE SURRENDER OF MOBILITY. `Q151.W4`: a base trades away the ability to leave. That is the defender's
  whole position in one line, and it is why the ATTACKER is the mobile one (a roaming party, third votes s5).
- OWNERSHIP IS AN ADDRESS. `Q151.W1`: building flips one flag, "findable", and the political simulation gets a place
  to happen. `Q151.W3` and `Q044.W4`: raids scale with visible prosperity; success breeds danger. So a base that the
  family helped to boom is the base most likely to be besieged. That is realism (bandits go where the food is) and it
  is the future going both ways at the scale of one place.
- WHY NOT THE AMALGAMATION. `Q085.P7` and `Q085.W8`: its threat is a fever that rises near the secret. Canon: it is
  custodial and kills only to protect the secret. It does not besiege bases. Sieges belong to the Destroyers (act 1),
  to factions and roaming parties (all acts). The one exception worth a design later: the data centre base near the
  secret, where a "siege" would be quiet (the lights go out one floor at a time), never an assault.

### 2. The warning: a build-up the player can read on the map

- THE WORLD'S CLOCK TICKS WHETHER YOU ENGAGE. `Q065.W6` (yours and the world's clocks) and `Q065.P5` (the world's
  actions: "a faction's plan, the Amalgamation's advance, a raid"). `Q087.W9` is the hard version: ignore the warning
  that your town will be attacked and "you'd come back to find it SACKED, ALL THE NPCs GONE". Its port `Q087.P5` calls
  that "not a fail message" but the world. This is the right teeth for a siege: the world can take a base.
- BUT THE CLOCK MUST BE GENEROUS AND LEGIBLE. `Q011.P12`: "make timed windows GENEROUS + legible (show the schedule,
  forgive near-misses)". `Q011.W5`: the countdown works as EMOTION (devotion under a falling sky), and `Q011.P6` names
  our own case: "a district under siege can use a COUNTDOWN as an emotional instrument". The flaws beside them are the
  limit: `Q095.P7` (a silent timer is a bug in a costume), `Q137.X3` (honest once, vicious always), `Q095.X1` (the hard
  fail unsignposted). QR-G checklist 3: every deadline counts in map days, said aloud and visible on the map.
- THE BUILD-UP IS THINGS, NOT A BAR. Battle Brothers shows it as TRACKS (every party leaves prints; a larger host shows
  as "a large host" with a destination line) and as SITUATIONS ("greenskins nearby") that move prices
  (01_WORLDMAP.md). The library backs each channel:
  - INVENTORY FIRST. `Q107.W4`: the rebellion "arrives as inventory shrinkage". The first sign of a coming siege is the
    target base's market: ammunition and medicine gone, water dear, people buying cart wheels.
  - PEOPLE WALKING THE OTHER WAY. `Q055.P6`: the story pivots on system state ("a supply run becomes a desperate
    evacuation"). A road event with a family pushing a cart away from the base is the warning with a face.
    `Q128.X5`: the sensible coward "deserves one scene of vindication-adjacent dignity". The family that leaves early is
    right and the game must not call them wrong.
  - A CORPSE OF A PLACE LIKE YOURS. `Q107.W2`: Winterhome, "another colony very similar", already fallen, sparks the
    uprising. The first base to fall in an act is the warning for the next: its refugees appear in the neighbours'
    halls and on the road.
  - THE WORLD'S OWN MEDIA. `Q088.W8`: radio and TV carry the timeline "until the stations go dark"; `Q101.W3`: the
    quest arrives by an automated distress call from a dead machine. In Bohemia the phone feed (city view only) may
    report the build-up as what the world did. It never delivers the contract.
  - TWO CHANNELS. `Q141.X3`: accessibility rides on sensory channels. Every siege warning is a picture on the map AND a
    sound (the base's night noise changes; generators run louder), QR-G checklist 15.
- EVERYONE KEEPS THEIR SCHEDULE. `Q128.W2`: "the world ends in three days and everyone clocks in", routine as courage
  and as denial; `Q128.W8`: the postman reads his regulations "for permission to be afraid". The besieged base's
  board keeps its ordinary offers next to the defend contract. That ordinariness is the analog horror line.
- A WARNING THAT SOMEBODY LIED ABOUT. `Q148.W3`: evidence built to be misread once. A minority of build-ups are not
  what they look like (the camp is refugees, not raiders; the "raiders" are the base's own sons). Rationed by
  `Q148.P4` and `Q148.X3`: one siege in five, not every siege.

### 3. The offer: two halls, two screens, one base

- THE BB SHAPES. The defender's contract is Battle Brothers' "protect a village from a raid (defend at night)" and
  the noble war's "defend a stronghold"; the attacker's is "assault a fortified camp" or "raze a village" and, during
  the War of the Noble Houses crisis, contracts where "settlements change hands" (08_CONTRACTS_EVENTS.md,
  01_WORLDMAP.md). [RECALL ONLY, not verified this round: the later expansions add proper siege contracts on noble
  strongholds with siege equipment to destroy. Nothing below depends on it.]
- THE FEE LOCKS BEFORE THE TRUTH. `Q148.W1`: haggling at maximum ignorance separates money from morality forever
  after. On a siege offer the fee, the advance and the stated day are set on the screen and never move (QR-O rule 3).
  The size of the attacking party is stated in skulls, as Battle Brothers states danger before the yes.
- DECLINING IS FREE, EVEN HERE. `Q148.W2`: the board is "a dignified front door ... a job system that never begs".
  A no to a defend offer writes nothing. The base's fate is then the world's, not a punishment for the no. QR-G
  checklist 11, `Q084.X5`, `Q131.X7`.
- THE ATTACKER'S HALL PAYS BETTER, AND THAT IS ALLOWED. `Q031.W5`: the regime pays you to hand over the desperate;
  virtue has a price tag. Between offers, taking a base can outpay defending one. Inside one contract, the crueller
  branch pays the same or less (`Q130.X9`, QR-G checklist 21). The price tag lives between the two halls.
- THE PRETEXT IS ON THE SCREEN. `Q082.W4`: "on what pretext shall we apprehend them?" "we need no pretext!" The
  take-contract client's two lines are a reason (they stole our water, they shelter the Destroyers), and the player
  prices it at ignorance. `Q096.W2`: atrocities are committed by careful people with a real threat on the other side.
  The attacker's client is never a cartoon.
- ALLEGIANCE REDRAWS THE MAP. `Q025.W7`: siding with a faction grants it territory; `Q025.W1`: favour and wrath are
  separate axes. Under third votes s5 there is no territory colour, so for us "redraws the map" means one base changes
  hands or goes dark. Taking a base for one faction earns that faction's favour AND the taken base's people's
  wrath, independently.
- THE POLITICS NEED MORE THAN ONE VERB. `Q151.X2`: "the collectors demand, the raiders raid, but there is no envoy TO
  them". Its port `Q151.P2` asks for tribute renegotiation, raid redirection, joint-defence pacts. On our screens this is
  the third option of every siege contract: TALK (pay tribute, buy time, open the gate on terms). `Q136.P5`: the
  fallback that costs the same; the talked ending is priced in a different currency, never free.

### 4. The days between the yes and the fight: preparation is the depth

- THE LEVEL REMEMBERS YOUR PREPARATION. `Q116.W9`. Accepting a defend contract gives the company the stated map days
  before the assault. Each day can be spent: raising cover (the base's house tiles get sandbags and cars),
  hiring the base's militia, moving the kids out, buying medicine. Rule 36's law says formation and gear are the depth;
  a siege is where formation meets ground the player shaped.
- ROLE ASSIGNMENT IS THE SKILL CHECK. `Q006.W4` and `Q137.W4`: the finale's authorship is WHO goes where; `Q006.P3`
  names our version outright: "who holds the plant, who leads the evacuation, who fronts the negotiation". The siege's
  one pre-fight screen is a formation screen with three posts: the roof, the gate, the way out.
- THE ESCORT LIVES BY LEAVING. `Q137.W5` and `Q137.P5`: every crisis event includes one removal-as-safety assignment,
  a companion who survives certainly by exiting (escorting evacuees). A defend contract always has the WAY OUT post:
  the member placed there walks the civilians out and cannot be struck down in this fight.
- EVERY DEATH IS A RECEIPT. `Q137.W1` and `Q137.P3`: each casualty names its cause in player history. `Q137.X1`: the
  rules must be visible or the wiki is mandatory. Rule 36's 20% and the veteran's 10% are TUNING's numbers; the
  siege's job is to make every struck-down member's cause legible (the roof with no cover, the gate held by two).
  `Q087.X6` is the warning against a coin flip: randomized permadeath punishes the unlucky, not the careless, so the
  20% roll must sit on a legible choice the player made.
- THE NIGHT BEFORE. `Q006.W10`: a quiet beat right before the point of no return makes the stakes land. The camp
  event on the last map day before the assault is one face, one line, no choice required (QR-G checklist 26's warm
  beat).
- STASIS IS NOT FREE. `Q132.X2` (the situation holds forever while you tour) and its fix `Q132.P6` (the held situation
  degrades on the settlement clock). The stated day is real: if the company does not arrive, the base fights without
  it. But QR-J's rule holds: the TAKEN contract is not expired; it turns into its aftermath leg (see 7).

### 5. The fight on the fight board

- ONE BLOCK, ONE FIGHT. `Q060.W4`: a small grid "so you hold the WHOLE puzzle in your head"; our board is small because its tiles are house-sized. QR-G checklist 8: the
  work fits one leg and one block. A siege is ONE fight on the base's own drawn block (third votes s2: the fight board is
  the fight's ground), against a group, on the beat, with the companion, as every fight is. Waves are beats inside
  that fight, not extra fights. A siege is a TOUGH fight: 8 to 15 minutes, never past 15 (rule 40a); the HOLD count
  of bars is set inside that ceiling (at 120 BPM, 15 minutes is 450 bars, so a hold is far fewer).
- THE CLOSED BOX. `Q007.W1` and `Q007.P1` ("a besieged shelter"): a sealed location with a fixed cast concentrates the
  design. Inside the fight, the base is a closed box with ONE exception, next line.
- THE FLEE OPTION NEVER CLOSES. `Q151.W7`: abandoning everything mid-siege stays on the table, "and its standing
  availability is what makes staying a CHOICE instead of a checkpoint". `Q140.W8`: the off-ramp is real. Every siege
  fight board has a way-out edge; the company can leave through it at any beat. Leaving is the priced refusal (`Q139.P2`,
  QR-G checklist 12): the contract finishes at a stated lower pay, the base's outcome is computed from what stood.
- HIGH GROUND IS A ROOF, AND ROOFS FALL. Third votes s7: a roof still standing is the high ground; a collapsed roof is
  not. `Q099.W4`: "the building takes your floor in the first ten seconds". A siege is the one fight where the ground
  changes during the fight: the attacker's goal can be a roof, and a roof can go. The mound stays the only terrain
  effect (the overworld law), and a roof is a mound that can break.
- THE WIN IS NOT "KILL THEM ALL". `Q116.W6`: the fight can be unwinnable and still have stakes; `Q116.W2`: winning does
  not save him, staying does. A defender's win condition is HOLD (a number of bars on the beat) or BREAK THEIR NERVE (the
  party's leader down), never extermination. An attacker's win is the HALL or the PUMP held at the end. `Q142.W10`:
  the fight settles nothing by design, which is why the outcome is decided by what stands, not by the body count.
- NOBODY IS A BYSTANDER. `Q030.W1`: re-centre catastrophe on the powerless; `Q030.W8`: community is a survival
  resource. The base's own people are ON the fight board as their own side (the militia hold a door, a cook carries water).
  `Q116.W8`: you can hide in a closet and let your brother handle it; the militia can hold without the company, badly.
- NOT A FARM. `Q151.X1`: siege AI collapses into patterns; veteran bases "harvest their own attackers". `Q151.P3`: sieges
  that LEARN (the next attacker reads the last breach point and varies). `Q061.X5` and `Q030.X3`: sieges get repetitive
  over hundreds of hours. So: a base is besieged at most once per act, and the second siege of the same base in a
  later act comes at the wall that held last time.
- THE COMBAT IS NOT THE ONLY DOOR. `Q017.X1`, `Q098.X2` and `Q095.X3`: forced fights in front of the best scene. `Q079.W3`:
  every obstacle has a non-violent solution. Every siege contract has a talk option on the offer screen or in the
  preparation days (tribute, a gate opened on terms, an evacuation), and the emotional scene of the contract is
  reachable through it (QR-G checklists 23, 24).

### 6. Defending and attacking are different jobs

| | DEFEND | TAKE |
|---|---|---|
| who posts it | the besieged base's hall (a camp-tier base: its one offer) | the attacking faction's hall, or a roaming party's broker at a third base |
| pay | lower, often part in kind (hours of power, food, treatment; QR-O raided shape) | higher, in batteries (`Q031.W5`) |
| clock | the attacker's: a stated day, in map days | the player's: he chooses the night, but the defenders prepare too |
| ground | known, shaped by the preparation days (`Q116.W9`) | unknown until scouted; knowledge is the gate (`Q146.W7`) |
| people on the fight board | the base's civilians and militia, on the player's side (`Q030.W1`) | the same civilians, now in the player's way; the lane out stays open for them (`Q151.W7` mirrored) |
| win | HOLD for N bars, or break the leader | the hall or the pump held at the end |
| the talk option | pay tribute, buy days, parley at the gate (`Q151.P2`) | open the gate on terms: the defenders walk out with what they carry (`Q136.P5`) |
| the priced refusal | leave by the way out with the civilians, half pay (`Q137.W5`) | "I won't go inside": hold the road only, half pay |
| what it writes | HELD: the base rises; or FELL WITH US: the base's people remember who stood | TAKEN (works for the new owner) or RUINED (the player chose to burn); the signed derive falls |

Two library laws decide the moral shape. `Q030.W1` and `Q030.W4`: the victims' view, one concrete image, so the take
contract always shows one ordinary thing inside the walls (a school timetable on a door). `Q061.X6`: "honorable lords
still raid villages" was left unexamined in its game, and the lesson is that a gritty setting should EXAMINE it. So
the take contract is offered like any other job and priced honestly, and the ledger reads the player's version of it,
never a scold (`Q004.W4`, QR-O rule 6).

TAKEN is not the same as RUINED. `Q061.W7`: capture-and-rule is the ceiling of a bottom-up rise; `Q061.W8`: war leaves
lands exposed. A TAKEN base keeps working (its pump runs for a new owner, its board changes clients and pays in the
new owner's terms); a RUINED base goes dark (its board shows nothing but SCAVENGE). The player who takes a base for a
client chooses between the two at the end of the fight, and the choice pays the same (`Q130.X9`).

### 7. The outcome: three states, written everywhere, applauded nowhere

- DEFEAT CONTINUES INSTEAD OF ENDING. `Q146.W1`: losing produces a new game state rather than a load screen, "the
  single rule doing the most narrative work in the entire design". `Q110.W1` and `Q110.W2`: defeat is a transfer, the
  failure state is a starting position. `Q087.W1`: defeat scenarios instead of death. For a lost defence: the MAIN
  CHARACTER NEVER DIES (third votes s7). The company breaks; the struck-down roll rule 36 (20% dead, else 30 to 40 days;
  a veteran of about 20 fights at 10%); the rest are carried out by the way out. Default: they wake at the nearest held
  base's clinic, where QR-O rule 12 says the injured visibly spend their days. That clinic's hall carries the next
  offer (see 8).
- THE WORLD NEVER APPLAUDS. `Q151.W10`: "stand the siege and nothing congratulates you; the region just quietly
  re-prices." That is BB's SITUATION model exactly (raided: food scarce and dear, 07_ECONOMY.md). No banner, no toast
  (QR-G checklist 2). BUT `Q146.X1` (the triumph unmarked starves the arc) and `Q151.X3` (the interior life is silent)
  are the other half: `Q151.P5`, THE SETTLEMENT TESTIFIES, residents reference the siege in bark-scale lines. So: no
  applause, and one person in the base's hall who says one line about the night.
- THE BEST-RUN BASE STILL GETS STORY. `Q107.X6`: the arc resolves by dissolving, and the best-run city gets the least
  story. A HELD base posts the rebuild contract on the next visit (QR-O rule 7). `Q089.W4`: the crowdfunded highway; a
  held base rises by the sum of the contracts done for it.
- THE THREE LEDGER LINES. For every siege the ledgers write (a) the base's STATE (held, taken by whom, ruined), (b) the
  company's DEED if a contract was taken (held, broke, left by the way out, took, burned), and (c) the siege's FOLD
  ENTRY (`Q151.P3`: the breach point, the kill box, the casualty ledger) for the next siege of the same base. A
  declined offer writes only (a), which the world would have written anyway.
- THE LATER ACTS DERIVE IT, SIGNED. Third votes s3: built rises, raided falls. `Q137.W8`: the dead stay dead across a
  sequel; `Q144.W7`: inherited people speak in the past tense. A base that fell in act 1 is, in act 2 or 3, a ruin with
  squatters or a raider base, never a blank (QD-Q09 and QD-Q13 on the shelf already land both). `Q004.W5`: the payoff
  lands displaced, in a different quest; the grandchild's hall is where act 1's siege is paid.
- THE FAILURE IS VISIBLE GEOGRAPHY. `Q147.W7`: "the failure state is visible geography"; `Q128.W6`: the world holds its
  sadder configuration; its flaw `Q128.X2` requires one tell. A fallen base's settlement screen is drawn fallen: lights
  out (brownout canon, never total dark: one lamp), the hall's door boarded, one person.

### 8. How a player who was elsewhere learns of a fall

The library's loudest family on a phone is the SILENT FAIL (QR-G rank 1): `Q071.X1` (you can break it and never know),
`Q116.X2` (the game never tells you the rule), `Q092.X6` (a consequence the player never discovers is trivia),
`Q078.X2` (nobody reacts). QR-G checklist 1: within two map stops or one city-view open, the player meets a person,
place or thing that shows it. For a fall, in the order the player will usually meet them:

1. THE MAP. The base's marker changes: the lights of its blocks go out except one (`Q147.W7`), and a large party's
   tracks lead away from it, heavy (BB tracks, 01_WORLDMAP.md).
2. THE FEED, in the city view only. One post from someone inside or near (`Q088.W8`: the world explains itself through
   its own dying media). What the world did, never a contract and never a popup.
3. THE PRICES. The neighbouring bases' markets move: water or power dearer where the fallen base supplied it
   (`Q151.W10`, `Q082.W9`: the ruin's wine price is still tracked).
4. THE ROAD. A refugee road event near the fallen base (`Q107.W2`, `Q055.P6`). A face, two or three choices.
5. THE NEXT HALL. The nearest held base's hall posts the fallen base's people's contract: rescue or retake
   (`Q044.W7`: after a hard hit, send relief; QR-O rule 8).
6. THE CONSEQUENCE AS SCENERY, LATER. `Q116.W3`: "the consequence arrives as scenery, hours later". `Q116.W5`: the
   building lives while you are gone (a squatter moves in). The next visit to the base itself is the aftermath event.

### 9. The loss stays content: witness, rescue, retake, and the named party

- WITNESS. `Q141.P1`: the missing-persons organ; the settlement is where the dead are counted. `Q146.W6`: witnesses share
  the condition. `Q039.W6` and `Q023.P4`: the story in the walls, a barricaded home told by props. The aftermath event
  at a fallen base is a face and a count: who is here, who was carried off, who is under the car park. `Q137.P6` makes
  the count visible even when it is good. `Q138.X5` (the child who ran a siege has no decompression node) and
  `Q138.X8` (the siege home is one room deep) say the ruin must have domestic texture and the survivors a line of
  their own.
- RESCUE. `Q110.W1`: the defeated are CARRIED somewhere (the cannibals carry the unconscious to their camp). `Q110.W8`:
  rescue is a patrol route, not a quest. When a base is TAKEN, some of its people are carried to the attacker's home
  base: that base's location is known (the tracks go there) and a rescue contract appears in the exiles' hall. When
  a company member is lost in a siege and the roll was not death, the default is the clinic; a capture variant is
  OPEN 2.
- RETAKE. `Q146.W9`: the sequel self-generates: "the return-and-burn-it ending is unprompted ... near-universal";
  institutions that teach their victims recruit their destroyers. The fallen base's people, now in a neighbour's hall,
  post the retake. `Q087.W5` and `Q087.P4`: losing generates your nemesis for free.
- THE NAMED PARTY. `Q048.W1` (any grunt becomes a named rival) and `Q048.W3` (defeat fuels the rivalry: the one who beat
  you rises). The party that took a base gets one line of its own and a name on the next boards (QR-O rule 8).
  `Q048.X2`: without the hand-made line it stays a party.
- LOSING IS FUN, IF IT HAS A SHAPE. `Q045.W2` and `Q045.W6`: rise, disaster, spiral, fall is a dramatic shape.
  `Q044.W1`: "what story happened". `Q045.W7`: collapse from your own overreach. A family that boomed a base until it
  drew the siege authored the siege (`Q151.W3`).
- BUT A LOSS SIZED FOR A PHONE. `Q093.X2`: "players can lose hours or days of progress in a single raid", and it drives
  them out. `Q144.X2`: grind outweighs grief. QR-G checklist 10: the worst failure costs only this leg. The fall of a
  base costs the family ONE place's board and prices, and the company its struck-down members by rule 36; it never
  costs the main quest, the family's batteries in the treasury, or a replay.
- A TRIUMPH IN THE DARK. `Q041.W10`: the rare triumph makes the dread bearable; `Q041.X3`: unrelenting tone makes people
  bounce. `Q044.X1`: bad-luck sieges feel unfair. A siege table needs held nights as well as falls: TUNING's numbers
  should let a defended base hold most of the time when the player took the contract and prepared.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDING LANES (numbered, testable)

1. A siege is a CHAIN on one base: WARNING -> OFFER(S) -> PREPARATION DAYS -> ONE FIGHT -> OUTCOME -> AFTERMATH. No
   siege mode, no extra screen type, no second fight inside one contract. (`Q060.W4`, `Q007.W1`, QR-G 8)
2. The build-up is visible at least N map days before the assault (TUNING; suggested 4), in at least two channels on
   the map and one sound: converging tracks, a growing camp marker, a price move at the target's market, a road event
   with people walking away. (`Q011.P12`, `Q107.W4`, `Q141.X3`, `Q087.W9`)
3. Every siege posts a DEFEND offer in the target's hall; a TAKE offer is posted in the attacker's hall only when the
   attacker is a faction with a home base (a roaming party does not hire). Both are ordinary offer screens: portrait,
   2 to 4 lines, skulls, pay, advance, accept or decline. Declining writes nothing. (`Q148.W1`, `Q148.W2`, `Q084.X5`)
4. The assault day is spoken on the offer screen in map days and never moves. The assault never happens while the app
   is closed; the day counts map days travelled. (`Q095.P7`, `Q137.X3`, QR-G 3, 4)
5. Every siege contract has a TALK option (tribute, buy days, open the gate on terms) priced in a different currency,
   never free. (`Q151.X2`, `Q151.P2`, `Q136.P5`)
6. Before the fight, one formation screen with three posts: ROOF, GATE, WAY OUT. The member on WAY OUT cannot be struck
   down in that fight and walks the civilians out. (`Q006.P3`, `Q137.W4`, `Q137.P5`)
7. The fight is on the base's own fight board, with the base's people as a side, a way-out edge that never closes, and a
   win that is HOLD or HALL, never extermination. (`Q151.W6`, `Q151.W7`, `Q116.W6`, `Q030.W1`)
8. Roofs are high ground and can collapse during a siege; the mound is still the only terrain EFFECT. (third votes s7,
   `Q099.W4`)
9. THE MAIN CHARACTER NEVER DIES. Struck-down members roll rule 36 (20% dead, else 30 to 40 days, gear kept damaged);
   veterans of about 20 fights at 10%. Every struck-down line names its post and cause. (`Q137.W1`, `Q137.X1`, `Q087.X6`)
10. The outcome is one of HELD, TAKEN (by whom) or RUINED, and it writes three lines: the base's state, the company's
    deed (only if a contract was taken), and the fold entry (breach point, kill box, casualties). (`Q151.P3`, `Q146.W1`)
11. The same base is besieged at most once per act; the next siege of that base reads the fold entry and comes at the
    wall that held. (`Q151.X1`, `Q151.P3`, `Q061.X5`)
12. A fall is shown within two map stops or one city-view open, by the map marker (lights out, one lamp), one feed
    post, a price move, and the nearest held hall's new offer. Never a popup. (`Q071.X1`, `Q092.X6`, `Q116.W3`, QR-G 1, 2)
13. Every fall opens at least two of: a WITNESS event at the ruin, a RESCUE contract (people carried to the attacker's
    base), a RETAKE contract (in the exiles' hall). A fall is never a closed door. (`Q141.P1`, `Q110.W1`, `Q146.W9`)
14. A held base posts a rebuild contract on the next visit, and one person in its hall says one line about the night.
    No applause. (`Q107.X6`, `Q151.W10`, `Q151.P5`)
15. TAKEN and RUINED pay the same to the player who chooses between them at the end of a take contract. (`Q130.X9`,
    `Q061.W7`)
16. The later acts derive the base from its siege, signed: HELD rises, TAKEN works for its owner and remembers,
    RUINED falls and is a ruin with squatters, never a blank. The default read for a siege never played is written.
    (third votes s3, `Q137.W8`, `Q144.W7`, `Q121.X1`)
17. A build-up prefers bases where the family has a deed, and prosperous bases draw more. (`Q151.W3`, `Q044.W4`, `Q093.W6`)
18. A contract taken to defend a base whose day passed before the company arrived is not failed silently: it becomes
    its aftermath leg (witness or rescue) at a stated lower pay. (`Q132.P6`, `Q128.X2`, QR-J)
19. One siege in five carries a twist (the besiegers are not who the build-up said), drawn from the base's own
    history. (`Q148.W3`, `Q148.P4`, `Q148.X3`)
20. The Amalgamation does not besiege. Sieges are the Destroyers' (act 1), the factions' and the roaming parties'.
    (`Q085.P7`, canon)

21. HOW YOU HOLD BRANCHES. (folded 10/9 from QR-U) Reform is tested by defence, not peace (`Q197.W1`); the old skills come back
    (`Q197.W3`); how you win matters (`Q197.W6`); the manner of the defence produces different outcomes (`Q197.P4`);
    the second chance can be lost or betrayed (`Q197.W9`). Rule 10's HELD gains a second axis: held as themselves, or
    held hardened (burned the approach, armed the children, took the attackers' stores). Test: the two HELD states
    write different next situations for the base (`Q197.X3`), and the theme is never bypassed as a firefight
    (`Q197.X1`). Which acts count as hardened, and whether the fight board shows them, is COMBAT's call.

SHARPENED (folded 10/9 from QR-U):
- Rules 12 and 13 (a fall is shown; it opens contracts): a fall is written as what the attackers did; the player's
  failure is never the headline, even when his contract failed (`Q235.W2`).
- Rule 6 (the WAY OUT post): two known people in lethal danger at once, one save, lives here (`Q226.P1`).

## OPEN (what the library could not answer)

1. HOW OFTEN. No study gives a siege rate for a phone game of ~100 hours. Third votes s5: the hard ones late in an act.
   Suggested start for TUNING: 2 to 4 sieges per act across fourteen bases, never two build-ups visible at once.
2. CAN THE MAIN CHARACTER BE CAPTURED? `Q087.W1` and `Q110.W1` make capture a strong defeat scenario, and it would turn a
   lost siege into a rescue of the family's own. It also forces a scene on a player who did nothing (rule 32a) and
   touches the main character's rule. Default here: no capture; he is carried to a clinic. The fork is creative and
   Paolo's if he wants it.
3. AUTO-RESOLVE. When a base is besieged with no company present, the world computes the result. The library has
   no formula. FACTIONS and TUNING own it: the defender's tier, the preparation contracts done there, the attacker's
   size.
4. THE CUSTOM SETTLEMENT. QR-O's base 11 is the one the player built for. Whether it can be besieged at all, and whether
   the family may defend it without a contract, is Paolo's (it is the closest thing we have to "the player's base").
5. BB's own siege contracts (the expansion ones with siege equipment) were not verified this round; the page does not
   lean on them. FUTURE DEEPER PULL for the Battle Brothers study (#236).
