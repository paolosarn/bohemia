# QR-Q: WHAT DOES THE LATE GAME OF A LONG GAME NEED?

QUESTION: row [third shelf]. Bohemia is about 100 hours and its third act (Gen 3, the Angel era, the rocket, the Network crumbling) is the thinnest shelf in the design library. What does the 152-quest library say the late game of a long game needs: escalation, the payoff of early choices, endgame side content, and not running out of reasons to play? Cite by id.

STATUS: draft:true, research only, nothing built (rule 35). Author: QUESTS lane, writer Q, round 9/28.
FOLDED 10/9 (QR-AK): QR-U rules 13 to 17 and the sharpens of rules 2, 9 and 12, at the end of THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS, each marked in place.

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The library's loudest verdict on late games is a complaint, repeated across a dozen studies: the back half sags, thins, or solves itself (`Q050.X3`, `Q051.X4`, `Q053.X2`, `Q090.X1`, `Q091.X2`, `Q065.X1`, `Q109.X1`). The cause is almost always the same: the player has outgrown the systems (money, danger, scarcity stop mattering, `Q087.X1`, `Q126.X9`), and the content budget ran out before the end (`Q080.X4`, `Q145.X1`, `Q123.X5`). The fix the library shows is also consistent and it fits Bohemia unusually well. First, escalation must be a change of KIND, not of numbers: a new verb at every step (`Q075.W9`, `Q075.P7`), a threat that grows with what you know rather than with your level (`Q085.P7`, `Q038.W9`), and never an endless stat treadmill (`Q049.X2`, `Q050.X4`). Second, the late game is where the early game is PAID: the origin's loss returns (`Q042.W4`), one set piece reads every standing you built (`Q042.W5`, `Q006.W8`, `Q136.W10`), and people who inherited you speak of you in the past tense (`Q144.W7`). Third, side content in the last act must be connected, never a disconnected bonus dungeon (`Q069.X5`, `Q084.X1`), and it must keep its people real: a repeated contract format loses personhood fast (`Q148.X3`). Fourth, reasons to play come from what can still be LOST: a home base that can fall (`Q093.W6`), a threat that remembers (`Q048.W2`), a survival economy kept tight to the end (`Q065.P9`, `Q109.P8`). Bohemia's two locked rules make most of this structural: the three acts are open together once unlocked (rule 39c: act 1 first, the next generation unlocks later) and the future is computed from the past, so act 3 IS the payoff shelf; and THE FUTURE GOES BOTH WAYS, so a home base raided in act 2 falls in act 3, which means the late game always has something at stake. What we must add by design: act 3 contracts that pay a DIFFERENT currency than act 1 (people, parts for the rocket, standing with the returning), an assembly or roll call that reads the ledgers aloud, and an honest refusal to let batteries become meaningless.

## THE FINDINGS

### 1. THE SAG: WHAT GOES WRONG IN THE LAST THIRD

- THE LAST THIRD SAGS is its own finding in the library, not an impression. A game whose first two thirds keep momentum gives way to "a less-focused, sidequest-heavy final act" (`Q050.X3`). The same shape appears as "thin late-game content", fixed by a remake that added about 30 hours (`Q051.X4`), as "Act 1 > the rest" (`Q053.X2`), and as "the back half is rushed and everyone knows it" (`Q091.X2`).
- THE GRIND: "for the first half of the game, I was really enjoying my time... entering the back half was when things" fell apart (`Q090.X1`). The study's port says a progression built on access "still needs its late game to stop taxing you with early-game chores" (`Q090.P8`).
- THE MIDDLE-TO-END TEMPLATE: a political thriller turns into its third artifact temple (`Q080.X5`). The late game repeats a structure the player already mastered.
- THE CLIMAX ABANDONS THE GAME: the final act "devolves into corridor fighting, betraying the earlier social and horror richness" (`Q039.X4`). The lesson for us: the rocket act must stay a people-and-contracts game, not become a combat gauntlet.
- COGNITIVE LOAD: big late scenes pile up until players use a notepad (`Q054.X4`). On a phone, the late game must read its state back to the player, not ask them to remember it.
- THE BIGGEST BRANCH IS UNDERBUILT: the most consequential choice "resolves largely in epilogue text and a boss gauntlet" (`Q142.X2`), and a rocket ending can land offscreen and unresolved (`Q079.X2`). For a game whose third act is literally a rocket, `Q079.X2` is the most pointed warning in the library.

### 2. THE SOLVED ECONOMY: WHY THE LATE GAME STOPS MATTERING

- "The back half loses its tension" is the central critique of one study: by midgame you can "essentially ignore resource management" (`Q065.X1`). Its port, banked hard: "SUSTAIN the scarcity/tension to the END" (`Q065.P9`).
- "Within hours, money, energy and condition, your three stats, take care of themselves" (`Q109.X1`). The port calls a survival game whose survival solves itself "a spent premise" (`Q109.P8`).
- Survival systems trivialized by cheap passive abilities (`Q087.X1`).
- A toll of twenty thousand gold "is not a cost by act 3" (`Q126.X9`). The exact risk for batteries: a late price named in act 1 money is no price at all.
- Optimal paths flatten the ethics into a spreadsheet (`Q029.X1`), and the economy can overpower the theme for power players (`Q149.X1`).
- NG+ and carry-over power trivialize challenge (`Q050.X4`). Our flip between acts is a kind of carry-over: gear and batteries earned in one act must not make another act's fights free.
- WHAT THIS MEANS FOR BOHEMIA: act 3 is a boom (the future gets better), and a boom economy is exactly where batteries go soft. Act 3 contracts must pay in things a boom cannot print: rocket parts, people who agree to go, standing with the ones coming back, a fallen base retaken. Batteries stay, but the scarce thing changes.

### 3. ESCALATION THAT CHANGES KIND, NOT NUMBERS

- The best escalation ladder in the library is "absurd and earned": each step is a set piece with a different verb (`Q075.W9`), and the port says so directly: "each step a set-piece with a DIFFERENT VERB" (`Q075.P7`). Our 60 mini bosses each hand over a verb; the late game should spend verbs the player has only just earned.
- Escalation upholstered in routine: every increment is a verb you were taught, so the unthinkable arrives via the ordinary (`Q133.W2`).
- The gradual escalation: helpful, then controlling, then murderous, so slowly you cannot name the turn (`Q036.W4`, `Q036.P4`).
- The mundane hook that becomes cosmic dread (`Q009.W2`, `Q009.P3`). Late contracts can open as ordinary jobs and land on the Network's collapse.
- The world has an immune system: the port says threat escalation should be a feature of the world, not a difficulty curve (`Q085.P7`). The fanatic army guarding the secret is made of pilgrims who came looking for it (`Q085.W7`, `Q085.P5`). This is our Amalgamation's rule (threat scales with proximity to the secret) already, and it answers `Q049.X2`: an endless scaling treadmill discourages the long player.
- Consequence with a hunter: a taboo act sends scaling bounty hunters over time (`Q038.W9`). For us: roaming parties on the map that remember an act 1 deed.
- The escalating rulebook: the system's growth is the difficulty curve (`Q031.W4`, `Q031.P4`), with the warning that rhythm and human intrusions keep it from boring (`Q031.P9`).
- A countdown can be an emotional instrument in the endgame (`Q011.W5` via `Q011.P6`), but the soft clock that punishes dallying (`Q006.P5`) must be stated aloud and generous, per our phone rules (QR-G line 3).

### 4. THE PAYOFF OF EARLY CHOICES

- THE ORIGIN'S TRAGEDY PAYS OFF LATE: the beginning's nemesis or loss returns as a main-plot payoff (`Q042.W4`, `Q042.P3`). Its flaw is the warning: origin content is front-loaded and uneven, some origins get late callbacks and some do not (`Q042.X1`), and the port says sustain callbacks across the whole game for every background (`Q042.P8`).
- THE CHOICE-CULMINATION SET PIECE: one late scene where the whole game's reputation and alliances pay out at once (`Q042.W5`, `Q042.P4`). The Landsmeet study turns this into our act finales as assemblies (`Q136.P2`), where "the room counts aloud" (`Q136.W10`), with a named abstainer (`Q136.P4`) because a roll call with no abstentions is cleaner than any parliament (`Q136.X10`).
- THE ROLL CALL: end by tallying the survivors, forcing the player to face who their preparation saved (`Q006.W8`, `Q006.P7`). Assign allies to roles in the final push (`Q006.P3`) and stage their deaths as legible consequences, not random (`Q006.P4`).
- THE ODDS ARE THE INVESTIGATION: everything you did earlier silently stacks the climax's checks (`Q005.W8`). Choice reads the whole playthrough (`Q016.W3`).
- DELAYED, DISPLACED CONSEQUENCE: the payoff lands quests later in a different quest (`Q004.W5`, `Q004.P1`). Many choices pay off hours later (`Q024.W8`). The payoff can land a thousand years later in another game (`Q080.W9`, `Q080.P7`); ours lands a generation later in another act.
- THE WORLD REMEMBERS ACROSS GAMES: dozens of choice flags import (`Q042.W9`, `Q042.P6`). Inherited NPCs speak in the past tense, "the cheapest possible reactivity" (`Q144.W7`).
- YOU LIVE WITH BEING MAYBE WRONG: the accused and their kin carry your choice for decades (`Q026.W6`).
- THE PERSISTENT, UNFORGETTING SAVE: an atrocity sticks and resists laundering (`Q028.W4`, `Q028.P4`). This matters for the flip: a player who sees act 3 cannot erase an act 1 deed, only add to it (QR-D's rule).
- THE LEVEL REMEMBERS YOUR PREPARATION (`Q116.W9`); one early window changes who stands beside you at the end (`Q116.P5`).
- The honest warning: "only slightly" is the whole problem. When a guide says the outcome barely matters, the plant was wasted (`Q119.X1`). An accession fork that is weightless downstream is the same failure (`Q152.X2`), and so are hidden flags set hours earlier that nobody can trace (`Q125.X6`).
- The budget warning: a conquest that promises a hundred world states reads back the big axes loudly and the fine grain thinly (`Q145.X1`); reactivity is expensive (`Q042.X2`). The port: BUDGET reactivity so the endgame pays off (`Q025.P9`).

### 5. HOW THE PAYOFF IS SHOWN

- The payoff can be twelve words answering an offhand comment (`Q118.W2`, `Q118.P3`). Short, spoken, from a known face.
- The reunion plays while you keep working (`Q108.W7`, `Q108.P3`). For a contracts game: the payoff can play in the corner of an ordinary job.
- The settlement testifies: residents reference the siege afterwards (`Q151.P5`).
- The reactive voicemail epilogue: tailored per-person messages, cheap and high impact (`Q063.W9`, `Q063.P6`). Companions serve from love or fear and the epilogue shows which (`Q025.W9`, `Q025.P7`).
- The world's indifference to the triumph is a flaw: the escape completes and nothing marks it (`Q146.X1`).
- The payoff becomes the new home: the reward for the arc is the home you rebuild with it (`Q075.W10`). For act 3: the reward for a contract can be a home base standing again.
- Bittersweet, not triumphant (`Q062.W9`); the writing is the payoff (`Q062.W7`).

### 6. ENDGAME SIDE CONTENT THAT STAYS CONNECTED

- The post-game is disconnected: a bonus dungeon "far less relevant to the overall plot" (`Q069.X5`). The main quest as a diversion with the real plot post-credits (`Q084.X1`) and the port: don't gate the theme behind post-game content (`Q084.P9`).
- The best optional threads pay into endings: a side quest that is an ending (`Q023.P7`); a patient optional thread seeded across the long arc with a transcendent payoff (`Q062.P2`), with the warning not to hide the crown jewel from most players (`Q062.P8`).
- The geographically buried quest: late gating means most players never meet the tenderest quest (`Q138.X10`). Act 3 side content must be where act 3 players already travel.
- Repeatable format, diminishing personhood: across dozens of contracts the twist becomes the expected shape (`Q148.X3`). Late contracts need plain jobs with real people, and a rationed twist.
- The base is a hostage: days of work and your heart pounds returning to it, knowing everything could be lost (`Q093.W6`). This is THE FUTURE GOES BOTH WAYS as a feeling.
- The antagonist is the people you saved (`Q107.W1`), and the murder is downstream of your own escalation (`Q107.W6`, `Q107.P4`): late side content can be the consequences of early help.
- The person you save becomes a supply line (`Q119.P6`). In act 3, the people the family helped in act 1 and 2 can be the ones who supply the rocket.
- The retroactive payoff: paid for map locations you already discovered (`Q101.P6`).

### 7. NOT RUNNING OUT OF REASONS

- A patient offer that improves across generations: every generation the offer stays refused, the terms get better (`Q126.P7`). A reason to come back to a person in act 3.
- The enemy remembers and references your wins, losses and methods (`Q048.W2`, `Q048.P2`).
- Doomed runs become testament runs: endgame play reorganizes around dying well, banking and bequeathing (`Q144.W6`). For us: the Gen 3 heir leaves for good, so the late game is about what the family leaves behind.
- Easy to complete, hard to master: the bonus is the real game, self-imposed difficulty (`Q007.W2`).
- The emotional payoff of comprehension: the finale lands because you understand (`Q018.W9`, `Q018.P8`), and escalating endings map depth of engagement to outcome (`Q020.W7`, `Q020.P7`).
- Choice timing determines the ending (`Q050.W3`, `Q050.P3`), and quality over quantity in endings (`Q050.P9`); seven endings sharing one mission read as swapped epilogues (`Q102.X6`).
- A twist must change the gameplay or the peak sags (`Q033.X2`, `Q033.W10`).
- The stories a system generates are not the experience it delivers (`Q094.X2`): we cannot count on emergence alone to fill 30 late hours; authored contracts and main beats carry the spine (`Q061.X1`, `Q061.P9`).
- The ruling phase becomes a chore: the endgame of a rise from nobody turns into tedious management (`Q061.X3`). The rocket as a city-builder puzzle is exactly this risk.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS

1. ACT 3 PAYS IN A DIFFERENT SCARCITY. Every act 3 contract's pay names at least one thing a boom cannot print (a rocket part, a person who agrees to go, standing with the returning, a base retaken) alongside batteries. Test: no act 3 contract pays batteries only. (`Q065.P9`, `Q126.X9`, `Q109.P8`)
2. EVERY LATE ESCALATION STEP ADDS A VERB OR A PLACE, NEVER ONLY A NUMBER. Test: an act 3 fight or contract that is an act 1 fight with bigger stats does not ship. (`Q075.P7`, `Q049.X2`, `Q080.X5`)
3. THREAT FOLLOWS THE SECRET, NOT THE LEVEL. The Amalgamation's pressure in act 3 scales with proximity to the secret; roaming parties scale with deeds they remember. Test: no global enemy level-up. (`Q085.P7`, `Q038.W9`, `Q048.W2`)
4. EVERY ACT 3 DESIGN READS AT LEAST ONE EARLIER LEDGER LINE, AND HAS A FULL DEFAULT READ. Test: grep the design for its read and its default. (`Q042.P8`, `Q121.X1`, `Q096.X1`)
5. A FALLEN HOME BASE IS CONTENT, NOT A GAME OVER. Any home base raided in an earlier act appears fallen in act 3 with at least one contract that is about it (retake, rescue, or witness). (`Q093.W6`, `Q075.W10`, `Q146.W1`)
6. ONE ROLL CALL PER ACT FINALE, SPOKEN, WITH AN ABSTAINER. (`Q136.W10`, `Q136.P4`, `Q006.W8`)
7. THE LATE GAME READS ITS OWN STATE BACK. The phone feed in the city view names what changed; no late scene asks the player to remember an early one unaided. (`Q054.X4`, `Q146.X1`)
8. NO BONUS DUNGEON. Every act 3 side contract touches the rocket, the Network's crumbling, or a family thread. (`Q069.X5`, `Q084.P9`)
9. THE ROCKET LANDS ON SCREEN. Whatever the ending, the ground-side consequence is shown by people the player knows. (`Q079.X2`, `Q142.X2`)
10. THE TWIST IS RATIONED IN ACT 3 TOO. At least two of every three act 3 contracts are plain jobs. (`Q148.X3`)
11. NO COMBAT GAUNTLET CLIMAX. The last hours stay contracts, people and choices; fights are on the fight board as everywhere else, never a corridor of them. (`Q039.X4`, `Q017.P9`)
12. THE MAIN CHARACTER NEVER DIES; OTHERS DO, LEGIBLY. Company deaths in late set pieces follow rule 36 (20% dead, else 30 to 40 days hurt) and are readable consequences of assignment. (`Q006.P4`)

13. THE ROCKET IS A FAITH NOBODY CAN CHECK. (folded 10/9 from QR-U) The destination is unverifiable (`Q190.W1`); the launch is real, the
    dream achievable and fatal, not a fizzle (`Q190.W5`); faith aimed at what you cannot verify before you commit
    (`Q190.W6`); the throttle on a doomed faith (`Q190.P1`); one believer is known (`Q190.P4`). Test: the launch is not
    the path that simply pays best (`Q190.X1`, QR-G line 30).
14. THE ONE-WAY TRIP IS A GOODBYE, AND THE GOODBYE IS CORRECT. (folded 10/9 from QR-U) The closeness is the goodbye (`Q188.W1`); leaving
    is right, not a problem to solve (`Q188.W3`); even the happy path carries the cost (`Q188.W8`, `Q188.P2`); saving
    your home costs your home (`Q228.W2`), paid in belonging (`Q228.W8`). Test: the exile honours the home's logic in a
    line, or it reads as cruelty (`Q228.X1`).
15. THE NETWORK IS A PARADISE WHOSE EXIT IS DEATH. (folded 10/9 from QR-U) The paradise is the prison (`Q212.W1`); liberation and
    mercy-killing are one act (`Q212.W2`); the residents are neighbours, named (`Q212.W9`, port `Q212.P1`); rewriting a
    mind keeps the body and kills the self (`Q191.W3`); the consumed testify in their own voices (`Q156.P3`); the
    archive of the dead bargains (`Q175.W1`). Test: the Network crumbling is these people, never a boss.
16. MASTERY IS NOT THE REWARD. (folded 10/9 from QR-U) Total accomplishment as its own ending (`Q216.W1`); the master at the end of the
    player's road reports it hollow (`Q216.W6`); a refusal needs a felt consequence (`Q216.X2`, `Q216.P3`). Test: a late
    fight the company wins easily is allowed to say something.
17. THE LATE TREASURE MAY BE THE TRAP. (folded 10/9 from QR-U) The fortune is the thing that kills you (`Q213.W1`); letting go is the only
    win (`Q213.W2`). Test: survives optimisation, a partial carry is still a choice with a cost (`Q213.X1`).

SHARPENED (folded 10/9 from QR-U):
- Rule 2 (every late step adds a verb or a place): read with rule 16, an easy late win says something.
- Rule 9 (the rocket lands on screen): read with rule 13.
- Rule 12 (others die legibly): choose which loss (`Q173.W10`); some late payoffs are unverifiable bets with the
  world at stake (`Q229.W5`).

## OPEN

- The library has almost no long game with THREE eras open together; its closest cases are sequels and generational fold studies (`Q042.W9`, `Q080.W9`, `Q144.W7`). How often a player flips between acts in a 100-hour game, and whether act 3 gets played "last" by most people anyway, is a playtest question.
- How the rocket as a city-builder puzzle keeps from becoming the ruling chore (`Q061.X3`) is a design question for RUN and ECONOMY, not answerable here.
- What the Moon itself is (and whether LIBERATE, RESPECT, BECOME are played or shown) is Paolo's.
- How much a fallen home base can come back (retaken fully, partly, never) needs a number from TUNING.
