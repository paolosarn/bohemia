## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Moving the door was not enough, because the words did not move with it. Measured over both shelves, the lines a company
member says at the fire were hall talk: what, where and how much. Of 70 lines, 13 told a story; every veteran talked money
in their own mouth and eleven of twelve ended on it. Real people at a real fire talk the other way: in Wiessner's 174
recorded Ju/'hoansi conversations, about four in five firelit talks were stories, while daytime talk was about a third
economics and a third complaints and gossip. So the camp door now has a shape of its own, proposed as checklist line 33:
the first of the three lines is a STORY (a past moment with a person in it), the second is the THING on the ground and
the PLACE, the third is the ASK; the PRICE is on the button, never in the mouth; a friend is not haggled with and pays no
advance. All twenty follower jobs are rewritten to that shape, the stranger keeps the hall alone, and the thing sits in
the camp picture for nights before the ask. The line-by-line pass over the tenth shelf found what QR-AT found on the
ninth: the shelf's own claim at birth was wrong. All 20 failed at least one line; 18 of 20 "priced refusals" were the
free no moved after the yes, five of the seven dead-follower jobs contradicted the very job they claim to land, and the
money tilted the fork on nine. All twenty are fixed in place and now pass all 32 lines and line 33.

## 1. THE FINDING THAT PROVES US WRONG (measured against the repo)

QR-AU's correction was a door: "a company member's own job is offered at the night camp, by their mouth, with their thing
on the ground." The door was right. But a door is only where the words come in; the words were never checked.

Counted this round, line by line, before any edit:
- QD-AQ01 to AQ20, the lines the member says on the hall card: 34 lines. 10 tell a story (a past moment with a person in
  it: "She ran that clinic with tape and prayers", "My father heard everything through that dish"). 24 are the job:
  where, what, and five of them the price ("15 batteries", "It's what I have"). In act 1 not one member line told a
  story: all seven act-1 asks were pure business.
- QD-AU01 to AU20, the veterans' own camp asks, written FOR the camp by QR-AU: 12 designs, 36 lines. 3 tell a story
  ("I kept one thing off each of them", "I left it up", "She said she'd do it someday"). All twelve talk money in their own mouth
  ("I'll pay you out of my share. 12 batteries", "I'll give you a tenth", "I'm not paying. I'm asking"), and eleven end on it.
- Together: 13 story lines in 70 (19 percent). The shelf written for the camp scored lower (8 percent) than the shelf
  written for the hall (29 percent).

The real world says the fire is the opposite room. Wiessner recorded 174 day and night conversations among the
Ju/'hoansi of Botswana and Namibia (only talks with five or more people). Daytime talk was close to a third economic
(hunting, foraging, trade) and about a third complaints, criticism and gossip, with joking about a sixth; at night, by
the fire, about 81 percent of the long conversations were stories, many about people not present, and the paper's
argument is that firelight talk is where trust, reputation and the knowledge of absent people are built [web: the
abstract, Scientific American, the Namibian, UNCG; the daytime story share was not found and is not used]. A friend at
the fire who opens with a price is day talk at night. The library says the same in its own words: the client as a
vending machine is a face with a fee and no want of its own (`Q236.X3`); a scripted ask that fires "whether or not you
have ever looked at him" is a card with a face (`Q108.X1`, `Q108.P8`); the sympathetic explanation must arrive BEFORE
the damning fact (`Q125.P25`, `Q125.W4`); the night camp is where the Zone's people "eat, joke and play music", not
where they bargain (`Q085.W2`); the bond is built on ordinary nights, and the ask comes out of them (`Q063.W2`, `Q063.P2`,
`Q108.W1`, `Q108.P2`).

So the finding: QR-AU moved the member's job to the camp and kept the hall's talk. A card read aloud at a fire is still a
card. The correction is line 33 (section 3), and every camp ask on both shelves is rewritten to it.

## 8. THE THREE FAILURES, BY SHAPE

1. THE NO MOVED AFTER THE YES (18 of 20, line 12). QR-AU's section 2 rule 9 said "every veteran's job has a D road and a
   written fate for a lost fight ... this shelf writes both at birth." It wrote the second and not the first: eighteen D
   roads were the free no relabelled ("NOT TODAY: he keeps his 12", "LET IT GO", "NOT THIS ACT EITHER", "WALK PAST").
   A priced refusal is a smaller job that still finishes the contract at a stated price (QR-G OPEN 4, `Q131.X7`,
   `Q134.X2`, `Q134.X10`, `Q139.P2`, `Q075.X4`, `Q103.X1`); a no after the yes with nothing paid is a drop, and the
   library's worst offender is the refusal that is only an exit (`Q131.X7`). The fix is one road each: the part the
   player will not do is named, the client does the rest or less, and the fee is a half or less (walk him to the yard's
   edge, the range sign two hundred metres short, the broth and not the pozole). This also changed QD-AU20: since the D
   roads now finish their jobs, only the free no at the fire leaves a veteran's job open for an heir.
2. THE LANDING THAT CONTRADICTS ITS PLANT (5 of the 7 dead followers' jobs, line 18). The tenth shelf was written as the
   eighth shelf's second half and did not read it: the driver's voice sent the company to "Garza's yard" with a truck's
   keys, a debt QD-AQ03 never planted; the child returned the ring to a MOTHER, and QD-AQ04's claimant is the sister;
   the clinic's living medic was "the apprentice from QD-AQ05", who is the act-2 medic the design needs dead; the tape
   shop's claimant was a son, and QD-AQ06 planted a niece; the madrina's was a grandson, and QD-AQ20 planted a
   granddaughter. Six of the seven had no read for a plant never set, which the three acts at once make the common case,
   not the edge (rule 31: a player can stand in act 2 before act 1 has killed anyone). The library's rule is blunt: a
   promissory note across acts must land what was planted and read cleanly when it was not (`Q121.X1`, `Q096.X1`,
   `Q022.X2`, `Q108.X3`). Each now names the plant, lands it, and says "this never fires" when the plant is not set.
3. THE MONEY AT THE FORK, AGAIN (9, lines 21 and 30). Four of the nine were QR-AU rule 3 broken in the same stroke: the
   fee was a share of what a stranger paid (half of Arce's wages, a quarter of the paymaster's, a tenth of the sale, the
   ring night's purse), so the road that took the stranger's money paid more than the road that gave something back. The
   design even wrote the tilt down ("A pays least"). A share is the haggle wearing a friend's face (`Q236.X1`). Now each
   is one fee from the member's own pocket on every road that counts as done (`Q190.X1`, `Q191.X1`, `Q164.W4`, `Q108.X2`,
   `Q130.X9`). The other five: a hush bribe shown but not counted, the living road paid bowls, the recruiting road paid
   four free boys (people are pay, QR-AT's reading of AQ07), the story road paid goods, one road of three paid at all.
