# QR-M: WHAT THE LIBRARY SAYS ABOUT THE COMPANY, THE PEOPLE WHO TRAVEL AND FIGHT WITH YOU

QUESTS lane (chat 19), research only, round 9/28. Row [company voices]. Writer M. Nothing here is built (rule 35).
FOLDED 10/9 (QR-AK): QR-U rules 16 and 17 and the sharpens of rules 1, 6, 8, 10 and 14, at the end of THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS, each marked in place.
Written for PEOPLE (who owns the person, the injured, the dead), WORDS (the lines), RUN (the event and offer screens),
COMBAT [gambits] (where the company's behaviour lives) and DYNASTY (the company across the three acts). Every id
below resolves in records/BOHEMIA_QUESTBOOK_LAW_INDEX.json. Battle Brothers facts are from
reference/library/battle_brothers/06_BACKGROUNDS.md, 08_CONTRACTS_EVENTS.md and 10_UI_AND_FEEL.md (recall volumes).
Rulings this page is written under: rule 35 (declining is free), rule 36 (struck down: 20% dead, else a debilitating
injury of 30 to 40 game days that leaves a permanent mark), and the 9/28 ruling that contracts are Battle Brothers
OFFER SCREENS in a settlement's hall and road events are the Battle Brothers EVENT SCREEN (a face, two or three
choices).

QUESTION: What does the 152-quest library say about the COMPANY, the Battle Brothers-style roster that travels with
the player, fights, gets hurt, and sometimes dies? Specifically: the company quarrel; the companion who disagrees with
a choice; loyalty WITHOUT a meter; a companion's own ask (a personal contract); how a companion reacts to a contract
you take or decline (declining stays free); grief when one dies; what they say on the road; how an automated
companion earns a personality in a phone game with little text; and how the company carries across the three acts.

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The library's best companion writing (Baldur's Gate 2, Mass Effect 2, Cyberpunk's Judy, Shadowheart) and its best
roster systems (Darkest Dungeon, Kenshi, RimWorld, the Suicide Mission) agree on one thing and disagree on another.
They agree that the company is PEOPLE WHO ACT, not a morale bar: they have their own agendas and refuse to be servants
(`Q043.W1`), they react to each other and not only to you (`Q043.W2`), and virtue works best when it is simulated as
behaviour, "party members who QUIT", instead of scored (`Q083.W6`). They disagree on the meter, and the library's own
flaws settle it: every approval number in the corpus eventually fights the character it measures (`Q076.X3`), turns a
deliberation into a min-max surface (`Q132.X8`), pays points for a hug (`Q125.X9`), or freezes a relationship into a
binary fuse (`Q137.X2`). So Bohemia's company has NO loyalty meter; loyalty shows as what a person DOES in the fight
and at the fire, which on a phone with little text is also the cheapest place to show it. Attachment is manufactured
mostly by labour, by the hours you kept someone alive (`Q151.W9`, `Q055.W9`), and made specific by a past shown in one
glance (Battle Brothers' backgrounds; `Q045.W5`). Grief must be a receipt, not a dice roll: the death names its cause
(`Q137.W1`), the body carries no loot (`Q125.X8`), and the world, the company and the feed notice (`Q078.X2`,
`Q129.X9`). Rule 36 is the library's own recommendation made law: rare death, common and visible injury, a body that
remembers (`Q055.W4`, `Q041.X1`). And across three acts, the company is the family we did not choose: persistent
anchors cross the generations (`Q067.W6`), the heir can fight beside the next generation (`Q069.W3`), and a single
choice in act 1 decides who stands beside you at the end (`Q116.W10`). The one line for the builders: the company is
known by what it does, never by a number, and a no to a contract is never theirs to comment on.

## THE FINDINGS

### 1. The company is people who act, and the library's first failure is a blank roster
- The root of all companion craft is autonomy: companions have opinions, refuse to be servants, and pursue their own
  agendas; "you're one of the crew, not a puppeteer" (`Q043.W1`). Baldur's Gate 2 is named in the library as the root
  that Mass Effect 2 and Dragon Age built on (`Q043.P1`).
- The company is a bundle of stories, not stat-sticks: each member has a personal arc to invest in (`Q043.W7`),
  officers are slow-burn wells drawn out over voyages (`Q066.W6`).
- The loudest failure in the whole generational cluster is a roster with no people: Phantasy Star III asks you to
  marry and raise children with party members who have "virtually no dialogue or characterization", and two of them
  "never spoke again" after joining (`Q067.X2`). Its lesson is direct for a game that runs 100 hours on a phone: a
  company of silent bodies does not carry three acts.
- The opposite failure is volume. Hades' "noticed" feeling needs a mountain of lines (`Q047.X1`), and the Nemesis
  system's imitators felt like "a shallow puddle" because they kept the procedural skeleton and skipped the authored
  flesh (`Q048.X2`). The fix is identity first: define who the person is, then make the weapon, outfit and voice
  support it (`Q048.W5`).
- Battle Brothers solves this in one glance: a BACKGROUND (Beggar, Hedge Knight, Cultist) makes the roster a cast at a
  glance, events pick men by background, and "a man's death is a story because his past was shown"
  (06_BACKGROUNDS.md). Ours, already on PEOPLE's row: what they did the day the money died (lineman, nurse, dealer,
  casino floor, delivery, ex-con).
- People talk about what matters to THEM, not your quest (`Q083.W2`), and character is best established in staging,
  not dialogue: Yennefer is introduced already winning a bar fight she did not need help with (`Q095.W3`).
- Attachment is manufactured by labour. Players care about unauthored characters "because you LEVELED them from
  nobody to hero" (`Q055.W9`), and in Kenshi "unwritten recruits become devastating losses purely through invested
  hours" (`Q151.W9`). This is the strongest single finding for a Battle Brothers roster: most of our company will be
  generated, and the library says generated people can break a player's heart if the player kept them alive.
- Specificity is the soul of it: who died, how, who grieves, why this one snapped (`Q045.W5`).

### 2. Loyalty without a meter: what the library says replaces the number
The library argues against the meter from inside the games that used one.
- The meter fights the arc. Shadowheart's approval rewards the very conditioning her story wants her to escape, so an
  optimizing player is nudged to keep her caged (`Q076.X3`).
- The meter turns advice into a shop. At Redcliffe the companions' advocacy becomes "a min-max surface (who to
  please)" (`Q132.X8`).
- The meter prices kindness. The Paragon interrupt over a friend's father's corpse pays five points for comforting
  her; "the game turned compassion into a pickup" (`Q125.X9`). The library prints it as the single best argument for
  our no-karma-bar law (`Q125.P17`), and the same flaw decides Legion's choice by build (`Q121.X2`).
- The meter nags. Viconia "bitch[es] and moan[s] constantly" when reputation rises, contradicting her written
  personality; reactions must stay in character (`Q043.X2`, `Q043.P9`).
- The meter is a fuse. Mass Effect 2's loyalty is binary and forever: one mishandled argument flips a flag no later
  devotion resets (`Q137.X2`), and the model gives no partial credit to "argued-with-then-reconciled", which is where
  "most real relationships live" (`Q137.X9`). The library's own fix is a gradient with a scar (`Q137.P4`).
- The meter hides the rules. The Suicide Mission's categories are unstated and "the wiki is mandatory" (`Q137.X1`).
- Silent state is the worst of it: romances in Baldur's Gate 2 "silently end" on fragile triggers (`Q043.X3`).

What replaces it, in the library's own best cases:
- Behaviour. Ultima VII has "no karma bar, just people who saw what you did, party members who QUIT, and companions
  who eventually ATTACK you" (`Q083.W6`). Arcanum's reputation "is a memory, not a bar": strangers cite your specific
  deeds, and companions join or refuse by what you actually did (`Q086.W8`).
- Loyalty as armour. The best mechanical statement in the library is Mass Effect 2's: being known by your commander
  converts to survival odds, "loyalty is armor, literally" (`Q137.W3`), with the math invisible but fair (`Q006.W7`).
  Its flaw is that the rule was hidden (`Q137.X1`); its truth is that loyalty belongs in the fight.
- Two axes, not one. People can respect you more for defying them (`Q024.W4`); favour and wrath accumulate
  independently (`Q025.W1`); companions serve from love or from fear, and it shows in how they end (`Q025.W9`).
- A values test, not a counter. Judy's bond is "earned by consistently being someone they'd love (a values-test,
  not a flirt-counter)" (`Q063.W5`). Loyalty is earned, fragile and losable, with upkeep (`Q006.W3`).
- Patience. Pushing a companion too early makes it worse (`Q076.W6`); the winning move is permission, not
  persuasion: "make your own choice" (`Q076.W5`).

For Bohemia this lands as one sentence: a company member's loyalty is shown by where he stands, whom he covers, and
what he will not do, all visible on the fight board and at the fire, and never by a number on his page.

### 3. The quarrel: the company reacts to itself
- The core innovation of the whole companion lineage is that companions react to EACH OTHER, independent of you
  (`Q043.W2`), and rivalries have "mechanical teeth": wrong pairings argue, come to blows, or make one leave
  (`Q043.W3`). Mass Effect 2 stages the clash between two allies with no time to repair (`Q006.W3`).
- The best staging of a disagreement in the library is two of your best people arguing in your server room while you
  are elsewhere, "Tali and Legion are having a heated discussion in the AI core" (`Q121.W9`). The quarrel happens with
  or without you; the player walks into it.
- The best deliberation scene has "every option [with] a living voice arguing it in the room" (`Q132.N1`).
- The failure is the chorus. When every companion disapproves together, "a chorus with no dissent is not a
  conscience, it's a laugh track" (`Q126.X5`); the fix is one companion who wants you to do the thing the others
  hate (`Q126.P40`). The narration and the chorus never grade the choice (`Q126.X4`).
- The other failure is silence between people: a game about a community whose "characters don't interact with one
  another much, meaning there's no strains on relationships or loyalties" (`Q109.X4`), and a Kenshi freehold whose
  recruits have "no reaction to any of it" (`Q151.X3`).
- Stress spreads. One afflicted hero stresses the others and "one broken mind can doom the party" (`Q041.W3`); in
  Dwarf Fortress one death can cascade grief into a spiral that ends the fort (`Q045.W4`).
- There is no universally correct side: the stance that wins one person loses another (`Q024.W3`).

### 4. The companion who disagrees with a contract you took, and the one who says nothing about a no
- The strongest disagreement in the library is a red line: at the Landsmeet, one choice makes Alistair leave
  "permanently, furiously, mid-scene; no charm, no history, no approval total un-writes" it (`Q136.N2`), ported as
  one companion per generation carrying "an un-charmable limit" (`Q136.P3`). Dragon Age generalizes it: approval
  gates who stays and who leaves (`Q042.W10`).
- A disagreement can be about the best thing you did. In Cyberpunk's sacrifice ending your friend calls the noblest
  act "sick" and threatens revenge (`Q102.W8`). Your friends do not read your intentions.
- A companion may ABSTAIN. Legion, on his own species' fate, "clearly doesn't have a preference at all" (`Q121.W3`);
  the flip side, a companion loyal whatever you choose, is flagged as double-edged (`Q121.X3`).
- A companion can carry the fight so you do not have to: in Deus Ex you can hide in a closet and "Paul will
  eliminate agents by himself" (`Q116.W8`). A disagreeing member can ALSO decline a part of the job himself.
- And a companion stays only if you honoured their values (`Q063.W3`).
- The no is not theirs to punish. Benching a companion makes Nalia start crying, and the library calls party-cap
  friction "guilt-inducing" (`Q043.X1`); the port is "a home base where benched survivors wait happily, no
  guilt-laden busywork" (`Q043.P9`). The corpus-wide lesson on refusal: a refusal must be a path, never a punishment
  (`Q084.X5`), and the strongest choice most players make, looking at a price and walking, is invisible in most games
  (`Q131.X7`). Under rule 35 that invisibility is what we WANT for a declined contract: nobody in the company says a
  word about a job you did not take.
- The fee is settled before anyone knows what the job is (`Q148.W1`), so a companion's later objection is never about
  money. Inside a taken job, the priced refusal (`Q139.P2`) is where a disagreeing companion's voice belongs.

### 5. The companion's own ask
- Side content equals survival: Mass Effect 2's loyalty missions are "optional in name but load-bearing for the
  ending" (`Q006.W2`), and bonding unlocks exclusive content (`Q043.W6`).
- The best-built ask in the library comes from someone who never asked for anything. Sergiu stands outside your booth
  for 24 days, then asks one favour (`Q108.W1`). The ask lands because of the silence before it.
- The ask is triggered by the world, not by a menu: Shadowheart's secrets surface "from contact with things that
  hurt", a statue, a rescued child (`Q076.W4`). For us: a companion's ask fires when the company's route passes a place
  from their background.
- The choice is bound to a person's soul: the fork decides who the companion becomes (`Q016.W2`); the ceiling is a
  companion so real you choose the ending for them (`Q063.W1`).
- Each companion is a face of the theme: vanity, revenge, appetite (`Q072.W5`); in Dead Money "every companion is a
  different way of not letting go" (`Q105.W6`); companions as thesis-carriers (`Q064.W6`). Ours has one theme
  handed to it: TRUE FAMILY, costly and mortal, against the counterfeit family that loses nothing.
- Denial can absolve you and be worse: "I understand. Please tell Sergiu I will always love him" (`Q108.W6`). And he
  can let you keep the locket (`Q108.W4`). The library's gentlest cruelty is a person who does not blame you.
- The kid who triages himself, "aid the other children first", is the model for a companion's ask that is about
  someone else (`Q113.W6`).

### 6. Grief: the death that is a receipt
- Death with dignity: permanent and legible, "staged as the character failing at a specific job, not random"
  (`Q006.W5`). Every death is a receipt that "names its cause in player-history" (`Q137.W1`), and the port makes the
  receipt the funeral rite (`Q137.P3`).
- The ceremony must match the arithmetic. The Suicide Mission's dead "resolve as brief holo-plaques; the funeral budget
  is one scene for up to a dozen dead" (`Q137.X5`). Christine in Dead Money "simply vanishes", and "an offscreen goodbye
  is a bug in an emotional system" (`Q105.X5`).
- The world must notice. After Tenpenny, "companions don't comment; the world doesn't reference it. The loudest
  silence in the game" (`Q129.X9`). Skingrad has no funeral and "no memory organ" (`Q130.X10`); "no one ever finds
  out" undercuts the weight (`Q131.X2`); the moral centre who does not care about twelve deaths breaks the scene
  (`Q078.X2`).
- No loot in a scene with a named body. Mass Effect harvests 3,000 credits from the room where your friend's father
  died thirty seconds before his last message (`Q125.X7`); the law: no loot where the player knows the body's name
  (`Q125.X8`). For a Battle Brothers roster this is sharp: the dead member's gear is not "dropped", it is handed on
  by a person, at the grave, or it stays with him.
- Grief that is dice is not grief. Darkest Dungeon's cascades can feel "PUNISHING and unfair" when a bad-luck streak
  wipes a party "through no misplay" (`Q041.X1`); RimWorld's Randy produces "unavoidable sacrifices" (`Q044.X1`). Rule
  36 answers this with its 20%: the death is rare and the injury common, and the roll only fires on a struck-down
  member, which the player's formation choices produced.
- Heroes are fragile and grieved (`Q041.W4`); a roll call at the end makes you face who your preparation saved
  (`Q006.W8`). Grief turns flat when the estate is tedium: "death then reads as losing PROGRESS toward a spreadsheet
  rather than losing a person" (`Q144.X2`).
- Community is load-bearing: survivors telling stories and caring for each other mitigates trauma (`Q030.W8`).

### 7. The injured (rule 36): the body remembers, and the injured stay in the company
- Kenshi's per-limb consequence: injury "is persistent/visible + reshapes what a character can DO", and the body
  remembers (`Q055.W4`), ported as persistent, visible bodily consequence, with respect (`Q055.P5`).
- In Kenshi companions exist BECAUSE of the bleeding system: a downed character "is likely to bleed out... unless there
  is someone to provide first aid, which is why it is wise to acquire a companion" (`Q110.W5`). The company is the
  medical system. For us, medicine is a currency: who carries whom is a real cost.
- Recovery is a limited, budgeted resource (`Q041.W5`), but rotation can become busywork (`Q041.X2`). A 30 to 40 day
  injury in map days, on a map where a trip is days, is long enough to matter and short enough not to be a bench.
- Traits from injury must humanize with respect, never punch down (`Q049.X4`).
- Defeat continues instead of ending: losing produces a new state, not a load screen (`Q146.W1`). An injured member is
  that rule at human scale.

### 8. What they say on the road, with very little text
- Ambient banter is "our cheapest, highest-impact characterization tool" (`Q043.W4`, `Q043.P3`).
- The best line in the library about a character is twelve words answering a comment about the temperature: "It's
  hot as hell in here." / "You see it too? For me, it's always like this." No music sting, no zoom (`Q118.P3`). This is
  the exact size of a Bohemia road line: under a dozen words, answering something ordinary, carrying the one wrong
  detail.
- The turn of a 60-hour game can be a quiet conversation (`Q073.W6`); bonds grow through "quiet, ordinary,
  emotionally-available moments" (`Q063.W2`). Tone can swing from grim to absurd and back, and even the silliest bit
  has a human core (`Q013.W4`); a light thread can turn devastating by contrast (`Q013.W7`).
- The "noticed" feeling: lines that react to your recent death, loadout, progress (`Q047.W5`), paid for without a
  mountain by reacting to a FEW things well (`Q047.X1`).
- Pawns shout the knowledge they learned in someone else's game (`Q084.W2`), and the same pawns became the most
  annoying thing in it: "'Tis weak to fire!" forever. A bark system needs "a COOLDOWN and variation budget"
  (`Q084.X6`).
- Portraits and a score do the heavy lifting in a text-light game: "you care about the people you survive alongside;
  portraits + score build affection" (`Q065.W9`).
- The player fills the silence. Kenshi's slave arc has a mute protagonist and a villain with no speech, "which is why
  every testimony sounds authored: it was, by its teller" (`Q146.W5`). Much of an emergent story is apophenia, the
  player finding patterns (`Q044.X2`); the design should feed that and make it retellable (`Q044.W9`).

### 9. The automated companion: personality through behaviour
Our fight gives the company over to gambits (COMBAT [gambits], Final Fantasy XII, the reference for this and only
this). The library's own study of that system is `Q080.P6`: players "happily PROGRAM behavior with prioritized If/Then
macros... and enjoy watching it run", and it "automates the mundane and focuses macro-strategy". What the library
says about giving an automated fighter a personality:
- Personhood by proximity. Dragon's Dogma insists its pawns cannot feel, then shows "fear, worry, and grief" because
  the player's soul rubs off over time (`Q084.W3`). A companion whose gambit behaviour slowly bends toward how the
  player plays is that finding on the fight board.
- The resolve check. At breaking point, a Darkest Dungeon hero either cracks and acts on their own or overcomes with a
  rare clutch buff (`Q041.W2`); the virtue is "an unplannable ~15% overcoming" that makes the dread bearable
  (`Q041.W10`). This is the one time an automated companion should break its orders: rarely, visibly, and in
  character.
- The AI must not embarrass the person. Arcanum's companions wear poisoned clothes and faint mid-fight
  (`Q086.X5`); Reilly's Rangers "may randomly become hostile... just reload" (`Q101.X3`); Skyrim's follower "cannot
  fear" and walks to the altar chatting (`Q131.X4`). An automated person who acts stupid reads as a thing.
- Abilities that combine across characters make coordination a mechanic (`Q050.W7`); relationships can reach into
  moment-to-moment mechanics (`Q024.W5`), even change a boss fight (`Q024.W6`).
- Knowing your people IS the skill check: the Suicide Mission is won by assigning the leader to lead and the tech to
  the vents (`Q006.W4`), "the casting is the choice" (`Q137.W4`), and the one guaranteed survival is the assignment
  that exits the fight (`Q137.W5`, `Q137.P5`). Formation (rule 36's depth) is the same idea at the size of one block.

### 10. The company across the three acts
- Persistent companions cross generations: Phantasy Star III's androids persist while mortal leads change
  (`Q067.W6`). Our two-age flip (rule 31) makes an ageless anchor possible, but the more human version is below.
- The generations can fight side by side: in Dragon Quest V "you don't just become the next generation, you FIGHT
  ALONGSIDE it" (`Q069.W3`); the whole life earns the family made whole, "watched by the dead" (`Q069.W8`).
- One window in hour four decides who stands beside you at the end of the world: "if Paul is dead, then Gary Savage
  appears in his place" (`Q116.W10`). The dead stay dead across a sequel (`Q137.W8`).
- Opening a generation with a permanent loss of beloved prior companions gives instant stakes (`Q043.W9`).
- The heir is a person, not a respawn (`Q144.W4`), and the line is made real by what it will NOT carry: "unbanked
  secrets burn... stranded arcs stay stranded" (`Q144.W3`). The heir must not re-read prose the player already read
  (`Q144.X1`).
- OUR DEFAULT SINCE 9/29, THE COMPANY INHERITS (rule 39d, Paolo): each act starts with the HEIRS of the last act's
  company, prepared from the ledger; gear stays in the family; about 12 men an act, 36 lives a game; recruiting stays
  Battle Brothers-hard inside an act, so keeping killers alive is the pipeline. The library's warrant is this whole
  section: the heir is a person, not a respawn (`Q144.W4`), the generations fight side by side (`Q069.W3`), prior
  choices shape the heir's starting world (`Q025.W6`), and a lost man's line is a real loss because it does not
  carry (`Q144.W3`, `Q116.W10`).
- Warning: Crusader Kings' heirs "inherit hatred", which can feel arbitrary (`Q046.X5`). A companion's grandchild
  must not arrive pre-angry at the player for something the player could not see.
- The accused's kin carry your choice for decades across acts (`Q026.W6`); prior generations' choices shape the
  heir's starting world, and NPCs reference deeds from before the heir was born (`Q025.W6`).

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)

1. NO LOYALTY METER. No company member has a loyalty, approval or morale number visible anywhere, and none is written
   to a player-facing screen. PEOPLE may keep an internal gradient (`Q137.P4`) but it is never shown, and it is always
   expressed as behaviour: formation, gambits, camp position, one line. (`Q083.W6`, `Q076.X3`, `Q125.X9`, `Q137.X2`)
2. LOYALTY LIVES IN THE FIGHT. A member's regard for the player changes what he DOES on the fight board, never his
   damage number: whom he covers, whether he takes the flank you give him, whether he steps back to help a downed
   friend. Every such change has a tell a player can see in one fight (a line, a stance, a tile he will not enter).
   (`Q137.W3`, `Q006.W7`, with `Q137.X1` fixed by the tell)
3. A NO IS NOT THEIRS. On the settlement's offer screen, before the yes, no company member speaks. After a decline,
   no member mentions the contract, ever. A companion's reaction to a contract exists only after it is TAKEN, inside
   the job. (rule 35, `Q131.X7` inverted on purpose, `Q043.X1`)
4. DISAGREEMENT IS A PRICED PATH, NOT A LEAVE BUTTON. A member who objects to a taken job may ask to sit it out
   (one fewer in the formation for that job), may offer a third way on the fight board, or, for his one red line, leave
   the company at the next settlement with his reason said aloud. One red line per member, stated to the player in
   a road line before it can ever fire. (`Q136.P3`, `Q139.P2`, `Q116.W8`, `Q137.X1`)
5. NO CHORUS. A company reaction to a choice never has every member agreeing. If two or more speak, at least one
   argues the other side. (`Q126.X5`, `Q126.P40`, `Q126.X4`)
6. THE QUARREL HAPPENS WITHOUT YOU. Company quarrels are event screens that fire at camp or on the road and are
   CAUSED by the ledgers (a split of loot, a job one of them hated, a background clash), never rolled from nothing.
   The player walks into it; one face speaks; two or three choices. (`Q121.W9`, `Q043.W3`, `Q044.W2`)
7. THE PERSONAL ASK IS EARNED BY SILENCE AND TRIGGERED BY A PLACE. A member's own ask fires at most once per member
   per act, only after the member has been in the company for a stated minimum of map days, and only when the route
   passes a place from their background. It is an EVENT SCREEN at camp, or a CONTRACT in a settlement's hall that
   the member points to. Declining it is free and nobody mentions it again. (`Q108.W1`, `Q076.W4`, `Q043.W7`)
8. EVERY DEATH IS A RECEIPT. When the 20% roll kills a struck-down member, the next camp event names the cause in
   one line in a companion's mouth (the flank left open, the wall he held), shows his face, and offers the burial
   choice. No loot screen, ever, for a named dead member: his gear is handed on by a person at the grave or buried
   with him. (`Q137.W1`, `Q137.P3`, `Q125.X8`, `Q105.X5`)
9. THE WORLD NOTICES THE DEAD. Within two map stops or one city-view open, a dead member appears somewhere: the feed,
   his settlement, a person who knew him. (`Q129.X9`, `Q130.X10`, `Q131.X2`, and QR-G checklist line 1)
10. THE INJURED STAY AND COST THEIR DAY. A member with a 30 to 40 day injury stays in the company (rides in the back),
    eats his ration, is not in the formation, and has road lines about it. His permanent mark changes his portrait
    and one thing he does. (`Q055.W4`, `Q110.W5`, `Q049.X4`, rule 36)
11. ROAD LINES ARE TWELVE WORDS. A company road line is under a dozen words, answers something ordinary, and has a
    cooldown: no line repeats within ten map days, and no member speaks twice in a row. (`Q118.P3`, `Q084.X6`,
    `Q043.W4`)
12. THE AUTOMATED COMPANION BREAKS ITS ORDERS RARELY AND IN CHARACTER. A gambit-driven member overrides the player's
    gambit at most once in a long while, only at a breaking point, only in a way his background explains, and the
    override is visible and sayable afterwards. His AI never walks into obvious death, never idles through a
    threat. (`Q041.W2`, `Q041.W10`, `Q086.X5`, `Q131.X4`)
13. ATTACHMENT BY LABOUR IS TRACKED. PEOPLE records per member: days in the company, fights survived, times carried,
    times he carried someone. Those four numbers drive which road lines and asks unlock, and none is shown as a
    score; they show as lines ("that's four times you've dragged me"). (`Q151.W9`, `Q055.W9`, `Q045.W5`)
14. THE COMPANY CROSSES ACTS AS PEOPLE, NOT STATS, AND AS HEIRS (swept 9/29, rule 39d). Each act starts with the
    heirs of the last act's company, prepared from the ledger: a member alive at the end of an act hands his place

## HOW THIS SITS WITH BATTLE BROTHERS (the named reference for the company)

Battle Brothers already has most of the SKELETON the library recommends (08_CONTRACTS_EVENTS.md): company events
(two men fight over a woman, a man deserts with your crowns, a man finds a puppy), background events (the Thief
steals, the Cultist recruits), relationship events (two men become friends or rivals, a mentor-student pair), injury
and trait events (a man loses an eye). Outcomes: morale, items, traits, a man leaves, a new man joins. Cooldowns keep
them rare; the company's composition drives which fire. The roster page is "where the story lives" (10_UI_AND_FEEL.md).

What the library adds on top of that skeleton, which is what "with a better story" means for the company:
1. A voice. BB's men speak through narration on an event screen. The library's best companions speak for themselves in
   a line (`Q118.P3`, `Q083.W2`). Ours: the event screen's face is the member, and the line is his.
2. Specificity at death. BB's loss "of a man with a name" is the feel; the library makes it a receipt with a cause
   (`Q137.W1`) and a world that notices (`Q129.X9`).
3. The quarrel with teeth, and without a chorus (`Q043.W3`, `Q126.X5`).
4. The personal ask, which BB mostly does not have (`Q043.W7`, `Q108.W1`).
5. The company across generations, which BB cannot have (`Q069.W3`, `Q116.W10`): the heirs of the last act's company
   start the next act (rule 39d), so a BB company's losses become a family's.
What we take from BB unchanged: morale as a fight stat (TUNING's), backgrounds as the cast-at-a-glance, cooldowns, and
composition-driven events.

## OPEN (what the library could not answer)

1. ROSTER SIZE ON A PHONE. Kenshi's UI "can get cumbersome as your group's numbers grow" (`Q110.X1`), and BB runs up
   to 20. No study measures how many named, voiced people a thumb can keep straight. PEOPLE and UI pick the number
   and measure; this page's guess is that only the members in the formation (not the whole roster) get road lines.
2. THE MORALE STAT. Battle Brothers has a morale number in the fight. Rule 1 bans a LOYALTY meter, not a fight
   morale stat. Whether fight morale is shown as a number or only as a stance is TUNING's and COMBAT's call.
3. THE GAMBIT OVERRIDE RATE. `Q041.W10` gives Darkest Dungeon's ~15% virtue rate as a thrill at a breaking point. No
   study gives the right rate for an override in a 120 BPM fight. TUNING measures.
4. THE SAME PERSON IN TWO AGES. Because the acts are open together once unlocked (rule 31, rule 39c), and the company INHERITS (rule 39d: each act starts with the heirs of the last act's company, prepared from the ledger, the gear kept in the family), an act 1 member and his act 3 grandchild
   can both be on screen in one session (the grandchild usually IN the company, as an heir). The library's across-generations cases (`Q067.W6`, `Q069.W3`) are linear.
   DYNASTY owns how the flip reads a member who is alive in act 1 and a memory in act 3.
5. DOES THE COMPANY SPEAK SPANGLISH? Rule: people speak Spanglish, the player does not. Company members are people;
   the default here is yes, lightly. WORDS owns it.
