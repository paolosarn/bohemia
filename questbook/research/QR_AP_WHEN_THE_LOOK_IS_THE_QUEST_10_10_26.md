# QR-AP: WHEN THE LOOK IS THE QUEST. THE PAYOFF YOU SEE CHANGE IN THE TOWN PICTURE

The best side jobs in games pay you with something you can see: a window that was dark is now lit, a wall that had a hole is fixed, a market that was shut is open again. In Bohemia that means every job should change the town picture a little, so you remember what you did by looking, not by reading a number.

QUESTION: the QUESTS row [ninth shelf] (VAMILY, rule 88, LOOKS FIRST): designs whose payoff is a thing Paolo SEES change on the settlement screen or the map picture (a lit window, a rebuilt wall, a painted sign, a faction's colours going up), each naming the asset it needs, for COOK and DIRECTION to read.

STATUS: research only (rule 35, laws/BOHEMIA_ADDENDUM_QUESTS_IS_RESEARCH_ONLY_9_27_26.md). Nothing here is built. Every design is draft:true. Every asset line is a REQUEST to COOK and DIRECTION, never an order; they own the pictures, the look program and the twin (rules 82, 82a, 87).

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The library agrees, across more than twenty studies, that the payoff a player keeps is the one the world wears: the dry river that stays dry until you fix it (`Q147.W7`), the home you rebuild with the reward money (`Q075.W10`), the road that appears because people walked it (`Q089.W6`), the manor that grows tower by tower over generations (`Q049.W8`). The flaw on the other side is just as consistent: a quest that ends and "nothing changes" (`Q097.X3`, `Q127.X6`) is forgotten, and a meter you pump (`Q029.X2`) teaches the player to farm a number instead of caring about a place. For Bohemia, under rule 86 (the map is for looks; what you build lives only in the base's settlement screen) and rule 88 (looks first), the rule is: most contracts and many events end with ONE small, permanent picture change on the settlement screen of the town where it happened (a layer swapped on the camp or fortress picture, day and night), rarer bigger ones on the map's settlement picture, never a pop-up and never a number as the show. Keep it cheap: one change is one layer, drawn once, reused across towns of the same tier; about one visible change per two or three finished contracts, so the town picture visibly fills over an act (a year, rule 84) without COOK drawing a new town for every job.

## THE FINDINGS

### 1. The world wearing the quest is the cheapest, strongest reactivity

- `Q147.W7`: skip the Ikana suite and the dry river stays dry for the whole game; "the world wearing its open quests is reactivity's cheapest" form. The open problem IS a picture, and fixing it changes the picture.
- `Q147.P2`: the port, already written for us: every standing unresolved problem renders as VISIBLE world state (the dry channel, the dark district). Our version: the dark window on the settlement screen is the job; the lit window is the pay.
- `Q147.W2`: utilities and grief are one system; the river is dry because a soul is. Our power and water are held by share (canon), so a lit window or a running tap is never decoration, it is the town's state.
- `Q147.W8`: the reward is water, cashed downstream by strangers. A visible change helps people you never met; the picture shows them using it.
- `Q147.P4` and `Q147.W10`: maintenance as meaning, counted; repairs can carry a count the world refers to. A wall patched three times shows three colours of patch.
- `Q114.N1`: every substory entry is a VISIBLE ODDITY in the world, never a UI element. The same holds for the exit: the job begins with something you see wrong and ends with it right.
- `Q072.W10`: the base game seeded its late story as "visual white noise" (posters, graffiti). Pictures carry story more cheaply than prose.

### 2. The payoff becomes the home

- `Q075.W10`: the arc's reward (20,000) almost exactly pays for the rebuilt Sanctuary (19,000). The money's real form is the house you rebuild with it. Our contracts pay in the six resources (rule 47); the BIG visible changes are what the company chooses to spend it on in the base's settlement screen (rule 86), and the small ones come with the job.
- `Q075.W4`: ten quests spent making you love a house. A home is loved because you watched it change.
- `Q075.P5`: the highest-value port: make the player love a home, then let it be threatened. You cannot threaten a picture the player never watched fill in.
- `Q049.W8`: Rogue Legacy's manor tower: "progress is a HOME the family visibly builds across generations." `Q049.P3`: our settlement is that manor. `Q049.W2`: individuals die, the legacy grows; the picture is the legacy.
- `Q047.P3`: drip-feed the story through play and anchor it in the persistent hub, unlocking by rebuilding.
- `Q228.W2` and `Q228.X3`: saving your home costs your home, and the home's worth must be established first or the sacrifice weighs nothing. A town whose picture you changed five times is established.

### 3. Many hands, one picture

- `Q089.W4`: the highway is crowdfunded by strangers; later waves drive on it. `Q089.W2`: other people's builds appear in your world. `Q089.W3`: people build when the only reward is being seen. For us: a few contracts in one town each add a piece (a panel, a pipe, a stall) and the full picture appears only when the pieces add up.
- `Q089.W6`: paths smooth as people walk them. The map picture can wear the company's habit (a road the company keeps using shows tyre ruts) as long as it stays a look and never a micro feature (rule 86).
- `Q089.W1`: the rope, not the stick: the best visible payoffs are connections (a cable strung, a bridge plank, a shared tap), not trophies.

### 4. The settlement is a readout, and so is its look

- `Q236.W1`: the board is the world's current problems; contracts are generated by settlement situations and write situations back. The picture is the other half of that readout: the hall shows what is wrong, the town picture shows what got fixed.
- `Q236.W2`: one contract at a time keeps the promise legible. One picture change per contract keeps the payoff legible.
- `Q236.W3`: the skulls are a reading; danger is stated before the yes. The offer screen can show the "before" (the dark window) in the client's portrait background, so the player sees what the job will change before accepting.
- `Q151.W1`: ownership is an address; building flips one flag and the world finds you. `Q151.W3`: raids scale with VISIBLE prosperity. A lit, painted, busy town draws trouble; the picture is also the bait. That is a feature: the looks create the next contracts.
- `Q151.W5`: the gate is the posture; door policy is reputation without a meter, values as architecture. A gate painted open or a gate chained shut says who this town is.
- `Q151.W10`: the world never applauds; nothing congratulates you, the region just re-prices. The change happens quietly; no banner, no fanfare, the window is just lit next time you arrive.
- `Q151.X3`: the interior life is silent; nobody credits the held wall. Our fix: the person who offered the contract stands in front of what you fixed, once.
- `Q055.W7`: factions with their own agendas change the world's state; their colours going up or down on a town picture is how the player reads it without a menu.
- `Q066.W5`: theme through the systems; relief at home. The lit town at night is that relief, drawn.

### 5. Why a visible change beats a number

- `Q029.X2`: hope and discontent meters feel gamey; players learn to pump hope with buildings. LESSON in the study: tie the meters to visible human things. We go further: no meter at all, only the picture.
- `Q129.P4`: no system stamp (reward, faction number, title) may fire at a settlement's handshake. The proof of a deal is what the town looks like later, not a counter.
- `Q097.X3`: "nothing changes": whatever you tell Plaisance, the building stays doomed. The great quest's real failure is that the world does not wear it.
- `Q127.X6`: banishing the strongest antagonist changes nothing visible; he "exits without residue." Residue is the point.
- `Q050.P4`: small choices teach big choices; front-load an early beat where tiny prior actions VISIBLY shape an outcome, so the player learns the world listens. The first contract in act 1 should end with a lit window, so the player learns this rule in the first hour.
- `Q050.W5`: actions in one era reshape another; with three acts open at once (9/23), a lit window in act 1 is the lit block in act 2. Visible changes are the cheapest proof that the future is computed from the past.
- `Q144.W7`: inherited NPCs speaking in past tense is "the cheapest possible reactivity"; inherited PICTURES are cheaper still, no line to write.
- `Q027.W8` and `Q027.P7`: visible degradation of a body mirrors moral collapse; the same works for a town, both ways (rule: THE FUTURE GOES BOTH WAYS, 9/27).
- `Q048.W6`: persistent scars on the enemy; your acts physically rewrite things. A burned stall stays burned.
- `Q234.W3`: the town carries an accusation for generations. Some changes should be unwelcome: a painted-over name, a boarded door.
- `Q204.W9`: the town fears what he became; a visible change can unsettle, not only reward.
- `Q188.W4`: the drowned town is the image; a loss made a place. One design below ends in a loss you see.
- `Q045.X4`: the richest stories stay unseen unless surfaced; a picture surfaces them for free.

### 6. How often, and how to keep it cheap

- `Q114.W1`: one hundred written mini-dramas shipped because they had a TYPED spec (`Q114.W4`). Picture changes need a typed spec too: a short list of layer kinds (light, repair, paint, colours, stall, smoke, plant, sign) that every town tier carries as slots.
- `Q114.X1`: the hundred is padded and players notice. Do not count a recolour as a new change; one layer kind per contract, honest.
- `Q049.X2`: endless scaling after the manor caps out discourages. A town picture has a finite set of slots (about eight per tier) and FILLS over an act; when it is full, the next act's picture is the reward (act 2's town is the act 1 town grown, rule 84 and 85).
- `Q045.W5` against `Q045.X3`: depth of detail makes events rich and also breaks things. Keep each change to ONE layer that never interacts with anything else; no simulation behind it.
- `Q066.X1`: the story becomes a vending machine when it repeats. The same layer kind should not be the reward of two contracts in a row in the same town.
- `Q151.W8`: the scale shift is seamless; the same change should read at the map's settlement picture (a tiny warm dot, a colour pip) and at the settlement screen (the full window). Two sizes of one asset, never two systems.
- `Q236.W5`: greed has a clock; the haggle stays on the offer screen. The picture change is not negotiable and not part of pay; it is what the place does with your help.

### 7. Who stands in front of it

- `Q151.P5`: post-event, the interior speaks; residents remember. For us: the next time you open that settlement screen, the client stands in front of the change for one visit (a portrait line, from a mouth, never a pop-up).
- `Q228.X2`: the transformation must be felt; a changed town that nobody in it notices rings false.
- `Q144.W9`: the second life proves the first one happened; the heir arrives in a town already lit by the grandparent.
- `Q069.W8`: the family-restoration payoff, watched; across acts, the town picture is where the family sees what it did.

## HOW OFTEN, IN NUMBERS (drafts for TUNING to set)

- About 1 visible change per 2 to 3 finished contracts, per town. A camp tier town carries about 8 slots; a fortress about 12.
- Events (road events) change the MAP picture rarely: about 1 in 6, and only things that read at map size (a fire's smoke column, a colour pip, a lit pin).
- A declined contract changes nothing (the QUESTS law: declining is free). A taken and failed contract may change the picture the other way (a stall burned, a window boarded): THE FUTURE GOES BOTH WAYS.
- Every change exists in day and night (the settlement art already ships camp_0 / camp_0_night and fortress_0 / fortress_0_night).

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS

1. Every contract design names ONE picture change, written as "ASSET NEEDED: before -> after, where on the settlement screen". If it has none, it says why (`Q097.X3`).
2. The change is a LAYER on the town tier's picture (camp or fortress, day and night), in a named slot, drawn once and reused by every town of that tier and district look.
3. No number, toast, banner or pop-up announces it (`Q151.W10`, `Q129.P4`, the hard rule NOTHING POPS UP). The player sees it the next time the settlement screen opens.
4. The client stands in front of the change once, with one portrait line (`Q151.P5`).
5. The first contract a new player can finish in act 1 ends in a lit window (`Q050.P4`).
6. A failed taken contract may reverse a slot (`Q027.W8`); a declined one never touches it.
7. A change that reads on the map does so at map size as one tiny mark (a warm pixel cluster, a colour pip, a smoke line), never a new icon system (rule 86).
8. No two consecutive contracts in one town reward the same layer kind (`Q066.X1`).
9. Changes carry into the next act's town (rule 85: they are visible after the flip) as the grown version, not the same layer (`Q050.W5`).
10. Every change has ONE wrong detail (analog horror), drawn, not explained: the lit window that is lit at noon too.

## WHAT TO AVOID

- `Q097.X3`, `Q127.X6`: the quest that changes nothing.
- `Q029.X2`: a meter the player pumps with buildings.
- `Q114.X1`: padding the count with recolours.
- `Q049.X2`: an endless scale with no full picture.
- `Q151.X3`: a town that never notices its own repair.
- `Q045.X3`: changes that interact and break; one layer, no sim.
- `Q066.X1`: the same reward on repeat.

## OPEN

- The library cannot say how many slots a settlement picture holds before it looks cluttered at phone size; that is DIRECTION's eye and the twin's call (rule 89: shown as a before and after of the same picture, side by side).
- Whether a change made in a town the company does not own should persist after a faction takes the town (rule 43): default yes, the new owner paints over the colours, keeps the lit windows.
