# QR-T: WHAT DOES BUILDING IN A HOME BASE DO FOR THE QUEST BOARD, AND HOW DO WE KEEP IT FROM BECOMING A CHORE LIST?

QUESTION: row [fifth shelf], letter T. Rule 40b brought BUILDING back inside the settlement screen of a home base the family owns (tap a lot, build a thing, it costs batteries and time, it shows on the map, on the fight board cut from that block, and in the derived future). The quest board (QR-A, QR-N, QR-O, QR-S) is written by each base's situation. How do the two feed each other, from the library, and what keeps "build a thing" and "take a contract" from collapsing into a list of jobs the player owes the game? Twenty designs are cut from this row (QD-T01 to QD-T20); five of them are the build-meets-board shapes, four are THE COMPANY INHERITS, three are THE FIRST UNLOCK, one is the elder dying (QR-S situation 25).

STATUS: draft:true, research only, nothing built (rule 35). For LIFE+CITY [build a lot], RUN [settlement screen], WORLD [the block], ECONOMY, TUNING and DYNASTY. Battle Brothers facts are from reference/library/battle_brothers/ and `Q236` (the hall and the haggle); Battle Brothers is cited for the contract layer only.

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

A build is worth something to the quest layer only when it CHANGES A SITUATION, because the board is the situation read out loud (`Q236.W1`, `Q148.P1`, `Q044.W2`). The library gives five ways a build meets the board, and each is a different kind of design: a build can WRITE offers that did not exist (a charge shed draws trucks, a lookout sees a party; `Q236.W9`, `Q151.W1`, `Q151.W3`), ANSWER an offer as a fourth road next to the fight (a wall instead of a night's guard; `Q024.W9`, `Q197.P4`), ALLOCATE a scarce output so several clients bid for it at once (a transformer with headroom for one line; `Q169.W1`, `Q169.W3`, `Q169.P4`), be the PAY for a contract (a print room built on the family's lot; `Q107.W9`, `Q232.W1`), or CROSS the acts so the next generation meets it (a wall your mother built, a book she wrote; `Q089.P4`, `Q144.W2`, `Q144.W9`). The chore list is the library's best-documented failure of this genre: ruling becomes admin (`Q061.X3`), the back half becomes construction and grinding (`Q090.X1`), resources stop mattering once you have enough (`Q065.X1`), recovery becomes rotation (`Q041.X2`), the story engine goes quiet and actions feel rote (`Q046.X1`), and the player stops reading and starts farming (`Q066.X1`). All six have one cause: the build produced TASKS FOR THE PLAYER instead of SITUATIONS FOR THE WORLD. So the rule is simple and testable: a build never asks the player for anything by itself; its costs and rewards arrive as situations at bases, which write offers, which the player may decline for free. Building is the family making the world; the board is the world answering. Three findings matter most. First, every build is a HOSTAGE as well as an asset (`Q093.W6`, `Q151.W6`, `Q151.W4`), and that is what makes it worth fighting over without any upkeep timer. Second, the allocation shape (`Q169`) is the richest and least used: one build, three faces, one line, and the two left in the dark say so by name. Third, the build is our cleanest carrier across the three acts, because a thing in a place survives the generation that made it (`Q089.W7`, `Q049.W8`, `Q228.P4`), and the company that inherits can be met by the buildings its parents left.

## THE FINDINGS

### 1. Why a build belongs to the quest layer at all

- OWNERSHIP IS AN ADDRESS. `Q151.W1`: building flips one flag and the whole dormant political sim acquires "a place to happen", the cheapest trigger for the deepest consequence. `Q151.P1` ports it: the moment a plot crosses visibility thresholds (walls, light, smoke, traffic), collectors, scouts and traders find it. A built lot is the most readable thing a base can show the sim.
- ATTACHMENT IS MANUFACTURED BY LABOR. `Q151.W9` and `Q055.W9`: people care about what they levelled or raised by hand, not about backstory. A wall the family paid twelve batteries and three days for is worth defending in a way a scripted fortress never is.
- THE SIEGE HAPPENS WHERE THE HOURS LIVE. `Q151.W6`: the fight's arena is the player's own labor, so every breach costs biography. Rule 40b says the build shows on the fight board cut from that block; that is this finding, built. The wall in QD-T02 is a house tile of cover in every later fight on that block.
- THE BASE IS A HOSTAGE, NOT A MECHANIC. `Q093.W6` and `Q093.P4`: players spend days building and then return to it with their heart pounding, because it can be taken. The pressure is the love. A build needs no maintenance timer to matter; it needs to be takeable (third votes: a home base can be taken or ruined).
- BUILDING IS THE SURRENDER OF MOBILITY. `Q151.W4`: the real armour was always the ability to leave; a base trades it away. For us the batteries in a wall are batteries that cannot ride with the company. That exchange rate is the only cost a build needs.
- THE SCALE SHIFT IS SEAMLESS. `Q151.W8` and `Q055.W8`: lone survivor to squad to settlement in one system set, no mode switch. Rule 24 cut the separate builder screen; building inside the settlement screen is exactly this law.
- IT IS A CITY-BUILDER QUEST INSIDE THE CAMPAIGN. `Q169.W10`: restart a plant, allocate its output, shape a region, "Bohemia's exact genre smuggled into a shooter". The library's best infrastructure quest is a building quest.
- PREPARATION SHAPES OPTIONS. `Q024.W9` and `Q024.P7`: what you built and stockpiled before a crisis shapes the options inside it. A build is prep that the next contract reads.

### 2. A build WRITES the board (supply)

- THE BOARD IS THE WORLD'S CURRENT PROBLEMS. `Q236.W1` and `Q236.P1`: offers are generated from a base's situation and write situations back. A build is the family writing a situation into its own base: a surplus of power (QD-T03), a queue at a print room (QD-T05), a party seen from a roof (QD-T06).
- RENOWN GATES THE TIERS. `Q236.W9`: bigger clients appear as the company grows, so the board is its own progression with no quest log. We add a second axis: bigger clients appear as the BASE grows. A caravan master stops at a charge shed who never stopped at a field (QD-T03).
- PROSPERITY IS A MAGNET. `Q151.W3` and `Q044.W4`: raids scale with visible prosperity, success breeds danger, loss stays proportionate. Every build that makes a base richer raises its raid draw (QR-S rule 11). The charge shed draws trucks AND a party. That is the build's upkeep, paid by the world, never by a menu.
- THE TAX IS THE RECOGNITION. `Q151.W2`: the world's first acknowledgement that you exist arrives as a bill. A build is the likeliest thing to bring the tribute collector (QR-S situation 29) to a base the family owns.
- THE STORYTELLER READS THE STATE. `Q044.W2`: an algorithm reads the state and picks the event that makes the best story. A build is state the storyteller can read without any new rule: a lookout means parties seen further; a transformer means a line to bid for.
- HOPE IS A BUILDING WITH A COST. `Q107.W9`: the House of Prayer's second ability costs 20 food rations. A build that helps people should cost something the people around it can see (the queue at the print room, the raid it draws).
- THE STORY PIVOTS ON SYSTEM STATE. `Q055.P6`: a supply run becomes an evacuation when the district is raided. A built base has more state to pivot on.

### 3. A build ANSWERS the board (a fourth road)

- THE BUILD IS A WAY THROUGH, NOT THE ONLY WAY. `Q197.P4`: how you defend branches, and different manners of defence make visibly different communities. In QD-T02 the wall is road B next to "hold it as it is" and "build and hold"; in QD-T19 the farm's buried rifles are the same question without a build. Neither design gates the contract on the build.
- WHAT YOU AIM IT AT IS THE CHOICE. `Q197.W4`: reform is not the absence of the old skills but a change in their purpose. A wall aimed at keeping a party out is a different base from a wall aimed at keeping people in (`Q228.W6`, the shelter that excludes its saviors).
- THE COST IS SHOWN BEFORE THE PRIZE. `Q156.W2`: the player feels the price before seeing what it buys. The lot on the settlement screen shows its price in batteries and days BEFORE the contract is taken; the offer screen can mention it ("The lot's there. Just saying.").
- THE RESULT IS THE BASE'S NEW SITUATION. `Q236.P4`: a finished pump job flips the base from dry to running. A build that answers a contract makes that flip permanent: the wall does not leave when the offer does.
- THE PRICED REFUSAL STILL EXISTS. `Q139.P2`: inside a taken job, turning back is on the sheet and priced. Building instead of fighting is a priced road, never a free skip.

### 4. A build ALLOCATES (the richest shape)

- INFRASTRUCTURE IS A MORAL ACT. `Q169.W1`: holding the switch for a region's power is deciding who deserves the light; the console is a verdict and distribution is the drama.
- THE RESOURCE IS SCARCE. `Q169.W2`: no setting lights everyone. `Q232.W4`: the scarcity is the choice. A build with headroom for exactly one outside line is the smallest honest version.
- FOUR THEORIES OF DESERT. `Q169.W3`: the many, the few, the faithful, or force; the menu is a moral philosophy. In QD-T04 the three offers are three theories with faces: the clinic (need), the racks (the faithful who kept the block alive, `Q169.W6`), the Strip (money).
- THE CONSOLE JUDGES NOTHING. `Q169.W5`: no option is flagged right. Our offers carry different pay and that is allowed BETWEEN offers (QR-O rule 15), never inside one.
- THE CONSEQUENCE IS DOWNSTREAM. `Q169.W8`: the switch's body count is deferred and quiet; the dark towns never see the console. `Q169.P2` and `Q169.P4`: make it legible and felt, and hold the switch-holder to it. The dark places say it on their own settlement screens by name.
- NO COMBAT, JUST A SWITCH. `Q169.W4`: a decision can be the whole stakes. The allocation contracts still carry a routine fight (the cable run, 2 to 4 minutes), because the hours go to the world (rule 40), but the weight is the pick.
- THEME THROUGH SYSTEMS. `Q029.W7`: hard choices are made under pressure, never read about. `Q029.W2`: one committed law at a time, slow enough that each is felt. One line, one transformer, one pick per act at a base is our Book of Laws cadence.

### 5. The board PAYS in a build

- A CONTRACT CAN BUILD INSTEAD OF PAY. When the client has no batteries but has hands (the chapel's nurses, the co-op's masons), the hand-in can be a building on the family's lot (QD-T05). It is the most realistic payment in a bust and the most interesting in a boom.
- THE CURE IS HOARDED, NOT ABSENT. `Q232.W1` and `Q232.W7`: the remedy is withheld for profit, and the maker has a claim. A build paid for by a contract can carry the moral of how it was paid for: the print room prints for whoever the family lets in.
- NO BUILD IS A STRAIGHT UPGRADE. `Q184.X2`: when the fiction says a thing is dangerous and the mechanics hand it over as better gear, the mechanics win and the fiction loses. `Q184.W6`: the prize is the warning. Every build, paid or bought, writes at least one pressure situation (a queue, a raid draw, a collector).
- THE BOON IS SMALL AND PERMANENT. `Q166.W8`: a modest reward that lasts all game marks the choice forever. Paid builds should be small and stay.

### 6. A build CROSSES the acts (the family's shape)

- WHAT THE FIRST BUILDS, THE THIRD INHERITS. `Q089.P4`: "even if you construct things just for yourself, they will help others"; in our fold that is literal. `Q089.W4`: waves of strangers each pay a piece of a highway and later waves drive on it. `Q089.W6`: paths smooth as people walk them.
- HELP ARRIVES AS OBJECTS, NOT MESSAGES. `Q089.W7`: an actual ladder, not a hint. The wall on lot two is the act-1 lead's message to act 2 (QD-T12's lot tour: "Your mother's wall. We sit on it at lunch.").
- THE MANOR IS THE METAPHOR. `Q049.W8` and `Q049.W2`: progress is a home the family visibly builds across generations; individuals die, the legacy grows. `Q049.P3` ports it: the settlement is the persistent structure the family builds.
- KNOWLEDGE IS THE GOLD STANDARD. `Q144.W2`: what crosses generations best is what was learned. `Q088.W9`: players invented the notebook the next survivor finds. A build can be a book (QD-T11 road C, QD-T01 road C).
- THE SECOND LIFE PROVES THE FIRST HAPPENED. `Q144.W9`: the heir's early game is repriced by inherited geography. The first flip (QD-T12) proves it in one screen: built lots stand, raided lots are shells.
- THE HOME MUST BE WORTH BELONGING TO. `Q228.P4` and `Q228.X3`: if losing a home is the tragedy, the home must first be shown warm. `Q075.W4` and `Q075.P5`: spend quests making the player love a house, then let it be taken, and across the fold, a home built by one generation can burn in another's life.
- THE CHOICE COMES DUE ELSEWHERE. `Q187.W1`: a choice detonates hours later and miles away. `Q187.X1`: if the stakes were under-signalled, it lands as a gotcha. A build's cross-act consequence must be SHOWN on the first act-2 screen (QD-T12), never discovered by accident later.
- A FOUNDATION CAN HIDE SOMETHING. `Q154.W3`: the prosperity is built on the suppression; `Q192.W8`: the foundation is a generation deep; `Q218.W1`: the peace is built on lies. A base built on a raid (the lot the family took) can carry that into act 3 as a situation.

### 7. The base must speak about what was built

- THE INTERIOR LIFE IS SILENT. `Q151.X3`: nobody resents the defied tax or credits the held wall; the settlement never speaks about itself. `Q151.P5`: the fix is that residents reference the event, drawn from ledger entries, echoing for a generation.
- INHERITED PEOPLE SPEAK IN THE PAST TENSE. `Q144.W7`: the cheapest reactivity (a line variant) carries the heaviest freight. One line per lot, from whoever uses it now, is our whole reactivity budget for builds.
- THE WORLD NEVER APPLAUDS. `Q151.W10`: the region quietly re-prices. `Q146.X1`: but a triumph met with nothing at all starves the payoff. A finished build gets a price change and a line, never a fanfare.
- THE PERSISTENT HUB. `Q047.W4`: returning to people and evolving sub-plots makes repetition meaningful. A base the family owns is our hub; its builds are what change between visits.

### 8. How building turns into a chore list (the danger, measured)

- RULING BECOMES ADMIN. `Q061.X3`: the conqueror-king phase is "a major burden", tedious management less fun than the rise.
- THE BACK HALF IS CONSTRUCTION. `Q090.X1`: the mission leads into "a lot of construction and grinding", with no escape from it however good you get.
- THE WALLET STOPS MATTERING. `Q065.X1`: by midgame you can ignore resource management and the survival tension evaporates.
- RECOVERY BECOMES ROTATION. `Q041.X2`: managing stress means rotating heroes and repeating dungeons; attrition needs pacing so recovery is not busywork.
- THE ERRAND GRIND. `Q074.X1`: earning legitimacy from every faction becomes dozens of hours of fetch work; the connective tissue must stay interesting or it is a checklist. `Q071.X3`: the fetch chain middle is "quite irritating".
- THE STORY ENGINE GOES QUIET. `Q046.X1`: between the good procedural moments, play feels like dry administration.
- THE STORY BECOMES A VENDING MACHINE. `Q066.X1`: you pick the option that yields supplies over the one with the story; grinding for survival overwhelms the writing.
- THE FIRST HOURS ARE A JOB. `Q089.X1`: the system that makes it sing does not populate until later. For us: the first build should come with the first base, not after a long act.
- THE OPTIMAL ORDER FLATTENS THE ETHICS. `Q029.X1`: min-maxers find efficient law orders and the moral weight becomes a spreadsheet; keep scarcity tight enough that hard choices stay hard.
- THE CRUNCH FORCES THE YES. `Q236.X4`: the wage burn makes refusing expensive in practice; keep a second income so a no is never a slow death. A build that produces (a charge shed, a stall) IS that second income.
- COMPLEXITY UP FRONT. `Q046.X2` and `Q065.X2`: reveal complexity gradually; do not front-load systems.
- THE FIX, ACROSS ALL OF THEM. `Q031.P9`: give administrative work rhythm, escalating variety and human intrusions; depth can come from many small weighted choices. `Q013.W1`: side content exists to make the place feel alive, not to pad a checklist. `Q023.W1`: the world is the quest log. `Q100.W8`: the war can be a maintenance ticket, if the ticket is a person who wants nothing to do with it. The common move is to turn every would-be task into a person in a place with a want (`Q236.X3`), who the player may walk past.

### 9. Where Battle Brothers stops and we start

The Battle Brothers contract system (`Q236`) is the hall, the offer screen, the haggle with a ceiling, the skulls, and the outcome that changes the map (`Q236.W8`). Its company has no home to build: the company IS the home, and money out every day keeps every offer weighed (`Q236.W10`). We keep the hall, the haggle and the wage pressure, and add three things: fourteen home bases the family can own, lots inside their settlement screens, and a build that writes, answers, allocates, pays or crosses. [bb build] Battle Brothers teaches that a contract changes a town's prices; our twist is that the family can change the town first, and the hall reads what they built.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)

1. EVERY BUILD WRITES OR CHANGES A SITUATION. A build record names the situation it writes, answers or blocks, at its own base and at up to two bases within two map stops. Test: no build id without a situation effect. (`Q236.W1`, `Q151.W1`, `Q044.W2`)
2. A BUILD NEVER ASKS FOR UPKEEP BY MENU. No repair timers, no "your wall needs attention", no recurring cost line. A build's costs arrive only as situations the world draws (a raid, a collector, a queue), which write offers. Test: zero recurring build costs outside the situation draw. (`Q061.X3`, `Q090.X1`, `Q041.X2`, `Q046.X1`)
3. A BUILD IS A ROAD, NEVER A GATE. A contract that a build can answer is always finishable without it. Test: for every contract with a build road, a non-build road exists with the same pay. (`Q197.P4`, `Q024.W9`, `Q139.P2`)
4. PROSPERITY PULLS PRESSURE. Every build that raises a base's visible wealth raises its raid-expected draw by a TUNING number (suggested: one in four for a producing build). Test: raid draw rises with built value. (`Q151.W3`, `Q044.W4`, `Q093.W6`)
5. NO BUILD IS A STRAIGHT UPGRADE. Every build writes at least one good situation and at least one pressure situation over its life. Test: each build id lists both. (`Q184.X2`, `Q151.W4`, `Q232.W4`)
6. AN ALLOCATING BUILD POSTS ALL ITS OFFERS AT ONCE, each a face with its own pay; the choice is which offer; the unchosen places say so on their own settlement screens, by name, on the next visit. Test: an allocation writes N offers and N-1 named lines. (`Q169.W3`, `Q169.P2`, `Q169.P4`, `Q169.X3`)
7. PAID-IN-A-BUILD IS RATIONED. At most one contract in five pays in a building, and the building it pays writes its own situation. Test: the ratio across a base's board history. (`Q148.X3`, `Q236.X2`, `Q166.W8`)
8. THE BUILD SHOWS IN FOUR PLACES: the settlement screen (a picture and a sound), the map, the fight board cut from that block, and the derived future of every later act. Test: each build id has all four assets or a stated default. (`Q151.W6`, `Q141.X3`, `Q089.W7`)
9. THE BASE SPEAKS ABOUT ITS BUILDS. Each lot carries one line from whoever uses it, read from the ledger, with a default for an empty lot. Test: every lot has a line id and a default. (`Q151.P5`, `Q151.X3`, `Q144.W7`)
10. FEW LOTS, SHORT MENU. Lots per owned base by tier (suggested: camp 2, town 3, fortress 4) and a menu of what the assets allow, so building is a choice, not a list. Test: no base shows more lots than its tier. (`Q029.X1`, `Q046.X2`, `Q065.X2`)
11. THE FIRST BUILD COMES WITH THE FIRST BASE. The house (QD-T11) and one lot appear the day the base is the family's; nothing is said in its first 60 seconds on screen. Test: the first base's screen has a lot on its first visit; no speech in the first minute. (`Q089.X1`, `Q067.X4`, `Q235.W7`)
12. A PRODUCING BUILD IS THE SECOND INCOME. At least one build kind produces batteries slowly, so a declined contract is never a slow death. Test: a family that declines every offer for an act still ends it solvent if it built one producer. (`Q236.X4`, `Q236.W10`)
13. DECLINING STILL WRITES NOTHING AT THE FAMILY'S OWN BASE. The base's own people cope, and the board shows the world's result, never a punishment. Test: ledger bytes identical after a decline at an owned base. (`Q236.P3`, `Q126.W3`, `Q084.X5`)
14. A BUILD CROSSES THE ACTS WITH A DEFAULT. What was built in act 1 stands (or is a shell, if raided) in act 2 and act 3; a never-played act reads a default lot. Test: every lot in acts 2 and 3 resolves to built, ruined or default. (`Q089.P4`, `Q144.W9`, `Q049.W8`)
15. THE FIRST FLIP SHOWS THE BUILDS. The first use of the flip lands on the base where the second generation unlocked and shows its lots aged in place before anyone speaks. Test: the first flip's first screen is that base. (`Q187.X1`, `Q228.P4`, `Q199.P3`)

## THE ANALOG HORROR LINE

A build is an ordinary thing that one day is slightly wrong: the meter on the shed that reads the same number every morning, the mason's string nobody remembers tying, the switch box with a fourth position in a stranger's hand. Never a monster in the wall; always a wall that is almost right.

## OPEN (what the library could not answer)

1. THE BUILD MENU. What can be built, from which assets, is LIFE+CITY's [build a lot]. This page assumes a short list per tier: a wall, a lookout, a producer (shed, stall), a service (clinic room, print room), a house. The library does not say how long a menu can be before it becomes a checklist; `Q029.W2`'s one-law-at-a-time cadence suggests one build per base per few map days.
2. THE NUMBERS. Build costs (batteries, days), the raid draw a build adds, and the second income's rate are TUNING's. Suggested starts are in the designs (a wall 12 and 3 days, a shed 16 and 4, a transformer 20 and 5, a lookout 8 and 2).
3. BUILDING AT A BASE THE FAMILY DOES NOT OWN. Rule 40b says an owned base. Whether a contract can pay in a building at the CLIENT'S base (the family builds for a town it does not own) is open; the library's crowdfunded bridge (`Q089.W4`) says it would be loved.
4. WHAT A RAIDED BUILD LEAVES. A shell on the lot is the default here. Whether a shell can be rebuilt cheaper, and whether the next generation can see who ruined it, is WORLD's and DYNASTY's.
5. THE ALLOCATION CADENCE. How often an allocating build re-opens its line (never, once per act, when the client's situation ends) is RUN's; this page suggests once per act, so the pick is heavy.
