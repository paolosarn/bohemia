# QR-E: HOW DOES A PERSON IN A PLACE HAND YOU A JOB?

QUESTION (row [the ask in a place], question (d) of laws/BOHEMIA_ADDENDUM_QUESTS_IS_RESEARCH_ONLY_9_27_26.md):
How do the best of the 152 hand the player a job through a PERSON in a PLACE, never a board or a card?
What does the person say FIRST, what do they withhold, what does the place itself tell you before anyone
talks, and how do body, location and timing do the work? How is the price stated and argued? Then: the
SHAPE for our asks at the place the player arrives at after travel (swept 9/29: the settlement screen, not a
walked street): never forced, nothing in
the first 60 seconds, a mouth with a portrait, and the free no.

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

In the best of the library the job is never announced; it is NOTICED. The place goes first: something
visibly wrong in an ordinary spot (a posture, a crowd, a dry river, a building every business dies in) is
the hook, and the player walks toward it or does not (`Q114.N1`, `Q013.W6`, `Q147.W7`, `Q097.W1`). The
person is introduced by what they are already doing when you find them, not by a speech (`Q095.W3`,
`Q108.W1`, `Q083.W1`). Their first line is about THEM and THE PLACE, not about you and not about a quest,
and it usually carries the whole situation in one plain sentence (`Q125.W2`, `Q120.P3`, `Q001.W1`). What
they withhold is the WHY and the RISK, never the TERMS: the great givers lie in the passive voice about
what happened to the last person (`Q003.N1`) and omit the one fact they cannot survive saying
(`Q152.W1`), but the job, the place and the pay are stated clean, so that after the yes you cannot say
you were tricked (`Q126.N5`, `Q126.N6`). The price is fixed BEFORE the truth of the job is knowable, which
keeps every later choice about the people and not the coin (`Q148.W1`); arguing it is allowed and has a
shape (a different currency, or up front), and pushing past that shape is where it ends (`Q126.N8`, our
own haggle asset). And the no is free: the person is unbothered, the offer stays standing where it stood,
nothing is locked, nobody talks about it (`Q126.W3`, `Q126.N12`, `Q126.N13`, `Q134.N1`). The library is
blunt that a punished no is the most common way great quests fail (`Q103.X1`, `Q084.X5`, `Q100.X1`,
`Q106.X6`). For our street that gives one shape: ARRIVE, SEE, APPROACH, OPEN LINE, TERMS, ARGUE, YES OR
NO, and the decision is always the player's feet.

## THE FINDINGS

### 1. THE PLACE SPEAKS FIRST (the ask is a visible oddity, never a UI element)

- The strongest production evidence in the library is the Yakuza substory engine: a hundred written
  mini-dramas, none on the map, all found "just by walking around the streets" (`Q114.W1`, `Q114.W2`).
  Its node 1 is exactly our rule: "every substory entry is a VISIBLE ODDITY in the world (a posture, a
  crowd, a sound), never a UI element", and its port asks for SEEN gates, "the dynast walked within
  sightline", not journal pings (`Q114.N1`). The same engine is praised in its earlier study because the
  city snags you as you pass: "substories start by WALKING PAST something, not from a menu" (`Q013.W6`,
  `Q013.P7`), and because the point is sense of place, not a checklist (`Q013.W1`).
- The place can BE the unfinished job. In the Ikana suite the dry river is the open quest: skip it and
  the dry river stays, "walkably, for the whole game" (`Q147.W7`), and the reason the water is gone is a
  person's grief (`Q147.W2`). Infrastructure as emotional state is, in the study's words, "Bohemia's
  native tongue". For us: a dark window, a tap that does not run, a shelf that stays empty.
- The oddity should be ORDINARY. Disco Elysium's best economic quest begins with a building where "no
  business will ever thrive there" (`Q097.W1`), and its haunted netherworld is a failed gym with a
  barbell and a planning board (`Q097.W5`, `Q097.P4`). Cyberpunk's crucifixion is made horrible by a
  bored executive smoking and "a dressing room" (`Q122.W4`, `Q122.P3`). Ultima VII's collapse is ambient:
  "the population feels SPIRITUALLY EMPTY FOR REASONS NOBODY CAN EXPLAIN" (`Q083.W7`). This is the
  analog horror law at the source: an ordinary place with one wrong detail, never explained.
- Environmental storytelling as testimony: architecture, props and placement narrate before anyone
  speaks (`Q023.W4`, `Q023.P4`, with the port naming "a barricaded home, a shrine of old currency, a
  child's toy in the ruins"), and the space later confirms what a person said (`Q001.W9`). The Baron's
  study adds that the character should be "the soul of a place", born from it rather than placed on it
  (`Q001.W13`).
- The place can bait honestly. Kenshi's domed ruin has "A SUSPICIOUS TRAIL OF FOOD, MEDICINE AND OTHER
  EXPENSIVE ITEMS" up the stairs and no script: your greed is the mechanism (`Q110.P5`). Kenshi's
  outposts show that a gate is a statement: "Door policy broadcasts the settlement's ethics to everyone
  who approaches" (`Q151.W5`).
- The place can warn. The library's consent rule for dangerous content is that "the warning layer is
  world-building that doubles as consent": testimony, visible activity on roads (`Q146.P4`). And the
  world can reply in city, never in interface: "Health bars, locked shelters, a newspaper headline"
  (`Q149.W9`).
- A place can call from a distance. New Vegas's roof quest "arrives by broadcast, from a dead machine",
  receivable from three districts (`Q101.W3`, `Q101.P4`); Project Zomboid's world "explains itself
  through its own dying media" (`Q088.W8`). On our map this is the loop that reaches you on the road; on
  the street it is a speaker in a doorway.

### 2. THE PERSON IS SHOWN DOING SOMETHING BEFORE THEY SAY ANYTHING

- "Character established in staging, not dialogue": Yennefer is met "GAINING THE UPPER HAND ON SOME
  UNRULY YOKELS", and the port asks that "our district's NPCs should introduce themselves through what
  they're doing when you find them" (`Q095.W3`, `Q095.P6`).
- The greatest ask in the library is from a man who stood in the same place for 24 days and never asked
  for anything: Sergiu, the guard outside the booth (`Q108.W1`, `Q108.P2`). The ask has weight because the
  body was there first, doing its job. The ask itself is through glass (`Q108.N1`).
- Every person has a life that is not about you: "wake, work, tavern, home, sleep ... they are there
  because they are" (`Q083.W1`); the world "doesn't revolve around you" and treats YOU as the stranger
  (`Q051.W2`); Majora's townsfolk keep their timetables into the apocalypse (`Q011.W1`, `Q128.W2`). The
  flaw on the other side: citizens that "idle" between quests read as "a diorama between story beats"
  (`Q149.X3`).
- A person who wants something already acts on it. Kenshi and S.T.A.L.K.E.R. show NPCs with names,
  inventories and plans (`Q085.W2`), and S.T.A.L.K.E.R. shipped NPCs finishing side quests themselves
  (`Q085.W3`). For our asks: the woman with the empty shelf is dusting it; the man in the queue is
  holding his number.

### 3. WHAT THE PERSON SAYS FIRST (see the catalogue in section 9)

- Open with the job, not the sadness. The Baron's rule: "Don't open with 'here's a sad story', open
  with a job, let the sadness grow underneath" (`Q001.W1`). His meeting states the trade immediately:
  he has intel, he wants "a small favor" (`Q001.N1`).
- The first line can carry the verdict of the whole situation. Tali's aunt renames her in a friendly
  greeting in the first thirty seconds: "the trial's outcome is announced by how someone says hello"
  (`Q125.W2`); the port turns it into a naming grammar for our factions (`Q125.P6`).
- The first line can be true in a way the speaker does not know. Deionarra: "Perhaps in death I still
  hold some shred of use for you...? ...my Love." She means bitterness; it is the mechanism (`Q120.P3`),
  and she opens with rage and forgives in the same breath (`Q120.N1`).
- The honest devil opens with the full pitch, "in full, without hedging" (`Q126.N2`), and says what he
  wants it FOR (`Q126.N3`). Clarity is its own menace.
- The thing that knows why you came "bids before you speak" (`Q004.N1`): the ask can pre-empt the
  player's own reason for being there.
- Some places greet with hands. Pathologic's station: "Combat-as-greeting. The town speaks first with
  hands" (`Q098.N1`); Papyrus's first attack is "DESIGNED TO MISS (a greeting)" (`Q117.N1`). For us this is
  an EVENT on the road, not an ask on the street, and it is noted here only as the boundary.
- Register can smuggle the kindness: Sheogorath's tea table states the deal "inside three jokes" and the
  warm fact is "never stated, only staged" (`Q135.N1`). The flat answer is its own register: "Are you the
  curse?" / "I make dice." (`Q097.N3`).

### 4. WHAT THE PERSON WITHHOLDS (the why and the risk, never the terms)

- The institutional lie is in the passive voice. Hildern hires you with "crop data, vault, fee" and
  withholds the last team; asked, he says "records are incomplete". And the question the quest turns on,
  "Is the data safe to bring back?", "cannot be asked at the desk. It can only be answered in the vault"
  (`Q003.N1`). That is the model withholding: a real question the counter cannot host.
- The patron omits "the one fact they cannot survive stating" (`Q152.W1`, `Q152.P2`).
- The pretext is allowed to drop later: the missing mage was about the djinn all along, and "THIS TIME
  SHE'LL BE FORTHCOMING" (`Q095.W4`); the port values "a quest that admits it lied to you, at the right
  moment" (`Q095.P6`).
- The first lie is free: the Baron's "kidnapped" costs nothing "the first time. That's what makes it a
  lie" (`Q001.N1`).
- Withholding must be MOTIVATED. Catherine hides the copy-truth because she needs Simon compliant
  (`Q034.W8`); where the game withholds for the author's convenience instead, players call it a
  contrivance (`Q034.X2`).
- Some things nobody can say: Isidor's list comes with "NO explanation" (`Q113.W2`), and the flaw is that
  it is "NEVER EXPLAINED IN TIME" (`Q113.X5`). Dark Souls' opening "tells you everything except the WHY"
  (`Q091.W7`). The line: withhold the why, never the what.
- The terms, by contrast, are stated clean. Raphael's OFFER_TERMS: "He states them clean. No subclause.
  No trick. The contract says what he said", and that node "exists to remove your excuse" (`Q126.N5`,
  `Q126.N6`). Our own asset landed the same rule from the other side: "terms you find out after you
  agreed are not terms" (records/BOHEMIA_THE_JOB_SAYS_WHAT_IT_PAYS_9_11_26.md).
- The NOVERB is a withholding tool: the question a decent person would ask is simply not on the list.
  Dishonored: "What happens to her after?" cannot be asked (`Q014.N2`); Raphael: you "cannot ask what it
  costs everyone who isn't in the room" (`Q126.N4`); the paranoid client has no node for "What if you're
  wrong?" because HE lacks it (`Q130.N1`, `Q130.W6`).

### 5. BODY, LOCATION AND TIMING DO THE WORK

- Where the person stands is part of the ask. Sergiu asks through glass (`Q108.N1`); Anju asks in her
  kitchen at 11:30 PM, trusting "the SHAPE" of her fiancé's mask (`Q128.N1`); the resemblance itself is a
  price the port wants us to charge (`Q128.P4`). The paranoid hires you at a chapel at midnight through a
  passed note, and the staging "is the diagnosis" (`Q130.N1`, `Q130.W9`).
- Timing can be a window nobody tells you about: Sergiu's favor has two days (`Q108.W8`); Godwin's
  appointment is "time as a gate with no UI emphasis" (`Q106.N1`) and missing it silently deletes the
  best content in the act (`Q106.X5`). Majora builds a whole quest from WHEN (`Q128.W1`) and pays for it
  with guide dependence (`Q128.X1`). The library's own caveat: "make timed windows GENEROUS + legible"
  (`Q011.P12`). Our shape is "at your pace", so windows are the exception, and a missed one leaves ONE
  diegetic tell, "an uncollected letter in the box, a light left on" (`Q128.P3`, `Q128.X2`).
- How help is offered decides whether it is accepted. "HELP DECLARED IS HELP DENIED" (`Q138.W2`); the
  child at the door lies at eye level and "the offer of help CONFIRMS the secret" (`Q138.N1`). Sticky
  refuses medicine as charity and accepts it as work: "AID HAS A GRAMMAR" (`Q113.N1`). This matters for
  our asks in reverse: the person asking has dignity too, and their ask should be phrased as a job, not a
  plea.
- The honest sentence can be useless: "You need a healer, not a spy" closes the paranoid's quest
  (`Q130.N1`, `Q130.W7`). Early truth at the counter is a valid choice with a real result.

### 6. THE PRICE: STATED, FIXED, ARGUED

- The single most useful design in the library for contracts: "THE FEE LOCKS BEFORE THE TRUTH. Haggling
  at maximum ignorance separates money from morality forever after" (`Q148.W1`). The port makes it a hard
  schema rule: "no fee renegotiation node may exist after stage 2" (`Q148.P1`). The client's closure is
  then the same whatever you chose (`Q148.W9`), which is what makes the job a PROFESSION.
- Haggling that feels like leverage and is not: "The terms do not move, and you have now told him you're
  a seller" (`Q126.N7`, `Q126.N8`). A price asked by a patient power is not for arguing.
- A toll with alternatives: Helsik takes the gold, or half, or an artifact, or you bypass her (`Q126.N16`),
  and you can pay the toll with the proceeds of the heist (`Q126.W8`, `Q126.P27`).
- Some asks have no market. Sergiu's favor: "no price can be discussed; the favor has no market"
  (`Q108.N1`). The Baron's intel "is not for sale, only for work" (`Q001.N1`). A contract says what it
  pays; a favor between neighbours says nothing about pay, and trying to price it is the wrong verb.
- The price of admission can be the client's frame: you must claim to be a psychic investigator to be
  hired (`Q097.W3`, `Q097.N1`), and asking "Has anyone checked the books?" costs warmth.
- Extortion can be a subscription: the bounty hunter lets you live for a weekly bar tab (`Q109.W3`,
  `Q109.N1`). That is an ask where the person IS the price.
- Rewards must not decide the moral: Whispering Hillock pays the same either way (`Q004.W4`); Disco pays
  70 XP for the truth or the lie (`Q097.W10`). When the good choice also pays more, "you built a puzzle
  with a nice skin" (`Q108.X2`).
- New Vegas routes a bribe through a skill: "[Barter 45] Heritage is expensive. Pay me." (`Q002.N1`).
  That is the player naming a price to the giver, the reverse haggle.
- OUR ASSET (engine/bohemia_haggle.js, 9/11): the Battle Brothers shape without its numbers. The job says
  what it pays BEFORE the yes (sayPay lands before ask). You may ask for two KINDS of thing: A DIFFERENT
  ONE (the same one unit in another currency) or UP FRONT (the same one, now). "Two asks land. The third
  ends it." It is deterministic and said out loud before the ask that would cost you, and EVERYTHING
  COSTS ONE holds. This fits the library: the fee locks at the counter, before the truth (`Q148.W1`); it
  can move only in shape, never in size; and a third push ends the conversation the way Raphael's terms
  refuse to move (`Q126.N8`).

### 7. THE FREE NO

- The best refusal in the library: "He is unbothered. The offer stands. He'll be at the brothel ...
  LOCKS: NOTHING" and "a refusal that costs nothing and forecloses nothing is rarer than any branch in
  this file. Most games punish 'no' to make the 'yes' feel weighty" (`Q126.N12`, `Q126.N13`). The craft
  claim: "An unpunished 'no' is a temptation that lives in the save file" (`Q126.W3`).
- The same shape three more times: Boethiah's "And if I refuse?" / "the quest simply waits, forever"
  (`Q131.N1`, `Q131.W4`); Molag Bal's "I refuse" loops with "no punishment, no escalation" (`Q134.N1`);
  Kenshi's flee option "never closes" and that is "what makes staying a CHOICE" (`Q151.W7`).
- The exit is at the door. "Kill her at hello and it's over, free. Every subsequent step is priced in the
  distance from that door" (`Q133.W3`), and the port: put "the clean exit at the DOOR" (`Q133.P3`).
  Sinnerman's family can have him and "the quest respects the refusal totally" (`Q122.N1`). The off-ramp
  before the fight is real and "the scene proves the player CAN stop, so that continuing means what it
  means" (`Q140.W8`), recorded "WITHOUT weight" (`Q140.P4`).
- This is exactly Paolo's contract rule: free at the door, a deed after the yes. The library's own
  counter-examples show why the line must be at the yes and not later: Sinnerman has "~50 back-out
  points" and makes completion "a CHOICE you keep making" (`Q040.W2`), but also "early-exit branches
  with real lockouts" (`Q040.W10`). Boethiah's walk-back runs to the altar (`Q131.W5`). We choose the door
  (`Q133.P3`) because Paolo chose it: once taken, you finish it.
- A no must not be answered with hurt. Baldur's Gate 2's benched companions "act hurt" and the study calls
  it guilt-inducing (`Q043.X1`); Vault City throws you out for questioning (`Q100.X1`); Kingdom Come
  "punishes abstinence" (`Q106.X6`); Dragon's Dogma makes refusing "a losing button" (`Q084.X5`); the
  Dark Brotherhood has "NO REFUSAL" (`Q103.X1`). Skyrim's only true refusal is abandoning a journal entry
  forever, which "renders as an itch, not a stand" (`Q134.X10`). For us the itch is gone because there is
  no journal: the offer is a person standing on a street, and you can walk past them.
- Walking away mid-scene pauses, never fails (`Q114.N2`), and substories "cannot be missed" in
  completion terms (`Q114.W3`).

### 8. VOLUME: MOST JOBS ARE JUST JOBS

- "The job system's moral forks must be a MINORITY of contracts, on an unpredictable schedule. Most jobs
  should be exactly what they say" (`Q148.X3`), rationed at generation level (`Q148.P4`). Our six designs
  below are ask-moments, and at most one twist each; in the running game most asks carry none.
- Yakuza's typed spec (Normal / Business / Minigame / Training / Shakedown) is the proof that a hundred
  asks need a small vocabulary (`Q114.W4`), and its padding is the warning (`Q114.X1`). Range across a
  batch should be checked, not hoped for (`Q114.N4`). Every light ask still needs a human core
  (`Q013.W4`).

Two rules for every first line, from the library: it must be about the speaker and the place (never "I
have a quest for you", never the player's name unless the speaker has a reason to know it), and it must
be true to that person's register, including their Spanglish (the player does not speak Spanglish,
THE FIRST VOTES). The flaw on the other side is the lore dump: long talky chunks lose players
(`Q068.X3`), and a climax delivered as a wall of text has to make the delivery itself the reward
(`Q111.X6`). On a phone screen, an opening line is one sentence.

## 11. THE SHAPE FOR OUR ASKS AT THE PLACE (was: ON THE CLOSE STREET)

The player arrives from the map onto the place's settlement screen (swept 9/29: not a walked street). The shape below is
what the library says an ask there should be, bound to our laws (32a: nothing forced, nothing in the
first 60 s, never forced (walking past is now not tapping); rule 19: a mouth with a portrait; and the 9/27 contract shape: declining is free).

1. ARRIVE. The first 60 seconds after load are silent by law. After travel, arrival is quiet too: the
   settlement is looked at before anyone looks back (the library's reason: the place speaks first,
   `Q114.N1`). Nobody comes toward him. Nobody's line fires on arrival.
2. SEE. The ask is a PLACE THAT ASKS (section 10) with a person in it, doing their thing (`Q095.W3`,
   `Q083.W1`). No marker, no icon, no floating text (`Q114.W2`, `Q023.X4`). What makes it findable is the
   drawing: a settlement that holds an ask must look different from one that does not (`Q037.W2`, the
   fairness fix).
3. APPROACH. The person does not stop him. When he taps their building they look up (a delay, per the
   second votes' gate). They may say ONE ambient line to the air, the FL01 kind, which is overheard, not
   addressed (32a: "a rumour is OVERHEARD"). If he taps elsewhere, that is the whole interaction.
4. OPEN LINE. Only if he taps them. Portrait up, one sentence, the FL pattern of that ask. About them and
   the place.
5. TERMS. Before any yes: WHAT, WHERE (a place on the map or in this settlement), WHAT IT PAYS (one, in one
   currency), in that person's mouth. The why and the risk may be withheld (`Q003.N1`); the terms may not
   (`Q126.N5`). The fee is fixed now and never renegotiated after the yes (`Q148.W1`, `Q148.P1`).
6. ARGUE (optional). The haggle asset's two kinds: A DIFFERENT ONE, or UP FRONT. Two land; the third
   ends it, and the person says so out loud before it would (engine/bohemia_haggle.js). A favor with no
   price has no haggle (`Q108.N1`).
7. YES OR NO. Two words. YES makes it a contract: the pay lands from their hand when he comes back, and
   dropping it is a deed. NO: one neutral line, they go back to what they were doing, the offer stays
   where it stood until the WORLD changes it (`Q126.W3`, `Q023.W6`), and nothing is written.
8. THE RESULT IS IN THE PLACE. When the job is done the place that asked changes where he can see it:
   the shelf has cans, the window is lit (rule 19b, `Q149.W9`, `Q147.W8`).

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)

1. THE PLACE FIRST. Every ask is bound to a PLACE THAT ASKS: a building on the settlement screen whose
   drawing differs from the same settlement without the ask. Test: render the settlement screen with and
   without the ask; the difference must be visible at phone size without any person present. (`Q114.N1`, `Q147.W7`, `Q023.W4`)
2. NOBODY SPEAKS FIRST TO HIM IN THE FIRST 60 S, AND NOBODY ON ARRIVAL. Test: the speech log is empty for
   60 s after load and for a set delay after every arrival from the map. (32a; `Q114.N1` SEEN gates)
3. THE PERSON IS DOING SOMETHING. Every asker has an idle action at their place that is not "stand and
   wait for the player". Test: no asker's idle clip is the default stand. (`Q095.W3`, `Q083.W1`, `Q149.X3`)
4. NO STOP. An asker never blocks a building, never opens a screen on him, never follows him to the map.
   Test: no asker's line or portrait appears on a screen he did not open. (Swept 9/29: the old test, about
   cells on a walked street, died with the walk.) (32a; `Q013.W6`)
5. THE ASK OPENS ON HIS TAP, NEVER ON PROXIMITY. Proximity allows at most one ambient line, not addressed
   to him, once per visit. Test: no line with a second-person ask fires without a tap. (32a)
6. A MOUTH AND A FACE. Every ask line has a speaker id and a portrait bound. (rule 19, THE FACE PERFORMS)
7. THE FIRST LINE IS ONE SENTENCE ABOUT THEM OR THE PLACE, tagged with an FL id. Test: the opener has no
   "you" as its subject and no quest word ("quest", "task", "mission", "contract"). (`Q001.W1`, `Q068.X3`)
8. TERMS BEFORE YES. WHAT, WHERE and PAYS are all spoken before the yes option appears. Test: the yes
   node is unreachable from any path that skips the pay line. (`Q126.N5`, `Q126.N6`, the 9/11 disclosure
   rule)
9. THE FEE LOCKS AT THE YES. No node after the yes can change the amount or currency. Test: no post-yes
   node writes the pay. (`Q148.W1`, `Q148.P1`)
10. THE HAGGLE HAS TWO KINDS AND A SPOKEN LIMIT. Different currency or up front; two land; the warning is
    spoken before the third. Test: the asset's own gate. (engine/bohemia_haggle.js; `Q126.N8`)
11. THE NO IS FREE AND NEUTRAL. The no line carries no guilt, no sigh, no "everybody's busy", no
    reference to the player's worth. It writes no deed, no standing change, no rumour. The asker returns
    to their idle action. Test: the no branch writes nothing to the deed ledger, standing or feed; a
    word-list check on no lines for guilt markers. (`Q126.N12`, `Q126.N13`, `Q043.X1`, `Q106.X6`)
12. THE OFFER STAYS UNTIL THE WORLD MOVES. A refused ask is still there when he comes back, unless the
    world has changed the place (someone else filled the shelf). Test: a refused ask persists across a
    map trip unless its place changed. (`Q126.W3`, `Q131.W4`, `Q085.W3`)
13. THE YES IS THE LINE. After the yes the contract is his to finish; dropping it is a deed and the only
    one. (Paolo 9/27; `Q133.W3`, `Q133.P3`)
14. THE RESULT IS VISIBLE IN THE PLACE. The completion writes a change to the same building that asked. Test:
    before and after renders differ at the ask's place. (rule 19; `Q149.W9`, `Q147.W8`)
15. ONE TWIST, RATIONED. At most one twist per ask, and most asks none. Test: the generator's twist rate
    per batch stays under a set minority. (`Q148.X3`, `Q148.P4`)
16. REWARD DOES NOT PICK THE MORAL. Where an ask has a moral fork, the endings pay the same one.
    (`Q004.W4`, `Q097.W10`, `Q108.X2`)

17. THE ASK COMES FROM SOMEONE WHO NEEDS YOU. (folded 10/9 from QR-U) Purpose arrives as a small grounded task from someone who needs

## OPEN (what the library could not answer)

1. THE HAGGLE AND THE FREE NO MEET IN ONE PLACE. Our asset writes a deed when a haggle is pushed past its
   limit and the offer withdraws. The library supports the withdrawal (`Q126.N8`) but Paolo's 9/27 rule
   says nobody remembers a no and only a taken-and-dropped contract is a deed. A pushed haggle is not a
   taken contract. Our reading: the withdrawal stays, the deed goes. That is a mechanism call for the
   building lane (RUN/ECONOMY), flagged here, not decided.
2. HOW MANY ASKS A SETTLEMENT HOLDS. The library has density (`Q013.W1`, a hundred substories) and the
   hoarding flaw (`Q037.X2`), but no number for a phone screen. PLUMBER and RUN measure it on the settlement
   screen (QR-O answers most of this since).
3. WHETHER A REFUSED OFFER EVER EXPIRES. Raphael's never does (`Q126.W3`); S.T.A.L.K.E.R. lets NPCs take
   the job themselves (`Q085.W3`). Both fit the free no. Which one a given ask uses is content.
4. THE ACROSS-ACT ASK. An ask refused in act 1 cannot write a deed, so it cannot be planted for act 3.
   Only a TAKEN contract can carry across (question (c)'s page owns this).
5. THE PORTRAIT ON A SMALL FIGURE. The library's staging assumes a readable body at play scale. Ours is a
   figure drawn in a settlement's one view (swept 9/29: the one-cell body died with the walk). How much of
   "shown doing something" survives at that size is a CHARACTER/ANIMATION question; the portrait carries
   the face, the drawing carries the action.
