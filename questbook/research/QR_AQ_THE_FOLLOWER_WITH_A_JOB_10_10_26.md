# QR-AQ: THE FOLLOWER WITH A JOB. WHEN A MEMBER OF YOUR COMPANY IS THE CLIENT, AND WHEN THEIR OWN TROUBLE IS THE CONTRACT

QUESTION: row [eighth shelf] (QUESTS, research only). Followers (QR-AD) and company members already exist on paper as
people who change a rule. This page asks four things the library can answer:
1. How should a member of your company (a fighter or a follower) bring a job to the settlement offer screen?
2. When does a follower's own trouble become a contract?
3. What breaks when a companion quest is a loyalty gate?
4. How does each of these carry across the three acts, given the canon: followers age BETWEEN acts only (rule 84), the
   company inherits (rule 40), and the main character never dies (the third votes)?

EVENTS (the Thief steals, the Cultist recruits a brother, the Hedge Knight demands respect), and those events change
morale, items, traits, or make a man leave [R, 08_CONTRACTS_EVENTS.md and 06_BACKGROUNDS.md]. Its contracts always come
from a stranger in the hall [R]. So the bridge this page builds is new: a company member's background or want becomes
the SOURCE of an ordinary hall contract, offered on the ordinary screen, with the member standing beside the client.

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

A member of your company brings a job the same way a stranger does: on the settlement's offer screen, in the hall,
with a portrait, two to four lines, a fee locked before the yes, and a free no. The difference is one line and one
face: the member stands beside the client (or IS the client, when the trouble is their own), and the screen says why
this job is theirs. The library is unanimous that companions are people with their own arcs (`Q043.W7`, `Q043.P5`,
`Q066.W6`) and that the strongest side content is load-bearing for the people you travel with (`Q006.W2`). It is
equally unanimous about what goes wrong: loyalty kept as a hidden meter turns a person into a min-max surface
(`Q132.X8`, `Q121.X2`), loyalty as a binary flag punishes one bad week forever (`Q137.X2`, `Q137.X9`), an approval
system can fight the companion's own arc (`Q076.X3`), invisible rules make the wiki mandatory (`Q137.X1`, `Q071.X1`),
and a timer can make players postpone character content (`Q121.X6`). So Bohemia's rule is: a follower's trouble
becomes a contract when it has a PLACE, a THING and a STRANGER on the other side, so it can be offered in a hall like
any job; it is never a gate on the follower's rule, their staying, or the main line; declining is free and the
follower says nothing about it; and the payoff is a picture or a person that changes (`Q236.W8`, `Q151.W10`), never a
number going up. Across acts, a follower's job does not die with the act: the member ages at the turn, their heir or
apprentice may carry the unfinished thing (`Q144.W7`, `Q144.W2`), and in act 3, when the company is the heirs of
heirs, the best contracts are the ones where the job of a grandparent's follower is finally finishable.

## 1. HOW A COMPANY MEMBER BRINGS A JOB TO THE OFFER SCREEN

### 1.1 The client must be a person with a want, and a follower is the cheapest way to get one
Battle Brothers' own weak point is the client: a portrait and a mood with no want beyond the fee, no memory beyond a
relations number (`Q236.X3`). The face is already the negotiation UI (`Q236.W4`), and the board is already the world's
current problems (`Q236.W1`). What is missing is a reason the player CARES who is asking. The library's answer is that
the people you already travel with are where the caring is stored: attachment is manufactured by labor and hours, not
by authored backstory (`Q151.W9`); companions are slow-burn wells drawn out over voyages (`Q066.W6`); the party is a
bundle of stories (`Q043.W7`). A follower standing beside a client converts the stranger's fee into a friend's
favor without adding a single new screen.

### 1.2 The quest-giver can be a coworker, not a companion
The cleanest example in the library is not a romance companion at all. It is a coworker who stands outside your booth
for 24 days and then asks one favor (`Q108.W1`). That is our follower: the cook, the medic, the driver, at the fire every
night, asking once, plainly, for one thing. It also matches how mundane intimacy builds bonds (`Q063.W2`): the ask
lands because of the ordinary nights before it. The follower is "functional" (they change a rule, QR-AD), and
humanizing the functional is exactly what turns a system into a person (`Q022.W5`).

### 1.3 Three ways a member stands on the screen (draft shapes for RUN [settlement screen])
- THE MEMBER IS THE CLIENT. Their own trouble, with a place and a stranger on the other side (section 2). The portrait
  is the member; the screen's last line names who the job is against or for. Pay comes from the member's own share or
  from the people the job helps, never from nowhere.
- THE MEMBER STANDS BESIDE THE CLIENT. A stranger in the hall offers an ordinary job, and a member's background makes it
  theirs: the medic knows the clinic that is asking, the driver used to drive the road. The screen shows two portraits;
  the member speaks one line. The fee and the job are the stranger's; the member's line is the reason to care.
- THE MEMBER CARRIES THE OFFER IN. A follower who visits other settlements (the trader, the walker) brings back a job
  from a place the company has not reached. It still opens on that place's offer screen when the party arrives; the
  follower only marks it on the phone board (the board that only SHOWS, rule 51).

### 1.4 What the screen must never do
- Never hide the rules. Who is affected, what it costs, what changes, all on the screen before the yes (`Q137.X1`,
  `Q236.W3`). The accept button is the only promise (`Q236.W7`).
- Never put the follower's rule on the line. The medic's rule works whether or not you took the medic's job. This is
  the anti-gate (section 3).
- Never take the fee after the yes, and never guilt the no (the round-one agreement, QR-AF's rule 4). The decline is
  free in the ledger AND on the treasury: keep a second income so crunch does not force the yes (`Q236.X4`).

## 2. WHEN A FOLLOWER'S OWN TROUBLE BECOMES A CONTRACT

### 2.1 The test: a place, a thing, a stranger
A follower's trouble is a contract only when it can be done on the map and the fight board like any other job. The
library's strongest personal arcs are all tied to things in the world, not to talk: an arc triggered by a statue, a
rescued child, an object that hurts (`Q076.W4`); a medicine clock with a real place where the pills are (`Q119.W3`,
`Q119.W4`); a father in a database you must physically reach (`Q119.W9`). When the trouble is only a feeling, it is a
conversation at the fire (QR-AD's "one small moment", `Q043.W7`), not a contract. When it has a PLACE (a clinic, a pump
house, a yard), a THING (a ledger, a tool, a body, a truck) and a STRANGER who has it or wants it, it goes to the hall.

### 2.2 The world triggers it, not a menu
A personal job should surface from contact with the world: the party passes the follower's old town, a named enemy
is sighted, a held base gains a clinic (`Q076.W4`, `Q071.W6`). That is how it reaches the hall without a pop-up: the
follower notices, the next visit to the right settlement shows the offer with their face on it. Reputation that is a
memory, not a bar (`Q086.W8`), is the same principle: the world cites what actually happened.

### 2.3 The member asks for permission to choose, not for you to choose for them
The best companion resolution in the library is the player giving the companion the right to choose (`Q076.W5`). A
follower's contract should end with THEIR call at the end of the job (keep the ledger, burn it, bring the man home),
not the player's. The player's choices are about how the job is done (fight, trade, quiet), on the ordinary pattern.
The companion as a protagonist (`Q043.W7`) means they get the last word in their own story.

### 2.4 The trouble may be ugly, and the screen must let you say so
A follower's request can be wrong. Tali asks you to bury her father's record and the game never lets you say his
conduct killed people (`Q125.X10`). A follower contract must have room for the honest no (free) and, once taken, for
the other way to do it. Moderation is complicity in the library's own reading (`Q081.W6`), and privilege is shown in
a household (`Q081.W4`): our followers are the household, and their asks can show who they think is beneath them.

### 2.5 The person you help becomes a supply line
When a follower's job is done well, the result is a person or place that keeps paying the company in small ways
(`Q119.W8`): the medic's old clinic sends 2 MEDS when you pass, the driver's brother lets the cart refuel. This is the
Battle Brothers outcome-changes-the-map rule (`Q236.W8`) at family size, and QR-AP's picture change (the client stands
in front of the changed thing for one visit).

### 2.6 Three levels in one job
A personal job hits the person, the follower's arc and the world at once (`Q016.W4`). For us: the follower (one line
changes, one look changes), the company (one of the six resources), the settlement (one picture). Never more than
three things written; never zero.

## 3. WHAT BREAKS WHEN A COMPANION QUEST IS A LOYALTY GATE

The library studied the most famous loyalty system in games twice (`Q006`, `Q137`), and its most famous approval
systems several more times. The good is real and the bad is specific.

### 3.1 The good, which we keep without the gate
- Side content as survival: the friend's job matters because the friend's life hangs on it (`Q006.W2`).
- Being KNOWN by your commander as protection (`Q137.W3`): our version is the medic who knows which fighter takes
  pain badly, which is a follower's craft, not a meter.
- Every death is a receipt with a paper trail (`Q137.W1`), and permadeath is staged as a specific failure
  (`Q006.W5`). Rule 36 already gives us this: struck down is 20% death, else 30 to 40 days hurt.
- Role assignment tests whether you know your people (`Q006.W4`), and a roll call faces you with who you saved
  (`Q006.W8`).

### 3.2 The breakages
1. THE META-SURFACE. The moment approval is a number, every conversation becomes "who do I please" (`Q132.X8`), and a
   moral dilemma becomes a build decision (`Q121.X2`). Our no-karma-bar law exists for this. Virtue simulated, not
   scored: people saw what you did, members who quit (`Q083.W6`).
2. THE FUSE. Loyalty as one binary flag that one bad argument flips forever (`Q137.X2`) and that gives no partial
   credit to the human middle (`Q137.X9`). Real relationships live in the middle.
3. THE INVISIBLE RUBRIC. Players lose loved people to hidden rules and learn them from a wiki later (`Q137.X1`); a
   companion line can be broken by an unrelated act and the game never tells you (`Q071.X1`, `Q071.X4`).
4. THE ARC THAT FIGHTS ITSELF. The approval system rewards the opposite of what the arc wants (`Q076.X3`). If the
   medic "approves" of fights where nobody gets hurt, the player stops taking hard contracts, and the medic's own arc
   (learning to lose people) can never happen.
5. THE POSTPONED FRIEND. A timer on the critical path makes the optimal play "do not do the companion quest yet"
   (`Q121.X6`), and a hidden crew timer punishes the thoroughness the game taught (`Q137.X3`).
6. THE COSMETIC TURN. The choice that "earns loyalty" has no effect, or the companion is loyal either way (`Q121.X3`,
   `Q121.X1`). A gate that cannot close is a lie.
7. THE NAGGING. A crude reputation hook makes a subtle companion repeat complaints (`Q043.X2`), and relentless barks
   ruin the best idea in the game (`Q084.X6`).
8. THE GUILTY BENCH. A slot cap where benched people cry (`Q043.X1`); the bank's fix is a home base where benched
   people wait happily (`Q043.P9`).
9. THE MISSING ENDING. Endings gated by sidequest completion (`Q063.X5`): miss one follower's job and you miss an
   ending. Our endings (LIBERATE / RESPECT / BECOME) must never require a follower's job.
10. THE OFFSCREEN EXIT. The best companion simply vanishes with no scene (`Q105.X5`). A follower who leaves gets a
    portrait and a line, always.
11. THE TRUST PRICED FLAT. Housecarl, hireling and friend all count the same (`Q131.X1`), and no one ever finds out
    (`Q131.X2`). If a job asks who matters, the answer must differ by person.

### 3.3 So, the rule
Loyalty is not a number and not a gate. A follower's job, taken or not, never changes whether the follower stays,
whether their rule works, or whether a main beat opens. What a finished job DOES change is a person and a picture:
one new line, one new look, one supply line, one place that remembers (section 2.5). What a TAKEN and DROPPED job
changes is the only thing that costs: a deed the follower remembers in one line, once, in character (never a nag).
This is the BB contract rule (failing after accept is what costs, `Q236.W7`) applied to a friend.

## 4. ACROSS THE THREE ACTS

### 4.1 The canon this has to fit
- Followers age BETWEEN acts only (rule 84). Inside an act nobody visibly ages. At the turn, a follower is found older,
  gone, or replaced by an heir who OFFERS to take the place (QR-AD 3.6).
- The company inherits (rule 40): each act starts with the heirs of the last act's company; gear stays in the family.
- The main character never dies; a veteran dies at 10% (the third votes); a member struck down: 20% dead, else 30 to 40
  days hurt (rule 36). A follower never fights (QR-AD rule 1), so a follower never dies on the board.
- All three acts are open at once after the flip unlocks (rule 85); the future is derived from the past ledgers.

### 4.2 What the library says about jobs that cross time
- Inherited people speak of the last head in past tense (`Q144.W7`); knowledge is what crosses best (`Q144.W2`), and
  the refusals and stranded arcs are what make the line real (`Q144.W3`). An unfinished follower job in act 1 is a
  stranded arc; act 2 or act 3 can offer it again to the heir.
- Persistent companions across generations anchor the fold while leads change (`Q067.W6`), but the same study warns
  that if the characters are thin the generations mean nothing (`Q067.X2`).
- The household outlives the head: staff as the realm's spine (`Q152.W8`, `Q152.P4`). In our game the followers ARE
  that staff.
- Companions carry what they learned between worlds (`Q084.W2`); the servant can inherit the hero's life if it
  learned to grieve (`Q084.W9`).
- A father's real apprentice can be a kid you never knew about (`Q113.W3`): the heir of a follower may not be blood.
- The heir is a person, not a respawn (`Q144.W4`), and the scion is paid for with present strength (`Q144.W5`).
- Permanent loss at the start of a generation gives instant stakes (`Q043.W9`).

### 4.3 The three-act shape (draft for DYNASTY and PEOPLE)
- ACT 1 (anarchy decade): follower jobs are small and close: one tool, one debt, one missing person. Pay is low and
  mostly FOOD, WATER, TAPE. A follower job is how the player first learns a follower is a person.
- ACT 2 (the world clawing back): the follower's job meets institutions: a clinic that wants a license, a pump board, a
  faction ledger. The heirs of act-1 followers arrive with past-tense lines, and an act-1 job left unfinished can come
  back as their offer. Pay mixes MEDS and BATTERIES.
- ACT 3 (cyberpunk healing, the rocket, the Network crumbling): the company is the heirs of heirs. This is where the
  library's generational findings pay most, so the shelf is weighted here (8 of 20 designs). Act-3 follower jobs are
  three kinds: (a) FINISHING a grandparent follower's job with tools the past never had (the clinic now has power,
  the archive is now readable); (b) jobs against the Network's offer of a counterfeit family (the Amalgamation offers
  to keep the old cook "alive"; the true family is the costly one, the core theme); (c) the rocket: who stays on the
  ground, which follower's craft goes up. Pay is BATTERIES, MEDS, and rounds for the last fights.
- ACROSS: a job that opens in act 1 and is only finishable in act 3, through the flip (Ocarina is the reference for
  this only). The act-1 follower asks; the act-3 heir finishes; the act-1 picture changes when the player flips back.

### 4.4 The main character never dies, so the follower is where death in the family lives
Because the head of the family cannot die on the board and followers never fight, the generation turn (rule 84) is
the only place age takes a follower. That makes the turn the moment every follower job is re-read: finished jobs
appear as a look or a line on the heir; unfinished ones appear as an offer. Nothing is lost silently (`Q105.X5`).

## 5. PAIRS: A FOLLOWER JOB SHARES A ROAD (rule 51)

Rule 51 gives two contract slots. QR-AE's pairing law (two jobs share a road, a twist in one may touch the other)
applies unchanged. A follower job is the BEST second slot, because it is personal and short, and the first slot is
the stranger's job going the same way. Every design on this shelf names its pair: either an existing design (QD-AE,
QD-AP and others) or another AQ design. The phone board shows the pair as two pins on one road, no more (QR-AE 4).

## 6. THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)

1. A follower or company member's job is offered ONLY on a settlement's offer screen, in a building (the hall, or the
   building that fits). Test: no follower job has a trigger outside a settlement screen.
2. The offer screen of a follower job shows the member's portrait, either as the client or beside the client, and one
   line in their voice. Test: every follower-job row has a member speaker id and a line.
3. The fee is shown and locked before accept, in named units of the six resources. Test: the fee field is set and
   read-only after accept.
4. NOT NOW is always there and writes nothing; the member never mentions a decline. Test: save diff after decline is
   empty; no line keyed to a declined follower job exists.
5. A follower job never gates the follower's rule, their staying, a main beat, a boss verb, or an ending. Test: no
   condition anywhere reads a follower-job flag except that job's own outcome lines and pictures.
6. No loyalty, approval or affinity number exists. Test: the data schema has no such field.
7. A follower job needs a place, a thing and a stranger. Test: every row has all three fields filled.
8. The follower makes the final call in their own story (keep, burn, bring home). Test: every follower-job row's end
   node is spoken by the member.
9. A finished job changes at most three things: one member line or look, one resource or supply line, one picture.
   Test: the outcome row lists 1 to 3 writes.
10. A taken and dropped job writes one deed, remembered in ONE line, ONCE, in character. Test: the dropped-job line has
    a play count limit of 1.
11. Reactive follower lines carry a cooldown (`Q084.X6`). Test: no follower line repeats within TUNING's N rests.
12. An unfinished follower job at the generation turn becomes an OFFER from the follower's heir in the next act, never
    an auto-carried task. Test: after the turn, the job list is empty until accepted.
13. An inherited follower's job lines speak of the previous head in past tense (`Q144.W7`). Test: every crossing row has
    a past-tense variant.
14. A follower who leaves gets a portrait and a line on screen (never offscreen). Test: every leave path has a scene id.
15. Follower jobs pair: every row names a pair contract on the same road. Test: pair field set.
16. Act 3 carries at least 40% of follower jobs (this shelf: 8 of 20). Test: count by ACT in the data file.

## 8. FLAGS FOR THE LANES

- RUN [settlement screen]: the offer screen needs a second portrait slot (the member beside the client) and a member
  speaker id. Two contract slots already exist (rule 51); a follower job uses one.
- PEOPLE [followers]: each of QR-AD's 24 drafts needs at most one personal job row (place, thing, stranger, pay, pair,
  end line). Not every follower has one; uneven richness is a flaw (`Q043.X4`), so the first pass is 8 followers with
  one job each, spread across the three acts.
- WORDS: the member's line on the offer screen, the end line (their call), one past-tense variant per crossing row.
  Spanglish for them, never for the player.
- DYNASTY: the generation turn reads every follower job: finished to look/line, unfinished to heir's offer.
- TUNING (research only): the pay bands for follower jobs (lower than a stranger's, because a friend asks less; the
  draft here is 60 to 80% of a same-skull stranger job).
- MODS (research only): one data file for follower jobs, fields as in rule 6 above.
- ECONOMY: the supply-line outcome (2 MEDS or 1 WATER when passing) needs a cap so it never replaces contracts.
- DIRECTION / COOK: each job's picture change, per QR-AP.
- UI [phone contracts]: a follower job shows on the board with the member's thumb, nothing more.

## 9. OPEN (what the library could not answer)

- How often should a follower's job appear? The library's companion quests are one per companion per game; ours span
  three acts and up to 24 followers. Draft: at most one follower job on the board at a time; TUNING sets the rate.
- Whether a fighter (a company member who fights) should get the same treatment as a follower. Battle Brothers men are
  disposable by design [R]; giving every fighter a job would fight that. Draft: only veterans (about 20 fights) and
  followers get personal jobs.
- What the Amalgamation does with a follower's job in act 3 (the counterfeit family offering to "keep" a dead
  follower). The shelf has two designs on it; the ending lane (DYNASTY, the Assassin's Creed frame) owns the rest.
