# QR-AI: THE THREE JOBS THAT END THE DEMO

The demo needs an ending, so this page gives it three jobs from one woman in one town, each harder than the last. When the third job is handed in, one small thing is wrong, a calm voice says one line, and a last screen says thanks and opens NOTES.

QUESTION: row [the demo's chain] (QUESTS, release line 13, rule 72). The three-contract chain for the demo's one settlement: each offer screen, skulls, pay in the six resources, the fight it meets, what the last hand-in changes, the closing beat and the thanks screen's words. A page RUN cites.

## THE CHAIN

### CONTRACT 1: WALK THE WATER CART TO THE PUMP

THE OFFER SCREEN (THE HALL, LUPE's portrait, head and shoulders, she does not look up from the ledger on the first line):
- "The cart goes to the pump at dawn, mijo. Two hands."
- "Road crew sits on the wash road some mornings. Mostly they just look."
- "One battery when it comes back full. I pay on the full, not on the walk."
SKULLS: 1. PAY: 30 BATTERIES on completion, plus 2 WATER for the company's own barrels. Advance: none offered (the first job teaches the shape plainly; `Q236.W6` gives the choice from contract 2). Haggle: one ask allowed, Lupe's mouth tightens, ceiling 36 (the face is the negotiation UI, `Q236.W4`; a spoken ceiling, not dice, `Q236.X1`).
KIND: haul-caravan (QR-AF, HAUL), id ct-escort-water, already on the board.
THE ROAD: no event (rule: never in the first minute; and the first job stays clean). The cart's marker follows the party on the map, it never steers itself (QR-AF finding 5).
THE FIGHT: three road crew kids with pipes step out at the halfway mark, one fight. Board per rule 79: the two lines 3 wide, Battle Brothers' five-tile gap, a margin of two: about 7 by 11 house tiles, camera opened zoomed in at the man's 112 px. Routine: 2 to 3 minutes. The cart sits on the back row as a thing to protect, not a unit to steer.
HAND-IN: the cart comes back full. On the settlement screen the pump's light is on, the STALL lists WATER cheaper for the rest of the demo, and Lupe's next line is ready. The deed ledger writes "walked the water, the Wash".
WHY THIS ONE FIRST: the opening job must be small and readable (`Q051.X1`, `Q055.X2` warn against a brick-wall first hour; `Q065.X2` against a barrage of systems), and it must still matter to the place (`Q236.W8`). Water is our healthiest leg and the cheapest thing to make felt.

### CONTRACT 2: RUN THE ROAD CREW OFF THE CORNER

HOW IT UNLOCKS: the moment contract 1 is handed in, Lupe's second offer appears in the hall (the same face returning, `Q031.W6`). If the player declined contract 1, the offer still appears after two in-game days on its own, because the corner trouble grows whether or not he helped (`Q055.W1`, the world lives without you).
THE OFFER SCREEN (LUPE, now looking at you):
- "The kids on the wash road? They work for somebody. He sits on the corner by the stall."
- "Six of them on the corner, pipes and one pistol. They leave, you get paid. Simple."
- "Nena cannot open while they sit there." (one origin line may replace this: for THE BLOCK WATCH, "Your neighbours know these boys. Do not let that slow you.")
SKULLS: 2. PAY: 60 BATTERIES. Payment mode is now offered: all on completion, or 20 up front and 40 after (`Q236.W6`, the bet on your own company). Fee locks at accept (`Q148.W1`). Haggle: up to two asks, ceiling 72; past it she says "Then somebody else," and the offer stays on the board for later, nothing written (`Q236.W5` without the penalty, `Q236.P3`).
KIND: clear-crew (QR-AF, CLEAR), id ct-road-crew, already on the board.
THE ROAD (one QR-AG event allowed): THE TRIKE ON THE SHOULDER (QR-AG event 1), only if the player takes the long way along the highway leg to reach the corner from the far side; never inside the settlement's own leg. It is the cheapest honest trade in QR-AG: STRIP IT, HELP PUSH IT, KEEP MOVING (`Q108.X2`, `Q126.X2`: neither choice dominates; `Q107.W7`: the injury odds are on the button). The narrator reads it (rule 45). If the player goes straight, no event: nothing is forced.
THE FIGHT: six crew, pipes and one pistol, plus their boss standing back. Board: lines about 6 wide, gap five, margin two: about 10 by 11. Routine: 3 to 4 minutes. The boss does not fight: when four are down he runs off the board's edge (a partial win is a real outcome, QR-AF finding 6).
HAND-IN: the corner is clear. On the settlement screen NENA's STALL opens its second shelf (more stock), and the bar's rumour about the corner is replaced. The boss's last line, said by Lupe as she pays, sets contract 3: "Your corner boy says he only sat there because the dogs took his lot." This is the escalation: the small human trouble was sitting on a bigger animal one (`Q009.W2`, the mundane hook opening onto something worse; `Q036.W4`, a turn so gradual you cannot name it).

### CONTRACT 3: CLEAR THE DOG LOT (the tough one)

HOW IT UNLOCKS: on hand-in of contract 2, or after four in-game days whatever happened, because the dogs start showing on the map as a roaming party leaving tracks near the wash (rule 24 overworld). If both slots are full, it waits as the third offer (rule 51).
THE OFFER SCREEN (LUPE, standing now, the ledger closed):
- "The dogs took the lot behind the wash. Not pets no more."
- "One of them is big as a calf. Manny says it has a tag in its ear."
- "Clear it and the lot is ours again. Ours means yours too, if you want to build on it."
SKULLS: 3. PAY: 90 BATTERIES plus 2 MEDS (from the clinic, she says, "for after"), and the lot itself as a place the company can build on (rule 43: contracts can hand you PARTS). Payment mode: on completion only; this is the job where `Q236.W6`'s bet is all in. Haggle: one ask, ceiling 100.
KIND: hunt-kind (QR-AF, HUNT) with a camp flavour, id ct-dogs, already on the board. The dogs are lab-made (rule 42): feral dogs plus one dire-wolf-sized lab animal, never a mutant.
THE ROAD: if the trike did not fire on contract 2, it may fire here instead; one event in the whole chain at most.
THE FIGHT: seven feral dogs and the big one. Board: the dogs come as a loose line about 8 wide, gap five, margin two: about 12 by 13, still far under the 20 by 15 (rule 79 keeps the big board for the endgame). Tough: 8 to 12 minutes, never past 15 (rule 39). Tough here means the enemy (more of them, faster, the big one shrugging a routine hit), not a trick. Struck down follows rule 36 (20% dead, else a 30 to 40 day injury), the main character never dies.
HAND-IN: on the settlement screen the lot behind the wash is lit and shows as THE COMPANY'S (a lot to build on, the first piece of a home base, rule 43). The bar's dog rumour is gone. Lupe pays. The deed ledger writes "cleared the dog lot, the Wash". Then the closing beat.

## THE CLOSING BEAT

Lupe pays, and then hands over one more thing without being asked: the big dog's ear tag, "You keep this. I do not want it in the hall."
THE ONE WRONG DETAIL: the tag is stamped with a lab number and, under it, the name of the player's company, spelled right, in the lab's own font.
NOBODY EXPLAINS IT. Lupe says nothing more about it. The tag lands in the inventory as an item with no use and no sell price.
THE NARRATOR'S ONE LINE (rule 45, the calm, slightly wrong synthetic voice, the only time it speaks at the hall): "The tag was printed before the company had a name."
Why this and not a twist: the wrong detail is ordinary (an ear tag), said plainly, and supported by the bar's rumour from minute one, so it is foreshadowing that only lands now (`Q010.W8`, foreshadow in hindsight); it never resolves (`Q010.W7`, `Q009.W6`, withheld closure as theme); and it points at the full game's secret (the lab, the Amalgamation's records) without naming it, the way a strong opening seeds a late payoff (`Q042.W4`, `Q053.W9`). It is not a betrayal of the guide (`Q036.W2` stays banked for the full game; Lupe stays honest, like the honest dealer in `Q126.W1`).

## IF THE PLAYER DECLINES OR LOSES (the chain always reaches the end)

1. DECLINE IS FREE (`Q126.W3`, `Q236.P3`, `Q236.W7`): no line, no ledger byte, no cold shoulder. The offer stays on the hall's board. Lupe's next offer still arrives on the timer (2 days for contract 2, 4 for contract 3), because the town's trouble grows on its own (`Q055.W1`, `Q051.W2`).
2. DECLINE EVERYTHING: after six in-game days the dogs, never cleared, come to the edge of the wash as a roaming party and stop the company on the map: the fight of contract 3, unpaid, as a defend (QR-AF hold-defend). Win it and the closing beat fires the same (Lupe pays in MEDS only and hands over the tag). The demo always has an end.
3. LOSE A FIGHT (the company breaks or retreats): no game over in the demo. The company wakes at the clinic, hurt per rule 36, and the contract is FAILED, which is a deed (`Q236.W7`): "failed the water, the Wash". Lupe re-offers the same job one day later at a lower pay band (she is honest about it: "Less, because now I know"), the same enemy group, one fewer of them. That is the Battle Brothers failure cost in one face, made kind enough for a demo.
4. LOSE THE SAME FIGHT TWICE: the third offer comes with SARGE from the yard walking with you (one extra unit), the way `Q117.W2` waves a struggling player through without saying so; `Q117.X2` warns this help must not be a secret only bad players find, so Sarge's line says it: "Third time. I am coming."
5. ABANDON A TAKEN CONTRACT: a deed ("dropped the dogs, the Wash"); the chain still reaches the timer path above.

## THE PLAY TIME TARGET

- Map, arrival, settlement screen, reading the first offer: 3 to 5 minutes (the first sixty seconds are a game, QR-F; nothing speaks in them).
- Contract 1: travel 1 to 2, fight 2 to 3, hand-in 1. About 5 minutes.
- Contract 2: travel 2, the road event 1 if it fires, fight 3 to 4, hand-in 1. About 8 minutes.
- Free time between (market, barber, scavenge, the bar): 5 to 8 minutes, the player's own.
- Contract 3: travel 2, fight 8 to 12, hand-in and closing beat 2. About 15 minutes.
- TOTAL: 25 to 35 minutes to the thanks, under 15 minutes of it in fights (rule 39: the fight under a third to half of the demo's time; the world gets the rest). The `Q148.W10` one-sitting arc, and short enough that a stranger reaches the end (release line 14).

## THE FINDINGS (what the library says, grouped)

### 1. A chain of three from one face is the strongest small shape
`Q031.W6` (recurring faces make rules personal) and `Q031.P6` (the person you met comes back with a linked story) say the same client must return; `Q010.W10` says a figure gains weight each time he appears. `Q006.W2` shows side content carrying the climax. `Q148.W10` gives the one-hour arc: notice, job, payment, every time.

### 2. Escalation must come from the world, and must be legible
`Q236.W1` and `Q236.W8`: the board is the town's problems and the result changes the town. `Q236.W9`: bigger jobs appear as you grow, so the board is its own progression. `Q031.W4` (the escalating rulebook) and `Q036.W4` (the turn so slow you cannot name it) give the curve: water, then people, then animals. `Q044.W7` says ease off after a hard hit: the free time between contracts 2 and 3 is that ebb. `Q044.W4` warns that pressure rising with wealth is the shape, so contract 3 arrives when the player has the most batteries.

### 3. The opening must be small, the ending must sell the whole game
`Q051.X1`, `Q055.X2`, `Q065.X2`, `Q046.X2` and `Q045.X1` all say the same thing: an opening that dumps systems loses people. `Q057.X2`, `Q067.X4` warn of a slow first act. `Q053.W9` and `Q053.P9` ask for a seductive opening that earns the late game; `Q053.X2` warns the peak can be too early, so the demo's last beat must be its best. `Q042.W4` and `Q042.W7` say the opening seeds the ending: the tag is that seed for the full game.

### 4. The ending lands on one quiet image, not a speech
`Q228.W9` (the door closing is the image) and `Q228.W8` (the cost paid quietly) support ending on a small object handed over. `Q010.W7` and `Q009.W6` support leaving it unexplained. `Q009.W2` is the mundane hook that opens on dread. `Q063.W9` is the direct, personal closing message. `Q024.W10` says surface the consequence: the lit lot and the cheaper water are that. `Q001.W11` and `Q063.W8` say no clean win: the town is better, and the tag says it is also watched.

### 5. Saying no and losing must never close the road
`Q126.W3` (refusal costs nothing, the offer never expires), `Q236.P3`, `Q236.W7`. `Q117.W2` (lose three times and he waves you through) with `Q117.X2` (say it out loud). `Q045.W2` and `Q044.W1` reframe failure as story: a failed job is a deed, not a reset. `Q020.W8` says mastery may shorten the path: a player who wins first time reaches the thanks in 25 minutes.

### 6. Pay and choices stay honest
`Q236.W6`, `Q236.W4`, `Q148.W1`, against `Q236.X1` and `Q126.X1`. `Q108.X2` and `Q126.X2` keep the road event balanced. `Q107.W7` puts the odds on the button. `Q031.W3` is why STRIP tempts when TAPE is low. `Q002.W7`: the payout is a moment, not just a number, so Lupe says one line at each hand-in.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)

1. The demo's settlement offers exactly the chain ct-escort-water, ct-road-crew, ct-dogs from one client portrait (LUPE); ct-find-the-kid may sit in the second slot as a free extra and is never required.
2. Skulls 1, 2, 3; pay 30, 60, 90 BATTERIES (TUNING's), plus 2 WATER, nothing, 2 MEDS and the lot.

4. Every board is cut by rule 79; none is 20 by 15. Fights 1 and 2 finish in 2 to 4 minutes on a phone; fight 3 in 8 to 12, never 15.
5. At most one road event in the whole chain, QR-AG event 1, never in the first minute, never on contract 1.

7. Declining writes nothing. Failing writes a deed and re-offers at a lower band; a second failure brings Sarge.
8. The closing beat fires once: the tag item, one narrator line, then THE THANKS SCREEN with NOTES open. It fires on contract 3's hand-in or on the six-day defend, whichever comes first.
9. A loop gate leg: a driver can reach the thanks screen from a new game with zero declines, and with all declines (timer path), with no dead end.
