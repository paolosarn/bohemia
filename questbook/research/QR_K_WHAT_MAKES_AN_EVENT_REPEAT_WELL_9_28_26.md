# QR-K: WHAT KEEPS A ROAD EVENT FRESH THE TENTH TIME IT FIRES
QUESTS LANE (chat 19), research only, round two (9/28). Writer K. Nothing here is built (rule 35).
Written for RUN, WORLD, PEOPLE and WORDS, who will build the event system once the map exists. Every id below
resolves in records/BOHEMIA_QUESTBOOK_LAW_INDEX.json. The Battle Brothers facts come from
reference/library/battle_brothers/08_CONTRACTS_EVENTS.md (a recall volume; the library has no Battle Brothers study).
Companion designs: questbook/designs/*/QD_K01 to QD_K10, one per QR-B shape, each written as a second use of a shape
the first shelf already used (or a first use where the shelf had none).
FOLDED 10/9 (QR-AL): QR-U's rule 14 and the sharpens of rules 2, 5 and 7, at the end of THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS, each marked "(folded 10/9 from QR-U)" in place.

QUESTION: When an event SHAPE is reused many times over a hundred hours, the Battle Brothers way, what does the
library say keeps it fresh instead of turning it into a vending machine? Variation by who, where and what state;
the once-per-face rule; escalation; callbacks.

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH
A shape can repeat; a scene cannot. The library's clearest warning is Sunless Sea's: when a writing-led game makes
you revisit content to survive, you "stop seeing the writing and start seeing the game" and pick the option that
pays, not the one that means something (`Q066.X1`, `Q144.X1`). Witcher 3's contracts show the slower version of the
same death: a good formula repeated dozens of times telegraphs its own beats until "volume is the twist's predator"
(`Q148.X3`). What survives repetition, study after study, is four things. First, the SKELETON is reused but the FLESH
is authored per instance: identity first, then gear, voice and line (`Q048.W5`, `Q048.X2`, `Q056.P8`). Second, the
instance is chosen by the WORLD, not a die, so the same shape reads different causes in different places and states
(`Q044.W2`, `Q151.W3`, `Q088.W4`), and the player learns to read the map instead of the menu. Third, a FACE is spent
once: people recur, but a person never re-delivers the same scene; recurrence means that person has changed
(`Q031.W6`, `Q010.W10`, `Q144.W7`). Fourth, repeats ESCALATE or CALL BACK: the second time a shape fires it knows
about the first, either because the stakes climbed (`Q031.W4`, `Q036.W4`, `Q038.W9`) or because an old choice
comes back changed (`Q004.W5`, `Q048.W2`, `Q086.W8`). Under all four sits rationing: the twist and the dread are a
minority, on an unpredictable schedule, with relief after a hard hit (`Q148.X3`, `Q044.W7`, `Q032.P9`). Battle
Brothers says the same thing in two words: cooldowns and composition.

## THE BUDGET, IN NUMBERS
QR-B's open question 3 guessed 60 to 90 hand-made event instances across the three acts, over ten shapes. Hold that
guess and a hundred hours: each shape fires six to nine times in a whole game, roughly once every 11 to 17 hours of
play. That is the scale this page is about. It is not RimWorld's hundreds of events or Yakuza's hundred substories
(`Q114.W1`, whose guides already call part of the hundred padding, `Q114.X1`). Nine is few enough that every
instance can be authored and many enough that a player WILL notice the skeleton by the third. The question is what
the third, sixth and ninth must do that the first did not.

## THE FINDINGS

### 1. How a shape dies: the four ways the library records
- THE VENDING MACHINE. Repetition turns a story into a supply choice (`Q066.X1`). The port is explicit: rotate and
  gate events, add procedural texture, never force the player back through past-read vignettes (`Q066.P9`). At
  generational scale it compounds: every heir re-sails into known prose (`Q144.X1`), and the law written there is
  exact for our three acts: inherited knowledge changes what the heir can DO, never re-runs what the parent READ.
- THE TELEGRAPHED BEAT. A formula repeated dozens of times turns its best move into the expected move; late
  contracts telegraph their own sympathy beats (`Q148.X3`). A twist overused cheapens every use (`Q032.P9`). For
  events: the bait version of E1 dies the moment every stranger is bait.
- THE BARK. The same line forever turns the best system into the most annoying one (`Q084.X6`). A companion's read
  of the tracks (E3) or a quarrel pair (E4) needs a cooldown and a variation budget.
- THE ROUTINE THE PLAYER DESIGNED. Kenshi's raids path the same way until the base farms its own attackers: "a siege
  the player can schedule is a subscription, not a story" (`Q151.X1`). Mount and Blade's battles go samey over
  hundreds of hours and only mods refresh them (`Q061.X5`). Mr. Shakedown becomes a farm once the loop is known
  (`Q114.X6`). Any event the player can trigger on purpose will be triggered on purpose.
- Two quieter versions. Between the good procedural moments, play goes rote (`Q046.X1`); the fix is a strong quiet
  loop under the drama, not more drama (`Q046.P7`). And a beautiful rule executed by repetition becomes an errand;
  reward the insight, do not make the player re-perform it (`Q111.P8`, `Q111.X1`). The Anju and Kafei loop shows the
  cost when every retry replays known beats in real time (`Q128.X10`).

### 2. Variation by WHO: skeleton procedural, flesh authored
- The Nemesis study is the key craft finding: define identity first, then make weapon, outfit and voice support it;
  "procedural skeleton, handcrafted flesh" (`Q048.W5`). Its flaw is the other half: imitators that kept the skeleton
  and skipped the flesh felt like "a shallow puddle" (`Q048.X2`). An event shape without an authored person is a
  shallow puddle.
- Qud's method is the typed spec for it: break each generated thing into interacting input variables (history,
  relationships, culture) feeding the loop, "disciplined, typed-spec generation, not noise" (`Q056.P8`), inside a
  static authored backbone (`Q056.W2`). Subnautica says the same about art: you cannot teach an algorithm art
  direction (`Q090.W6`), and Bloodborne's procedural dungeons became the most-skipped content (`Q092.X4`).
- WHO INCLUDES THE COMPANY. Battle Brothers' own event families (company, background, relationship, injury and
  trait events) fire from composition. The library agrees: companions react to each other (`Q043.W2`), rivalries
  have teeth (`Q043.W3`), drama emerges from traits plus relationships (`Q046.W9`). A company that changes (rule 36
  kills one in five of the struck down and benches the rest for a month) changes which events can fire. That is free
  variety, and it is the only kind that costs no writing: QD-K04 exists only because rule 36 made a 34-day injury.
- WHO SPEAKS DIFFERS BY WHAT YOU ARE. Arcanum rewrites its script by build; strangers cite your specific deeds by name
  (`Q086.W5`, `Q086.W8`). Hades' lines react to your death and loadout and never repeat, which is why the player feels
  seen (`Q047.W5`), and the port scopes it: templated reactivity, not forty hours of voice (`Q047.P4`). The warning
  on scope is real: never-repeating reactivity is an enormous content mountain (`Q047.X1`), and bespoke origins cost
  so much that most players see each once (`Q042.X2`).
- Give the people agency, or they turn back into dispensers: companions have their own preferences and are not
  wish-fulfilment vending machines (`Q063.W6`); a quest-giver with no interiority is a contract dispenser and then a
  prop (`Q133.X2`).

### 3. Variation by WHERE and WHAT STATE: the world picks the instance
- The storyteller reads the state and picks the event that makes the best story, not raw random (`Q044.W2`). Threat
  scales with what you have (`Q044.W4`, `Q151.W3`); after a hard hit it sends relief (`Q044.W7`). Pure random is the
  named flaw (`Q044.X1`). This is also a freshness rule: the same shape under a different state is a different scene.
  E2 in the act 1 bust is a family's rope and a labour price (QD-K02); E2 in the act 2 boom is a booth that prints
  receipts (QD-B04). Same skeleton, different world, and the player can see why on the map.
- State has clocks. Project Zomboid's apocalypse is a process on a schedule (`Q088.W4`); Citizen Sleeper's clocks tick
  for you and the world (`Q065.W6`). E6 fires from the grid's real load: a collapse brownout in act 2 (QD-B05) and a
  boom brownout in act 3, when the rocket's test draws the feeder down (QD-K06). The cause changed, so the scene did.
- State lives on the ground. Paths smooth as people walk them (`Q089.W6`); a party must really be on the map to
  ambush you (`Q061.X4`, `Q050.W9`). In act 3 the paved roads make off-road tracks loud (QD-K03), where in act 1 every
  road was dirt (QD-B02). The terrain itself varies the shape.
- Systems colliding produce stories no script wrote (`Q044.W6`, `Q045.W1`). The freshest instance is the one whose
  cause is two systems at once: a toll (share) meeting a flood (weather), a brownout (grid) meeting the rocket (the
  act's crisis).

### 4. The once-per-face rule, and how a face may come back
- A SCENE fires once. Everything above points there: `Q066.X1`, `Q144.X1` and `Q084.X6` are three studies saying
  that re-served text is the fastest decay. QR-B's rule 5 already says each instance fires once; this page adds the
  reason: the unit of freshness is the scene, not the shape.
- A PERSON may come back, changed. Papers, Please's wife follows her husband back through the booth and the rules
  become personal (`Q031.W6`). The Strange Man threads two games, accreting weight with each appearance (`Q010.W10`).
  Sunless Sea's inherited officers speak of the previous captain in past tense, "the cheapest possible reactivity"
  (`Q144.W7`). So a returning face must carry the time between: older, in a new place, in a new state. QD-K07's woman
  walking IN in act 3 may be one of QD-B06's families who walked OUT in act 2; she is not the same scene twice, she is
  the second half of one life.
- The rare figure is rare by rule. The figure who knows you works because he is instantly uncanny (`Q010.W1`) and
  used sparingly; the same figure as a bark is dead (`Q084.X6`). QD-K08 and QD-B08 are both E8, and they are
  different people who know different things (the future on the map, and the past on the road).
- The no is not a face. A declined event or contract writes nothing (rule 35c), so it can never be a callback.
  Only things the player DID come back.

### 5. Escalation: the second fire knows about the first
- Papers, Please grows its rulebook daily until keeping up is frantic: the system's growth is the difficulty curve
  (`Q031.W4`), and its port asks for rhythm, escalating variety and human intrusions so the grind grips (`Q031.P9`).
  The toll shape escalates this way across acts: a rope, then a receipt, then (act 3) something that asks for
  nothing and is scarier for it.
- The slow turn: helpful, then passive-aggressive, then murderous, so slowly you cannot name the turn (`Q036.W4`);
  the mundane hook that escalates to cosmic dread (`Q009.W2`); the unthinkable arriving through the quest loop's own
  grammar (`Q133.W2`). A repeated shape can carry a slow turn only the fifth instance reveals.
- Escalation of stakes by memory. A taboo act sends scaling bounty hunters over time (`Q038.W9`); defeat promotes the
  rival who beat you (`Q048.W3`); a storyteller sends a rival when you steamroll (`Q048.W9`). The Amalgamation's
  threat is a fever, not a difficulty curve: the closer to the truth, the harder the world pushes back, diegetically
  (`Q085.P7`).
- Escalation of the offer. The Raphael port: every generation the offer stays refused, the terms get better, until
  the refusal is an expensive habit somebody's grandmother started (`Q126.P7`). That is the across-acts escalation
  our three acts can do and a single-life game cannot.
- Repetition as the point, once. Bal demands the submission again, and the second press feels nothing like the first
  (`Q134.W7`), but when the refusal loop has no new content it is only a wall (`Q134.X2`). If a shape repeats on
  purpose, each repeat needs new words.
- A ladder of different verbs keeps an escalation alive: beggar, bride, chef, banquet, body-double, warship, each a
  set-piece with a different verb (`Q075.W9`).

### 6. Callbacks: the repeat that pays an old debt
- The Whispering Hillock pays off in a different quest, hours later (`Q004.W5`); Alpha Protocol's world has a long
  memory (`Q024.W8`) and must surface it, or the reactivity is invisible (`Q024.W10`). Dwarf Fortress needs a narrator
  or a chronicle to be seen (`Q045.X4`), and its best output is the stories people retell (`Q045.W8`). Our surface is
  the city-view feed and the map itself.
- The enemy remembers and references your methods (`Q048.W2`); losing makes a nemesis for free (`Q087.W5`). A shape
  that fires a second time against a party that met you before should say so.
- The heir's game is repriced by what the first life learned, "progress the interface never has to claim" (`Q144.W9`).
  In our flip, a callback is often a PLACE: QD-K02's culvert worked in act 1 is a bridge with a family's name in act
  3; QD-K10's movie wall becomes a real screen with a plaque. The shape does not repeat; its consequence stands on the
  map.
- Recontext beats repetition: replaying as someone else reframes the same events, meaning changes, not difficulty
  (`Q022.W6`). The three acts at once are built for this: the same road event seen from Gen 1 and Gen 3 is not a
  repeat, it is the same road across forty years.
- Sustain the callbacks for everyone, or the promise goes hollow after hour one (`Q042.X1`, `Q042.P8`). If E9 only
  ever fires for one kind of deed, it will feel like a special case, not a law of the world.
- Foreshadow in hindsight: a line that only lands later makes the first instance play differently in memory
  (`Q010.W8`). The cheapest callback is a line.

### 7. Rationing: the twist is a minority, and the quiet is load-bearing
- The Witcher law: moral forks must be a MINORITY of jobs, on an unpredictable schedule; most jobs should be exactly
  what they say, so the twisted ones land as discoveries (`Q148.X3`). For events: most E1 strangers are honest, most
  E5 traders are traders, most E3 tracks are what they look like. Bait is the rationed minority.
- Tone range at the batch level: any ten written must span at least three tones (`Q114.N4`, marked there as a
  proposal pending Paolo). Juxtaposition makes both halves hit harder (`Q013.W2`), and a grim world needs its
  occasional triumph (`Q041.W10`, `Q041.X3`). The E10 good hour is the repeat that keeps the others fresh.
- Slow onset. Pressure absent early and creeping in lands as lived experience (`Q021.W3`). The first E1 of the game
  should be honest, so the first bait is a discovery.
- The flee is always open. Kenshi's standing exit is what makes staying a choice (`Q151.W7`), and refusal that costs
  nothing works because the player chooses again every hour (`Q126.W3`). A free "keep moving" also stops a shape from
  becoming a tax the player dreads.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)
1. ONE SCENE, ONE FIRE. Every event instance (person + place + first line) fires once per game. A checker lists all
   fired instance ids; a duplicate fails. (`Q066.X1`, `Q144.X1`, `Q084.X6`)
2. EVERY INSTANCE IS AUTHORED FLESH ON A SHARED SKELETON. An instance names its SHAPE (E1 to E10), a person with a
   portrait and one want, a first line of 4 to 12 words, and one wrong detail. No instance is assembled from
   interchangeable lines alone. (`Q048.W5`, `Q048.X2`, `Q056.P8`)
3. THE WORLD PICKS. Each instance declares its CAUSE fields (road owner and share, grid load, a party id and its
   tracks, a roster pair, a deed ledger entry, the act and boom or bust). The storyteller picks among instances whose
   cause is true right now. No cause true, no event. (`Q044.W2`, `Q061.X4`, `Q151.W3`)
4. NO TWO FIRES OF ONE SHAPE IN A ROW, AND A SHAPE COOLDOWN. After a shape fires, at least three other events (or
   the map-time equivalent) pass before it can fire again. With 6 to 9 instances per shape over 100 hours this is
   loose enough to never starve. (`Q084.X6`, Battle Brothers' cooldowns)
5. RATION THE TWIST. For any shape with a bait or twist version (E1, E3, E5), at most one in three fired instances is
   the twist, and the FIRST instance the player meets is always the plain one. (`Q148.X3`, `Q032.P9`, `Q021.W3`)
6. A RETURNING FACE CARRIES THE TIME. A person may appear in a second event only if their state changed (act, place,
   age, standing, what the player did to them), and their line says so. (`Q031.W6`, `Q144.W7`, `Q010.W10`)
7. THE SECOND FIRE KNOWS THE FIRST. From the third instance of a shape onward, at least half of instances read
   something from an earlier instance of any shape (a deed, a named party, a face, a place). (`Q004.W5`, `Q048.W2`,
   `Q086.W8`)
8. ESCALATE ACROSS ACTS, NOT INSIDE A SITTING. The same shape in a later act raises or changes the stakes (the rope,
   the receipt, the thing that asks for nothing), and NEVER costs more of the ledgers than its act 1 version (the future gets
   better, Paolo's second votes). (`Q031.W4`, `Q126.P7`)
9. CALLBACKS LAND AS PLACES AND POSTS. A callback's first surface is the map (a changed place) or the city-view feed,
   before any face says it. (`Q024.W10`, `Q045.X4`, `Q089.W7`)
10. NOTHING CAN BE FARMED. An event cannot be triggered on purpose by the player repeating an action, and no event
   pays more the second time its cause is recreated. (`Q151.X1`, `Q114.X6`, `Q111.P8`)
11. TONE RANGE PER TEN. Any ten consecutive fired events span at least three tones, and at least one is an E10 good
   hour. (`Q114.N4`, `Q013.W2`, `Q044.W7`)
12. THE COMPANY IS A SOURCE. At least a third of instances read the roster (a pair, a background, an injury under rule
   36), so a changed company changes the events. (`Q043.W2`, `Q046.W9`)
13. THE WRONG DETAIL IS PER INSTANCE, NEVER A SET. No two instances share their wrong detail, and no wrong detail is a
   reliable tell for the twist version (it appears in both versions or neither). (Our analog horror law; see OPEN 3.)

14. THE PARANOIA SHAPE. (folded 10/9 from QR-U) Isolated and threatened, people suspect each other (`Q233.W5`); isolate them,
convince them a threat is among them, and they destroy each other, at any scale (`Q233.W7`). A company event shape (a
theft in camp during a long siege or a snowed-in road) that reads the roster and has no villain. Test: the shape fires
only on a stalled company and names two members from the roster.

SHARPENED (folded 10/9 from QR-U):
- Rule 2 (authored flesh on a shared skeleton): the quiet-encounter shape collapses into one sentimental template
  unless the lives vary, funny, prickly, boring, evasive (`Q202.X2`); done well, the weight of many teaches that every
  corner holds a world (`Q202.W8`).
- Rule 5 (ration the twist): `Q236.X2`, a second study for the template problem.
- Rule 7 (the second fire knows the first): most harm resets; one grievance sticks, and later fires read it (`Q153.P3`).

itself. `Q084.X6` the bark with no cooldown. `Q151.X1` the event the player schedules. `Q061.X5` samey mid-game.
`Q114.X6` the farmable loop. `Q111.X1` the rule turned errand. `Q128.X10` known beats replayed. `Q046.X1` rote between
fires. `Q048.X2` the skeleton with no flesh. `Q092.X4` procedural filler holding the good content. `Q047.X1` and

## OPEN (what the library could not answer)
1. THE REAL NUMBER. No study measures when a player first recognises a skeleton. Rule 4's cooldown and rule 5's one
   in three are defensible defaults, not measurements. A playtest can count: after how many E1 fires does a player
   stop reading the stranger's face and start reading the button?
2. BATTLE BROTHERS ITSELF. The library has no Battle Brothers study. The recall volume says cooldowns and
   composition keep its events rare; it does not say how many events it ships or how often a player sees a repeat.
   A proper study of its event list would test rule 4 against the reference Paolo named.
3. THE WRONG DETAIL UNDER REPETITION. QR-B left this open and it stays open: no study tests whether an analog-horror
   detail wears out like a bark. Rule 13 is a guess: never reuse one, and never let one become a tell.
4. THE FLIP AS VARIATION. Our three acts at once let the same road be met in three eras. The library has recontext
   across playthroughs (`Q022.W6`) and inherited past tense (`Q144.W7`), but no game in it lets the player move back
   and forth between eras while events fire in each. Whether a player who flips often perceives act 1 and act 3
   versions of one shape as variety or as a repeat is untested.
5. MODS. Mount and Blade's variety came from mods (`Q061.X5`, `Q061.W10`), and chat 22 now owns a mods folder. Whether
   modders' event instances should obey rules 1 to 13 by the same checker is a MODS question, not a library one.
