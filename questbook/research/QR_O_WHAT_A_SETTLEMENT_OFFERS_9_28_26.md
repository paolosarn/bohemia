QUESTION: the settlement board as a system. Which of our fourteen home bases offer which contract kinds; how the
board reads boom and bust, the crisis stage, and whether the base was raided; how many offers at once; what the
other buildings (barber, scavenge, market, clinic) do for quests; how roaming parties feed the board.

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

A settlement's board is not a list of quests; it is the place READING ITSELF OUT LOUD. The library's best job
system says it plainly: "settlement boards post contracts ... generated from the world sim's actual events"
(`Q148.P1`), and the board works because it is "a dignified front door ... a job system that never begs"
(`Q148.W2`). Battle Brothers, Paolo's named reference for this layer, does the same: a settlement's BUILDINGS
decide what it sells and asks, and its SITUATION ("raided", "plague", "high demand for X") moves prices and
contracts (reference/library/battle_brothers/01_WORLDMAP.md, 07_ECONOMY.md). So for Bohemia: each of the
fourteen home bases has ONE JOB in the valley (the dam, the solar field, the wash camp, the farm tower, the
clinic town and so on), and its board offers the contract KINDS that job produces and suffers. Three inputs then
tilt the board: the ECONOMY (boom: haul, guard, build; bust: collect, recover, protect), the CRISIS STAGE of the
act (QR-J: each stage adds a client, a job and a pay line, and quiets some halls), and the BASE'S OWN HISTORY
(built rises, raided falls: a raided base's board goes thin and desperate and pays in kind, not batteries). The
count on the board is small and derived from the tier: a camp shows one offer, a town two, a fortress three,
never more than three on a phone (`Q037.X2`). The other buildings do quest work WITHOUT being quest givers: the
market shows a contract's effect as a price (`Q107.W4`), the clinic is where a struck-down man's 30 to 40 days
are spent and where medicine contracts are born, SCAVENGE is the player's own low-pay job that feeds the board
with finds, and the barber is where the company's deeds are worn on the face (`Q027.P7`). Roaming parties feed
the board by leaving evidence: tracks, a missing caravan, a raided shed become the next hall's contract (`Q055.W1`,
`Q044.W7`). What the library warns against, loudest: an empty beautiful town (`Q084.X3`), a board that repeats
one shape until the people in it stop being people (`Q148.X3`, `Q046.X1`), and a kindness or a raid whose effect
on a place nobody ever sees (`Q092.X6`).

## THE FINDINGS

### 1. The board is the world's own events, posted

- THE CORE PORT. `Q148.P1` is the whole loop: boards post contracts generated from the world sim's actual events,
  the player interviews the client and "HAGGLES AT IGNORANCE", and no fee renegotiation exists after the start.
  That is our offer screen with its negotiation (ask for more, ask for an advance), and it is round one's rule 1
  (the fee is spoken and locked before the yes).
- THE FRONT DOOR THAT NEVER BEGS. `Q148.W2`: public postings make paid work diegetic, repeatable and opt-in. This
  is the reason DECLINING IS FREE sits comfortably inside a settlement screen: the board does not chase you.
- ONE SITTING, WHOLE ARC. `Q148.W10`: "notice to payment in a session". A contract on the board must be sized for
  the phone sitting (QR-G checklist 8).
- MOST JOBS ARE JOBS. `Q148.P4` and `Q148.X3`: the twist attaches to a strict minority on an unpredictable
  schedule, because "the class's power dilutes with volume". On a board, this means the default generated offer
  is exactly what it says. Battle Brothers' own twists (the caravan carries contraband, the camp is bigger than
  said) are the rationed minority, not the rule (08_CONTRACTS_EVENTS.md).
- THE SIM PICKS WHICH ONE. `Q148.P4` again: the sim decides which threat happens to be somebody "based on the
  world's actual history". So the board should prefer twists that come out of this base's own ledger.

### 2. The place is the content: density over sprawl

- THE EMPTY BEAUTIFUL TOWN. `Q084.X3`: "two real settlements and a lot of road. LESSON: our DISTRICT must be
  dense." Fourteen bases is already a lot for a phone; each one must be worth tapping.
- SIDE CONTENT AS SENSE OF PLACE. `Q013.W1` and `Q013.P2`: substories exist to make the city feel alive; density
  makes a small map feel vast. A board whose offers come from the base's job is sense of place in two lines.
- A PLACE AS A DENSE HUB. `Q070.W4`: a single social event built like a hub city using the game's major
  mechanics. The settlement screen is our hub; its buildings are the major mechanics (market, clinic, barber,
  SCAVENGE, the hall).
- THE HUB MAKES REPETITION MEAN SOMETHING. `Q047.W4`: returning to people and evolving sub-plots is what makes
  coming back matter. `Q049.P3` goes further: the settlement is "the persistent structure the family visibly BUILDS
  across generations". Across our three acts at once, the same base is the same place in three eras, and its
  board is how the player reads what the family did to it.
- FUNCTIONAL PEOPLE ARE PEOPLE. `Q022.P4`: the ledger-keeper, the plant operator, the caravan voice get inner
  lives. The client on the offer screen is a portrait with one want (QR-G checklist 25), never a job dispenser.
- NEVER GATE THE WHOLE TOWN. `Q100.X6`: "don't gate a main-path settlement behind a stat check with no fallback."
  Every base's screen opens; renown can thin the board, never lock the door.

### 3. Renown and tier: what decides the quality of the offer

- RENOWN GATES THE ASCENT. `Q061.W4` and `Q061.P2`: reputation is the ladder that unlocks each tier. Battle
  Brothers: higher renown, better pay and harder contracts (01_WORLDMAP.md). Faction Towns (9/4) adds the second
  axis, WHERE you ask: a fortress offers better work than a camp.
- MULTIPLE VIABLE WAYS TO MAKE A LIVING. `Q061.W9`: merchants and entrepreneurs are as valid as warlords. So not
  every base's best contract is a fight: the solar field's is a haul, the clinic town's is a transport.
- THE FAMILY LEVELS. `Q046.W7`: family-wide renown across generations. For three acts at once, the family's name
  carries between eras: a grandfather's renown at the dam is why the dam's hall offers the grandson the good work.

### 4. The board reads the economy: boom and bust as the SHAPE of the offers

- THE CRISIS SHOWS AS INVENTORY FIRST. `Q107.W4`: the rebellion "arrives as inventory shrinkage", thieves in the
  food stores. A bust is a board where the offers are about recovering what went missing.
- THE APOCALYPSE AS INFRASTRUCTURE. `Q088.W4` and `Q088.P2`: electricity, water, food, medicine run down; our
  three currencies are three clocks. Under our canon (brownouts, never total dark) these are stages that tilt the
  board, not meters to zero (QR-J finding 1).
- TRIAGE AS THE ASK. `Q021.P5`: allocate scarce medicine, power and food across districts with never enough. In a
  bust, the clinic town's board is literally a triage board: two offers that cannot both be taken in time.
- SLOW ONSET. `Q021.W3`: the pressure creeps in. A bust does not flip the board in one tap; one offer changes
  per visit until the whole board has turned.
- THE REGIME PAYS FOR THE CRUEL JOB. `Q031.W5`: the system rewards the immoral choice so virtue has a price tag.
  In a bust, some bases post the cruel contract (evict, collect, hand over) at the best pay on the board. It is
  allowed on the board. What is NOT allowed is the crueller BRANCH inside one contract paying more (QR-G
  checklist 21, `Q130.X9`). The price tag lives between offers, never inside one.

### 5. The board reads the crisis stage

- A CRISIS CHANGES WHAT EVERY TOWN NEEDS. Battle Brothers (01_WORLDMAP.md): "A crisis changes what every town
  needs and what contracts appear." QR-J already set the rule: each crisis stage rewrites the board, prices and
  road, and never expires a taken contract.
- THE WORLD'S CLOCKS TICK WHETHER YOU ENGAGE. `Q065.W6`: two trackers, yours and the world's. The world's is read
  off the board: the hall at the dam posts a different client in the second stage of the rocket build than in
  the first.
- THE WORLD HAS AN IMMUNE SYSTEM. `Q085.P7`: the Amalgamation's threat is a FEVER, not a curve. Near its secret,
  the boards of the bases closest to the data (the Network's seat) go strange: offers that pay too well, clients
  who know your name. That is the analog horror line of the board.
- THE BEST-RUN CITY GETS THE LEAST STORY. `Q107.X6`: reward good play with content, not silence. A base the
  player kept whole through a crisis must NOT go quiet; its board posts the rebuild contracts, the thank-you job,
  the festival guard.

### 6. The board reads the base's history: the future goes both ways

- THE STORY PIVOTS ON SYSTEM STATE. `Q055.P6`: "a supply run becomes a desperate evacuation when the district is
  raided." A raided base's board is a different kind of board, not the same one with lower numbers.
- THE KINDNESS THAT FEEDS THE ATROCITY MUST BE SEEN. `Q092.X6`: "a consequence the player never discovers isn't a
  consequence, it's trivia." If the player raided the farm tower in act 1, act 3's farm tower board must SHOW it
  (thin, desperate, paying in kind), and the client must be someone who remembers (never someone who scolds).
- THE MERCY IN THE MANIFEST. `Q123.W5`: the spared come back later as a count. A base the player protected in
  one era posts a richer board in the next.
- THE CROWDFUNDED HIGHWAY. `Q089.W4`: many small contributions build what nobody could alone, and later travellers
  drive on it. Bases rise by the sum of contracts done for them (built rises), which is our derive signed upward.
- THE WORLD NOTICES. `Q078.X2`: nobody reacts is the reactivity hole. The board is the cheapest reactivity we own:
  a changed offer is a changed world, no cutscene required.
- THE BOARD IS NOT A MORAL SCOLD. `Q010.W3` and `Q004.W4`: some tests carry no mechanical stake. A raided board
  never says "because of you"; it just offers what a raided place can offer.

### 7. How many offers at once

- FOCUS OR DROWN. `Q037.X2`: "taking many quests at once floods the journal." Round one's default was one open
  contract next to the main quest; (folded 10/1 from rule 51) Paolo 9/30 set it at TWO contract slots next to ONE
  main quest, with a third offer waiting, because a haul to a city and a hunt near it belong on one road.
- THE HUB MUST NOT BE A SPREADSHEET. `Q065.X2`: an overwhelming first hour. `Q046.X1`: between good procedural
  beats, play feels like dry administrative busywork. A board of ten rows is that busywork.
- SO: offers on a board = the tier: camp 1, town 2, fortress 3. A crisis stage can add ONE (the crisis client)
  and a raid can remove ALL BUT ONE. The phone never shows more than four. (folded 10/1 from rule 51: the phone's
  read-only board lists what each settlement offers right now, so this cap reads per settlement: no hall and no
  settlement's row on the phone shows more than four; the phone never accepts.) Battle Brothers boards run small for
  the same reason (08_CONTRACTS_EVENTS.md: a handful of contracts per hall).

### 8. What the other buildings do for quests

- THE MARKET SHOWS THE CONTRACT'S EFFECT. `Q107.W4` (supply first) and Battle Brothers' situation prices
  (07_ECONOMY.md: raided, food scarce and dear). The market never offers contracts; it shows their results as a
  price. QR-G checklist 22 (the world shows it) is met by a price, which is the only non-mouth tell that is still
  people, places and things.
- THE CLINIC IS WHERE THE LAW OF 36 LIVES. A struck-down man spends his 30 to 40 days somewhere. `Q146.W1`: defeat
  continues as a new state. The clinic holds the injured, and the clinic's own board is where medicine and
  transport contracts originate. `Q021.P5` makes its bust board a triage.
- SCAVENGE IS THE PLAYER'S OWN LOW-PAY WORK. It is the free option that is always there (a no to every hall
  offer still leaves SCAVENGE). `Q023.P3` puts lore in objects: a find from SCAVENGE can be the evidence that
  makes the hall post a contract on the next visit (the salvaged receipt names a missing caravan). `Q148.W3`: the
  evidence built to be misread once.
- THE BARBER WEARS THE LEDGER. Third votes s9: customization happens at the barber. `Q027.P7`: the character art
  can show a dynast's moral cost physically. `Q005.W5`: identity as loot. The barber offers no contracts; the
  barber is where a finished contract's mark (a scar kept, a colour of a base you saved) can be worn, and where
  the barber's one line about what the chair hears is the town's rumour (`Q001.W2`: rumour, then the human).
- THE HALL IS ONE PERSON AT A TIME. `Q022.P4`: every client is someone. The hall shows up to three portraits;
  tapping one opens the offer screen.

### 9. How roaming parties feed the board

- THE WORLD LIVES WITHOUT YOU. `Q055.W1`: factions war, trade and patrol independently. `Q085.W1`: the player is
  "merely another inhabitant". `Q080.P5`: the world is the protagonist. Third votes s5: many roaming parties, far
  more than fourteen.
- THE DIRECTOR SENDS RELIEF AFTER A HIT. `Q044.W7` and `Q044.W2`: the storyteller reads the state and picks the
  event that makes the best story. A base just hit by a roaming party posts the recovery contract; a base that
  has been quiet a long time gets a caravan arrival and a guard job.
- NPCS ON REAL SCHEDULES. `Q011.P2`: a caravan's arrival is clockwork the player threads. A caravan party that
  leaves the solar field every fourth map day is a standing escort offer on those days only, stated aloud.
- A PARTY WITH A NAME. `Q048.W1`: any grunt can become a named rival. A roaming party that has hit two bases
  becomes the named hunt contract on a third board. `Q048.X2` warns the magic needs the hand-made voice: the named
  party gets one line of its own, or it stays a party.
- THE TRACK IS THE CLUE. Battle Brothers' tracks (01_WORLDMAP.md) plus `Q148.W3`: tracks are evidence, sometimes
  misread (the prints look like raiders and are refugees). The board says "tracks toward the wash", the map shows
  them.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDING LANES (numbered, testable)

1. Every home base has ONE job in the valley, and its board's contract kinds are drawn from a list keyed to that
   job (the table above is the draft). A contract kind not on its base's list is not offered there. (`Q148.P1`,
   `Q013.W1`)
2. The count on a board is the tier: camp 1, town 2, fortress 3; a crisis stage adds at most one; a raid removes
   all but one; never more than four on screen. (`Q037.X2`, `Q065.X2`)
3. Every offer is a portrait with one want; tapping a portrait opens the offer screen; the fee and the advance are
   settled on that screen and never change after the yes. (`Q148.W1`, `Q022.P4`)
4. Boom and bust change the KIND of offer, not just the pay: boom posts haul, guard, build; bust posts recover,
   collect, protect, triage. At least one offer changes kind per visit when the economy turns; never all at once.
   (`Q107.W4`, `Q021.W3`)
5. Each crisis stage of the act adds one named crisis client to the boards of the bases nearest the crisis, and
   quiets at most one base's board to its minimum. No crisis stage expires a taken contract. (QR-J, `Q095.P7`)
6. A raided base shows it on the board: fewer offers, pay in kind (hours of power, food, treatment, chips), a
   client who remembers. The board never names the player as the cause. (`Q055.P6`, `Q092.X6`, `Q004.W4`)
7. A base kept whole through a crisis posts at least one rebuild or thank-you contract the next visit. The best
   run base never goes quiet. (`Q107.X6`, `Q123.W5`)
8. A roaming party that hits a base leaves (a) tracks on the map and (b) a recovery offer on the nearest hall
   within two map stops. A party that hits two bases becomes a named hunt with one line of its own. (`Q055.W1`,
   `Q044.W7`, `Q048.W1`, `Q048.X2`)
9. Twists are a minority, drawn from this base's own history, never more than one board in five. (`Q148.P4`,
   `Q148.X3`)
10. The market never offers contracts; every finished contract changes at least one price at the base it served.
    (`Q107.W4`, `Q078.X2`)
11. SCAVENGE is always available and always pays little; one find in N (TUNING's number) is evidence that seeds a
    hall offer on the next visit. (`Q023.P3`, `Q148.W3`)
12. The clinic holds every struck-down company member for their 30 to 40 days, visibly, and its board is where
    medicine and transport contracts are born. (`Q146.W1`, `Q021.P5`)
13. The barber offers no contracts; it lets a finished contract's mark be worn and gives the base's one rumour
    line. (`Q027.P7`, `Q005.W5`, `Q001.W2`)
14. Renown thins or enriches a board; it never locks a base's screen. (`Q061.W4`, `Q100.X6`)
15. A cruel contract may be the best-paid offer on a board in a bust; inside any one contract, the crueller branch
    pays the same or less. (`Q031.W5`, `Q130.X9`)
16. Across the three acts at once, the same base's board in a later era is derived from the contracts done and the
    raids made there in earlier eras, signed both ways. (`Q049.P3`, `Q089.W4`, `Q046.W7`)

17. A PROSPEROUS BASE MAY SIT ON A LID. (folded 10/9 from QR-U) The order and the silver work on top of a buried conquest (`Q192.W1`);
    the peace and the crime are one structure (`Q154.W3`); the hostility rises in readable steps when you pull the
    thread (`Q154.W9`). Test: a boom base's board may carry one offer whose job is the thread, and pulling it changes the
    base's situation (QR-S rule 9).
18. A BUILDING THAT ALLOCATES. (folded 10/9 from QR-U) Restore a scarce resource and allocate it at one console, no combat, a switch and
    its downstream shape (`Q169.P1`); the switch-holder is accountable to the bases he lit or left dark (`Q169.P4`,
    with `Q169.W1`, `Q169.W2`, `Q169.W8`, `Q169.X3`). Test: a pump house or substation built on a home base's settlement
    screen holds a share switch whose setting shows on another base's board.
19. A BASE OF THE REFORMED. (folded 10/9 from QR-U) A community that left a dark past defends the new life with the old skills
    (`Q197.P1`). Test: at most one of the fourteen home bases is this town, and its sieges read QR-R rule 21.

## OPEN (what the library could not answer)

1. THE BOARD REFRESH RATE. Battle Brothers refreshes contracts over days; the library has no phone number for it.
   TUNING owns it. Suggested start: one offer changes per three map days per base.
2. THE SIEGE. Third votes s5 names the siege as a research question. The library has no settlement siege study.
   A siege is probably a string of contracts on the defender's board plus one on the attacker's; left open.
3. WHO SITS WHERE. The draft seats in the table follow act1_power only; which faction holds which kind of base is
   Paolo's by MAP LAW.
4. DOES AN OFFER EXPIRE ON THE BOARD (not taken)? The library says visible expiry or none (`Q095.P7`). Default: an
   untaken offer stays until the board's next refresh, and the client says when she will ask someone else.
