# QR-B: WHAT AN EVENT ON THE ROAD SHOULD BE
QUESTIONS LANE (chat 19), research only, round 9/27. Row [bb events], question (a). Nothing here is built (rule 35).
FOLDED 10/9 (QR-AK): QR-U rules 13, 14 and 15 and the sharpens of rules 4, 7, 8 and 11, at the end of THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS, each marked in place.
Written for RUN, WORLD, PEOPLE and WORDS, who build events when the map exists. Every id below resolves in
records/BOHEMIA_QUESTBOOK_LAW_INDEX.json. Teardowns are cited as "teardown #N" from
laws/BOHEMIA_ADDENDUM_BEST_QUESTS_COLLECTION_7_13_26.md. The Battle Brothers facts are from
reference/library/battle_brothers/08_CONTRACTS_EVENTS.md (a recall volume, not yet checked against the wiki).

QUESTION: From the 152 studied quests and the 22 teardowns, what should a Battle Brothers-style EVENT on our
overworld map be? An event stops the travelling party on the road: a situation, a face, two or three real choices,
a consequence written into the ledgers (batteries, standing, people in the company, time). Deliver ten event
shapes, how often events fire, how they read the world state, and how they differ by act.

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH
An event is a person the world put on your road for a reason you can see. The library is blunt about three
things. First, an event must be CAUSED by the simulation, not rolled from nowhere: a party that is really on the
map left the tracks, the brownout is really on the grid, the toll is taken by whoever really holds that road
(`Q044.W2`, `Q061.X4`, `Q050.W9`). Second, the choice must be a real trade priced in the ledgers, stated honestly,
with no option secretly dominant and no option graded by the game (`Q107.W7`, `Q126.X2`, `Q126.X4`, `Q107.X1`).
Third, rarity is the whole value: the best-loved systems in the library die by repetition, when "the story becomes
a vending machine" and players pick the option that pays instead of the one that means something (`Q066.X1`,
`Q148.X3`, `Q084.X6`). So: one event per two or three trips at most, a storyteller that reads the ledgers and sends
relief after a hard hit (`Q044.W7`), one face, one short line, two or three choices of which one is always "keep
moving", and a consequence that comes back later somewhere else, usually on the city feed or on the road itself
(`Q004.W5`, `Q024.W10`). The ten skeletons below are what those rules produce when they meet our valley.

## THE FINDINGS

### 1. An event is the simulation with a face, not a dice roll
- The model is a storyteller that READS the state and picks the event that makes the best story, not raw random
  (`Q044.W2`). It reads wealth: threat scales with what you have, so success breeds danger (`Q044.W4`), and the
  Kenshi outpost study says the same in one line: raids scale with visible prosperity, modesty is a defence
  (`Q151.W3`).
- It ebbs. After a hard hit, send relief: a caravan, a traveller (`Q044.W7`). Quiet, loud, quiet.
- Pure random is the named flaw. "Randy" produces unfair runs of events and loss by bad luck (`Q044.X1`); the
  Darkest Dungeon study calls it RNG-driven misery (`Q041.X1`); Citizen Sleeper says a bad run of rolls must stay
  fair enough that the loss feels like consequence (`Q065.X4`).
- The world must be seen doing it. Chrono Trigger's courtesy was visible enemies and no random encounters, which
  respects the player's time (`Q050.W9`). Mount and Blade's seams show when AI lords recruit from nowhere and
  teleport (`Q061.X4`). For us: an ambush event requires a hostile party that was actually on the map, within
  reach, and its tracks visible before the stop. No party, no ambush.
- The world runs without you (`Q055.W1`, `Q061.W2`) and its factions have their own agendas that react to you
  (`Q055.W7`). An event is the moment that running world crosses your road.
- The Battle Brothers volume (08) lists the same families: company events, background events, location events,
  relationship events, injury and trait events, with cooldowns keeping them rare and the company's composition
  driving which fire. The library agrees with every one of those and adds the causation rule above.

### 2. The choice is a trade with prices on it
- Frostpunk's crowd scene prices each option in population: let him talk and lose 15 people, call him a liar and
  lose 7, or disperse the crowd (`Q107.W7`). The price is on the option. That is our model for a ledger line.
- But the option must match its outcome. "Disperse the crowd" turning into a beating is the game putting its
  thumb on the scale (`Q107.X1`). If a choice says "wave them through", it cannot secretly mean "they rob you".
- A tempting option that is strictly worse is not a temptation, it is a difficulty setting (`Q126.X2`); the Raphael
  deal is mechanically dominated and every guide says so (`Q126.X1`).
- If the moral choice is also the profitable one, the sacrifice deflates (`Q108.X2`); and the moment kindness gets
  a stat line the player optimizes it (`Q126.X7`, `Q125.X9`). The Whispering Hillock's masterstroke is ZERO reward
  difference between the branches, so the choice is purely about the people (`Q004.W4`).
- Put a real number on temptation: the mutagen in the corpse is scarce and build-relevant, and that beats any
  devil's speech (`Q148.W8`). Darkest Dungeon's light lever lets you dial danger up for reward (`Q041.W6`).
- Mercy is a line item. Papers, Please lets you break protocol and eat the citation, and citations cost your
  family (`Q108.W3`); scarcity turns compassion against your own (`Q031.W3`). This is the sharpest event vise we
  have: help the stranger with the medicine and a brother in the company goes without.
- Never grade it. The narration never grades the choice, ever (`Q126.X4`); a companion chorus with no dissent is a
  laugh track, not a conscience (`Q126.X5`).
- Every choice has no clean option in the best quests (`Q004.W3`, `Q030.W3`) and a player can survive as the kind
  one, harder (`Q030.W10`).

### 3. The face is a person, and one vivid image beats a policy
- This War of Mine's Quiet House (rob the scared old couple) and Frostpunk's child-labour slider work because each
  is ONE concrete image (`Q030.W4`, `Q029.W4`). An event is one image: a man on the shoulder, a woman with a toll
  book, a boy counting cables.
- Recurring faces make rules personal: the wife following her husband comes back through the booth (`Q031.W6`).
  Our roaming parties and the people we met on the street are the cast for events; a face you have seen before is
  worth ten new ones.
- Every stranger is Schroedinger's threat (`Q093.W3`), but that tension only exists in Rust because hundreds of
  real people make it; a single-player game has to MANUFACTURE it (`Q093.X5`). The wounded-stranger event is how we
  manufacture it.
- The figure who knows you, who holds your history and reflects it back, is instantly uncanny (`Q010.W1`); his
  moral tests carry no mechanical stake on purpose (`Q010.W3`); one line reframes everything ("many have",
  `Q010.W6`); he recurs across the saga (`Q010.W10`). This is the Amalgamation's road face, used rarely.
- Recognition from strangers is the reward of a long game: reputation is a memory, and strangers cite your
  specific deeds by name (`Q086.W8`).

### 4. The consequence comes back later, somewhere else
- The Whispering Hillock pays off quests later, in a different quest (`Q004.W5`; teardown #8). Alpha Protocol's
  world has a long memory (`Q024.W8`).
- Surface it. Deep reactivity is invisible on one playthrough unless an NPC references it or an ending tallies it
  (`Q024.W10`, `Q024.X2`); Dwarf Fortress's richest stories need a narrator or a chronicle to be seen (`Q045.X4`).
  For us the surface is built already: the city-view phone feed. An event's result becomes a post two or three trips
  later, from the other party's point of view.
- Consequence can pursue you: a taboo act sends scaling bounty hunters over time (`Q038.W9`). Losing makes your
  nemesis for free (`Q087.W5`), and the enemy remembers and references your methods (`Q048.W2`).
- Broken promises cost more than silence (`Q107.W8`). An event that asks for a promise must price the breaking.
- But do not detonate a choice with no in-scene warning: the spare trap that punishes without telegraphing makes
  players feel tricked, not taught (`Q149.X2`); the fix is that the world testifies first (`Q149.P4`). A villain
  given a sword who starts a war with no hint is the same flaw (`Q086.X7`), and an invisible counter that turns
  habit into an ending is worse (`Q118.X1`).
- A defeat on the road should continue, not end: Outward's defeat scenarios send you to a mine, get you robbed, or
  rescued by strangers (`Q087.W1`); Kenshi's captivity is a new game state, not a load screen (`Q146.W1`). An
  ambush event you lose is a story that keeps going.

### 5. Leaving must always be on the table
- Kenshi's flee option never closes, and its standing availability is what makes staying a choice (`Q151.W7`).
- Refusal that costs nothing works because every hour you do not take it you are choosing again (`Q126.W3`). For
  events: "keep moving" is always one of the choices, and it costs exactly what the situation costs (time lost to
  the stop is already spent; nobody curses you for passing). Declining is free, as Paolo ruled for contracts.
- The personal exit is always open and always smaller (`Q142.W8`).

### 6. The world state an event reads
- TRACKS. Death Stranding's paths smooth as people walk them (`Q089.W6`): traffic writes itself on the ground. A
  murder mystery's killers hide tracks and plant red herrings (`Q054.W8`); a contract plants a clue built to be
  misread once (`Q148.W3`); Fallout trusts the player to reason from the land (`Q038.W3`), but naturalistic hints can
  be too subtle (`Q038.X3`). Our map already has parties that leave tracks. A track event is the one place the map
  asks you to READ it.
- THE GRID. Project Zomboid's apocalypse is a process with a clock: electricity fails, water stops, on a schedule
  (`Q088.W4`). Majora's Ikana suite makes utilities and grief one system (`Q147.W2`), and the Pamela rescue sits
  seven infrastructure links deep (`Q138.W3`). Teardown #4 (That Lucky Old Sun) is allocation as drama: route the
  power, pick winners and losers. Our brownouts are never total dark; a brownout event is a pump, a clinic, a
  checkpoint that just lost power while you pass.
- THE SHARE. Power and water are held by share between factions. Who holds the road decides who stops you
  (`Q055.W7`). The checkpoint that only searches the poor is a faction's posture made physical (`Q100.W10`), and the
  gate is the posture: door policy broadcasts a settlement's ethics (`Q151.W5`). The tax collector's visit is the
  world's first acknowledgement that you exist, delivered as a bill (`Q151.W2`).
- BOOM OR BUST. Boom draws raiders and tolls (`Q044.W4`, `Q151.W3`). Bust draws desperation: Frostpunk's rebellion
  arrives as inventory shrinkage, thieves in the food stores (`Q107.W4`), and the people you saved become the
  antagonist (`Q107.W1`). Citizen Sleeper's clocks tick for you AND the world whether you engage or not (`Q065.W6`).
- THE COMPANY. The party is a powder keg: companions react to each other (`Q043.W2`), rivalries have teeth and can
  make one leave (`Q043.W3`), one broken mind stresses the rest (`Q041.W3`). The composition of the company decides
  which company events can fire, exactly as Battle Brothers does it.

### 7. What kills an event system (the phone list)
- Repetition. The vending machine (`Q066.X1`), diminishing personhood across dozens of contracts (`Q148.X3`), the
  heir who re-reads vignettes he has read (`Q144.X1`), the same pawn barks forever without a cooldown (`Q084.X6`),
  scavenging encounters that go rote (`Q030.X3`), and "when the story engine is not firing, actions feel rote"
  (`Q046.X1`).
- Sparseness. The opposite failure: a world so sparse that long stretches have nothing happening (`Q087.X3`), and
  traversal that is tedious without markers or events (`Q051.X3`). Death Stranding's first ten hours read as an
  "Amazon delivery simulator" (`Q089.X1`).
- Load. Tutorial overwhelm (`Q046.X2`, `Q065.X2`), late-game load that needs a notepad (`Q054.X4`), and touch
  controls that were not designed for a thumb (`Q060.X4`). The key moment playing in a corner of the screen while
  the player looks elsewhere is missed by most (`Q108.X4`).
- One correct script. If the scene has one right answer sequence, players follow a guide (`Q114.X4`).
- Unrelenting misery. The grimness needs texture and the occasional triumph (`Q041.X3`, `Q030.X2`, `Q031.X2`).
  Tonal swing is a feature: the absurd side story next to the grim main makes both hit harder (`Q013.W2`), and even
  Project Zomboid keeps a cartoon raccoon against the bleakness (`Q088.W10`).
- Balance going slack. Once the wallet is full, the choices stop costing (`Q065.X1`); a toll with four bypasses
  is not a cost (`Q126.X9`).

## THE TEN EVENT SHAPES (the reusable skeletons)
Each shape is a skeleton for RUN and WORDS: the trigger it reads, the face, the choices, the ledgers it writes,
the ids it comes from, and the flaws it must avoid on a phone. Every shape has "keep moving" as one choice.

### E1. THE ONE ON THE SHOULDER (the stranger who may be bait)
- TRIGGER: a person alone on a dirt road, near a place where a hostile party's tracks ended. The bait version fires
  only when a real hostile party is within reach; the honest version fires when none is. The player cannot know
  which, but the world testifies first: the tracks are on the map before the stop.
- FACE: the stranger, hurt or pretending. One line.
- CHOICES: stop and treat (costs medicine or batteries and an hour; may gain a recruit or a debt of gratitude);
  call out from the truck without getting down (costs nothing, learns one thing, the bait gets impatient); keep
  moving.
- LEDGERS: batteries or medicine, time, people (a recruit, or a wounded brother if it was bait), and a feed post
  later either way.
- FROM: `Q093.W3`, `Q093.X5`, `Q110.W3`, `Q031.W3`, `Q149.P4`, `Q030.W10`.
- AVOID: `Q149.X2` (the trap with no warning), `Q107.X1` (the option that does not match), `Q061.X4` (a party
  from nowhere).

### E2. THE TOLL (whoever holds the road takes a cut)
- TRIGGER: crossing a road segment held by a faction, weighted by its share and by how rich your company looks.
  Fires more in boom.
- FACE: the toll-taker, dressed in the faction's colour, a ledger in hand.
- CHOICES: pay (batteries, standing up a little); argue the rate down (standing with that faction, time); turn
  around and take the dirt road (time, and the dirt is slower). A fourth, violent answer exists only on the street
  scale, not in the event.
- LEDGERS: batteries, standing, time.
- FROM: `Q151.W2`, `Q100.W10`, `Q151.W5`, `Q108.W3`, `Q044.W4`, `Q151.W3`.
- AVOID: `Q126.X9` (a toll with four bypasses is no cost), `Q130.X7` (the exit too cheap), `Q065.X1` (the full
  wallet that makes it nothing: the toll scales with what you carry).

### E3. THE TRACKS AT THE FORK
- TRIGGER: the road forks where a roaming party's tracks leave it. Reads the tracks layer directly.
- FACE: a companion kneels over the ground and says what he reads (the companion is the mouth; the tracks are the
  thing).
- CHOICES: follow the tracks (time; a fight, a find, or an empty camp); take the other fork (time saved or lost);
  mark it and move on (the place goes on the map; nothing else).
- LEDGERS: time, and whatever is at the end.
- FROM: `Q089.W6`, `Q054.W8`, `Q148.W3`, `Q038.W3`, `Q050.W9`.
- AVOID: `Q038.X3` (hints too subtle: the companion says it plainly), `Q148.X2` (a trail that cannot be misread:
  ours sometimes leads to the wrong party).

### E4. THE QUARREL IN THE COMPANY
- TRIGGER: two people in the company whose traits or histories clash; fires only when both are aboard. Reads the
  company roster.
- FACE: one of them, then the other; two portraits, two mouths.
- CHOICES: back one (standing inside the company, one may leave); split the difference (time, a small cost to
  both); let them settle it (a fight on the fight board later, or nothing, or a friendship).
- LEDGERS: people in the company, morale, sometimes batteries (the thing they fight over).
- FROM: `Q043.W2`, `Q043.W3`, `Q041.W3`, `Q151.W9`, `Q055.W9`.
- AVOID: `Q126.X5` (a unanimous chorus: one side must have a good point), `Q043.X2` (reactions out of character),
  `Q043.X3` (silent state changes: the result is said out loud), `Q151.X3` (a company with no interior life).

### E5. THE TRADER WITH ONE GOOD THING
- TRIGGER: a trader party on the road; the goods on offer read from the nearest faction town's buildings, and the
  price reads boom or bust.
- FACE: the trader.
- CHOICES: buy the thing (batteries; the thing has a real use and a real catch); trade a person's gear or a
  favour instead (standing or people); keep moving.
- LEDGERS: batteries, an item, sometimes standing with whoever the goods were stolen from.
- FROM: `Q148.W8`, `Q041.W6`, `Q126.W8`, `Q092.W6`.
- AVOID: `Q126.X2` (strictly worse offer), `Q108.X2` (the right choice also the cheap one), `Q114.X6` (a trader
  loop the player can farm: once per trader party, then it is gone).

### E6. THE LIGHTS GO DOWN WHILE YOU PASS (the brownout)
- TRIGGER: a scheduled or share-driven brownout on the grid touching a place on your road: a pump house, a clinic,
  a hydroponic shed. Never total dark.
- FACE: whoever is working the place when it dims.
- CHOICES: lend your batteries to keep it running (batteries now, standing and a feed post later); help them
  reroute from the next place over (time, and somebody else goes dim: allocation); keep moving.
- LEDGERS: batteries, standing, time, and the grid's own ledger (who got the power).
- FROM: `Q088.W4`, `Q147.W2`, `Q138.W3`, `Q065.W6`, teardown #4.
- AVOID: `Q065.X1` (a rich company that can always pay: the ask scales), `Q029.X1` (the optimal allocation that
  flattens the ethics: two good causes, never one obvious).

### E7. THE PEOPLE WALKING THE OTHER WAY
- TRIGGER: a group on foot leaving a place: bust, a faction losing its share, a rumour of the reckoning. Reads the
  faction share and the act's crisis.
- FACE: the one who talks for the group.
- CHOICES: give them what they ask for (medicine, water, batteries); tell them what you know about where they are
  going (standing, maybe they turn back, maybe they do not believe you); keep moving. They may be right about the
  risk and wrong about the answer.
- LEDGERS: batteries or medicine, standing with the place they are leaving, the valley's population (a real
  number that moves).
- FROM: `Q107.W1`, `Q107.W3`, `Q030.W4`, `Q021.W5`, `Q031.W6`.
- AVOID: `Q030.X3` (the same refugees every time: each group has a face and a reason), `Q031.X2` (unrelenting:
  some groups are going somewhere good), `Q107.X6` (the arc that resolves by dissolving: the group appears
  again, on the feed or on a street).

### E8. THE ONE WHO KNOWS YOU
- TRIGGER: rare, once or twice an act, never before the player has a history worth reading. The Amalgamation's road
  face, or someone it has touched. Fires only where nothing records: dirt roads, under bridges, in the dark between
  pylons (its blind spot is the unrecorded).
- FACE: an ordinary person who knows one thing they cannot know.
- CHOICES: answer; lie; say nothing. No reward difference between them.
- LEDGERS: nothing visible, a private flag that later scenes read.
- FROM: `Q010.W1`, `Q010.W3`, `Q010.W6`, `Q010.W10`, `Q004.W4`, `Q085.W6`; teardown #15.
- AVOID: `Q126.X4` (the game grading the answer), `Q084.X6` (the figure turning into a bark), `Q010.W7` done
  badly: ambiguity is fine, contradiction is not.

### E9. THE BILL COMES DUE (a consequence that finds you on the road)
- TRIGGER: a past deed, dropped contract or road choice, recorded in a ledger, now within reach of the party it
  hurt. A delayed payoff, often across acts.
- FACE: the person the deed hurt, or someone sent.
- CHOICES: pay (batteries or a person's service); talk (standing, maybe the debt is reduced, maybe raised); fight
  (a fight on the fight board, and if you lose, the story continues: robbed, held, released).
- LEDGERS: all four, depending on the deed.
- FROM: `Q038.W9`, `Q004.W5`, `Q024.W8`, `Q087.W5`, `Q048.W2`, `Q107.W8`, `Q087.W1`, `Q146.W1`.
- AVOID: `Q086.X7` (untelegraphed: the original event said out loud who saw), `Q118.X1` (an invisible counter),
  `Q023.X2` (hidden ordering dependencies).

### E10. THE GOOD HOUR (relief)
- TRIGGER: the storyteller after a hard hit, or after three events that cost. Also the act's texture: more of these
  in act 3.
- FACE: someone with something to give: a family cooking, a wedding on the road, a kid who fixed a solar panel and
  wants you to see.
- CHOICES: stop and share (time, maybe a small gift back, morale up); leave something (batteries, a small line in
  standing); wave and keep moving.
- LEDGERS: morale, time, a feed post. Never a big number.
- FROM: `Q044.W7`, `Q041.W10`, `Q088.W10`, `Q013.W2`, `Q013.W4`, `Q089.W7`.
- AVOID: `Q041.X3` (unrelenting tone), `Q125.X9` (scoring kindness: the good hour pays no stat worth farming).

## HOW OFTEN EVENTS FIRE
The library's two failures are the vending machine (too often, `Q066.X1`, `Q046.X1`) and the empty road (too rare,
`Q087.X3`, `Q051.X3`). Battle Brothers solves it with cooldowns and composition. Our proposal, in numbers a
builder can test, all draft:
1. NEVER in the first 60 seconds of play and never before the first arrival on a street (the law). The Pathologic
   study's slow onset is the model: pressure is absent early and creeps in (`Q021.W3`), and a first hour that
   throws every system at once nearly loses players (`Q065.X2`).
2. AT MOST ONE event per trip. Never two in a row without an arrival between them.
3. A GLOBAL COOLDOWN measured in map time, not real seconds: after an event, no event for the next one to two
   in-game days of travel. Early game, aim for roughly one event every two or three trips; mid game, every two.
4. THE STORYTELLER RULE: after an event that cost a person or a large share of batteries, the next event is E10
   or E3, never E1, E2 or E9 (`Q044.W7`, `Q048.W9`).
5. EVERY EVENT INSTANCE FIRES ONCE. Skeletons repeat; people, places and lines do not. A skeleton cannot fire twice
   in the same act with the same face. Any batch of ten events written spans at least three tones (`Q114.N4`).
6. WEIGHTS COME FROM THE WORLD: the road's owner and share weight E2; the tracks layer weights E3 and E1; the grid
   weights E6; the company roster weights E4; the deed ledger weights E9; the act weights E8 and E10.
7. THE PHONE: an event is one portrait, one line of four to twelve words, and two or three choice buttons with the
   price on each. The price line uses the ledger icons, not a paragraph. Nothing else on screen moves while it is
   up, so it cannot be missed in a corner (`Q108.X4`).

## HOW EVENTS DIFFER BY ACT
The acts are eras of one family, and the future is derived from the past acts' ledgers. (Swept 9/29, rule 39c:
they are not all open at the start. Act 1 is played first with one person; the next generation unlocks later,
when the act's first endgame crisis starts (rule 85, around day 80 to 100), and after that the flip is his. An act 3 road feature written by an act 1 event is
seen once act 3 has unlocked.)
- ACT 1, THE RUIN (Animal). Events are about the body and the stranger. The stranger is a threat until proven
  otherwise (`Q093.W3`), you start as nobody (`Q055.W3`), and most events price medicine, batteries and the lives
  in the company. The shapes that fire most: E1, E3, E4, E7. The Destroyers are a force on the road, and their
  tracks are the ones you learn to fear. Tolls exist but are crude: whoever has a gun.
- ACT 2, CLAWING BACK (Human). Events are about institutions. Tolls get receipts (`Q151.W2`), the grid gets
  allocation choices (teardown #4), and the slippery slope of reasonable rules is the texture (`Q029.W3`), the
  way complicity through mundane paperwork is (`Q031.W1`). E2 and E6 fire most. E7 carries the reckoning: people
  walking away from rumours of the broadcast and the nuke. E9 starts to fire as act 1 deeds come due.
- ACT 3, CYBERPUNK HEALING (Angel). Events are about what to keep and what to hand on. The roads you and strangers
  built are smoother, faster, safer (`Q089.W4`, `Q089.W6`), so fewer ambushes and more E10. People you met in act
  1 speak about your grandparents in past tense (`Q144.W7`). E8 fires as the Network crumbles and its road faces
  start saying things that make no sense. E9 lands hardest here, where a choice from two generations back reaches
  the road (`Q004.W5`). Never worse than the start: an act 3 event never costs more of the ledgers than the act 1
  version of the same shape did.
- THE FLIP (three acts at once). An event in act 1 may write a ROAD FEATURE into act 3: the stranger you treated on
  the shoulder founds the rest stop on that corner; the toll you broke is a ruin. This is the cheapest visible
  cross-act consequence we have, and it is on the map where he looks.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)
1. Every event names its CAUSE in the world state (a party id, a map tile, a road owner, a roster pair, a ledger
   entry). An event with no cause does not ship. (`Q044.W2`, `Q061.X4`)
2. Every event has a face with a portrait and a mouth, and two or three choices. One is always "keep moving".
   (`Q151.W7`, `Q126.W3`)
3. Every choice shows its price in ledger terms before it is picked, and the outcome matches the words of the
   choice. (`Q107.W7`, `Q107.X1`)
4. No choice is strictly dominant, and the kind choice is never also the profitable one. (`Q126.X2`, `Q108.X2`)
5. No narration, companion chorus or feed post grades a choice. (`Q126.X4`, `Q126.X5`)
6. Every event writes at least one ledger line AND schedules one echo: a feed post, a later event, or a road
   feature. (`Q024.W10`, `Q004.W5`)
7. A delayed consequence is telegraphed at the time of the choice by something in the scene (a witness, a track,
   a line). (`Q149.P4`, `Q086.X7`)
8. An ambush you lose continues the story; it never ends the game on the road. (`Q087.W1`, `Q146.W1`)
9. Frequency limits: none in the first 60 s, one per trip, cooldown by map time, relief after a hard hit, each
   instance fires once. (`Q044.W7`, `Q066.X1`, `Q084.X6`)
10. Prices scale with what the company carries, so a full wallet never makes an event free. (`Q065.X1`, `Q044.W4`)
11. Any batch of ten written events spans at least three tones. (`Q114.N4`, `Q013.W2`)
12. Each event carries ONE wrong detail (analog horror), said plainly, never explained.

    share the fire), never a gate on reading; no two quiet encounters in a batch of ten share a mood.
