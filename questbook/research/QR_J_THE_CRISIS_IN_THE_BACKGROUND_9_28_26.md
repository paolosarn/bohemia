# QR-J: WHAT DOES A WORLD CRISIS DO TO THE CONTRACT BOARD WHILE IT RUNS IN THE BACKGROUND?

STATUS: draft:true, research only, nothing built (rule 35). Letter J, row [thin shelves], round 9/28.
Written for RUN, WORLD, PEOPLE, WORDS, ECONOMY and DYNASTY. Every name and identity choice is Paolo's.
Steered mid-round by Paolo's 9/28 ruling (records/BOHEMIA_PAOLO_QUESTS_ARE_THE_SETTLEMENT_CONTRACT_SCREEN_9_28_26.md):
contracts are Battle Brothers contracts, offered on a SETTLEMENT SCREEN, in the hall, by a client with a portrait,
with a price and a negotiation. So the central lever of this page is WHAT IS ON THE BOARD: which settlements offer
what, at what pay, and which halls go quiet or close while a crisis runs.
FOLDED 10/9 (QR-AL): QR-U's rules 19 and 20 and the sharpens of rules 8, 9, 11 and 17, at the end of THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS, each marked "(folded 10/9 from QR-U)" in place.

QUESTION: The crisis shelves are thin (after round one: 1 design on the earth-side nuke, 2 on the whisper
broadcast, against 26 with no crisis). What do the 152 do with a WORLD CRISIS that runs in the background
while side content continues (an invasion, a plague, a war, a countdown, a collapse)? How does a crisis change
the contract pool, the prices, the people who ask and the road events, without a punishing timer? How does side
content FEED or SHADE the crisis? How do the best endings land the stance (our three: LIBERATE, RESPECT, BECOME)
through the side work the player did? Then, per canon crisis (the Destroyers, the whisper broadcast, the
earth-side nuke, the rocket, the Network crumbling), the shape of the contracts and events that belong to it.

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

In the library, a crisis that works in the background is never a timer on the screen; it is a PROCESS the world
is going through, and the player reads it off the things the world sells and asks for. The strongest version
is Project Zomboid's: the apocalypse is not the monsters but the infrastructure running down on a schedule
(`Q088.W4`), told through the world's own dying media (`Q088.W8`). The second strongest is Frostpunk's: the
crisis shows up first as a supply number (food going missing) before it is a person (`Q107.W4`), and the people
it produces are right about the risk and wrong about the answer (`Q107.W3`). Battle Brothers, Paolo's named
reference for the campaign layer, does exactly this at the settlement: a crisis "changes what every town needs
and what contracts appear," and a town's SITUATION ("raided", "plague", "famine", "high demand for X") moves
its prices (reference/library/battle_brothers/01_WORLDMAP.md, 07_ECONOMY.md). So for Bohemia the rule is: a
crisis is a small set of world-state STAGES, each stage rewrites the board (new clients, new jobs, new pay,
some halls quiet), rewrites the prices, and rewrites the road, and NONE of it ever expires a contract the
player already took or punishes a player for being slow. The library is just as clear on what goes wrong: a
silent clock on content the player loves is "a bug wearing a design costume" (`Q095.P7`), a hidden timer is
"honest ONCE and vicious always" (`Q137.X3`), and a crisis that ends by quietly dissolving leaves the best
player with the least story (`Q107.X6`). Side work FEEDS or SHADES the crisis by being a real input to the
world's own numbers (the crowdfunded highway, `Q089.W4`; the mercy that shows up in the passenger manifest,
`Q123.W5`), and the shade has to be visible, because a kindness that secretly fed the atrocity is a
consequence nobody learns (`Q092.X6`). Finally, the best endings do not ask the player which stance they
want; they READ the life (`Q085.W9`: the machine grants what you actually wanted, based on how you played),
count it aloud (`Q136.W10`), and judge HOW you survived, not whether (`Q029.W6`). For Bohemia that means the
same contract row is read three ways at the end: LIBERATE reads it as a hand, RESPECT as a restraint, BECOME
as a kindness done from above. A row is a voice in the ending, never a toll for a stance.

## THE FINDINGS

### 1. The crisis is a process the world is going through, not a clock the player is racing

- THE APOCALYPSE AS INFRASTRUCTURE RUNNING DOWN. `Q088.W4`: "ELECTRICITY EVENTUALLY FAILS. WATER SYSTEMS STOP
  FUNCTIONING. FOOD SPOILS. MEDICAL SUPPLIES DISAPPEAR." Its port `Q088.P2` names our three currencies
  (medicine, electricity, resources) as "three CLOCKS that run down on a schedule." Bohemia's canon softens this
  (brownouts, never total dark; the future gets better, never worse than the start), so our crisis clocks are
  STAGES that tilt the board, not meters that run to zero.
- SLOW ONSET. `Q021.W3`: "the pressure is absent early and creeps in by day 4-5 ... you LEARN the cruelty by
  living it." `Q021.P3`: "stage a Bohemia crisis so the pressure CREEPS (calm early, dire by mid)." A crisis
  that arrives all at once is a cutscene; one that arrives as a price on a board is a world.
- THE WORLD'S CLOCKS TICK WHETHER YOU ENGAGE. `Q065.W6` ("multi-step trackers (yours + the world's, ticking
  whether you engage)"), `Q065.P5` ("the world's actions (a faction's plan, the Amalgamation's advance, a raid)").
  `Q085.W1`, `Q055.W7`, `Q061.W2`: the world runs without you. BB is the same: crises roll across the map.
- BUT THE SPINE DOES NOT RACE YOU. QR-H already settled this for the main line (`Q126.W3`, `Q114.N2`). Here is
  the crisis half of it: the library's counter-cases are the hidden clocks (`Q006.W6`: "wandering KILLS them";
  `Q006.P5` ports it as "DALLYING costs lives") and the countdown as emotion (`Q011.W5`, `Q011.P6`). Their own
  flaws say what they cost: `Q137.X3` (vicious always), `Q038.X1` ("the hard clock alienates"; the sequel removed
  it), `Q026.X1` (the time limit is the biggest negative), `Q095.P7` (a silent timer is a bug), `Q128.X1` (three
  days of exact hours: blind completion is "near-mythical"), `Q128.X10` (every retry replays in real time), and
  the caveat the Majora study itself banked, `Q011.P12` ("make timed windows GENEROUS + legible"). For a phone,
  at your pace, the crisis moves the WORLD, never the player's taken work.
- WHAT MOVES A STAGE. `Q044.W2`: the storyteller "READS your state + picks the event." `Q071.W6`: gated by
  world-state, not markers. For Bohemia the canon already supplies the two drivers: in act 1 the Destroyers
  COALESCE by the city strand's own rules (ACT1 PROCEDURAL ENDING law); in act 2 the secret crises move by the
  threat logic ("threat scales with PROXIMITY TO THE SECRET"), which means they move when the player LOOKS
  (`Q085.W8`, the fever). A player who never looks lives in a valley whose crisis stays at its quiet stage.

### 2. The crisis rewrites the board: who asks, what they ask, what they pay, which halls go quiet

- THE CRISIS APPEARS FIRST AS A SUPPLY LINE, THEN AS A PERSON. `Q107.W4` ("THIEVES BROKE INTO THE FOOD STORES")
  and `Q107.P5` ("surface dissent as a NUMBER before it's a person"). On a BB board this is literal: the first
  sign of a crisis is a new KIND of contract (guard the stores, find the thief) appearing in halls that never
  offered it.
- THE APOCALYPSE IS AN ECONOMY. `Q086.W2` (the world is ending because of FACTORIES), `Q097.W1` (the curse IS the
  economy: "no business will ever thrive there"), `Q094.W1` (the biggest war in games started with an unpaid
  bill). For an economic-crash game this is the house style: every crisis must be readable as work offered and
  prices charged, before it is readable as a monster.
- THE CRISIS BRINGS STRANGE CLIENTS. `Q096.W3` ("the deal pays in the EXACT currency you need": a fleet during
  an extinction war), `Q092.W6` (the Church sells the plague AND the cure and armed the hunters), `Q031.W5` (the
  regime PAYS you to hand over the desperate), `Q113.W7` (the rival doctors bring their own lists). A crisis is
  when enemies post jobs in each other's halls. Bohemia's canon names the purest case: the Destroyers are hated
  even by the Cartel, because the Cartel "at least needs some civilization standing." So in act 1 the Cartel
  hires the company to keep a doctor alive (QD-J07).
- EVERYONE KEEPS THEIR SCHEDULE. `Q128.W2`: "the world ends in three days and everyone clocks in. Routine as
  courage, and as denial." `Q128.W8`: the postman reading his own regulations "for permission to be afraid."
  Most of the board does NOT change in a crisis: the escort, the delivery, the lost goat stay. The crisis is a
  few new rows and a few repriced ones on a board that is otherwise stubbornly ordinary. That ordinariness is
  the analog horror.
- HALLS GO QUIET, AND THE QUIET IS A PLACE. `Q147.W7` ("the failure state is visible geography": the dry river
  stays, walkably), `Q128.W6` (failure is ambient: the world "quietly holds its sadder configuration"), with
  `Q128.X2`'s repair (it needs one tell). `Q151.W10`: "the region just quietly re-prices." In BB terms, a hall
  that closes is a settlement screen with the hall's door boarded and one line from whoever is still there.
  Never a message; a building.
- PROSPERITY DRAWS THE WRONG KIND OF ATTENTION, BUT NOT FROM THE SECRET. `Q151.W3` (raids scale with visible
  prosperity) and `Q044.W4` (wealth-scaled pressure) are right for the Destroyers (bandits go where the food is)
  and WRONG for the Amalgamation, whose canon rule is that building is safe. So the act 1 board tilts toward
  guard work where the player built; the act 2 board tilts only where the player looked.

### 3. Prices are the crisis's voice

- BB's SETTLEMENT SITUATIONS move prices ("raided: food scarce and dear; well-supplied: cheap"; buying raises the
  local price; prices recover over days; 07_ECONOMY.md). The library backs it: `Q082.W9` (the ruin is a real
  town and the wine's PRICE is still tracked), `Q107.W9` (hope is a building "that COSTS 20 FOOD RATIONS"),
  `Q089.W6` (paths smooth as people walk them: use changes the world's costs).
- THE CRISIS MUST KEEP COSTING. `Q065.X1` (by midgame you "hit max wallet" and ignore the economy) and `Q144.X2`
  (grind outweighs grief: death reads as lost progress) are the two ways a background crisis dies. The fix the
  library names is to price the crisis in the things a rich player still needs: people, time, standing, and
  scarce specific goods (paper in the broadcast's build-up, a mechanic's hours in the rocket's).
- A PRICE IS A RUMOUR YOU CAN BUY. `Q018.W3` (the rumour web, no waypoints) and `Q001.W2` (rumour, then human,
  then tragic return). A price that jumps (paper at three times the usual in one town) is a thread. The player
  who asks "why" at the counter is choosing to look, and in act 2 looking is what raises the heat.

### 4. The crisis has a face: the people who ask change

- ONE FACE, NOT A STATISTIC. `Q030.W4` (the emblematic dilemma: one concrete image), `Q030.W1` (catastrophe
  re-centered on the powerless), `Q031.W6` (recurring human faces return). On a settlement screen the crisis is
  whose portrait is in the hall: a Cartel lieutenant in a clinic town, a printer in a trade town, a tunnel
  keeper instead of a merchant.
- THE PEOPLE THE CRISIS MAKES ARE RIGHT ABOUT THE RISK. `Q107.W1` (the antagonist is the people you saved),
  `Q107.W3` (right about the risk, wrong about the answer), `Q128.W3` (a woman declines rescue and stays for a
  maybe), and the flaw beside it, `Q128.X5` ("the sensible coward deserves one scene of vindication-adjacent
  dignity"). Bohemia's crisis people should include someone who LEAVES, and the game must not call them wrong.
- THE TOWN INVENTS A MONSTER. `Q098.W7`: the shabnak-adyr, "BROUGHT UP BY A GRIEVING TOWN AS AN EXPLANATION."
  This is the act 1 ghost law in one line: in act 1, and for most people in act 2, the secret crises are told
  in superstition. A client can be wrong about what the job is for, and the job is still real.

### 5. The crisis on the road

- THE CORPSE OF ANOTHER CITY LIKE YOURS. `Q107.W2`: the uprising is sparked by Winterhome, "another colony very
  similar," already fallen. Bohemia already owns the purest form: Dubai. A road event can carry Dubai as a
  thing, never as an explanation (the ACT1 ghost law and the Dubai addendum both say do not over-explain).
- THE WORLD EXPLAINS ITSELF THROUGH ITS OWN MEDIA. `Q088.W8` (TV and radio "until the stations go dark"),
  `Q101.W3` (the quest arrives by an automated broadcast from a dead machine), `Q090.W7` (the story arrives by
  radio "just when you might be lost for things to do"). In Bohemia the phone feed is the city view's radio: it
  may REPORT a crisis stage (the feed law); it never delivers a contract. (folded 10/1 from rule 51: the phone may also
  LIST which settlements offer what, read-only, so a crisis client can show on it as a board line; accepting is still
  only at the place.)
- RELIEF AFTER THE HIT. `Q044.W7` (after a hard hit, send relief), `Q088.W10` (the bleakest sim ships a cartoon
  raccoon "TO COUNTERACT THE BLEAKNESS"). A crisis road table needs good-hour events too, or it becomes
  `Q031.X2` (relentless) and `Q088.X6` ("it's not a typically fun game").
- DO NOT LET THE ROAD BECOME A VENDING MACHINE. `Q066.X1` (revisited islands: you "stop seeing the writing and
  start seeing the game"), `Q030.X3` (repetition across a long siege), `Q044.X1` (Randy: unfair groups of
  events). A crisis event fires once per face, and the crisis's road table is small and hand-made.

### 6. Side work FEEDS or SHADES the crisis, and the player can see which

- THE SIDE WORK IS THE WIND-UP. `Q006.W1`, `Q006.W2` ("optional in name but load-bearing for the ending"),
  `Q136.W2` (the ballot was cast weeks ago), `Q137.W3` (loyalty is armor, literally). This is QR-H's ballot,
  seen from the crisis side: every finished contract in a crisis stage is an input to that crisis's resolution.
- THE CROWD FUNDS THE BRIDGE. `Q089.W4` (no single player can afford the bridge; waves each pay a piece; later
  waves drive on it) and `Q089.W3` (people build with no reward). The whisper broadcast and the rocket are both
  this shape in canon: many small hands, one thing that goes up. A contract that FEEDS the crisis is one piece
  of the bridge, and it should be visible as a piece (a ream on a shelf, a bolt in a rack).
- MERCY SHOWS UP IN THE MANIFEST. `Q123.W5` ("EVERY FERAL GHOUL YOU SPARE NOW IS ONE THAT WE CAN SAVE LATER"),
  `Q125.W8` (the best outcome is purchased with mercy you did not know you were banking), `Q145.W7` (mercy
  planted blind pays off named). The feed is often a kindness done for its own sake that the crisis later
  counts.
- THE SHADE: YOUR ESCALATION IS UPSTREAM OF THE MURDER. `Q107.W6` (the murder "can be already prevented by
  choosing a different option earlier"), `Q029.W3` (the slippery slope: each reasonable step greases the next).
  A contract can SHADE the crisis: take the war work while a faction conflict is being quietly accelerated and
  you are part of the acceleration (QD-J03).
- THE SHADE MUST BE VISIBLE. `Q092.X6` ("you can cause atrocities by being kind and never find out": a
  consequence the player never discovers is wasted), `Q086.X7` ("a consequence the player couldn't have foreseen
  isn't a choice"), `Q104.X6` (side content that silently fails main content is a shipping failure). So every
  feed or shade writes one thing in the world the player can find: a person, a building, a price.
- UTILITIES AND GRIEF ARE ONE SYSTEM. `Q147.W2` ("the river is dry because a soul is") and `Q147.W8` (the
  reward is water, "cashed downstream by people the player hasn't met"). Bohemia's native tongue: a crisis
  contract pays in the currency the crisis is draining, and its effect lands downstream on a board the player
  sees later.

### 7. The crisis must not dissolve, and must not be won for free

- `Q107.X6` (max your hope and the Londoners "just... stop. There's no scene"), `Q152.X3` (the cycle's breaking
  is free, "a boss health bar"), `Q079.X2` (the payoff is offscreen), `Q097.X3` (nothing changes), `Q078.X5` (a
  sealed box: nothing outside changes). Each crisis stage that ends must end in a PLACE the player can walk
  into: the reopened hall, the returned families, the repriced counter.
- NOBODY ELSE FINISHES IT. `Q085.W3`: the cut feature was an NPC completing the main mission first; side quests
  only. The world may resolve contracts inside a crisis without the player (the board refills, strangers take
  the job the player declined, as QD-A06 already shows); the crisis's own climax waits for the player.
- THE REPEATING CRISIS FARMS ITSELF. `Q151.X1` (the siege AI collapses into patterns; veteran bases farm their
  attackers). A crisis whose contracts can be farmed for pay has turned into a vending machine; each crisis
  row has a stock (a number of offers per stage), and it runs out.

### 8. How the best endings land the stance through the side work

- THE MACHINE READS WHAT YOU ACTUALLY WANTED. `Q085.W9`: "five of seven endings are the machine reading your real
  desire ... based on how you PLAYED." `Q050.W3` (when and how you face the final threat authors the ending, not
  a menu). The story master's stances resolve at the Moon, shaped by the act 2 reckoning; this is the craft
  that makes that reading feel earned.
- WHO DID YOU BECOME. `Q029.W6` ("the ending judges the RAMIFICATIONS OF HOW you survived, not whether"),
  `Q029.W9` (the dark laws are optional and the drift is tallied), `Q025.W9` (companions serve from love or fear,
  and it shows in the epilogue).
- THE ROLL CALL WITH RECEIPTS. `Q136.W10` (the room counts aloud), `Q137.W1` (every death is a receipt),
  `Q006.W8` (end by tallying), `Q137.W4` (the casting is the choice), `Q042.W5` (the whole game's standing pays
  out at once). And the flaw it must avoid: `Q136.X1` (the pre-count is invisible until it is not), with
  `Q102.P8` ("show the player they're building keys").
- EACH STANCE HAS A LIBRARY SHAPE, AND IT IS BUILT OUT OF SIDE WORK:
  - LIBERATE (bastion, strings cut, one valley saved, "and that is enough"). `Q080.W3` (the heroes win by
    refusing the weapon), `Q080.W8` (the thesis proved by restraint), `Q085.W10` ("I'll never know if I was
    right"), `Q123.W10` (the best you can do is 12.5%), `Q071.W10` (an ambiguous liberation). Its roll call is
    of HANDS: the unrecorded contracts finished face to face.
  - RESPECT (coexistence, a grave that thinks, at peace). `Q021.W8` (two reasonable end-choices players flip
    on), `Q148.W7` (no reform is on sale: sparing a nature keeps the choice adult), `Q080.W7` (every faction is a
    different answer). Its roll call is of RESTRAINT: what the family could have taken and left.
  - BECOME (stewardship, the god, damned by success "made of kindnesses"). `Q078.W7` (the mercy is a massacre),
    `Q092.W9` (the whole game was an interview), `Q085.W7` (the fanatic army is made of pilgrims), `Q034.W7`
    (the false salvation: comfort for copies). Its roll call is of KINDNESSES DONE FROM ABOVE: the same
    contracts, remembered as the first times the family decided for other people.
  The same row, three readings. That is the design lever: a contract never needs three versions; the ENDING
  needs one line per row per stance. No row is required for any stance (QR-H rule 6), and nothing about the
  reading is shown as a score (`Q126.X4`: the narration never grades the choice).

### 9. Tone under a crisis

- `Q128.W10` (the end of the world attends a wedding: "meaning is a thing people do at deadlines"), `Q011.W5`
  (devotion under a falling sky), `Q128.W2` (routine as courage). And the cautions: `Q021.X1` (the survival
  meters can dominate the story), `Q030.X2` ("too much ethical design makes the player feel bored"), `Q031.X2`
  (relentless). Every crisis design needs one warm or funny beat (QR-G checklist line 26).

## PER CANON CRISIS: THE SHAPE OF ITS CONTRACTS AND EVENTS

Canon sources: laws/BOHEMIA_STORY_MASTER_7_18_26.md, ACT1_PROCEDURAL_ENDING_AND_DESTROYERS_7_19_26,
ACT3_MOONSHOT_STRUCTURE_7_19_26, AMALGAMATION_THREAT_LOGIC_7_1_26, DUBAI_PRECEDENT_SECRECY_7_18_26,
ACT1_AMALGAMATION_IS_A_GHOST_7_24_26, THE_THREE_ACTS_AT_ONCE_9_23_26, and GDD v3 (older) on the whisper network.
Where the canon does not say how something works, this page says OPEN and designs around the hole.

### THE DESTROYERS (act 1; a force, not a faction; scattered until they coalesce at the end of act 1)

- STAGES (a default for WORLD): SCATTERED (tracks on the map, one burned farm), GATHERING (two or three bands, a
  road that is not safe), COALESCED (one force, the door to the procedural climax, QD-H02). Driven by the city
  strand, and tilted toward where the player BUILT (prosperity draws raiders, `Q151.W3`), never by a clock.
- THE BOARD: guard work multiplies (defend a village at night, escort a caravan through a burned stretch, BB's
  "protect a village from a raid"). Strange clients appear, because every faction hates the Destroyers: the
  Cartel posts work in a clinic town (QD-J07). Halls in burned settlements go quiet: the screen shows the hall
  boarded and one survivor's line. Pay rises for fighting work, falls for trade work.
- PRICES: BB's "raided" situation. Food dear where they passed, looted goods cheap on the roads after (the ash
  market, QD-J08). Batteries tight.
- ROAD EVENTS: tracks at the fork (QD-B02 exists), the ash market, the good hour when two enemies share a fire.
- WHAT IT FEEDS: the procedural climax's ledger (what the player built, whom they stood with). Nothing about
  the Destroyers is secret, so nothing here raises proximity heat.
- EXISTING: QD-B02, QD-C01, QD-E02, QD-H02, QD-D04, QD-D06. NEW: QD-J07, QD-J08.

### THE WHISPER BROADCAST (act 2; the truth released at once through trusted people)

- CANON: the story master places it at the end of act 2. GDD v3 (older) describes a whisper network of "offline
  evidence hand-carried to trusted factions and their chapters, through tunnels and face-to-face meetings the
  Amalgamation's data compilation can't see," released on the player's signal. The player "barely knows" what
  the thing is even by the end of act 2 (Dubai addendum). HOW the broadcast goes out in the act 2 shape is OPEN.
- STAGES: QUIET (the feed deletes one post; nobody notices), GATHERING (people who carry things by hand are in
  demand; paper and face-to-face meetings get dear), THE NIGHT (QD-H04 exists). The stage moves with the
  player's LOOKING and with the hands the family has shaken (QR-H's ledger), never by a clock.
- THE BOARD: the jobs are the offline ones. Carry a sealed thing by hand; guard a printer; clear cameras out of
  a tunnel (QD-J06); escort paper (QD-J04). Clients do not know what the work is for; they know it pays this
  month. Nobody on a screen says the machine's name.
- PRICES: whatever is offline gets dear (paper, ink, a trusted courier's day). Whatever is recorded gets cheap
  (the relay kiosk's message fee falls, because the network wants traffic). That price gap is the whole theme
  of the recorded and the unrecorded, told as a price list (`Q146.W5`: the antagonist is a price list).
- ROAD EVENTS: a post that will not stay posted (QD-J05), a brownout while you pass (QD-B05 exists).
- WHAT IT FEEDS: the hands (QR-H), the roll call of QD-H04. Taking these contracts raises proximity heat a
  little: that is the canon cost of looking, and it is stated on the screen as a danger, never as a number.
- EXISTING: QD-B05, QD-E03, QD-H03, QD-H04, QD-D06 (landing). NEW: QD-J04, QD-J05, QD-J06.

### THE EARTH-SIDE NUKE (act 2; the family stops it; Dubai is the precedent)

- CANON: the family stops the earth-side nuke at the end of act 2; Dubai was nuked when the secret spread "too
  fast to too many"; the world does not know who did Dubai; the thing's response ladder is surgical (accident,
  quietly accelerated faction conflict, untraceable out-of-state hit squad, then activated out-of-state carriers
  "before the final confrontation"). HOW the nuke is readied, where it is, and how it is stopped are OPEN, and
  whether Vegas is "the next Dubai" is PENDING Paolo. So no design here shows the device.
- THE HARD PART FOR A BACKGROUND CRISIS: the public does not know. A secret crisis cannot change the board by
  announcement. It changes the board only through its SHADOWS, which canon already names: the ladder's rungs.
  The library's shape for this is the crisis that shows up as a number first (`Q107.W4`) and the town that
  invents its own explanation (`Q098.W7`).
- STAGES: STILL (nothing), THE RUNGS (accidents; a faction conflict that flares for no reason; strangers from
  out of state at the valley's edges), THE RUMOUR (half-right, in one neighbourhood: QD-B06 exists), THE STOP
  (the main beat). The stage moves with the player's proximity heat (threat logic), so a player who never looks
  never sees past STILL.
- THE BOARD: war contracts spike and pay too well (the accelerated conflict, QD-J03); edge towns post watching
  jobs about quiet strangers (QD-J01); the rich sell up and leave (QD-J02), which floods one road with cheap
  goods and closes one hall (their shop). Clients never say nuke; they say "something."
- WHAT IT FEEDS OR SHADES: taking the war work shades it (you are the acceleration, `Q107.W6`); watching the
  strangers feeds it (you are looking, and the heat rises); buying from the leavers is neutral commerce with a
  thread in it.
- EXISTING: QD-B06, QD-H04 (behind the broadcast). NEW: QD-J01, QD-J02, QD-J03.

### THE ROCKET (act 3; the crashed world builds a one-way rocket in the open)

- CANON: one-way; built by a city-state; the Act 3 city-builder puzzle; the severance lets it be built in the
  open; "one rocket test" is FLOATED only, not canon (do not use).
- STAGES: THE YARD OPENS, THE BUILD (the valley's skilled labour and parts pulled toward the yard), THE LIST
  (who goes, QD-E06 exists), THE EVE (QD-H06 exists).
- THE BOARD: a boom with a hole in it. The yard pays best, so every hall near it posts yard work; everywhere
  else, ordinary jobs that need a skilled hand go unfilled (QD-J09). Theft of parts appears (QD-B07 exists).
- PRICES: parts, metals, skilled hours go up; ordinary repairs become unaffordable. This is a real boom's
  shape: the crowding-out of everything else by the one project everyone is paid to build.
- ROAD EVENTS: the shift change on the highway (QD-J10), the trader with one good thing (QD-B07 exists).
- WHAT IT FEEDS OR SHADES: every hour of the valley's labour is either in the rocket or in the valley. Neither is
  wrong; the ending reads which.
- EXISTING: QD-A06, QD-B07, QD-E06, QD-F06, QD-G04, QD-H06. NEW: QD-J09, QD-J10.

### THE NETWORK CRUMBLING (act 3; war path or conversion path; carriers cannot be turned)

- CANON: set by act 2; on the war path the Network is cult-like and fights to reverse the severance; on the
  conversion path its un-implanted members help build the rocket; either way it falls apart; its leaders are
  the few who spoke to the thing; carriers are puppets and cannot be converted.
- STAGES: THE SILENCE (the god has gone quiet; carriers and believers disoriented), THE SPLIT (war or
  conversion), THE FALL (its buildings go dark; its hoards come out).
- THE BOARD: the Network's own halls close; its hoarded goods (the story master names the forge's "hoarded chips
  and knowledge") reach the open market through councils that need guarding (QD-J11). Its people appear as
  clients for the first time, asking for ordinary things.
- PRICES: whatever the Network hoarded crashes when it comes out; whatever it provided as a service (screens,
  relays, its clinics if it ran any: OPEN, not canon) goes dark and gets dear.
- ROAD EVENTS: the families of carriers on the road (QD-J12), the old woman under the maglev (QD-B08 exists).
- EXISTING: QD-B08, QD-C05, QD-E05, QD-H05, QD-D05, QD-G03. NEW: QD-J11, QD-J12.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)

1. A CRISIS IS A SET OF STAGES, NOT A TIMER. Each canon crisis has 3 to 4 named stages (above). A stage is a
   world-state value WORLD owns. Test: no stage change is caused by wall-clock time alone. (`Q021.W3`,
   `Q088.W4`, `Q095.P7`, `Q137.X3`)
2. THE SECRET CRISES MOVE ONLY WHEN THE PLAYER LOOKS. The whisper broadcast and the earth-side nuke advance on
   proximity heat (threat logic). Test: a save that never looks keeps both at their first stage for any number
   of map days. (`Q085.W8`, threat-logic addendum)
3. A STAGE REWRITES THE BOARD, AND THE REWRITE IS ON THE SETTLEMENT SCREEN. Each stage has a row table: which
   settlements add which contract kinds, at what pay band, and which halls go quiet. Test: two saves at
   different stages of the same crisis show different hall contents in at least three settlements. (BB
   01_WORLDMAP.md, `Q107.W4`, `Q151.W10`)
4. A CLOSED HALL IS A BUILDING, NEVER A MESSAGE. The settlement screen shows the hall boarded, dark or occupied by
   someone else, with one spoken line from a portrait. (`Q147.W7`, `Q128.X2`, rule 20 as amended for contracts)
5. THE CRISIS CHANGES PRICES THROUGH BB SITUATIONS. Each stage sets at least one situation per affected
   settlement (raided, high demand for X, well supplied) that moves at least two prices. Test: the price of
   the crisis's signature good differs by stage. (07_ECONOMY.md, `Q082.W9`, `Q107.W9`)
6. A TAKEN CONTRACT IS NEVER EXPIRED BY A STAGE CHANGE. If the world moves while a contract is open, the contract
   stays finishable as offered; the world around it may change (a twist from QR-C), never whether it can be
   done. (`Q148.W9`, QR-A, `Q104.X6`)
7. MOST OF THE BOARD STAYS ORDINARY. At most one in three contracts on any board belongs to the crisis; the
   rest are the valley's ordinary work. (`Q128.W2`, `Q148.X3`, `Q066.X1`)
8. THE CRISIS BRINGS AT LEAST ONE STRANGE CLIENT PER STAGE: someone who would never hire this company in peace.
   (`Q096.W3`, `Q092.W6`, `Q031.W5`)
9. NOBODY SAYS THE SECRET. In act 1 no screen, line or post names the machine. In act 2 no client names the
   Amalgamation or the nuke; they say "something" or they tell a superstition. (ACT1 ghost law, Dubai
   addendum, `Q098.W7`)
10. EVERY FEED OR SHADE WRITES ONE THING THE PLAYER CAN FIND: a person, a building, a price, a feed post after the
    fact. Never an invisible flag alone. (`Q092.X6`, `Q086.X7`, QR-G checklist line 22)
11. THE CRISIS KEEPS COSTING A RICH PLAYER. Crisis contracts price in people (rule 36: struck down, 20% dead, else
    30 to 40 days), time, standing and scarce specific goods, not only batteries. (`Q065.X1`, `Q144.X2`)
12. CRISIS ROWS HAVE STOCK. A stage offers a fixed number of each crisis contract kind; when they are taken or the
    stage moves, they are gone. No farming. (`Q151.X1`, `Q066.X1`)
13. EVERY STAGE THAT ENDS ENDS IN A PLACE: the hall reopens, the families come back, the counter reprices, and the
    feed reports it after. (`Q107.X6`, `Q152.X3`, `Q079.X2`, feed law)
14. THE ENDING READS THE ROWS THREE WAYS. For every crisis contract kind WORDS writes one epilogue line per stance
    (LIBERATE as a hand, RESPECT as a restraint, BECOME as a kindness from above). No contract is required for
    any stance; no reading is scored or graded. (`Q085.W9`, `Q029.W6`, `Q136.W10`, `Q126.X4`)
15. SHOW THE KEYS. Before each act's crisis resolves, the player can see in the world (people in places, the
    board) roughly what their side work fed. (`Q102.P8`, `Q136.X1`)
16. ONE GOOD HOUR PER CRISIS ROAD TABLE. Each crisis's road-event table includes at least one relief event.
    (`Q044.W7`, `Q088.W10`, `Q031.X2`)
17. SOMEONE LEAVES, AND IS NOT WRONG. Each secret or deadly crisis includes one person who leaves or refuses,
    given dignity, not scorn. (`Q128.X5`, `Q151.W7`, `Q107.W3`)
18. DECLINING A CRISIS CONTRACT IS FREE, EVEN IN A CRISIS. The screen may show that people need it; the no writes
    nothing, and the stage does not move because the player said no. Strangers may take the job (QD-A06).
    (`Q126.W3`, QR-A)

19. A CRISIS STAGE MAY HAND THE PLAYER THE SWITCH. (folded 10/9 from QR-U) Infrastructure is a moral act; the console is a verdict
(`Q169.W1`); the output is finite and every allocation leaves someone dark (`Q169.W2`); the console judges nothing
(`Q169.W5`); the consequence is downstream and quiet (`Q169.W8`). Power and water are held by SHARE; a stage that breaks
a share may put one allocation in the company's hands. Flaws: effects too diffuse (`Q169.X1`), nobody holds the
switch-holder to account (`Q169.X3`). Fixes: lit and dark bases differ on their boards (rule 3, `Q169.P2`) and people
remember who lit them (`Q169.P4`). Test: after an allocation, at least one dark base's board shows it.

20. THE HOARDED CURE. (folded 10/9 from QR-U) The scarcity is a choice; the hoarding IS the scarcity (`Q232.W4`). The poor cannot pay
(`Q232.W2`); the shining city arranged not to see (`Q232.W5`); at least one of the poor is a named person (`Q232.P4`).
Medicine is one of our three economy legs; a medicine crisis stage is this study. Test: a medicine stage names one
poor person with a face and says who holds the stock.

SHARPENED (folded 10/9 from QR-U):
- Rule 11 (the crisis keeps costing): every hour of deciding is a body, refusing to commit is a choice (`Q206.W4`);
  compel, never crush (`Q206.P3`); a different committer saves different people (`Q206.W9`). Prices move by map day,
  never wall clock (rule 1).
- Rule 9 (nobody says the secret): `Q218.W6`, `Q209.W8` (see QR-H). A client who half-knows says less than he knows,
  because saying it spreads the target.
- Rule 8 (a strange client per stage): the institution has abdicated and the player fills the vacuum (`Q160.W1`); a
  crisis that empties a base's law makes the strange client a widow with a murder the watch will not look at.
- Rule 17 (someone leaves, and is not wrong): the departure is correct, not a problem (`Q188.W3`); exile as
  incompatibility, not punishment (`Q228.W3`).
