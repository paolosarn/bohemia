# QR-L: HOW MUCH DOES A JOB PAY, AND HOW DOES THE PLAYER SEE IT AT A GLANCE?

QUESTION (row [second shelf], the CONTRACTS half, letter L): How does the library price a job? What is the fee measured against, how do the best games make pay legible, when is pay in kind better than money, how do advances work, and when is the pay a person's favour? Turn it into the rule for pricing Bohemia contracts in BATTERIES, on the Battle Brothers offer screen Paolo named on 9/28 (records/BOHEMIA_PAOLO_QUESTS_ARE_THE_SETTLEMENT_CONTRACT_SCREEN_9_28_26.md). The numbers are TUNING's (chat 21, laws/BOHEMIA_LAW_THE_FIGHT_GETS_DEEP_TUNING_AND_MODS_9_27_26.md); this page gives the SHAPE.

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The library prices a job against four things, and the best games show all four before the yes. First, TIME: the fee is what the days cost, because the world spends its own days meanwhile and the player's calendar is the real price (`Q146.W8`, `Q109.W5`, `Q011.W2`). Second, DANGER that was STATED: the pay rises with a risk the client said out loud, so "too strong for me" is a reading, not a guess (`Q060.W1`, `Q041.W6`, `Q052.W4`, `Q051.W6`). Third, what the CLIENT HAS: in a crash, clients pay in what they hold (medicine, food, a door, a name), and the deal that pays in the exact currency you need beats a bigger number in the wrong one (`Q096.W3`, `Q113.N1`, `Q126.N16`, `Q109.P7`). Fourth, the RELATIONSHIP: some asks have no market at all, and pricing a favour is the wrong verb (`Q108.N1`, `Q001.N1`, `Q089.W3`). On top of those four sit the library's two hard laws about pay: the fee LOCKS before the truth is knowable, so no later choice is about the coin (`Q148.W1`, `Q148.P1`, `Q126.N5`), and the pay NEVER picks the moral: the crueller branch pays the same or less, with no loot in the room (`Q004.W4`, `Q097.W10`, `Q108.X2`, `Q070.X2`, `Q130.X9`, `Q125.X7`). For Bohemia that becomes one formula and one screen. THE FEE = the company's KEEP for the DAYS the job takes, plus a DANGER step for the stated threat (priced in the injury days rule 36 makes real), plus the PLACE's SCARCITY, paid in BATTERIES unless the offer says otherwise. The offer screen shows it twice (a number and a mouth), in at most four lines, before the accept button exists. Asking for more moves the fee within a ceiling the client's face shows; asking for an advance moves part of it to now and turns into a named debt if the job is dropped; a third push ends the conversation and writes nothing.

## THE FINDINGS

### 1. What the fee is measured against

**1a. Time is the base unit.** The library's cleanest statement of pay is that the camp "charges calendar", and "the world's other lives visibly spend theirs meanwhile" (`Q146.W8`). The same study makes routine legible: the institution has a schedule, and the schedule is the puzzle (`Q146.W2`). The dice-and-clocks game says the threat "eats your calendar, not your health" (`Q109.W5`), and Majora shows a deadline as teeth, not decoration (`Q011.W2`). Battle Brothers, Paolo's named reference for the campaign layer, runs the same way: a company burns its wages and food every day, so a contract is first of all worth the days it costs (reference/library/battle_brothers/07_ECONOMY.md: "the game is a treadmill that forces contracts"). For us, the company's daily KEEP (food, the nightly loan of power, medicine for the wounded) is the yardstick, and every fee is read against it: a job that takes three map days and pays less than three days of keep is a loss, and the screen should let the player see that without arithmetic.

**1b. Danger is a stated premium, not a hidden roll.** The fairness ceiling in the library is perfect information: "every death is your own fault" because every threat was shown (`Q060.W1`). The greed lever is voluntary risk for more reward (`Q041.W6`), and the branching map is "elite-for-loot vs safe path", a strategic choice made before the fight (`Q052.W4`). A world that does not scale lets jobs that are too strong for you exist and be walked past (`Q051.W6`), and the consent rule for danger is that the world warns about itself before it can apply (`Q146.P4`). So the danger premium is only fair if the danger is IN THE OFFER: how many, what they carry, where. Rule 36 (the fight gets deep) makes the premium concrete: a brother struck down has a 20 percent chance to die and otherwise lies injured 30 to 40 days, so a dangerous job's real cost to the company is expected injury days of keep paid for a man who cannot work. The library's stakes law says the same: real stakes need "measurable economic value, and risk associated with permanent loss" (`Q094.W9`), and persistent loss (lose a battle, lose people) is what gives a sim its tension (`Q061.P7`).

**1c. The place sets the price, not the player.** Kenshi's outpost shows that prosperity draws raiders "by arithmetic" and "the difficulty curve is the player's balance sheet" (`Q151.W3`); the tax collector's visit is the world's first acknowledgment that you exist, arriving as a bill (`Q151.W2`). EVE's factions are legal entities with bills and balance sheets (`Q094.W6`, `Q094.P5`), and its greatest war began with one unpaid bill (`Q094.W1`). The living world runs without you (`Q061.W2`, `Q055.W1`) and NPCs even finish side jobs themselves (`Q085.W3`). So a settlement's offers come from its situation: a raided, bust settlement has jobs that matter and little to pay with; a boom settlement has money and competition. Battle Brothers prices per settlement by its situations and its type (07_ECONOMY.md); ours should too, which is why the fee carries a SCARCITY term owned by the place.

**1d. The fee must beat the keep, and must not solve the economy.** Two flaws bracket the number. The generational saga's early monsters "deal more damage than XP/money they give", which "punishes players before the story hooks" (`Q067.X5`). The bounty-hunter game's economy "takes care of itself" within hours, so its threat deflates (`Q109.X1`, `Q109.X2`). The devil's deal must be "genuinely the better deal on the numbers" or refusing it is a difficulty setting, not a choice (`Q126.P6`). So a fair contract pays a little more than its days cost at its stated danger, and the margin is what the company grows on; if a normal contract pays many times its keep, the treadmill stops and so does the game.

### 2. How the best games make pay legible

**2a. State the terms clean, once, before the yes.** "He states them clean. No subclause. No trick. The contract says what he said" (`Q126.N5`), and that node "exists to remove your excuse" (`Q126.N6`). The institutional hire names "crop data, vault, fee" in one breath (`Q003.N1`). Our own asset landed the same rule: "terms you find out after you agreed are not terms" (records/BOHEMIA_THE_JOB_SAYS_WHAT_IT_PAYS_9_11_26.md). The honest devil is horrifying precisely because "he does exactly what he said" (`Q126.W1`).

**2b. Lock the fee before the truth.** "Haggling at maximum ignorance separates money from morality forever after" (`Q148.W1`); the port forbids any fee renegotiation after the offer (`Q148.P1`). The client's closure is then the same in every world (`Q148.W9`), and the job fits one sitting, notice to payment (`Q148.W10`). This is also what keeps the no free: the player turned down a price, not a person.

**2c. Short, and in two channels.** The offer screen is not a place to read. The library's text flaws are loud: the late lore dump (`Q068.X3`), the truth hidden in books (`Q074.X5`), the climax delivered as a wall of text (`Q111.X6`). And any clue must exist in sound and picture, because players lose whole classes of evidence when one channel is missing (`Q141.X3`). On a phone with the sound off, the fee must be a NUMBER on the screen; with the sound on, it must also be SAID by the client's mouth.

**2d. The face is the meter.** Battle Brothers' negotiation shows the client's mood shifting as you push (08_CONTRACTS_EVENTS.md). The library agrees that a hidden counter is the wrong tool: our 9/11 asset replaced the annoyance roll with a deterministic, spoken limit, "told, out loud, before the ask that would cost you" (records/BOHEMIA_THE_JOB_SAYS_WHAT_IT_PAYS_9_11_26.md), and the haggle that "feels like leverage" but is not ends with "the terms do not move" (`Q126.N7`, `Q126.N8`). A meter in a corner is the karma-bar mistake in money form (`Q121.X2`, `Q078.X3`); the fix the library prefers is people reacting (`Q083.P5`), so the ceiling is read on the portrait.

**2e. Pay the same whichever way it goes.** Zero reward difference makes a fork purely ethical (`Q004.W4`); the truth and the lie pay the same 70 XP (`Q097.W10`). When the kind choice also pays more, "you built a puzzle with a nice skin" (`Q108.X2`); when the cruel one pays far more, the reward economy undercuts the moral (`Q070.X2`); when payment scales with confirmations, the economy editorializes toward the lie (`Q130.X9`). And the loot must not sit in the room where the grief is (`Q125.X7`). Legibility includes this: the player can see at the offer that the fee does not depend on HOW, only on DONE.

### 3. Pay in kind versus batteries

**3a. The right currency beats the bigger number.** The Dalatrass pays in "the exact currency you need", a fleet during an extinction war (`Q096.W3`). The toll-taker accepts gold, half, or an artifact (`Q126.N16`), even one stolen from the house you have not yet robbed (`Q126.W8`, `Q126.P27`). The reward that becomes the home you rebuild is the one players remember (`Q075.W10`), and a game with no levels makes pay into READINESS: gear and supplies, not stats (`Q087.W7`). In a crash this is realism: when money dies people pay in the durable, useful thing they hold (laws/BOHEMIA_ADDENDUM_BATTERIES_ARE_THE_MONEY_AND_A_TILE_IS_A_HOUSE_9_4_26.md s1). A bust kitchen pays in meals, a clinic in medicine, a workshop in repairs.

**3b. Aid has a grammar.** The apprentice refuses medicine as charity and accepts the same vial as pay for a day's work (`Q113.N1`). A poor client's dignity is protected by paying in kind as an EMPLOYER, which is why the in-kind offer belongs on the contract screen and not in a gift.

**3c. In kind can be horror, said plainly.** The same library shows where in-kind pay turns: a baby is legal tender at the town hall (`Q113.W8`); the inspector can adopt his niece for 40 credits (`Q108.W9`); the reward for exposing the mole is that your medicine supply dies with him (`Q109.P7`); the EVE contract's fee was symbolic and the real price was a corpse (`Q094.W3`). These are analog horror material (an ordinary receipt with one wrong line) and they belong to rare contracts, never the default.

**3d. In kind must be priced where it is paid.** Battle Brothers' prices move per settlement and recover over days (07_ECONOMY.md). A bag of rice is worth more in a raided settlement than in a fat one, so in-kind pay is valued at the price of the PLACE WHERE IT IS HANDED OVER, and the screen says what it is in batteries there ("food for your people, four days; that's what it costs here").

### 4. Advances

**4a. An advance is trust paid first.** The honest devil "delivers first, on trust" (`Q126.W1`). A dealer who pays part up front because the road is long is the round-one shape (questbook/designs/act2/QD_A04_THE_WARM_GENERATOR.md), and Battle Brothers lets the player ask for one (08_CONTRACTS_EVENTS.md).

**4b. An advance never comes before the terms.** The library's most brilliant contract-horror beat is exactly what a Bohemia contract must never do: "you're in debt before the negotiation opens, and the debt is a body" (`Q134.W2`). A payment that lands before the player could say no prices the offer and breaks DECLINING IS FREE. An advance exists only as something the player ASKS for after the terms are stated.

**4c. A dropped advance is a debt with a name.** A bail-point has real consequences and no free do-over (`Q040.W10`), and completion is a choice the player keeps making because the exit is always there (`Q040.W2`, `Q151.W7`). QR-A's C13 already prices the drop: the advance becomes a debt in batteries owed to a named person. Debt is our native plot (the opening quest is a debt, `Q087.W6`; the threat is a recurring bar tab, `Q109.W3`, `Q109.N1`), so the advance needs no new machinery.

**4d. An advance is a real trade, not a bonus.** The 9/11 asset states it: UP FRONT is "the same one, now, instead of at the end", and "you walk away afterwards holding their thing, and that is what walking away costs." A client who pays early is exposed; a bust client can rarely afford it, and the face should say so.

### 5. When the pay is a favour

**5a. Some asks have no market.** Sergiu's favour: "no price can be discussed; the favor has no market" (`Q108.N1`). The Baron's intel "is not for sale, only for work" (`Q001.N1`). The coworker who never asked for anything is the strongest giver in the library (`Q108.W1`). Trust built over time is the most valuable thing a person can spend (`Q094.W2`), and reputation is a memory, not a bar: shopkeepers discount you and strangers cite your deeds (`Q086.W8`).

**5b. Canon already has the currency.** Bohemia's three currencies are RESOURCES, ELECTRICITY (the batteries) and CLOUT, and clout "is what you cannot buy" (the 9/4 batteries law). A favour pays in clout. So the offer screen says it straight: "No pay. A favour." That is one of the 9/11 asset's three honest answers ("Pays nothing. They said so up front").

**5c. A favour must still pay SOMETHING you can see.** The Death Stranding study shows people give anyway when "the only reward is a like" (`Q089.W3`), but the same library warns that "there are no material rewards for this quest" is the most common complaint about Pathologic's best work, and players quit before the meaning pays (`Q113.X4`), and a like is still a leaderboard (`Q089.X5`). Rule: a favour contract names, on the screen, what the favour opens later (a door, a discount, a name that vouches), and that thing shows up in the world. Kindness shows once (`Q109.X5`).

**5d. A favour has no haggle.** Asking a neighbour for up-front pay on a favour is the wrong verb (`Q108.N1`); the negotiation buttons are absent on a favour screen, not greyed.

### 6. Pay and the twist

A fee locked at the offer lets the twist bank (QR-C) move WHO pays without moving WHAT was agreed. The reward can be gone (T11) and the client pays in kind; a second, real offer can appear from the other side (`Q150.W2`: "the bribe is real and only the accusation is false"); mercy can cost the player's own coin (`Q149.W6`), which is fair because the fee the client owes did not change. And the lie is "frictionless at point of sale" while its invoice arrives later with names on it (`Q130.W4`), which is why the fee must never be the thing that tempts the lie. Temptation "with a real number on it" is fine (`Q148.W8`) as long as the number was on the screen before the yes.

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable)

P1. **THE FEE IS KEEP x DAYS + DANGER + SCARCITY.** Every contract's fee is computed, not typed: FEE = K x D + R(band) + S(place), where K is the company's daily keep at its current size, D the map days the job takes there and back (the route the client names), R a step per stated danger band (one to three, the Battle Brothers skull shape), and S the settlement's scarcity term from its situation. TUNING owns K, the R steps and S; until he tunes, every value is 1 and tagged placeholder (EVERYTHING COSTS ONE, 8/15). Test: no contract file carries a literal fee; every fee resolves through the table. (`Q146.W8`, `Q060.W1`, `Q151.W3`, `Q067.X5`)

P2. **DANGER IS PRICED IN INJURY DAYS.** R(band) is at least the expected injury days of keep that band causes under rule 36 (a struck-down brother: 20 percent dead, else 30 to 40 days out). TUNING measures the struck-down rate per band with the one driver and sets R from it. Test: a validator reads R and the measured rate and refuses an R below the expected keep lost. (`Q094.W9`, `Q061.P7`, `Q041.W6`)

P3. **A FAIR CONTRACT BEATS ITS KEEP, A LITTLE.** At its stated danger, a normal contract's expected net (fee minus keep minus expected injury keep) is positive and small; a contract that pays many times its keep is a tuning bug, not a treat. Test: TUNING's table flags any contract whose fee exceeds its K x D + R by more than a set multiple. (`Q126.P6`, `Q109.X1`, `Q067.X5`)

P4. **FOUR LINES, ONE NUMBER, TWO CHANNELS.** The offer screen shows the client's portrait and at most four lines: WHAT, WHERE (with days), HOW MANY (the danger), and PAYS. The pay line is a NUMBER with the battery mark AND is said in the client's mouth. Test: the screen's text node count is at most four; the pay appears in both the text and the voice line. (`Q126.N5`, `Q141.X3`, `Q068.X3`, `Q074.X5`)

P5. **NO ACCEPT BUTTON UNTIL THE PAY IS SHOWN.** Test: the accept control is unreachable before the pay line has rendered and been spoken. (`Q126.N6`, the 9/11 disclosure rule)

P6. **THE FEE LOCKS AT ACCEPT.** No node after accept can change the amount, the currency or the timing, except that the twist bank may remove the payer (T11, T12) or add a second offer (T14). Test: no post-accept node writes the fee. (`Q148.W1`, `Q148.P1`, `Q148.W9`)

P7. **THE FEE DOES NOT DEPEND ON HOW.** Every ending that counts as DONE pays the same fee; DONE WRONG and FAILED pay what the screen said they pay (part or nothing), and the crueller branch never pays more. No batteries or goods lie in the room where the moral fork happens. Test: the ending table's DONE rows share one fee; a body-drop validator finds no loot on a fork's grid. (`Q004.W4`, `Q097.W10`, `Q108.X2`, `Q070.X2`, `Q130.X9`, `Q125.X7`) (folded 10/1 from QR-X: P7 is the contract half of THE HOUSE RULE in P16 and QR-G line 30; it does not bind road events or main beats.)

P8. **ASK FOR MORE HAS A CEILING YOU CAN SEE.** ASK FOR MORE moves the fee one step toward the client's CEILING (set by the settlement's wealth: low in bust, high in boom). The portrait's expression changes each step, and at the ceiling the client says so out loud ("That's all there is."). Test: the ceiling is a table value; the at-ceiling line exists for every client; no hidden random counter. (`Q126.N8`, 08_CONTRACTS_EVENTS.md, the 9/11 deterministic limit)

P9. **ASK FOR AN ADVANCE MOVES PART OF THE FEE TO NOW.** The advance is a share of the same fee, never extra. It is paid only after accept. If the contract is DROPPED, the advance becomes a debt in batteries owed to the named client (QR-A C13); if it is FAILED or VOIDED, it is not a debt. A bust client may refuse an advance, in words. Test: advance plus remainder equals the locked fee; the drop branch writes a debt row with the client's id. (`Q126.W1`, `Q040.W10`, `Q087.W6`)

P10. **NOTHING IS PAID BEFORE THE TERMS.** No battery, good or favour moves toward the player before the terms are on the screen and the player has accepted. Test: no pre-accept node writes to the purse. (`Q134.W2` is banned)

P11. **THE THIRD PUSH ENDS IT, AND WRITES NOTHING.** Two negotiation moves land; the client warns before the third; the third withdraws the offer for this visit. Nothing is written: no deed, no standing change (round one, rule 35c; QR-E OPEN 1). Test: the withdrawal branch has zero ledger writes. (`Q126.N8`, `Q126.W3`, `Q126.N13`)

P12. **IN KIND IS A SHAPE CHOSEN ON THE SCREEN.** A client may offer, or the player may ask for, the fee in a GOOD the client holds (food days, medicine, repairs, a part). The screen states its battery value AT THIS SETTLEMENT'S PRICE. It is still one fee: changing the currency is a negotiation move, not a raise. Test: every in-kind pay line carries its local battery value. (`Q096.W3`, `Q113.N1`, `Q126.N16`, `Q087.W7`)

P13. **A FAVOUR SAYS "NO PAY" AND NAMES WHAT IT OPENS.** A favour contract's pay line reads "No pay. A favour." plus one line of what it opens (a door, a price, a name). It has no negotiation controls. What it opens appears in the world within two map stops of finishing. Test: favour contracts have no haggle node and one `opens:` field that some later place reads. (`Q108.N1`, `Q001.N1`, `Q113.X4`, `Q109.X5`)

P14. **THE SCREEN PRICE AND THE STREET PRICE AGREE.** A contract offered in a bust settlement pays from that settlement's purse and its scarcity; the same job in a boom settlement pays more batteries and less in kind. Test: generate the same contract kind in a boom and a bust settlement; the fee moves by S and the in-kind share moves the other way. (`Q151.W3`, `Q094.P5`, `Q061.W2`)

P15. **DECLINE STAYS FREE.** Decline writes nothing, anywhere (QR-A C6). The offer stays in the hall until the world changes it. Test: state after decline equals state before the offer, except the clock. (`Q126.W3`, `Q126.N12`, `Q134.W3`)

P16. **A PRICE ON A PERSON OR A SACRED THING TEMPTS, AND IT LIVES ONLY ON EVENTS AND MAIN BEATS.** (folded 10/1 from QR-U, reconciled with QR-G line 30 by QR-X's house rule.) THE HOUSE RULE, written the same way on both pages so no sweep has to choose: CONTRACTS PAY ONE FEE ON EVERY BRANCH THAT COUNTS AS DONE (P7); EVENTS AND MAIN BEATS MAY CARRY ONE STATED TEMPTATION, WITH THE NUMBER ON THE BUTTON, AND THE OTHER SIDE FULLY BUILT. The library says why the number must be there: the miracle gets priced, cash held against a homecoming (`Q204.W5`); a price that is cartoonishly evil tempts nobody, so the money must matter to a desperate company (`Q204.X3`); "the temptation has a number on it, the only kind that tempts" (`Q166.W4`). And why it stays off the contract: when a side's reward buys the decision, the choice is bought (`Q164.W4`), and a contract's reward economy that tilts the fork undercuts it (`Q070.X2`, `Q108.X2`). The other side is fully built when it carries its own real weight (a person, standing, a later door), never only "the right thing" (`Q226.X2`, `Q157.X1`, `Q161.X3`; QR-G line 32). Tests: (a) no contract's DONE rows differ in fee (P7); (b) an event or main beat has at most ONE button whose number exceeds the others, and that number is printed on the button and said by a mouth; (c) the other button names what it gives or keeps. How often the road may carry such a price is TUNING's research (QR-U OPEN 1). (`Q204.W5`, `Q204.X3`, `Q166.W4`, `Q164.W4`, `Q226.X2`)

## WHAT TO AVOID (the flaws)

- THE FEE THAT LOSES MONEY: `Q067.X5`. An early contract that pays less than it costs punishes the player before the story hooks.
- THE ECONOMY THAT SOLVES ITSELF: `Q109.X1`, `Q109.X2`. Pay that outruns keep ends the treadmill and the tension.
- THE MORAL WITH A PRICE TAG: `Q108.X2`, `Q070.X2`, `Q130.X9`. The pay must not pick the branch.
- THE LOOT IN THE ROOM: `Q125.X7`. No batteries by a body at a fork.
- THE METER THAT DOES THE MATH: `Q121.X2`, `Q078.X3`. No reputation or mood bar on the offer screen; the face carries it.
- THE WALL OF TERMS: `Q068.X3`, `Q074.X5`, `Q111.X6`. Four lines at most.
- THE ONE-CHANNEL PRICE: `Q141.X3`. Number and voice, both.
- THE DEBT BEFORE THE TERMS: `Q134.W2`. Illegal in a contract.
- THE FAVOUR THAT PAYS NOTHING YOU CAN SEE: `Q113.X4`, `Q089.X5`.
- THE SPREADSHEET: `Q094.X1`. The player should never need a calculator to know whether a job pays; the screen shows DAYS next to PAY so the comparison is one glance.
- THE PUNISHED HAGGLE: `Q084.X5`, `Q043.X1`. Pushing too far ends the offer; it does not sulk, and it does not dock standing (this is where we part from Battle Brothers, blind spot 8).

## THE [bb pay] LINE

Battle Brothers is the reference for the offer screen and the treadmill: pay by difficulty in skulls, a mood face, ask for more, ask for an advance, and a company that burns crowns every day (reference/library/battle_brothers/07_ECONOMY.md, 08_CONTRACTS_EVENTS.md). We keep the screen, the face, the two asks and the treadmill. We change three things, each from the library: the fee is computed from keep, days, danger and place so it is readable without a wiki (`Q060.W1`, `Q146.W8`); pushing too hard withdraws the offer and writes NOTHING (Battle Brothers docks relations; `Q126.W3` is the evidence for Paolo's free no); and a fee can be in kind or a favour, said plainly on the same screen, because in a crash a battery is not the only thing worth being paid in (`Q096.W3`, `Q108.N1`).

## OPEN (what the library could not answer)

1. **THE NUMBERS.** The library gives shapes, never a keep, a danger step or a ceiling. TUNING owns every value in P1, P2, P3 and P8. Until he tunes, all are 1 (EVERYTHING COSTS ONE).
2. **HOW TO SHOW "IS THIS WORTH IT" WITHOUT A CALCULATOR.** P4 puts DAYS next to PAY. Whether the screen should also show the keep those days cost (a small second number) is UI's call. The library has no phone offer screen to copy; Battle Brothers shows the crowns and the skulls and leaves the math to the player.
3. **"ASK FOR MORE" AGAINST EVERYTHING COSTS ONE.** The 9/11 asset read "more" as a change of SHAPE because every pay was 1. Paolo's 9/28 words ("negotiation, ask for more") and the 9/27 TUNING law make "more" a real step.
