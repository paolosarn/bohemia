# QR-W: THE SHELF SWEPT FOR RULES 38 TO 40. WHAT CHANGED, WHY THE LIBRARY AGREES, AND WHAT WAITS ON PAOLO

QUESTION: row [canon sweep]. Paolo's rulings of 9/28 and 9/29 (VAMILY front page rules 38, 39 and 40) broke four
assumptions the quests shelf was written on: that the player walks a tiny figure through the city one cell at a time
(the "close grid"), that all three acts can be flipped from the first second, that each act's company is its own,
and that fights can run as long as they like. Sweep every page QR-A to QR-S and every design QD-A to QD-S, fix each in
place without losing a citation or a meaning, keep a log, and say what the library says about each change and what
could not be fixed without Paolo.

sweep touched only questbook/research/QR_[A-S]_*.md and questbook/designs/*/QD_[A-S]*.md; the QD-T and QR-T/U/V files
were being written by other hands and were not touched.

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

111 of the 169 files in scope changed. 105 carried the dead walk: "the close grid", one cell per press, a house that is
many cells, a squeeze from street to map. Every fight in them is now on THE FIGHT BOARD, house-sized tiles cut from
the city (a tile is the SIZE of a house, its content is whatever that piece of the city is: street, yard, roof, car),
and every non-fight job at a place is now people and things the player taps on that place's screen, reached by the
map. 21 files assumed the three acts could be flipped from the start, named "act 1 never played" as a possible state,
or kept a three-names screen; they now say one person starts, the next generation unlocks later (default: the first
home base), the new person is generated from the one before, and every across-acts landing is seen once its act has
unlocked. 8 files moved a company across acts; they now say the next act starts with the HEIRS of the last act's
company, gear kept in the family. 15 files named or implied a fight's length; they now carry the ceiling: a routine
fight 2 to 4 minutes, a tough one 8 to 15, never past 15. The library backs all four rulings harder than it backed
what they replaced: tile-by-tile movement is its named dated friction (`Q038.X4`, `Q060.X4`), a first generation the
player must slog through before the generational magic opens is its named weak opening (`Q067.X4`), the heir who is
a person and not a respawn is its best generational finding (`Q144.W4`, `Q069.W3`), and a fight that stands in front
of the scene is its most repeated combat flaw (`Q095.X3`, `Q098.X2`, `Q017.X1`). One thing waits on Paolo: whether any
place besides the Strip is walked on foot. Every design that leaned on "a special place" now defaults to the
settlement screen, and says so.

## THE RULES THAT CHANGED

### Rule 38: the walk is dead; the city is crossed on the map; the fight is on house tiles

Paolo 9/28, twice: "your character moving tile to tile throughout the city, it's not gonna be like that anymore",
and, correcting the manager the same hour, "for the combat a tile is as big as a house". Then (38h): "one tile is the
size of a house doesn't mean every tile is a house; it still has to look like a city". The walk's assets (sidewalk,
kerb, yard, wall, door, car) dress the fight board's tiles. The shelf wrote "the close grid" 108 times in 80 files,
most often as the place a contract's work happens.

WHAT THE LIBRARY SAYS. The case against the walk was in the library before Paolo made it:
- Tile-by-tile movement is the named dated friction of the foundational CRPG, "the design endures, the UX is rough"
  (`Q038.X4`), and a precise grid game on a phone "needs TOUCH controls designed for mobile from the start" (`Q060.X4`).
  A game whose normal surface is a map and a settlement screen has almost nothing a thumb can miss.
- "The quests today are an absolute mess, requiring you to go all over town" (`Q113.X1`): walking across town is a tax
  unless it is the theme. For us it is not the theme; the map crosses the city, and every trip has a road event or a
  place on it.
- Sparse travel reads as "prolonged sections of nothing happening" (`Q087.X3`), and an empty start reads as "nothing
  to engage with" (`Q055.X1`). A settlement screen is dense by construction: one view, every building a door.
- The board is "a dignified front door", "a job system that never begs" (`Q148.W2`), and the core port is a settlement
  board posting contracts from the world's real events (`Q148.P1`). QR-P found this already; rule 38 finishes it by
  taking the walk away from the job as well as from the offer.
- The fight board being small is a virtue, not a loss: "tiny (8x8) + short so you hold the WHOLE puzzle in your head"
  (`Q060.W4`). House-sized tiles make the board small in count while it stays a real piece of the city. Enemies on the
  field, fought in place, "a small-but-huge design courtesy" (`Q050.W9`), survives: the group is drawn in the
  settlement picture, and tapping into it cuts the fight board from that block.
- The siege happens "where the hours live", in the player's own labour (`Q151.W6`): the fight board of a besieged base
  is the same block the player has visited and built on, so house-sized tiles cost that finding nothing.
- The place still acts first: "the building takes your floor in the first ten seconds" (`Q099.W4`) is now a building on
  the settlement screen that answers when tapped, and a lamp on the map that browns out.

HOW IT WAS APPLIED.
- "on the close grid" became "on the fight board" wherever a fight happens there. Where the old text named cells in a
  fight ("the cell beside Dez", "fights one cell further back", "cover cells", "a deck three cells wide"), the unit is
  now a TILE and the size is a house (QD-M03, QD-M04, QD-M08, QD-R01, QD-R03, QD-R05, QD-S01, QD-M07).
- Where the job was not a fight ("the player walks four streets on the fight board, cell by cell", "the company reads
  markers", "one map day walking the camp with him, one cell at a time"), the work is now people and things tapped on
  the place's screen (rule 40c: interactable means people and things you act on in a place). A job's PLACE line now
  reads "(the fight board if it comes to a fight)".
- The six walked first-minute designs (QD-F01 to QD-F06) and QR-F were rewritten into the map-then-settlement-screen
  shape QR-P section 4 sketched, with every citation kept. QR-E's "place that asks on the close street" is now a
  building drawn wrong on the settlement screen.
- "Special place" as a license to walk (QD-F01, F04, F05, F06, D02, G03 and the QR-P note) was re-cut to the settlement
  screen; see WHAT WAITS ON PAOLO.

### Rule 39c: no flip at the start; the next generation unlocks later; the new person is generated from the old

Paolo 9/28: "you start the game you can't flip between the three people... customize just one person, and when you
hop into the second generation you'll be given an option to customize the person and it will start off generated
based on how you made the first." Default then: the second generation unlocks at the first home base (SUPERSEDED 10/10 by rule 85: the flip unlocks when the act's first endgame crisis starts, around day 80 to 100; QR-AN). The name-all-three
screen and the three faces from frame one are dead as built. The flip itself lives on: it is "a big transition
unlocked mid-act" (third votes).

WHAT THE LIBRARY SAYS. The unlock is better than the open start, on five counts:
- The first generation must not be a waiting room. "The first generation with Rhys is a boring stroll through
  monotony; the generational magic only opens up AFTER you have a son" (`Q067.X4`). The fix is not to open the magic
  in frame one; it is to make the first generation worth playing and to make the unlock an event. Rule 39c does both:
  the unlock is tied to winning something real (a home base).
- Three people and three eras in the first hour is the firehose. "A barrage of new mechanics + systems up front"
  nearly lost reviewers (`Q065.X2`); seven houses and two orders in a prologue is "a firehose" (`Q081.X4`); the
  dynasty game's onboarding "drowns newcomers" (`Q046.X2`). One person is one name, which is what QR-F rule 12 asks.
- Choices made before the player knows the world read back as accusations. Conquest's choices "are made in twenty
  minutes, before the player knows the world" (`Q145.X2`); Tali's best outcome rests on flags set "in mission two of
  the game" (`Q125.X6`). A player who can flip to act 3 in minute one would meet every landing before he could have
  made the plant knowingly. With the unlock, every landing he sees was planted by a person he has already lived as.
- The second life proves the first one happened: the heir's early game is "repriced by inherited geography and the
  player's own trained dread" (`Q144.W9`). That only works if the first life was LIVED first. And the heir is "a
  person, not a respawn", "an authored relationship, priced during the predecessor's life" (`Q144.W4`): generating the
  Gen 2 person from the Gen 1 person, then letting the player adjust, is exactly that pricing.
- Small choices must come first to teach that the world watches (`Q050.W4`), and origin content that is richest at
  the start fades if nothing calls back to it (`Q042.X1`). One person first, then an unlock that calls back to him, is
  the order the library recommends.
- The order-dependency risk shrinks. Our old "the future must exist before the past is done" rule answered hidden
  ordering (`Q023.X2`) and side content that silently breaks main content (`Q104.X6`). With act 1 always begun first,
  the default reads now cover a part of an act not reached, never a whole act unplayed.
- What the flip still buys, once unlocked, is untouched: actions in one era reshape another (`Q050.W5`), the past is
  a place you visit (`Q012.W3`), and the family's progress is a home you can see (`Q049.W8`).

HOW IT WAS APPLIED.
- "All three acts are open at once" became "open together once unlocked" in QR-B, QR-D, QR-H, QR-M and QR-Q, each with
  the rule's source. QR-D's "the player may flip to act 3 in the first minute" became: the play gap has a floor, the
  player has lived act 1 long enough to win a home base before any landing can be seen.
- "Act 1 never played" and "the player flipped here first" defaults (QD-M06, QD-R05, QD-K10, QD-Q09, QD-Q13, QD-I05,
  QD-I06, QR-G line 5, QR-M rule 14) now read "that contract never taken" or "that stretch not reached before the next
  act opened". Every default read is kept; only its trigger is honest now.
- QD-F05 and QD-F06 (the first minute of act 2 and act 3) now open with the first hop: the unlock, the customization
  of the new person generated from the one before, then the silent minute. QR-F rule 15 says the same, and QR-F's
  open question 4 (the three-names screen) is closed by the ruling.
- Across-acts designs that start in the future (QD-D04 "for the player who flips forward first", QD-G03, QD-O03) keep
  working: the landing is seen once that act unlocks, and the act 1 offer can still be untaken when it does.

### Rule 39d: the company inherits

Paolo 9/28, default in VOTE: each act starts with the HEIRS of the last act's company, prepared from the ledger; gear
stays in the family; about 12 men an act, 36 lives a game; recruiting stays Battle Brothers-hard inside an act;
keeping killers alive is the pipeline.

WHAT THE LIBRARY SAYS.
- The generations fight side by side: "you don't just become the next generation, you FIGHT ALONGSIDE it" (`Q069.W3`),
  and the payoff is a family "made whole across generations, watched by the dead" (`Q069.W8`). A company of heirs is
  that family at the size of a roster.
- Continuity through the fold is the persistent-companion finding (`Q067.W6`); one window deciding "who stands beside
  you at the end of the world" (`Q116.W10`) is the same finding with a price.
- Attachment is manufactured by labour: "unwritten recruits become devastating losses purely through invested hours"
  (`Q151.W9`), "you care because you LEVELED them from nobody to hero" (`Q055.W9`). Inheritance is how those hours
  cross an act instead of being thrown away at the act line.
- The line is made real by what it does NOT carry: "unbanked secrets burn, stranded arcs stay stranded" (`Q144.W3`),
  and the dead stay dead across a sequel (`Q137.W8`). A member who dies without an heir leaves a gap in the next act's
  roster; that is the loss, and it is the pipeline Paolo named.
- Inherited people speak in the past tense, "the cheapest possible reactivity carrying the heaviest freight"
  (`Q144.W7`); prior choices shape the heir's starting world (`Q025.W6`); kin carry a choice for decades (`Q026.W6`);
  the family itself levels up (`Q046.W7`).
- The two warnings stay binding: heirs must not inherit hatred the player never saw (`Q046.X5`), and carried power can
  trivialize the next act (`Q050.X4`). Gear kept in the family is TUNING's to price against the second one.

HOW IT WAS APPLIED.
- QD-M06 (a member's granddaughter as a client) now says why she is in a hall and not in the formation: she is from the
  branch of his family that did not ride on; the branch that did is already in the company. QD-Q10's apprentice is the
  one act 2 member whose line does not carry, because she left to found her own company. QD-Q03's one-line answer now
  comes from the member whose grandparent rode in act 1. QD-Q19's roster carries family names from act 1.
- QR-M rule 14 was rewritten: the company crosses acts as people AND as heirs; a surviving member hands his place to a
  child or grandchild; a member dead without an heir appears as a name, a tool, a grandchild outside the roster, or a
  place.
- QD-F05 and QD-F06 name the heirs as the company that arrives with the new person.

### Rule 40a: the fight has a ceiling, not a stopwatch

Paolo 9/29, correcting the manager's 90 seconds: "tough battles can be 30-40 mins... shorter than 30-40 even for tough
battles." Defaults in VOTE: routine 2 to 4 minutes, tough 8 to 15, never past 15. A fight is tough for three reasons
only: enemy level, enemy gear, your mistake. The hours go to the world, not the board.

WHAT THE LIBRARY SAYS.
- The fight in front of the scene is a tax: "the fight is the weakest part of its own climax" (`Q095.X3`), "the combat
  is the worst part of its own best quest" (`Q098.X2`), and a game that preaches four pillars then forces a boss fight
  hits a wall (`Q017.X1`, with its port `Q017.P9`). A ceiling keeps the fight from eating the quest around it.
- Long late fights betray the rest of the game: "the final act devolves into corridor fighting" (`Q039.X4`); sieges
  "feel repetitive over hundreds of hours" (`Q061.X5`); a long siege's loop "can grind" (`Q030.X3`); siege AI
  collapses into patterns and veteran bases farm their attackers (`Q151.X1`). Short fights are how a hundred sieges
  stay fresh.
- A lost long fight is the retry that replays known beats in real time (`Q128.X10`) and the failure that costs a full
  loop (`Q111.X2`). A 15-minute ceiling caps what a loss can cost a phone session.
- Grind can outweigh grief (`Q144.X2`): if the board is where the hours go, a death reads as lost progress, not a lost
  person. Rule 40's split (the fight under a third of playtime) protects the grief.
- Toughness from level, gear or a mistake is the fairness model: "every death is your own fault" because every threat
  was shown (`Q060.W1`), threats telegraphed so fights are plannable (`Q052.P6`, `Q060.P2`), and the dial earns its
  place (`Q095.P7`). The off-ramp before the fight stays real (`Q140.W8`), and the verdict arrives regardless of skill
  (`Q140.P2`).

HOW IT WAS APPLIED. Every siege or storm (QD-R01, QD-R02, QD-R06, QD-Q13, QR-R section 5) is marked tough, 8 to 15
minutes, with a HOLD of N bars set inside that ceiling (QR-R notes that at 120 BPM 15 minutes is 450 bars, so a hold is
far fewer; QD-R01's "sixteen bars", about 32 seconds, was a stopwatch and is gone). Multi-night jobs (QD-J03, QD-J11)
are a fight per night, each under the ceiling. The small fights of the first minutes and short jobs (QD-A01, QD-E02,
QD-E04, QD-F02, QD-F03, QD-F04, QR-F's budget) are marked routine, 2 to 4. QR-G's fight rule now opens with the ceiling.
No file on the shelf ever said 90 seconds; none said "chess".

## THE RULE FOR THE BUILDERS (numbered, testable; what the sweep holds the shelf to)

W1. NO WALK. No research page or design places the player's figure on a cell of a city street, counts steps in
cells, or uses "the close grid" except as history. Test: grep the shelf for "close grid", "cell per", "many cells",
"squeeze" outside history notes. (`Q038.X4`, `Q060.X4`, `Q113.X1`)
W2. A FIGHT IS ON THE FIGHT BOARD. Every fight names the block it is cut from; its tiles are house-sized and dressed
as that piece of the city. Test: every "fight" in a design has a place. (`Q050.W9`, `Q060.W4`, `Q151.W6`)
W3. A JOB THAT IS NOT A FIGHT IS TAPS ON A PLACE. Test: no non-fight job line says "walk". (`Q148.W2`, `Q087.X3`)
W4. ONE PERSON AT THE START. No design names or shows the Gen 2 or Gen 3 person before that act unlocks. Test: every
act 2 and act 3 first-minute design opens with the unlock and the first hop. (`Q067.X4`, `Q081.X4`, `Q065.X2`)
W5. THE NEW PERSON IS GENERATED FROM THE OLD. Test: every first-hop design says so. (`Q144.W4`, `Q144.W9`)
W6. DEFAULT READS COVER A STRETCH, NOT AN ACT. Test: no default says "act 1 never played". (`Q023.X2`, `Q104.X6`,
`Q145.X2`)
W7. THE COMPANY IS HEIRS. Test: every design that moves a company member across acts says whether the line carried
into the roster. (`Q069.W3`, `Q151.W9`, `Q144.W3`, `Q046.X5`)
W8. EVERY FIGHT CARRIES ITS CEILING. Routine 2 to 4 minutes, tough 8 to 15, never past 15; a HOLD counts bars inside
the ceiling. Test: every siege, storm or multi-night design names "tough" and the ceiling. (`Q095.X3`, `Q039.X4`,
`Q128.X10`, `Q060.W1`)

## WHAT COULD NOT BE FIXED WITHOUT PAOLO

1. WHICH PLACES ARE WALKED ON FOOT. Rule 38 keeps on-foot play for the Strip ("his list"), using the existing 112 walk
   art (default, in VOTE). Round two and QR-P had made the family house, a substation and a flood-channel camp
   "special places" to keep walking legal there (QD-F01, F04, F05, F06, D02, G03). That list is his. The sweep
   defaulted every one of them to the settlement screen and wrote so in each file; if he adds the family house to his
   list, QD-F01 and QD-F05 can walk again on the 112 art, never on a cell grid.

Everything else was a default this lane could set and did, and each is for a building lane, not for him:
- The trigger for act 3's unlock. Rule 39c names the default for the second generation (the first home base; superseded by rule 85, the first endgame crisis, QR-AN). The
  sweep assumed the same shape for the third (a home base won in the act before) in QD-F06. DYNASTY owns it.
- The family block at the start. Several round-one pages call the family's block "the family home base" from the
  first second. If owning a home base is what unlocks Gen 2, the start cannot be an owned home base; QD-F01 now says
  the family lives there and the company does not own it yet. QR-O and QR-P still use the older phrase; DYNASTY and
  RUN should pick the words once and the next sweep should follow.
- Who inherits. Rule 39d's default is heirs prepared from the ledger. Whether every surviving member hands on a place
  or only some (a member with no child, one who left) is DYNASTY's and PEOPLE's; QD-M06 and QD-Q10 are written so
  either answer works.
- A job's place screen. Non-fight work is now "taps on the place's screen". Whether that is the settlement screen of
  the nearest settlement or a small screen of its own is RUN's mechanism.
- Gear kept in the family against `Q050.X4` (carried power trivializes): TUNING prices it.

## OPEN (what the library could not answer)

1. The library has no game whose later generations unlock by a won place rather than by age or death; its closest
   cases (`Q067.X4`, `Q069.W3`, `Q144.W9`) unlock by a birth or a death. Whether a home base feels like a birth to a
   player is a playtest question.
2. No study measures a fight ceiling in minutes on a phone. The library shows the costs of long fights (`Q128.X10`,
   `Q061.X5`) but not the number; EYES [where the minutes go] measures the split.
3. The library has no fight on tiles the size of a house. `Q060.W4` argues small boards hold the puzzle; whether a
   house tile holds enough cover and height to make formation the depth rule 36 asks for is COMBAT's to find.
