## PAGE ONE. SCARY FIRST: IT IS HELL.

HIS WORDS, kept as he said them (voice to text; the garbles left out, nothing added, nothing softened):

> 'It is hell. People actually feel like they're in hell. They're in hell first, before they're in a barely surviving, one percent civilization experience. Whatever you do in this game, when you evoke a feeling out of the gamer, it is people reacting to how scary and real hell is. The emotions: it will be scary and then it can be scary happy, scary funny, scary serious. There still could be humor, but it has to be scary and then funny. I want everyone's voice to be: they can be scary confident, scary trying to be a leader, scary political. There's two words, but the first word is always scary, and whatever emotion you're trying to get out of people, the second one follows that.'

> 'I really do believe people will talk to each other more desperately. It'll be this kind of thing: damn bro, you're really not gonna help starving people out because of the kindness of your heart, another piece of shit, he's gonna do it for batteries, so I got some batteries for you.'

> 'The vibe I'm creating, from social media to people actually talking to you.'

### THE FIVE RULES OF PAGE ONE (each one is his sentence)

1. IT IS HELL FIRST. 'They're in hell first, before they're in a barely surviving, one percent civilization experience.' Whatever the game makes the player feel, 'it is people reacting to how scary and real hell is'.
2. EVERY VOICE IS TWO WORDS, AND THE FIRST IS ALWAYS SCARY. 'There's two words, but the first word is always scary, and whatever emotion you're trying to get out of people, the second one follows that.'
   The second words he has named so far: happy, funny, serious, confident, trying to be a leader, political.
3. HUMOR IS SCARY, AND THEN FUNNY. 'There still could be humor, but it has to be scary and then funny.' Never funny alone.
4. THEY TALK DESPERATELY, AND NOBODY PRETENDS HELP IS FREE. He believes 'people will talk to each other more desperately'. The sound of it, in his mouth:
   'damn bro, you're really not gonna help starving people out because of the kindness of your heart, another piece of shit, he's gonna do it for batteries, so I got some batteries for you.'
   (OUR READING, his to correct: the one who needs help knows the helper wants batteries, says so, and offers them.
   Kindness is not a reason anybody gives.)
5. IT HOLDS FOR EVERY MOUTH AND EVERY POST: 'from social media to people actually talking to you'.

THE TEST OF A LINE (rule 455(e)): A LINE THAT IS NOT SCARY FIRST IS THE DEFECT, WHATEVER ELSE IT DOES WELL.

THE HORROR UNDER IT (rule 456, the grinder; it is in his own enemy line below): 'We have to make mystery sausage out of this, because if not, we don't know what our next meal is.' 'Only the sick people will joke about it.'

### HIS OWN LINES, EACH BESIDE WHAT IT TEACHES (exactly as he gave them in the VOTE tab, rule 458(a))

1. THE KID WHO BEGS (a road stop)
   WAS (the game today): "Oye, jefe. Two days no food. I don't want batteries, just something to eat. My brother says ask the ones with guns, they got food."
   HIS: "Hey bro, I haven't eaten in two fucking days. My brother hasn't eaten in three fucking days, man. Can you guys please help me and my brother out? I know how to shine and clean some of your equipment I see."
   WHAT IT TEACHES (our reading, his to correct):
   - He swears about the hunger, not at you: 'two fucking days'. A scared kid swears.
   - He counts the days, and the count goes up: two, then three. A number is for what hurts.
   - He asks for help and says please, and then he pays his way: 'I know how to shine and clean some of your equipment I see'. Nobody asks for free.
   - He calls you 'bro', 'man', 'you guys'. No Spanish: ours opened 'Oye, jefe'.
   - He pours it out: 4 sentences, about 10 words each (ours clipped him to 6). A scared kid talks long.
   - He ends on the offer, while he looks at your gear ('I see'). No clever last line.

2. THE ENEMY'S WHY (their leader, as a fight opens)
   WAS (the game today): "The House holds this road, and you did not pay to use it."
   HIS: "We were told to be on the lookout for a rag tag bunch of fuck faces who didn't want to pay their battery tax. After we clean your body well turn you to mystery meat."
   WHAT IT TEACHES (our reading, his to correct):
   - It starts with an order somebody else gave: 'We were told'. Who gave it stays dark.
   - He insults you ('rag tag bunch of fuck faces') and names what you owe ('battery tax').
   - He ends on your body: 'turn you to mystery meat'. The threat is what happens to you, said flat.
   - Nobody says a crew's name. Ours said 'The House'.
   - 2 long sentences, about 18 words each. An armed man takes his time.

3. THE FEED'S JOKE POST (a regular person in the valley)
   WAS (the game today): "they don't know I have two jugs of water under the bed."
   HIS: "IDGAF if ur momma dying i got two jugs of water hiding under this bed"
   WHAT IT TEACHES (our reading, his to correct):
   - It is typed like a phone: 'IDGAF', 'ur', a small 'i', no apostrophe, no full stop.
   - It is cold: somebody's mother is dying and the poster cares about his water. That is the joke, and it is scary first, then funny.
   - The thing is the same as ours (two jugs under the bed). What changed is who the poster is.

4. THE OLD WOMAN'S JOB (a job giver at the board)  *** HE STARRED THIS ONE ***
   WAS (the game today): "Seven of them, big as ponies, and they come when the water comes. We cannot pay what you are worth, only 199 batteries, and if you say no it is fine, everybody says no."
   HIS: "My Dear, dark shadows keep stealing our water. Theyre going to kill us next. Please help us before they come back. You'll kill them for some batteries?"
   WHAT IT TEACHES (our reading, his to correct):
   - 'My Dear': she talks to you like family, not like a client.
   - She does not explain the beast: 'dark shadows'. No count, no size. Afraid people do not describe the thing.
   - She says what is at stake, in order: the water, then the people ('Theyre going to kill us next').
   - She says please and help, then the deal, as a question about you: 'for some batteries?' Never a number (ours said 199).
   - 4 short sentences, about 7 words each (ours ran 17). Old and scared is short of breath.
   - She ends on the question. Ours ended on a thought ('everybody says no').
   - He starred it. It is the first starred line of THE VOICE: the job board copies this shape first.
