## THE LAW

### 1. EVERY DISTRICT CELL IS ITS OWN LANDMARK
Not "some districts are landmarks." **Every one.** A cell the player stands in must be a
place they could describe to somebody else and be understood — *the one with the water
tower*, *the one with the drained pools*, *the one with the screen*. If a district can
be swapped for another district and nobody notices, it has failed this law.

This supersedes the softer reading in the legibility research, which proposed landmarks
only as a per-district *feature*. They are the **point** of a district, not an ornament
on it.

### 2. THE POCKET CITY 2 BAR
He named a specific game as the quality bar and said *"you gotta be able to rock with
that."* What the bar actually is, in one line from its own reviews:

> **"Everything looks unique enough to know what it is at a glance."**

Charming, colourful, isometric, and — the operative part — **individually identifiable**.
This is the same standard the squint test names from the other direction: ~70% of a
design's impact is its silhouette, and the harshest test is the smallest one. Two
districts that read the same at one tile ARE the same district.

### 3. DISTRICT THEMES: DIVERSE, FUN, UNIQUE — AND THAT IS THE DELIVERABLE
*"do a lot better and fun, unique district themes for the types."* The current 36
registered types are 36 correct-but-interchangeable arrangements of buildings and
pavement. They are accurate and they are boring. **A district needs a HOOK, not just a
programme.** Diversity and fun are now acceptance criteria, not polish.

## WHAT THIS CHANGES IMMEDIATELY

- **The build order proposed in the legibility research is re-ranked.** "Nodes and
  landmarks in every district" was item 1 and 2 of six; it is now simply THE JOB.
- **The WALKABLE-LAND law gains a companion.** That law says content must beat pavement.
  This one says something stronger: content must also be *particular*. A district can
  satisfy walkable-land completely — dense, purposeful, well-drivable — and still fail
  this law by being generic. The self-storage district is the standing density
  reference; it is now also a cautionary one, because rows of identical units are dense
  AND interchangeable.
- **A gate is owed.** A law without a machine gate is not enforced. The shape it should
  take is the squint test: render every district's icon at one tile, threshold to pure
  black, and require the silhouettes to be mutually distinguishable. Two districts whose
  black shapes match are, at map zoom, the same district. That gate does not exist yet.
