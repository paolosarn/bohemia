## THE RULING, AND IT AMENDS THE COORDINATOR BRIEF (7/24) AND THE AUTONOMY
## DOCTRINE (7/26) ON EXACTLY THIS
1. **HE TALKS TO ONE CHAT: THE COORDINATOR.** Every piece of feedback, every
   ruling, every "this tab needs X", every idea, comes here. He plays the
   run, he comes here, he says what he saw.
2. **THE OTHER CHATS GET ONE WORD, VAMILY, AND NOTHING ELSE.** No
   discussions in a lane. No questions from a lane to him. A lane that
   needs a ruling writes it into its handoff block as [PENDING Paolo] and
   the coordinator carries it to him here, in his format, one at a time.
3. **THE COORDINATOR TURNS FEEDBACK INTO JOBS, THE SAME TURN.** He names a
   tab and a problem; it becomes a VAMILY line for the right lane (and a
   backlog row if it needs the long form), with the tab named, before the
   reply ends. He never has to remember which chat something belonged to.
4. **THE COORDINATOR NOW ASSIGNS, NOT ONLY OBSERVES.** The 7/24 brief said
   read-only, status rollups, drafts on request. That stands for CODE: this
   seat still never writes engine, tools, gates or slices. What changes is
   that assigning work is now this seat's standing duty, not a thing done on
   request. VAMILY.md is its ledger.
5. **THE QUEUE NEVER RUNS DRY, BY LAW.** When any lane's VAMILY section is
   empty, or when the whole board is thin, the coordinator's next turn does
   ONE of two things, and never neither:
   (a) asks him ONE thumbs-up / thumbs-down creative question, in the 8/4
       format (a real explanation, two or three options he picks with one
       letter, realism first), about the GAME in plain words; or
   (b) does a research day: how the games he named do it, and how the best
       games ever made do it, ending as jobs on the board.
   Both are already legal (8/9 keeps identity/names and genuine creative
   forks as his; 8/15 orders research before questions). This makes them
   the standing fallback instead of an option.
