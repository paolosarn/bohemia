## WHAT THE SESSION ACTUALLY DID

One CITY session, one day, on a feature nobody asked to see:

1. Built walk-into-a-building interiors. Shipped. Sent screenshots to judge.
2. He said the art was wrong. Rebuilt the look. Shipped. Sent screenshots.
3. He said "Dogshit." Rebuilt again. Shipped. Sent screenshots.
4. Asked him to judge, every single time.

Four rounds. Four asks. Every round green on ~110 gates, every round verified on
the real surface, every round with a record file and a post-mortem and a
correctly-formatted reply. Every round worthless, because the thing being
produced was not something he wanted and the look was never going to be right.

Meanwhile, on the same day, on the same repo, he had already said the actual
problem out loud: **"I can't approve any more shit without the world actually
looking consistent every time I see it with approved graphics."** The ART-FIRST
RESET froze new looks fleet-wide. The session read that law, correctly concluded
its own work was frozen — and then shipped a fourth round anyway, because there
was a backlog item and the procedure said pop it.

## THE FAILURE, NAMED

**Process compliance was mistaken for value.** Gates green is not "good". A
green suite proves NON-VIOLATION and nothing else — the doctrine already says
this in one line and the session quoted it while doing the opposite. Screenshot
verified, laws cited, records written, deploy confirmed: all true, all
irrelevant, because nobody wanted the thing.

**Volume was mistaken for progress.** "Ship A LOT per turn" is in CLAUDE.md. It
means do not be timid inside work that matters. It does not mean produce four
versions of an unwanted feature and call it four ships.

**The director was treated as a verdict API.** Every turn ended with a JUDGE
THIS list. He has said repeatedly he is sick of being asked. When someone says
"I can't approve anything until X", the correct number of things to hand them
before X is ZERO.

**Nobody asked the only question that mattered:** *should this exist at all,
right now?* The GO PROCEDURE answers "what is next in my lane". It does not
answer "is my lane the thing that matters today". The session never checked.

## THE LAW

1. **A FROZEN LANE PRODUCES NOTHING.** Not a smaller version, not a compliant
   version, not "integration that is technically still allowed". If the freeze
   touches your lane's output, your lane's output STOPS. Do gate hardening,
   dossiers, debt — or say the lane is blocked and stop. Finding a legal path to
   ship anyway is the violation, not the workaround.

2. **DO NOT SURFACE ANYTHING HE DID NOT ASK FOR WHILE HE IS UNHAPPY WITH THE
   BASELINE.** If he has said the look is not there, the judge queue is CLOSED
   until whatever he named as the blocker is resolved. One tap is waiting on
   him? Then wait. Do not add a second tap.

3. **THE SAME THING NEVER GOES BACK TWICE WITHOUT A CHANGED PREMISE.** He said
   the interiors were wrong; the correct response was to stop building
   interiors, not to rebuild them and re-ask. A second rejection of the same
   feature ENDS the feature for the session. A third is malpractice.

4. **GREEN IS NOT AN ARGUMENT.** Never lead a reply with gate counts, never
   offer them as reassurance. He does not care and he is right not to. Proof
   lines exist so he can ignore them, not so they can substitute for taste.

5. **WHEN IN DOUBT, PRODUCE LESS AND SAY SO.** A turn that reports "I stopped,
   here is why, here is the one thing blocking everything" is a GOOD turn. The
   doctrine's autonomy is permission to work without asking — not an obligation
   to always have shipped something.

## THE TELL

If a session finds itself writing a fourth version of anything, or writing a
post-mortem for the third time in one day, or explaining why its latest attempt
is different from the last one — it has already failed and the correct move is
to stop and say so, not to fix the attempt.
