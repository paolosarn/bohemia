## WHY THIS IS A HARDER JOB THAN IT SOUNDS, AND WHAT THE CRAFT ANSWER IS

The icon research I did for him says plainly: **fewest parts wins, and detail
that dies at 32px is worse than no detail.** He has just asked for three-object
icons. That is a real tension and pretending otherwise would get the art
bounced.

It is not a contradiction, though — it is a known problem with a known craft
answer, and the answer is the thing the art lane must be told:

**THE THREE PARTS MAKE ONE SILHOUETTE.** The icon is not three drawings placed
near each other; the parts are composed — overlapped, nested, unioned — so their
OUTER EDGE is a single memorable shape. This is the Boolean-union approach
(combine the shapes so the outer path is the composite minus the overlaps) and
it is how heraldry, badges and crafting icons have always solved "several things,
one mark."

The test that decides it: **fill the icon solid black. If the black blob is
still recognisably THAT currency and not the other two, it works. If it becomes
a lump, the composition failed** — regardless of how nice the parts look.

Practical consequences the forms carry:
1. **One dominant part, two supporting.** A three-way equal split reads as
   clutter. One object owns the silhouette and the others resolve it.
2. **Different base shapes inside one mark.** Apple (round) + hammer (long
   diagonal) + duct tape (ring) is genuinely a good set because the three
   primitives are different. Same for jerrycan (rectangle) + battery (small
   cylinder) + bolt (zigzag).
3. **The bolt is the one to watch.** A lightning bolt is a high-frequency zigzag
   and it is the single most likely part to turn to mush at small size or to
   dominate everything else.
4. **The three currency marks must still be mutually distinct** — which his
   picks help with: RESOURCES is a cluster of hand objects, ENERGY is a
   container-plus-charge, CLOUT is people-plus-a-bubble. Three different
   subjects, not three different tools.
