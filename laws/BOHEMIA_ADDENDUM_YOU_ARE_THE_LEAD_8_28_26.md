## RULING 1 — ONE BODY IS YOURS. THE OTHERS ARE YOURS AND YOU DO NOT DRIVE
## THEM.
**THE PLAYER CONTROLS EXACTLY ONE CHARACTER, ALWAYS.** The rest of the
squad executes gambits — rules the player wrote before the fight.
**AND IT IS NOT OPTIONAL. THERE IS NO "TAKE MANUAL CONTROL" BUTTON.**
FF12 allowed it; he explicitly does not want it. That single word is what
keeps 20v20 from becoming twenty decisions a turn.

## RULING 2 — THE SCALE IS 20 VERSUS 20
Not a skirmish. Forty bodies. **THIS IS THE WHOLE REASON RULING 1 EXISTS**
— nobody issues twenty orders per turn and stays awake, and he already
said normal people will not tolerate a chess match.

## RULING 3 — THE BEAT IS STILL THE CLOCK, AT LEAST FOR YOU
120 BPM stands. His reading, and it is the right one: **YOUR** actions are
on the beat. Whether the other thirty-nine resolve on the same beat, on a
slower cadence, or continuously is a mechanism question for the study, not
a licence to drop the beat.

## RULING 4 — *** HE NAMED THE HARD PROBLEM HIMSELF: GUNS BREAK THE CHESS ***
"In Battle Brothers there's no gun, so it's a lot more chess-y, and we
have to think about the complexity of adding guns."
He is right and it is the single most important unknown in the whole
project right now. Battle Brothers' tactics are MELEE tactics: a front
line, adjacency, zone of control, shield walls, flanking, a reason to
close the distance. **GUNS DELETE THE FRONT LINE.** If everyone has range,
nobody has to close, and the positional chess collapses into forty units
shooting from maximum range.
### THE REAL WORLD ALREADY SOLVED THIS AND IT TOOK TWO HUNDRED YEARS
**PIKE AND SHOT.** From the late 15th century until the late 17th,
European armies fought with exactly this problem and the answer was not
"guns win." It was that **THE TWO ARMS PROTECT EACH OTHER**: musketeers
deliver massed fire from a distance, and pikemen — a solid block, 15-to-18
foot points — keep cavalry and chargers off them. The muskets flanked the
pike block on both sides. The Spanish worked it out first and it defined
warfare for two centuries: "the defensive solidity of a forest of steel
points with the offensive potential of massed musket fire."
**AND THE PUNCHLINE IS THE DESIGN RULE, HANDED TO US FOR FREE:** the pike
died when the BAYONET arrived. Once a musket could also fight up close,
musketeers could defend themselves and the combined formation stopped
being necessary. **SO: IF OUR GUNS ARE ALSO GOOD IN CLOSE, WE LOSE THE
WHOLE POSITIONAL GAME.** Guns being bad up close is not a realism note, it
is the load-bearing wall of the tactics. That is a hard constraint on
every weapon we ever write.

## RULING 5 — THE PRECEDENT FOR HIS CONTROL SCHEME EXISTS AND IT SHIPPED
**MOUNT & BLADE.** You are one body inside a battle of dozens or hundreds.
You fight personally, and you give ORDERS TO FORMATIONS rather than to
individuals — move, face, form, engage — and when it gets to be too much
**you can hand command to your sergeants and let the AI run it.**
So his scheme is not exotic. It is **MOUNT & BLADE'S CONTROL AT BATTLE
BROTHERS' DEPTH, WITH FF12'S GAMBITS AS THE ORDER LANGUAGE, ON OUR BEAT.**
That is one sentence and it is the design.

## RULING 6 — WHAT NO-MANUAL-CONTROL COSTS, AND THE ANSWER
Making gambits non-optional is right for pace and it has one real cost:
**when a squadmate does something stupid, it is entirely the player's
fault and there is nothing he can do about it mid-fight.** That is either
the best feeling in the game or the worst, and which one depends on a
piece we have not designed.
**THE ANSWER IS NOT MANUAL CONTROL. IT IS AN ORDER.** One shout, one beat,
that overrides everybody for a moment — fall back, focus that one, hold.
Mount & Blade has exactly this layer and it is why its scheme survives
chaos. **AND IT SITS PERFECTLY ON 120 BPM: an order is a thing you spend a
beat on.**
SECOND COST, and it is the one that actually kills systems like this:
**LEGIBILITY.** Thirty-nine bodies acting on rules every beat is a lot the
player cannot read. If he cannot tell WHY his squad did what it did, the
gambits feel broken rather than clever. FF12's answer was that the gambit
list is visible, ordered, and top-down. Ours has to answer it too.

## WHAT THIS DOES NOT CHANGE
RF4 stays the tactical vocabulary (8/17, 8/26). 120 BPM stands. No money,
no permadeath, the rewind, the dynasty, fun carries the tie, no damage
before the dial. And nothing routes as work until the study's day 5.
