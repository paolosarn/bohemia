## RULING 1 — ONE BODY IS YOURS, AND THE REST IS NOT OPTIONAL
**THE PLAYER CONTROLS EXACTLY ONE CHARACTER, ALWAYS.** No manual-control
button for anybody else, ever. FF12 allowed it; he explicitly does not
want it.

## RULING 2 — THE SCALE IS 20 VERSUS 20, AND A BATTLE IS QUICK
Forty bodies, and **"every time it's ONE FUCKING BATTLE, it's not a 40
MINUTE LONG CHESS MATCH."** That sentence is the acceptance test for the
entire tactical layer. If a fight reads as a puzzle to be solved slowly,
it failed, no matter how deep it is.

## RULING 3 — *** THE ORDERS ARE DUMB ON PURPOSE. THIS IS THE BIG ONE. ***
Not FF12's grammar. Not a rule list with targets and priorities and
top-down evaluation. **"A GO, SLOW DOWN TYPE OF VIBE."**
A handful of plain words that a person understands instantly and can
change in one tap: go, hold, slow down, fall back, focus that one. **SO
SIMPLE HE "BARELY WANTED IT TO BE KIND OF LIKE THE GAMBIT SYSTEM."**
**AND THE SIMPLICITY IS THE FEATURE, NOT A BUDGET CUT.** FF12's depth is
the thing that makes it a hobby. At twenty a side, a deep rule language
would be a spreadsheet with a battle attached, and he has ruled twice
against spreadsheet simulators. **THE DEEP UNDERSTANDING OF BATTLE
BROTHERS GOES UNDERNEATH IT, NOT INTO THE INTERFACE.**
It also solves the legibility problem for free: when a squad is running on
three or four words, the player can always tell WHY they did what they
did, because he can read his own order at a glance.

## RULING 4 — *** ONE SYSTEM DRIVES EVERYBODY. HIS IDEA, AND IT IS THE
## BEST STRUCTURAL IDEA IN THIS WHOLE DIRECTION. ***
The same simple order language controls **YOUR COMPANY, THE ENEMIES, AND
THE FACTIONS.** Not three AIs. One.
WHY THAT IS WORTH MORE THAN IT SOUNDS:
- **THE PLAYER LEARNS THE ENEMY BY WRITING HIS OWN ORDERS.** Every hour he
  spends setting his own squad is an hour spent learning to read theirs.
  Nothing has to be taught.
- **ONE SYSTEM TO BUILD, ONE TO BALANCE, ONE TO GATE.** Three behaviour
  systems is how a small project dies.
- **AND IT SCALES OFF THE BATTLEFIELD.** If factions run on the same few
  words, then "the Reds are pushing" is a real state of a real system
  rather than a line of flavour text. That is the campaign layer and the
  tactical layer speaking the same language.

## RULING 5 — GUNS ARE STILL THE HARD PROBLEM, AND HE NAMED IT
Battle Brothers is chess because it is MELEE: a front line, adjacency,
zone of control, a reason to close. **GUNS DELETE THE FRONT LINE.** If
everyone has range, nobody closes, and forty bodies shoot from maximum
distance.
**THE REAL WORLD SPENT TWO CENTURIES ON THIS AND THE ANSWER IS RECORDED:**
when firearms first arrived, armies did not abandon the spear. They put
the two together, and the two arms PROTECTED EACH OTHER — massed fire from
a distance, and a solid block of long spears keeping cavalry and chargers
off the shooters. That arrangement defined European warfare for two
hundred years.
**AND THE PUNCHLINE IS OUR DESIGN RULE.** The spear only disappeared when
the bayonet arrived, because a gun that could also fight up close no
longer needed protecting. **SO IF OUR GUNS ARE GOOD IN CLOSE, WE LOSE THE
POSITIONAL GAME ENTIRELY.** Guns being bad in your face is not a realism
note; it is the wall holding the roof up, and it is a hard constraint on
every weapon we ever write.

## RULING 6 — THE BEAT IS STILL THE CLOCK, AT LEAST FOR YOU
120 BPM stands, and his reading is the right one: **your** actions are on
the beat. Whether the other thirty-nine resolve on the same beat or a
slower cadence is a mechanism question for the study.
AND THE ONE THING THE SIMPLE ORDERS BUY BACK: **changing an order is a
thing you spend a beat on.** That is the recourse when a squadmate does
something stupid, and it is the reason no manual-control button is needed.

## WHAT THIS DOES NOT CHANGE
RF4 stays the tactical vocabulary. No money, no permadeath, the rewind,
the dynasty, fun carries the tie, no damage before the dial. Nothing
routes as work until the study's day 5.
