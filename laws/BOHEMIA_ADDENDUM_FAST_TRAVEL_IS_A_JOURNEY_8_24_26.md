## THE RULING, IN ONE SENTENCE

**Crossing the valley is something you PLAY, not something you skip.** A menu that
moves the player from A to B with a loading bar is dead on arrival in Bohemia.

## WHAT FRONTIER ACTUALLY DOES, RESEARCHED RATHER THAN REMEMBERED

Sources are listed at the bottom. The loop, stripped to its bones:

1. **You are a caravan on a map, not a cursor.** You pick a ROUTE, and routes have
   different lengths and different danger. The community's own first tip is
   "take short routes at the beginning, take longer ones when you're heavily
   armored" — which only means something if the route is a decision with a cost.
2. **You are carrying something worth taking.** Cargo is the reason the road is
   dangerous. Trade is not a menu you visit between journeys; it is the STAKE of
   the journey.
3. **The road interrupts you.** Bandits and rival caravans attack. The law stops
   and searches you for what you are carrying. Both are the same mechanism seen
   from two sides.
4. **You can spend money on surviving the road**, not just on goods: hire guards,
   upgrade the transport. The road is a thing you invest against.
5. **Standing still is punished.** "The only way to recover health is not to stay
   within 2 cities, you need to move around the map." Travel is the engine, not
   the tax.
6. **The law/outlaw axis rides on the SAME loop.** The Enforcer stops caravans;
   the Buccaneer robs them. One system, two seats.

The Oregon Trail half of what he said is the other pillar: **a journey has a
middle**. Things happen between the leaving and the arriving, and what you packed
before you left decides how they go.

## WHAT LOCKS (the shape)

- **A journey has a middle.** Departure, road, arrival. Anything that removes the
  middle is not fast travel, it is a teleport, and it is banned.
- **Route is a choice with a cost.** More than one way to get there, and they are
  not equal. Fast/dangerous versus slow/safe is the minimum honest fork.
- **You carry something, and it can be lost.** The road is dangerous because of
  what is in the vehicle, not because of a difficulty slider.
- **The road interrupts.** Encounters happen in transit and are the point of it.
- **You can prepare.** What you spend before leaving changes what the road does.
- **Being stopped and being robbed are one system.** Whoever holds a stretch of
  road decides which one you meet.
- **It obeys the clock we already have.** 120 BPM LAW, I-MOVE-YOU-MOVE, and
  TIME IS SPENT BY ACTIONS. A journey spends the day; it does not pause it.
- **NO DISTRICT IS A PRISON still holds.** A journey may be dangerous. It may
  never be the only way out of somewhere.

## WHAT IS EXPLICITLY *NOT* DECIDED HERE — HIS, AND RESERVED

MECHANISM-MINE / CONTENTS-PAOLO'S applies, and hard, because the temptation with
a reference this specific is to paste the reference in:

- **Enforcers and Buccaneers are FRONTIER'S names, not Bohemia's.** Bohemia has
  its own sixteen factions. Which of them police the roads, which of them rob
  them, and whether any of them do either is **his call and is not written here.**
  Casting a Bohemia faction as "the police" because a 2009 flash game had one
  would be canon nobody ruled, shipped inside a system.
- **Which roads exist and what connects to what.** MAP LAW: Claude never designs
  map layouts.
- **What is worth carrying**, what it is worth, and every number: distances, time
  costs, danger rates, guard prices, upgrade tiers.
- **What you travel IN.** The vehicle ladder exists (bike = 4 cells per beat) and
  what sits above it is unset.
- **Whether the player can ever BE the raider**, which is a story decision with a
  morality attached, not a mechanic.
