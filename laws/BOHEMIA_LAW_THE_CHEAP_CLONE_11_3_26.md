## 2. THE REAL HISTORY IT GROWS FROM (true today, the floor under the fiction)
- 2013: the first lab-grown burger (Mark Post, Maastricht) cost about 250,000 euros. Most of the cost was the broth the cells grow in:
  its growth factors were made for medicine, by the gram.
- 2020: Singapore approves lab-grown chicken for sale (Eat Just). 2023: the United States approves it (Upside Foods, Good Meat). 2024:
  Florida and Alabama ban it. The fight over it is already political.
- 2023: Vow (Australia) makes a meatball with a woolly mammoth's muscle protein, the gene filled in from an elephant. A dead animal
  on a plate as a stunt, years before the crash.
- 2025: Colossal Biosciences announces three "dire wolf" pups (Romulus, Remus, Khaleesi): grey wolves with about twenty edits from
  ancient dire wolf DNA, carried by dog surrogates. A company with billions of dollars of investment says the mammoth is next.
- Pet cloning is a shop today (ViaGen in Texas: tens of thousands of dollars for a dog or a horse). Cloning still fails most of the
  time: Dolly the sheep was one success out of 277 tries.
- 2017: a premature lamb grows for four weeks in a plastic bag of fluid at a Philadelphia hospital (the "biobag"). The womb can be a
  machine.
- Texas hunting ranches today hold herds of animals from other continents (axis deer, nilgai, blackbuck), and some have broken out and
  run wild in the Hill Country for generations. Rich people already pay to hunt animals that should not be there.
- Gypsum Cave, a short drive from the Strip, kept a Shasta ground sloth's dung for 11,000 years; scientists read its DNA from it (1998).
  The valley's dead are readable.

## 3. THE COORDINATOR'S DRAFT OF THE FUTURE (draft:true; his to change; realism first)
THE VAT YEARS (about 2030 to the crash, about 2050):
- The cost of the broth fell the way solar panels did: growth factors brewed by engineered yeast in steel tanks, then by the tonne.
  Meat from a vat got cheaper than meat from a field. The space programmes needed it (you cannot ship a cow to the moon), and the
  money that came for space made it cheap on earth.
- Cheap broth plus cheap gene editing plus the machine womb turned cloning from a lab miracle into a factory line. A clone embryo
  became a product with a part number. Failure stopped mattering, because a failure cost almost nothing: run a hundred, keep three.
- What cheap cloning sold, because vat meat was cheap but boring: SPECTACLE. Ice Age animals for the Strip's shows and its menus (a
  casino's mammoth burger, a Camelops steak, a sloth in a glass atrium), hunting ranches in the desert where a man with money could
  shoot a dire wolf, private zoos, guard animals for the rich, and the lab herds that grew them (the Tule Springs lab north of the
  city; others a state or two over, the 9/29 law).
- Every animal carried its barcode: an ear tag, a chip, a number tattooed in the ear, a batch code. Clones from one line share one
  genome, so a pack from one batch has ONE FACE: the same markings on every wolf.

THE DAY THE DOLLAR DIED:
- A vat needs power, clean steel and broth bought every week. When the money stopped, the vats spoiled first: contamination in days,
  then the smell. The cheap meat future died in a week. The animals it had made cheap did not.
- Nobody could feed a herd of 300 Camelops, a ranch of dire wolves, a casino's sloths. Some were let go on purpose (the owner opened
  the gates rather than watch them starve), some broke out, some were sold to whoever had a truck. That is why there are so many, and
  why they are everywhere a road goes.

WHY PEOPLE ARE FINE WITH IT (his line, reasoned):
- A beast is the only meat left that walks to you. The vat is dead, the stores are empty, the cattle were eaten in the first winter.
  A hunter feeds a block for a week off one Camelops. Hunting the lab beasts is a trade, a sport, a way to be somebody.
- Clones were made to be eaten: no wild fear of man, born in pens, fed by hand. Many still come to a human voice. That is the horror
  and the mercy of it: they trust you.

WATER (big-brain draft):
- The Mojave gets about four inches of rain a year. An animal the size of a camel drinks gallons a day; a wolf pack needs water every
  day or two. So the beasts live where the water is, and the water is where the people are:
  * the Las Vegas Wash (called the Wash in the game): the city's treated wastewater ran to Lake Mead at about 200 million gallons a
    day; after the crash it runs thinner and dirtier, and it is the valley's biggest river of beasts;
  * the lake itself, its receded shore and its mud;
  * the springs (Tule Springs, Corn Creek, Red Rock's seeps, the old Las Vegas Springs where the city began);
  * the guzzlers: the state's old wildlife water tanks in the desert, built for bighorn sheep, now the lions' and the wolves' wells;
  * the golf course ponds, the casino fountains, the backyard pools (tens of thousands of them), the leaking mains.
- So a beast in the street is a beast that came for a pool. Water is danger: the wash is a hunting ground both ways.
- The labs edited their lines to live in the heat (the 9/29 law's "every beast survives the Mojave"): within real DNA, the way a dire
  wolf is a grey wolf with edits.
