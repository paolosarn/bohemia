# BOHEMIA — THE PIXEL BIBLE
### The one document the ART lane lives by. Read it before touching a pixel. Every time.

> Paolo, 7/29/26: **"OKAY SAVE ALL YOUR KNOWLEDGE IN A MASTER PIXEL ART DOCUMENT
> THAT YOU WILL LIVE BY DIE BY EVERYTIME NEVER TO DRIFT OFF FROM."**

This is that document. Five research passes, two law files, one ignorance map and
every mistake I made on the way, consolidated into the single thing to read.

**HOW IT REFUSES TO DRIFT.** `gates/pixel_bible_gate.py` asserts that every law
below still exists here, that the honesty clauses are intact, and that the two
source law files still point at this file. If a future session quietly waters
something down, the build goes red. A bible nobody can check is a bible that rots.

**THE THREE HONESTY CLAUSES, which never come out:**
1. This is built from **search-returned summaries** of the primary sources. This
   environment's network policy 403s every direct page fetch.
2. **Pixel Logic (Michael Azzi) is UNREAD.** It is the standard book on this
   subject, buying it is ~$9, and it is on the board as ART -1c.
3. **I have never drawn a pixel by hand.** Everything I have made was generated by
   code I wrote. I have measurements, not an eye — and every single time a
   measurement was green and the picture was bad, I was wrong.

# PART 0 — IF YOU READ NOTHING ELSE, READ THIS

**1. VALUE FIRST, HUE LAST.** Decide in greyscale what is dark, mid and light
across the whole set. Lock that skeleton. *Then* choose colours to sit on it. I
have never once worked this way and every value failure in this project is
downstream of that. (M18)

**2. A TILE IS A DECISION PER PIXEL, NOT A PICTURE SHRUNK DOWN.** The corpus was
74% orphan pixels because it was continuous-tone art reduced, not pixel art drawn.
That is what "hallucinated AI slop" means and Paolo diagnosed it by eye. (L0)

**3. MOST OF IT IS SUPPOSED TO BE BORING.** If every asset shouts, nothing reads.
Almost everything is background; the contrast belongs to doors, openings and
landmarks. (M13, M3)

# PART 1 — THE NUMBERS (Bohemia's hard constants; changing one breaks a gate)

| | |
|---|---|
| **THE CORPUS CELL** | **44 px.** Every approved tile. Draw at anything else and the whole corpus resamples. |
| CELL_M | 0.75 m per cell — so **1 px ≈ 1.7 cm**. This is how you size real detail. |
| A door opening | **2 cells** (88 px) |
| A human | 1.75 m; a standing body clears **68–90%** of its own doorway (target 77%) |
| A car | **2 x 3 tiles**, read from `engine/bohemia_prop_scale.js`, never typed |
| Projection | axis-aligned oblique, north-up, **45-degree world view**, **SHEAR = 0** |
| Light | **ONE key, from the upper LEFT.** Shadows fall down and right. |
| Value bands | sky-lit top **1.30** > lit front **0.97** > away side **0.56**; top/away ≥ 1.6 |
| Scaling | **NEAREST only.** Integer multiples only. Never LANCZOS/BICUBIC/BILINEAR. |
| Outline | **No black keyline.** < 6% near-pure-black per plate. |
| Dither | **None in act 1.** Stipple crawls under a 2x/3x blit on a phone. |
| Act-1 glass | **DEAD DARK.** < 2% hot-yellow pixels. Never a warm night glow. |
| Layers | exactly one of: ground / structure / overhead / prop / portal |
| Edge contracts | exactly one of: SINGLE PLACEMENT / SELF-SEAMLESS / WANG-16 / BLOB-47 |

# PART 2 — THE CRAFT LAWS (level 1: how not to be bad)

Full text + sources: `laws/BOHEMIA_PIXEL_CRAFT_LAWS_7_27_26.md`

- **L0. A TILE IS DECISIONS, NOT A SHRUNK PICTURE.** *"AI learned what pixel art
  looks like, but never learned what pixel art IS."*
- **L1. PIXELS TRAVEL IN GROUPS.** A lone pixel of its own colour is noise —
  *"responsible for the image looking noisy and confusing."*
- **L2. A TILE GETS A RAMP, NOT A SPECTRUM.** 4–7 values per material, hue-shifted:
  shadows cooler, lights warmer.
- **L3. ONE STAIRCASE PER LINE.** Never a 1px step inside a run of 2px steps.
- **L4. AA IS INTERNAL, NEVER ON A SILHOUETTE** that meets an unknown neighbour.
- **L5. BANDS VARY IN WIDTH.** Uniform parallel bands make the eye read the seam.
- **L6. NO DITHER IN ACT 1.**
- **L7. ONE KEY, AND THE TILE MAY NOT ARGUE WITH IT.** Pillow shading is shading
  with no direction at all.
- **L8. MATERIAL IS A FEW SHAPES REPEATED, NOT EVERY GRAIN DRAWN.**
- **L9. ONE PIXEL SIZE IN A SCENE, INTEGER SCALING ONLY.**
- **L10. SILHOUETTE FIRST, SHADING LAST — AND IT MUST BE NAMEABLE.**
- **L11. UNIFORMITY BEATS REALISM.** *"In pixel art, uniformity takes priority over
  realism."* Plus: **a door is a HOLE, not a picture of a door.**
- **L12. TILE EDGES MATCH WITHOUT BEING IDENTICAL; high-traffic tiles get 2–5
  variants.**

# PART 3 — THE MASTERY LAWS (level 2: competent → great)

Full text + sources: `laws/BOHEMIA_PIXEL_MASTERY_LAWS_7_28_26.md`

- **M1. WEAR MUST BE MOTIVATED, NEVER SCATTERED.** Every mark answers *"what did
  this?"* in one word — traffic, water, sun, contact. Random is not natural.
- **M2. THE FLOOR IS QUIET.** Ground must be quieter than the structure standing on
  it. *(We measured the opposite: 102 vs 58.8. Backwards.)*
- **M3. CONTRAST IS A BUDGET.** Spend it on what the player should look at.
- **M4. AFTER YOU ADD DETAIL, TAKE IT AWAY.** The simplify pass is mandatory.
- **M5. DETAIL LEVEL MATCHES ACROSS THE SET.** Eighteen families are ONE job.
- **M6. HUE-SHIFT ON PERCEIVED LIGHTNESS, NOT RGB.** *(Debt: ours are RGB-spaced.)*
- **M7. A PRODUCTION PIPELINE BEATS HEROIC HAND-PIXELLING.** Dead Cells generated
  from 3D. **Generate the material, preserve the authored content.**
- **M8. RESTRAINT READS AS INTENT.** When in doubt, fewer.
- **M9. INDEX THE TILES** → night, rain, act 2, act 3 and colourways become palette
  swaps instead of new art. *(Debt: banks still store RGB.)*
- **M10. THE OFFSET TEST IS THE SEAM TEST.** Shift 50/50, look at the cross. *"Heavy
  borders that outline the tile guarantee it reads as a grid."*
- **M11. IRREGULAR CLUSTERING, NEVER EVEN SPACING.**
- **M12. BLOB-47: EDGES, THEN CORNERS, THEN ISOLATED.** Inner corners are the trap;
  build one first as the test.
- **M13. THE MAJORITY OF THE SET IS DELIBERATELY SUBORDINATE.**
- **M14. LAYERS SEPARATE IN VALUE, NOT IN HUE.** 18 points minimum between ground /
  structure / top. *(Found by LOOKING, not by any measurement.)*
- **M15. GROUNDING IS A LOOKING JOB, NOT A WRITING JOB.** The best environmental
  pixel artist alive works from photographs of real streets. Name a real location,
  not just a real material. She is praised for **perspective and light, never
  texture** — and every failure here was a texture failure that was a light-and-
  structure failure underneath.
- **M16. ANIMATED ENVIRONMENT EFFECTS ARE PALETTE CYCLES, NOT FRAME SETS** — rain,
  fog, smoke, running water, flicker. Shape changes stay frame animation. *(Needs
  M9 first.)* **We take the cycling and NOT Ferrari's dither.**
- **M17. ONE MASTER PALETTE FOR THE WHOLE GAME, EVERY FAMILY A SUBSET.** Six
  independent ramps is the amateur pattern, named in the sources, and it is what we
  built. *(BLOCKER: the master palette does not exist yet.)*
- **M18. VALUE STRUCTURE FIRST, HUE LAST.** The method, and the one I have never
  used.
- **M19. GLASS IS THE BACKGROUND, DARKENED AND FLATTENED**, plus one hard highlight
  — and **withholding that highlight is how act-1 glass reads dead.**

# PART 4 — THE METHOD (the order of operations; do not reorder)

Cooking any family, in this order, every time:

1. **READ THE FORM.** No form, no cook. A vague form gets bounced back, never
   guessed at. (`BOHEMIA_TILE_REQUEST_FORM.md`)
2. **SHOP FIRST.** `records/BOHEMIA_APPROVED_ASSET_INDEX_7_27_26.md`. Name the
   near-miss and why it fails. Reuse beats cooking.
3. **LOOK AT THE REAL THING.** M15 — a real Las Vegas location, not a description
   of Las Vegas materials.
4. **DESIGN THE VALUE SKELETON IN GREY.** M18. Where does this family sit against
   ground / structure / top? Hit M14's 18-point separation *by decision*, not by
   luck.
5. **PULL COLOUR FROM THE MASTER PALETTE.** M17. A subset, never an invention.
6. **BUILD THE MATERIAL AS A FEW CLUSTERS.** L8 + M11 — irregular, clumped.
7. **PUT THE WEAR WHERE SOMETHING HAPPENED.** M1. Traffic, water, sun, contact.
8. **SIMPLIFY.** M4. Fewer regions after than before, read intact.
9. **RUN THE OFFSET SEAM TEST** if it declares any seamless contract. M10.
10. **LOOK AGAIN.** `tools/bohemia_look_again.py` — flip, squint, greyscale, stand
    back. Part 5 below.
11. **RENDER ON THE REAL SURFACE**, 3x3 tiled, beside its approved anchor. Never
    judge a lone tile; a ground tile alone is a lie.
12. **RUN THE GATES.** Green is permission to show it, never proof it is good.
13. **SHOW PAOLO AN ASSEMBLED SCENE**, never loose pixels.

# PART 5 — THE RITUAL (how I look at my own work now)

`tools/bohemia_look_again.py <image>` — the four things real artists do, all of
which a program can perform:

- **FLIP IT** — mirrored, balance errors your eye has stopped seeing jump out.
- **SQUINT** — blur it. If the read dies when detail goes, detail was carrying the
  picture and there was no structure under it.
- **GREYSCALE** — if it does not read in grey, it does not read. This is what
  caught M14 when six numbers could not.
- **STAND BACK** — real size and map zoom. Judging at 6x zoom is a lie about a game
  played on a phone.

Then ask, out loud, in words:
- What is the player supposed to look at here, and is that the thing with the most
  contrast?
- What did each mark? Can I name it in one word?
- Would I know what this is if it were unlabelled?

# PART 6 — WHAT THE MACHINE HOLDS, AND WHAT IT NEVER WILL

`gates/pixel_craft_gate.py` measures: orphan share · single-use colours · pixel
block size · pillow score · cluster density · set-wide palette · **M2** floor-is-
quiet · **M5** detail spread · **M10** offset seam · **M14** value separation.

**THE MACHINE IS NOT A TASTE MACHINE, AND SAYS SO IN ITS OWN CODE.** It never
judges whether art looks good. Art can pass every check and be ugly; it cannot
fail them and be pixel art. **Paolo's thumb is the only verdict in this project.**

**A gate is never edited to let code through.** Only the reverse. When a gate
failed my work and turned out to be wrong (the light-direction check calling
`wall_under_eave` a defect for being dark under its own eave), the test was that
the *old, approved* set failed it just as badly — the instrument was broken before
my art ever met it. That test is the standard.

Not gated, and the law says so out loud: M1, M3, M4, M6, M8, M13, M15, M16, M17,
M18, M19. Whether a crack is motivated or contrast landed right is judgement, and
a machine claiming to check it would be fake verification.

# PART 7 — MY OWN FAILURE RECORD (read this before you feel clever)

Every one of these was mine, on this project, in three days:

1. **The corpus was 74% orphan pixels** and nothing in the machine was looking.
   Paolo diagnosed it by eye as "hallucinated AI slop" — the textbook term for the
   textbook failure — while I fixed the door, the garage, the barrel and the lamp
   one at a time.
2. **The first re-cooked tile looked like camouflage.** Perfect numbers, worse
   picture. Cause: scale. I drew 22 cm blotches on a tile that is 75 cm wide.
3. **I authored 17 tiles from nothing and deleted Paolo's drawing.** They came back
   clean and EMPTY — flat sand where his originals had cracks and weeds.
4. **The ground came out busier than the buildings.** I fixed the noise and never
   asked which surface deserved the detail.
5. **The wall seams got 3x worse** in the re-cook. Nobody would have known without
   the offset test.
6. **Roofs and ground ended the same value** — invisible in greyscale. Because
   nobody ever *decided* what value a roof should be.
7. **Six independent family ramps** — literally the amateur pattern the sources
   name.
8. **I reported a seam number of 33x that meant nothing**, because I measured wrap
   discontinuity on a door, which is supposed to be discontinuous.
9. **I called a build "live" at 0.8 MB** because every tab click had landed on the
   splash screen.
10. **I handed Paolo a PNG and a paragraph** when the standing law says he judges
    by tapping.

**The pattern in all ten:** a green number, and a picture nobody looked at.
