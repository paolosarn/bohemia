## Q1 -- TWO PEOPLE, THREE LINES EACH, NAMES REMOVED  (9/4/26)
# The finding these demonstrate: three lines is ~30 words, about 3% of what a
# forensic linguist needs to identify a person, so natural difference is INVISIBLE
# at this length. The difference has to be PLANTED, and 97% of the machine-visible
# signal turns out to be NEGATIVE SPACE -- what somebody refuses to say.
# So each pair below is built on a BAN, not a vocabulary. Read three lines and
# guess who is who; the ban is printed after, not before.

### PAIR 1 -- the lineman and the fixer   draft:true
A1  Nine at night, every night. I checked it twice, then I checked it again.
A2  It's warm about here. You feel that? That's ours going somewhere else.
A3  I'd rather know and be wrong than not know.
B1  Easy. Easy, easy, don't put your hand there.
B2  Somebody's always moving it, hermano. Today it's me moving it.
B3  Ask me tomorrow and I'll tell you something different.
# A never says a person's name and never says "I don't know". B never says a
# number and never finishes a promise about the future.

### PAIR 2 -- the keeper and the watch   draft:true
A1  Eleven in there and four of them can't walk out on their own.
A2  Sit down, drink that, then tell me the rest.
A3  I've got room. I don't have anything else, but I've got room.
B1  Nobody's turned away. Nobody's turned away twice.
B2  You're the one from the other block.
B3  Long as you're not taking anything.
# A never says "no". B never asks a question, and never uses a word longer than
# two syllables.

### PAIR 3 -- gen 1 and gen 3, the same family, ninety years apart   draft:true
# From the hundred-years round: grammar does NOT drift in a century, address terms
# and referents do. Same sentence length, same contraction rate, same question
# rate. What moves is what they call each other and what a word still points at.
A1  Check the meter before you pay anybody, son.
A2  It read fine last winter. It reads fine now. That's the whole trick.
A3  Your mother used to say the same and she was right, sir.
B1  Check the meter, primo. It doesn't read, it just sits there.
B2  We say meter. Nobody alive has seen it do anything.
B3  My mum said the same. She was right about most of it.
# Gen 1 says sir and son and believes the meter works. Gen 3 kept the word and
# lost the thing. Neither one changes how often they ask or how long they run.

### PAIR 4 -- articulate and inarticulate, and neither is stupid   draft:true
# From the Shakespeare-storyteller round: a small vocabulary is not a small mind,
# and the interesting characters are the MISMATCHED pairs.
A1  So the pressure drops, the main stops pushing, and every joint on this street
    turns into a mouth. That's your answer.
A2  I can draw it for you if that's easier.
A3  I'd want to be wrong about this. I'm not.
B1  It's. The water goes back. Backward, in the pipe.
B2  I can't say it right. Come look.
B3  Barrel out back. Look in the barrel.
# A has every word and no force. B has no words and is right, and knows he is
# right, and cannot make the sentence. Both are competent. One is listened to.

## HOW TO USE THIS FILE
Cover the ban lines. Read three lines. If you cannot tell A from B without them,
the pair failed and the fix is a sharper ban, never a fancier sentence.
