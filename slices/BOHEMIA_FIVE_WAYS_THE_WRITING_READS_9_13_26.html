FIVE WAYS THE WRITING READS

The cards that talk to you have their own lettering now, separate from the buttons and separate from the phone. Option A is what is in the game now. Tap a thumb. 9/13
THE WRITING WAS IN TYPEWRITER LETTERING, where every letter takes up the same amount of room, so an i is as fat as an m. That is the number one thing on the list of what makes a screen look machine-made, and your day card and your talking card had thirty of those between them. Nobody writes on a grid.

SO THEY GOT THEIR OWN LETTERING, cut from the same dots as the phone but set loose: capitals five dots wide, small letters four, an i is one. And its dots TOUCH, where the phone's dots have gaps, because the phone is made of little lamps and writing is made of ink. Same hand, two different things. The same sentence now fits on one line where it used to take two.

AND THE TEST CAUGHT ME TWICE IN TEN MINUTES. First: the little X that closes every card was a symbol I had not drawn, so that one mark was in a borrowed typeface, on every panel in the game. Second, and worse: the test that checks the letters are different widths was measuring the WRONG TYPEFACE, so it would have passed no matter what I did. I only found that by deliberately breaking the thing and watching the test report the exact same numbers.
