THE DECK, AND A FLIP IS A TAPE CHANGING

SOUNDS · 9/27 · the two you killed, rebuilt out of the machine itself
YOU KILLED BOTH OF THESE AND YOU WERE RIGHT. You said the tape "all sounded like sand" and the flip was "kinda dogshit". Both were built out of hiss with a machine's name on them.

A tape deck is not made of hiss. It is plastic being knocked, and wheels turning. So these are built out of the wheels: the standard says the tape moves 4.7625 cm every second, and that tells you how fast every wheel in the machine turns. Nothing here is a number I picked.

And here is the part that makes it sound tired instead of broken: the wobble gets SLOWER as the side plays, because the tape piling up on the reel makes the reel fatter. Same machine, slower breath. You can hear that below.
ITEM ONE · THE DECK · press play and a song comes up

A. THE WHOLE DECK

The lever goes over, the head and the roller arrive against the tape 45 ms later, and then the song slides up to speed instead of just starting. That slide is what a real deck does, and it is the thing a whoosh was standing in for. This is the realistic one, so it leads.

B. JUST THE MACHINE

The same two knocks with no song at all, for when nothing is playing. This is the plastic shell ringing its own notes, which is what a cassette actually sounds like when you handle one.

C. A WORN-OUT DECK

Same machine, flat spot on the roller. It breathes harder and faster. Worth knowing: this one is outside the range a cassette is supposed to hold, which is exactly what worn means.

AND THE WOBBLE SLOWING DOWN ACROSS A SIDE

Not an option, just proof. Same deck, start of the side and end of it. The second one breathes slower because the reel is fatter.
ITEM TWO · THE FLIP · one tap, hundreds of times
THE FLIP IS ONE TAP AND ALWAYS THERE, so you will hear it hundreds of times. That means the way it fails is not "too quiet", it is "I am sick of it". So it fits inside one beat, there is no riser, no whoosh, no sting. It is a tape changing, which is what the law already said a flip is.

And it is honest about what it is not: a real tape change takes several seconds. This is the part of one you actually hear, the shell seating and the machine taking it.

A. THE SHELL SEATS AND THE MACHINE TAKES IT

Two knocks, 155 ms apart, done inside one beat. This is the one that ships.

B. ONE KNOCK

Just the shell going in. Shorter and plainer. One knock reads as a button being pressed; two read as a machine doing something.

C. THE OLD ONE COMES OUT FIRST

Three knocks: the old tape out, the new one in, the machine taking it. It is the fullest of the three and it is the only one that does not fit inside a beat, which is said out loud rather than trimmed to look like it does.
