THE MARK ON THE WALL

Fourth sign, and the first one with no words on it and no post holding it up. Just paint, on somebody else's wall. 9/22
THE GROUND ALREADY KNOWS WHO CAME PAST, WHICH WAY THEY WENT, AND HOW LONG AGO. It has known since the tracks shipped. The line the game says out loud throws away the most useful part of it, which is which way they were going. A crew heading out is a problem forming. A crew heading back has already done whatever it did. By the middle of the day, two out of three sets of prints in this valley are on the way back and nothing tells you.

1. WHAT IT IS

A crew paints a mark at a crossing when they work it. Not a sign on a post: paint, straight onto whatever wall was already there. This is real, it is how people have always done this, and it is the one thing in the set with no words on it at all, so nobody has to be able to read.
The grammar is three things and no more. The bar is the crew, in their colour. The point on the end is the way they went. The strokes under it are how many came through. What each crew's own emblem looks like is yours, not mine, so there are no invented logos in here: this is the shared part that works for all fourteen.

2. THE FOUR STATES

A footprint fades in a day. Paint does not. That is the whole reason this exists: the prints are gone by morning and the mark is still there, so the wall is the valley's memory. The crossed-out one is the interesting one.

3. AND I NEARLY REPORTED A BUG THAT WAS NOT ONE

I swept all 9,216 cells looking for prints and found fourteen, every one of them zero steps old. That reads exactly like a broken feature. It is not. So the first hour of the game has a blank valley, and that is not a defect either, it is morning. But it does mean a mark that only reads live prints would show you nothing in the first five minutes, which is why the paint is the point. Marks from before you woke up are already on the walls.
Nothing here is in the game yet. The mark grammar is a draft and the emblems are reserved for you. Every number on this page came out of the valley, not out of my head.
