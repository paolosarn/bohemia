WHERE THE SAND IS

The song that plays on the map by day. Tap A, then B, then C. Same notes; A and B at the same loudness.
Every one of your 142 songs, played through the game's own sound, measured:
the squeeze at the end turns the typical song down 0.07 dB while it plays (a squeeze you can hear starts around 3 dB), and it only passes 3 dB in the first half second as a song starts.
What it really does is make them 8.0 dB louder.
One song is the exception: THE GAPS IN THE HYMNAL comes in 22 dB louder than the speaker can play, and the squeeze crushes it 19 dB, 96 percent of the time. That one is squeezed sand.
The hiss: 119 of 142 songs carry a white-noise layer, about 86 bursts in 32 seconds. Taking only that out of this song drops its top end by 17 dB while its loudness does not move (0.0 dB).
The voices: 25 percent of every note is a sawtooth, a sound an 8-bit machine cannot make.
