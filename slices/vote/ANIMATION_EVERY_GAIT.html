SIX MORE WALKS HAD THE SAME LIMP

Your call: the character is tiny again, one cell, one step per cell.

Last round I found the walk was drawing three pictures instead of four, because a step has two middles and the code drew them as the same picture. Your left leg passing forward and your right leg passing forward are not the same thing.

So I asked that question of every other way this guy moves. Nine more walks. Six of them had it, and four were broken in every single direction:

tired walking, swagger, walking with the gun out — all 8 directions the same picture twice. Sneaking 7 of 8. Wandering 5 of 8. Shoving something 4 of 8. Forty of forty-eight. Drunk, sprinting and scrambling were already fine, because each of those carries a second wobble on a different clock, and that is what saved them.

Fixed the same way, but the foot clears a different amount for each walk, because they are different walks: a crouch and a shove keep the foot down, a stroll lifts it, a swagger is meant to be seen.

And it was in the facing-you version too, at the source: one shared piece of code every walk runs through. Facing you, a passing leg reads as the knee coming at the camera.

Left is what it was, right is now. Eight frames, on the beat, and every picture here is the real one-cell sprite, blown up 4x so you can see it. The tiny one on the right is his true size.

One number worth knowing: how much of his body actually changes between those two middles, at one cell. It was zero for all six. It is now 5% to 13%, and the machine check refuses anything under that, so this cannot quietly shrink back.

I broke the coat doing it and the coat's own checker caught me. The first lift I tried made the trenchcoat jump 26% of itself in one frame. I measured four sizes of lift and took the biggest one the coat does not notice at all. That is why the facing-you lift is the number it is.
