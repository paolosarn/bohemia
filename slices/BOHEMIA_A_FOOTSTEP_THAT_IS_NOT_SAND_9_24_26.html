A FOOTSTEP THAT IS NOT SAND

SOUNDS · 9/24 · 120 BPM · you were right, here is the fix
YOU SAID IT ALL SOUNDED LIKE SAND AND YOU WERE RIGHT. Every sound I made started with a noise generator, and noise with the top cut off is exactly what sand sounds like. There is not one noise generator in B or C. They are built out of the physics of a heel hitting a slab, and the numbers under them come from concrete and asphalt, not from me picking.

Play A first. It is the one you called sand. Then B.

A. THE ONE YOU CALLED SAND

This is the old footstep, unchanged, so you can hear what you were complaining about right next to the new one. It is noise with the top rolled off, and measuring it properly this round found that nearly a third of it sits above the limit it was supposed to stop at. That leak is the sand.

B. CONCRETE, THE SIDEWALK

A heel hitting a 100 mm concrete slab. The click is the contact itself, which lasts less than half a millisecond because concrete is stiff, and that short contact is what makes it bright. Under it the slab rings at its own pitch, worked out from concrete's published stiffness and weight. Then a couple of dozen grains of grit crush under the heel, each one its own tiny impact, which is where the grain comes from instead of from hiss. And the foot goes flat 90 milliseconds later, because that is what a walk does.

C. ASPHALT, THE ROAD

The same model on a road. Asphalt is about ten times softer than concrete and far deader, so the contact lasts longer, the top goes with it, and the road thuds where the sidewalk rings. You are hearing the material, not a setting. This is the ear's half of the streets not reading right.
