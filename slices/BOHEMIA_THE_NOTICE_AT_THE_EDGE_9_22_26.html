THE NOTICE AT THE EDGE

The board a crew nails up where their ground starts. Third sign, and the first one somebody alive made. 9/22
THE VALLEY HAS THREE COMPLETELY DIFFERENT RENTS AND NOBODY HAS EVER TOLD YOU. I read them straight off the game. On a fortress you pay for every single block you walk on. In a town you get one block in three for free. In a camp you get two in three for free. Same valley, same night, three different deals, and which one you are standing in is invisible.

And you start the game on the worst one. You wake up on Mob ground, which is a fortress, which charges for every block. They also hold more of the valley than anyone else.

1. THE THREE DEALS, ON THE BOARD THAT SAYS THEM

Every number on these is read out of the game, not written by me. The bottom line is the part that changes everything and it comes straight from the same reading: how many blocks you get for nothing.

2. CLOSE UP

Two stakes and a plank. This is the only sign in the set that somebody alive made. The motel sign and the water authority are leftovers from before, printed and perfect and dead. This one got made last month, by hand, with a brush, and nailed over whatever was there. That is the difference you should be able to see from across a street: the printed signs are the old world, the painted ones are who runs it now.

3. WHO CHARGES WHAT, AND HOW MUCH GROUND THEY HOLD

Straight off the valley. Five crews run fortresses and charge for everything. Four run towns. Five run camps and are the cheapest ground in the valley to walk across.

4. WHY THIS IS THE THIRD SIGN

The first two signs tell you about the dead world. This one tells you something you can act on tonight: walk two streets over and it is cheaper. That is a real decision, made out of a rule the game already runs, and right now there is no way for a player to learn it except by losing batteries and guessing.
Nothing here is in the game yet. The words are drafts. The numbers are not: they come out of the same reading the nightfall bill uses, so a board and a bill can never disagree.
