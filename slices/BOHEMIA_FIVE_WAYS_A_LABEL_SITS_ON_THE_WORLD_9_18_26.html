FIVE WAYS A LABEL SITS ON THE WORLD

The little hint that floats over the street, and the line that teaches you to walk. They sit on top of the moving world, so they need an edge of some kind. These are the real things at the real size, on ground the colour of the street. 9/18
I COUNTED THE WRONG THING FOR EIGHT ROUNDS. The tool that counts this stuff reads the code, and the code said there were 46 thin grey lines left on the street screen. I measured what the screen is actually drawing: three. Almost all the rest were lines that something else already switches off, or lines inside panels that are closed.

THOSE THREE ARE NOW ZERO. The hint, the teaching line, and the bottom of the top bar. A is what they look like now. B is what they looked like before.

A. A SMALL PLATE (in the game now)

walking your own block.
WHAT IT IS. No line at all. Light on the top edge, shadow under the bottom, so it sits on the street like a thing rather than a box drawn on it.
WHAT IT COSTS. Nothing. Same look as every button in the game.

B. THE OLD ONE, FOR COMPARISON

walking your own block.
WHAT IT IS. A thin grey line around a rounded box.
WHAT IT COSTS. It is the look the research says people spot as machine made, floating over your world.

C. PAINTED ON THE GROUND

walking your own block.
WHAT IT IS. No plate. The words are stencilled onto the street itself.
WHAT IT COSTS. Hardest to read, and it gets worse the busier the ground is. On a dark tile it could vanish.

D. A LITTLE SCREEN

walking your own block.
WHAT IT IS. A lit readout, like a meter bolted to something.
WHAT IT COSTS. Green light in the middle of a dusty brown world. It also says "machine" when the hint is really just a thought.

E. TAPE ON THE GLASS

walking your own block.
WHAT IT IS. A strip of tape stuck slightly crooked, like somebody labelled the screen by hand.
WHAT IT COSTS. Full of character, and loud. Fine for one label, tiring if the game uses five.
