### MP.L01  THE GRIME CROSSES THE SEAM, AND THAT IS WHY IT IS ONE PLACE
LENS: consistency (Hodent) - the mechanism behind "it reads as one room"
SCREEN: the world
WHAT: Klubnika on texturing: he "added dirty and grimy leaks to every corner,
  which blends everything together rather than having different objects." One
  filth pass laid over everything, indifferent to where one object stops and the
  next begins.
WHY: A wall, a floor and a machine that share a stain are one building. Three
  surfaces each perfectly textured that share nothing are three assets. The dirt,
  not the palette, is what makes a room read as a place.
PORT: REFUSE, FOR THE INTERFACE. IT IS THE WORLD'S, AND IT ALWAYS WAS.
BECAUSE: He killed all three dirt options on the UI page on 8/27, and that is not
  a contradiction of this finding, it is a correction of where I aimed it.
  Klubnika's grime unifies THE ROOM YOU ARE STANDING IN. I took a world technique
  and put it on a menu, which is the one surface in the game that is NOT part of
  the room. The interface is not another object in the scene, it is the sheet of
  instructions laid over the scene, and dirtying it makes it look like a texture
  that failed rather than a thing that has been used. THE FINDING IS GOOD AND IT
  BELONGS TO THE WORLD LANE, WHERE THE GRIME SHEET IS ALREADY WIRED AT HIS RULED
  0.30. It does not belong on his buttons and he was right to say so.

### MP.L02  EVERYTHING IS THICK
LENS: form follows function (Hodent) - clarity (Hodent)
SCREEN: the table, the gun, the terminal
WHAT: The interface objects are deliberately CHUNKY. Heavy metal shapes, thick
  edges, industrial smoke, club light reflecting off the gun. Nothing in his work
  is a hairline.
WHY: A thick thing reads at a glance and reads in bad light. It also reads as
  MADE, out of steel, by somebody who was not thinking about elegance.
PORT: TAKE, AND HE ALREADY TOOK IT
BECAUSE: This is the fork he answered on 8/27. He said yes to the HEAVY line,
  2px at 3.78 to 1 against the panel, and no to the hairline that measured 1.22.
  His vote and Klubnika's steel are the same instinct, and the number now says so.

### MP.L03  THE TEXTURES ARE PHOTOGRAPHS OF REAL THINGS
LENS: clarity (Hodent) - the geometry axis (Fagerholt & Lorentzon)
SCREEN: every surface
WHAT: He photographs his own textures, mainly of electronics, and urban-explores
  abandoned factories for reference. The Volta electrical component facility in
  Tallinn, which he went back to "a dozen times".
WHY: Photographic surfaces are uncorrelated at the finest scale, which is a thing
  you can measure. It is why his rooms feel like rooms instead of like materials.
PORT: REFUSE
BECAUSE: Bohemia is 44px three-quarter pixel art on a phone and a photograph
  pasted into that is mush. But the finding explains something already measured
  here: his bought tile library reads edge 18.4 and grain 61% where painted art
  reads 9.4 and 26%, and the reason is that photographs ARE uncorrelated at the
  pixel. HIM LOVING THIS LOOK AND HIM BUYING THAT LIBRARY ARE THE SAME TASTE
  POINTING AT THE SAME PHYSICAL PROPERTY. The technique cannot come. The
  measurement it explains is already in the world lane's hands.

### MP.L04  THE ROOM IS MADE OF THREE MATERIALS AND NO MORE
LENS: consistency (Hodent)
SCREEN: every screen
WHAT: Concrete, steel, and a sick electric light. His environments are brutalist
  and the palette is what the building is physically made of, not a colour scheme
  chosen on top of it.
WHY: When the palette IS the material list, nothing on screen can be off-model,
  because every colour has a reason you could point at in the world.
PORT: ADAPT
BECAUSE: Bohemia's world is desert tan and gold light, and LIGHT = TERRITORY says
  the gold means somebody is keeping a lamp on. So our material list is already
  written: sun-bleached tan, dead concrete, and the gold of the few lights left.
  What ports is the discipline, not the colours: the interface may only use what
  the valley is made of, plus the one cold that the valley does NOT have, which
  is what he chose on 8/27 for exactly this reason.

### MP.R01  TELL THEM THE COUNT. HIDE ONLY THE ORDER.
LENS: signs and feedback (Hodent) - minimum workload (Hodent)
SCREEN: the table
WHAT: Buckshot Roulette loads the shotgun in front of you and TELLS YOU EXACTLY
  how many live rounds and how many blanks are going in. What it never tells you
  is the ORDER. Two live and three blanks, and no idea which one is next.
WHY: THIS IS THE BEST INFORMATION DESIGN IN EITHER GAME IN THIS BOOK. Complete
  information about the composition, zero information about the sequence. Nothing
  is hidden to create difficulty, so the player never feels cheated, and yet every
  single pull is a real decision. Hiding the count would make it a coin flip.
  Showing the order would make it arithmetic. He hides exactly one thing.
PORT: TAKE, AND IT IS THE PAIR TO FFX.R01
BECAUSE: Final Fantasy 10 showed us DO THE ARITHMETIC AND SHOW THE ANSWER.
  Machine Party shows us WHICH ONE THING TO WITHHOLD. Together they are a rule
  Bohemia can hold every screen against: the player should know everything about
  WHAT IS IN THE ROOM and nothing about WHICH ORDER IT COMES IN. A district that
  tells you it holds four dogs and a man with a pipe, without telling you what is
  around the next corner, is Valheim-style danger by place done the honest way,
  and it is the exact opposite of a health bar that hides its own number.

### MP.R02  YOUR LIFE IS A COUNT OF CHARGES, NOT A NUMBER
LENS: form follows function (Hodent) - diegetic (Fagerholt & Lorentzon)
SCREEN: the table
WHAT: Health in Buckshot Roulette is a DEFIBRILLATOR with charges, shown as
  lightning bolts. Two, three or four. Take a live round to the head and you spend
  a charge. There is no bar and no percentage.
WHY: A small countable thing is read instantly and felt physically. Nobody has to
  translate 62% into "how many more mistakes do I get". Three bolts means three
  mistakes, and the answer arrives before you have finished looking.
PORT: TAKE
BECAUSE: NO DAMAGE BEFORE THE DIAL is our law, so the numbers wait, but the SHAPE
  is free and it is a decision the health readout has to make anyway. A countable
  charge also survives the thumb rule: three big marks read at a glance in the
  corner of a phone, where a thin bar with a number in it does not.

### MP.R03  THERE IS NO HUD, BECAUSE THE MACHINE IS ALREADY IN FRONT OF YOU
LENS: non-diegetic minimisation (Fagerholt & Lorentzon) - diegetic (same)
SCREEN: every screen
WHAT: Buckshot Roulette is described as "diegetic systems and minimal UI", and
  s.p.l.i.t goes further: the entire game takes place inside a working terminal,
  with no HUD elements at all to break the illusion of sitting at the machine.
WHY: He can delete the HUD because a first-person camera puts a real object at
  arm's length, and the object carries the information on its own face.
PORT: REFUSE, AND IT IS THE SECOND TIME THIS BOOK HAS SAID THIS
BECAUSE: Final Fantasy 10 could delete its HUD because it is a corridor
  (FFX.W03). Klubnika can delete his because the machine is at arm's length in
  first person. BOHEMIA HAS NEITHER EXCUSE. We are three-quarter view at 44px in
  an open valley of 27 districts, and he has already reported walking through it
  lost. Two of the greatest interfaces we have studied both have almost no
  interface, AND BOTH OF THEM PAID FOR IT WITH SOMETHING WE DO NOT HAVE. That is
  not a coincidence to admire, it is a bill to notice. We earn a quiet screen with
  landmarks and light, or we do not get one.

### MP.R04  NOTHING WAITS
LENS: minimum workload (Hodent) - Pinelle/Wong/Stach, built from what players
  actually complain about
SCREEN: between everything
WHAT: Klubnika's stated design rule for Machine Party: "no fluff, no waiting, just
  back to back violent chaos." He says they cut down the dialogue, the transitions,
  and the board-game bits that minigame collections usually carry.
WHY: The connective tissue is where players quit, and it is invisible to the person
  who built it because they are proud of it.
PORT: ADAPT
BECAUSE: Bohemia is a hundred hours and a single character, so it cannot be back
  to back chaos and should not try. What ports is the AUDIT: every screen between
  him and the next decision is on trial. Measured already, the run takes 24.2
  seconds to first play on throttled 4G and he told us the game "kinda runs like
  shit". That is the same complaint in his words, and it is a demo blocker.

### MP.D01  THE MACHINE IS THE INTERFACE
LENS: diegetic (Fagerholt & Lorentzon) - form follows function (Hodent)
SCREEN: every screen
WHAT: Across his whole catalogue the interface is the thing in front of you:
  terminals, keys, switches, panels. s.p.l.i.t is "raw keyboard-driven
  interactions, terminal-based puzzles". The tension comes from OPERATING A
  MACHINE, not from reading a HUD laid over one.
WHY: A machine has weight, sequence and consequence. A menu has none of those; it
  has options. Operating something is dramatic in a way choosing something is not.
PORT: REFUSE, IN THE LETTER. THIS IS THE ONE PEOPLE WILL TRY TO COPY AND CANNOT.
BECAUSE: There is no "in front of you" in a three-quarter top-down game on a
  phone. The camera looks DOWN at a character; it never looks AT an object at arm's
  length, so a diegetic terminal has nowhere to be. Anyone who reads "Final Fantasy
  10 meets Machine Party" and starts drawing a first-person control panel has
  copied the thing that cannot come and skipped the four that can. What survives
  the port is one sentence of it, and it is MP.D02 and MP.D03 below.

### MP.D02  DESIGNED FOR A PURPOSE, WITH NO THOUGHT FOR THE USER
LENS: form follows function (Hodent), inverted on purpose
SCREEN: every object
WHAT: On the machines in Buckshot Roulette: everything is built to do one specific
  job and nothing about it was shaped for a human hand.
WHY: It is a rule you can hold an object against and get a straight yes or no,
  which almost no aesthetic note manages. It is also the whole difference between
  industrial-LOOKING and industrial.
PORT: TAKE
BECAUSE: Post-economic-apocalypse Las Vegas is full of machinery that outlived the
  people it was for, which is the same sentence from the other end. Hold every
  object in the valley against it: was this shaped for a hand, or shaped for a job?
  A parking meter, a fare box, a slot machine and a fuel pump all answer the second
  way, and all four are already in the districts. It is a test the world lane can
  run today without cooking anything.

### MP.D03  YOU OPERATE IT, YOU DO NOT COMMAND IT
LENS: user control and flexibility (Hodent) - error prevention (Hodent)
SCREEN: the table
WHAT: You do not select "shoot dealer" off a list. You pick the gun up, you point
  it, and you pull. The verb is carried out with your hands in stages, and each
  stage is a place you could still stop.
WHY: Stages make the decision feel like yours. A one-tap command from a menu is
  over before it is felt.
PORT: ADAPT
BECAUSE: Final Fantasy 10 already gave us the phone-sized version of this in
  FFX.D02: pick the verb, THEN pick the target, and back out at any point. That is
  operating in two stages with a way to stop, on a touchscreen, and the action
  button never changes what it means (UI-2). Klubnika's version is why that shape
  is worth keeping rather than collapsing into one tap for speed.

### MP.W01  NOWHERE TO REST
LENS: the fiction axis (Fagerholt & Lorentzon)
SCREEN: the world
WHAT: Brutalism is his named environment inspiration, and the stated reason is
  behavioural rather than visual: the player never really feels like they can rest
  anywhere. His work is described as taking after the relentlessness of military
  organisations.
WHY: A level rule, not a texture rule. The building refuses you a corner to sit in,
  and that does more work than any amount of grime.
PORT: TAKE
BECAUSE: It is the exact counterweight to FFX.W01, the save point you walk to.
  Bohemia needs BOTH: a valley where almost nowhere is safe, and a small number of
  places that are, which you can see from a distance because somebody is keeping
  the lights on there. Nowhere to rest is what makes a save sphere mean something.
  Together they are danger by place and light equals territory, already both law.

### MP.W02  THE SOUND IS THE OTHER HALF, NOT A LAYER ON TOP
LENS: signs and feedback (Hodent) - meta (Fagerholt & Lorentzon)
SCREEN: every screen
WHAT: Reviewers of Machine Party keep crediting the audio for the atmosphere, and
  Alex Peipman's score is called out for a grimy, oppressive consistency that
  deliberately contrasts with how silly some of the minigames are. Klubnika scores
  most of his own work.
WHY: In this family of games the industrial audio is not decoration over the art
  direction, it is a second channel carrying the same claim.
PORT: ADAPT, AND IT IS THE SOUND LANE'S, NOT MINE
BECAUSE: Bohemia has a rack of hundreds of cooked instruments and a 120 BPM law
  that everything quantises to, so the second channel exists and is further along
  than the first. But this lane owns ui_deny and the shared rule underneath it: a
  sound may be the best copy of a message and never the only copy. Klubnika can
  lean on audio because he is not shipping to a phone on silent in a bright room.

### MP.W03  FINAL FANTASY 10 MEETS MACHINE PARTY, AND THE SEAM IS THE POINT
LENS: consistency (Hodent) - the fiction axis (Fagerholt & Lorentzon)
SCREEN: all of them
WHAT: His own words on 8/27, and it is the first time anybody has named what the
  Bohemia look actually is. Two games at opposite ends of the same book: one is a
  readable list of numbers laid over a soft tropical world, the other is a filthy
  steel object with almost no numbers on it at all.
WHY: They are not opposites, they are two halves of one answer. Final Fantasy 10
  is right about INFORMATION: do the arithmetic for the player and show the answer.
  Machine Party is right about SUBSTANCE: the thing carrying the information should
  look like it was welded, not designed. What kills most interfaces is having one
  without the other, either a beautiful object you cannot read or a clean readout
  with no character in it at all.
PORT: TAKE. THIS IS THE THESIS OF THE LANE NOW.
BECAUSE: It is already what his 8/27 verdict describes and he may not have noticed
  he was describing it. THE CUT CORNER is a stamped metal tag, which is Machine
  Party. THE HEAVY LINE is a thing that was welded rather than drawn, which is
  Machine Party. GOLD AND COLD is two colours that MEAN two things, you and the
  machine, which is Final Fantasy 10's discipline exactly. ALL TYPEWRITER WIDTH is
  a readout on a device, which is both at once. And the dirt died because grime
  belongs to the room and not to the readout, which is the seam holding. THE LOOK
  HE PICKED IS ALREADY THE SENTENCE HE SAID, and the next thing built goes on this
  test: is it readable like Final Fantasy 10, and is it made of something like
  Machine Party. If it fails either half it is not Bohemia.
