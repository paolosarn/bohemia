THREE REAL BUGS THE CROWD BOARD FOUND, ALL INVISIBLE TO THE GREEN GATES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
None of these was caught by a gate. All three were caught by LOOKING at twelve
people, which is the entire argument for putting a mechanism on a surface. The
third one is a false claim I made about the codebase and repeated in four places.

1. THE DIALS WERE CORRELATED. Two of the first twelve citizens came out with
   BYTE-IDENTICAL bodies, and arms/shoulders/armLength repeated across half the
   board. Cause: plain FNV-1a ends on `h = (h ^ lastByte) * prime`, and the LOW
   bits of a product depend only on the LOW bits of its inputs -- so ids that
   differ only in their last character land in a small neighbourhood down there.
   Then `% 100000` reads almost entirely low bits and hands that correlation
   straight to the dials. Real ids are SEQUENTIAL (npc-0, npc-1, npc-2 ...),
   which is exactly the worst case.

   MEASURED, 64 sequential ids, distinct values per dial:
       BEFORE   height 31  belly 29  arms 24  shoulders 27  armLen 24  hips 17
       AFTER    height 59  belly 64  arms 60  shoulders 59  armLen 63  hips 60
   (the residual is 3-decimal rounding, which is correct.) 2000 sequential ids
   now give 2000 distinct bodies. Fix: a murmur3 finalizer -- three xor-shifts
   and two multiplies that push every input bit into every output bit.

   WHY THE GATE MISSED IT: it checked "200 people give 150+ distinct bodies" and
   read 188 the whole time. Distinct-as-a-TUPLE hides a dial that barely moves --
   six dials only have to disagree in ONE place to count as distinct. The gate
   now measures EVERY DIAL SEPARATELY on SEQUENTIAL ids.

2. THE ENGINE SYNC CHECK WAS A PREFIX CHECK. It asserted the inlined copy
   matched the engine module by comparing the FIRST 400 CHARACTERS -- which is
   the module's header comment. So the hash fix landed at the BOTTOM of the
   file, the alpha kept the old broken copy, and the sync check stayed green.
   A prefix comparison is not a comparison. It now compares all 6,974 bytes,
   and tools/bohemia_personlook_sync.py does the re-inline so it is never a
   hand edit again.

3. THE THIRD BUG WAS MINE, AND IT WAS A FALSE CLAIM ABOUT THE CODEBASE.
   BOH_PERSONLOOK's header stated: "Nothing in the repo varies a person's
   appearance -- no seed, no per-agent body, no per-agent outfit." That went into
   the module, the gate header, the commit message and the write-up. It is false.

   NPCFactory has been in engine/bohemia_engine.js SINCE 7/2/26. It is seeded and
   deterministic, it is wired into the RUN, the SLICE and the CHARACTER tab's
   RANDOM CITIZEN button, and it varies SKIN TONE (Paolo's 9 locked ramps) and
   HAIR COLOUR. Measured on 8 ids: 6 distinct skin tones, 5 distinct hair colours.
   It has been working the entire time.

   THE REAL GAP was narrower, and it was two halves of one person:
     NPCFactory      skin + hair. Cannot vary body (it predates the dials) and
                     cannot vary clothes -- it reads only PD.layers, which holds
                     exactly ONE option per slot.
     BOH_PERSONLOOK  body + clothes. Does not touch skin or hair.
   Neither knew the other existed, so nothing ever handed them the same id.

   AND THE FALSE CLAIM COST SOMETHING REAL. Because the board used only
   PERSONLOOK, its twelve citizens shared one skin tone and one hair colour, and
   that got written up as "a CONTENT gap -- fixing it means cooking new art,
   which is his call." It was not a content gap. It was me bypassing a working
   system, and it was one green-lit ask away from spending his judgement on art
   nobody needed.

   THE FIX: the board asks NPC_FACTORY for the SAME id it asks PERSONLOOK for.
   One id, one whole person. No new mechanism for skin or hair -- ENGINE SYNC
   LAW, the module that already owns those fields keeps owning them. Zero new
   art. The board went from 1 head shared by twelve to 12/12 distinct heads.

   WHY NO GATE CAUGHT IT: the crowd gate measured a whole-sprite signature and
   read 12/12 distinct, because bodies and clothes differ enough to mask a region
   that never changes at all. It now hashes the HEAD REGION SEPARATELY. A metric
   that averages over the whole sprite cannot see a constant sub-region.

THE BUG CLASS THIS WAS BUILT AGAINST
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Earlier the same day, SHUFFLE FIT passed every test written for it and changed
nothing on screen. Root cause: HD_CACHE keys off frameLookHash, and G_WORN was
not in the hash -- so putting on a red shirt changed the frame the renderer
WOULD build and never changed the key. The cache served the old body forever.
Every probe passed because every probe called buildFrame directly, which is a
SIDE DOOR around the cache. The surface he taps goes through drawChar.

So: the crowd calls drawChar and never buildFrame, and crowd_gate.js asserts
that statically as well as reading pixels off the live canvases in a real
browser. VERIFY ON THE REAL SURFACE (7/18) is the whole reason this gate is
shaped the way it is.

THE GATES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PERSON LOOK (gates/personlook_gate.js, 17 checks) — the function:
  same id -> same person, twice and after 1000 other lookups; no Math.random;
  shuffling the garment pool does not restyle anyone (this one CAUGHT A REAL
  BUG: the returned key order followed the input array's order, so a person
  "changed" whenever GARMENTS was reordered -- invisible to the eye, fatal to
  any save comparison); 200 people give 188 distinct bodies; a body is ONLY the
  six known dials; every dial inside the declared range; no sex term in the
  code; never varies skin tone; holds no garment names; only canon garments are
  ever worn; not everyone owns a coat; byte-identical to the engine module.

THE CROWD (gates/crowd_gate.js) — the pixels, in a real browser:
  twelve canvases, none blank, none identical; redrawing gives byte-identical
  pixels (no dice in the render path); NEW CROWD brings strangers; and his own
  look and his own canvas come back untouched after the board borrows them.

A note on the person-look gate's first run: three word-checks failed against
the module's own header, because that header QUOTES Paolo about the woman-belly
ruling, NAMES skin tone as a thing it refuses to touch, and says "no
Math.random". Documenting a rule is not breaking it. The gate now strips
comments and reads CODE. A checker that cannot tell a rule from its description
is the broken one.
