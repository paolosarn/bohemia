## THE RULING

1. **A NAME IS NOT GIVEN. IT IS ASKED FOR.** No person in Bohemia arrives with
   their name attached. You walk up to somebody and they are THE SCAVENGER until
   you ask, and asking is a thing you actually do in the conversation.
2. **THE GAME REMEMBERS THAT YOU ASKED.** "Once you ask their name, if you see
   them again, then they would be named." The name is not a one-time reveal in a
   text box; it replaces what they are called, everywhere, permanently, across
   saves. This is the part he called "really cool" and it is the point of the
   whole mechanic.
3. **THE EXCEPTION IS PEOPLE YOU ALREADY KNOW.** Main-quest people and backstory
   people are named from the first frame, because you have known them your whole
   life. "People that you're personally assigned to know story wise." WHO THEY ARE
   IS HIS — the table ships EMPTY.
4. **BEFORE YOU ASK, THEY ARE GENERIC.** "It could be generic, you know."
   The role word the world already uses (WORKER / SCAVENGER / KEEPER / WATCH).
   He offered "faction stuff" as an alternative flavour — NOT TAKEN, because
   factions are OFF by the BUILD THE WORLD ruling of the same day.
5. **THE REASON, in his words:** "I hate how in other games you know everyone's
   name off the bat and I think it's complete bullshit." This is the design
   principle, not a footnote: knowing a name is a small piece of earned intimacy,
   and a game that hands you three hundred of them for free has spent something it
   never charged you for.

## WHAT THIS IS, UNDERNEATH (the life lesson, never preached)

Nobody owes you their name. You get it by walking up to a person and asking, and
then you are carrying something about them that you did not have before. The game
never says this. It just never gives you a name you did not ask for.
