WHAT A RIVET IS AND WHY IT COULD NOT EXIST BEFORE
--------------------------------------------------
Scavenged armour is bolted together. A rivet is ONE pixel. At 56 a chest plate is three
or four rows tall and a stud row inside it would have been half the plate, so there was
nothing to draw. At 112 there is room, and hardware is what makes a plate read as bolted
metal rather than a painted rectangle.

ONE PASS, NOT FOURTEEN EDITS. genGear has fourteen kinds and each builds its own output
and returns it, so the rivets go on AT THE RETURN -- fifteen call sites rewritten
mechanically, and a new kind gets them for free instead of being forgotten. genBag got
the same treatment on its five.
WHERE THEY GO: the top and bottom row of each column's own run, every other cell across,
and only on runs at least three cells tall so a thin strap does not become a dotted line.
NOTHING ANY KIND DRAWS IS MOVED OR THINNED -- a rivet RECOLOURS a pixel the plate already
owned. S>1 only, so the 56 wardrobe is byte-identical and its 1,744 pinned hashes hold.

*** THE PICTURE LIED, AGAIN, AND DIFFERENTLY THIS TIME ***
------------------------------------------------------------
The first hardware picture had two rows -- CHEST PLATE and KNEEPADS -- showing a body
wearing nothing at all, before AND after, looking exactly like a real comparison where
the change did nothing. THE GARMENT NAMES WERE WRONG. I wrote 'SCRAP CHESTPLATE'; the
canon name is 'SCRAP CHEST PLATE'. A name that is not in the catalogue draws NOTHING, and
nothing beside nothing is a very convincing null result.

That is the SECOND picture in two days to lie in a different way (yesterday's had both
columns identical because genAcc never read the before/after flag). The failure mode is
the same underneath: A TOOL THAT CANNOT DISTINGUISH "NO CHANGE" FROM "NOTHING RAN" WILL
ALWAYS REPORT THE COMFORTABLE ONE. The tool now prints NOT IN THE CATALOGUE in red beside
any row whose garment does not exist, so the absence is visible instead of invisible.
The only reason either was caught is LOOKING AT THE PICTURE rather than trusting that it
ran clean.
