# BOHEMIA COORDINATOR SCHOOL, ROUND 1 OF 10: THE PRODUCER (10/10/26)

## THE QUESTION
How do the best small and mid-size studios run production day to day, and what does a great producer do every day and every week? The coordinator is Bohemia's producer: one chat that turns Paolo's sentences into jobs, sweeps 26 autonomous lanes, keeps the rules and runs the vote tab. This round studied Overhype, Supergiant, Ludeon, ConcernedApe, Klei and Hinterland, plus Mark Cerny's Method, Clinton Keith's Agile Game Development and the GDC Producer Bootcamp talks. Research note: page fetches were blocked from this container, so each finding rests on the search-result text of the cited page; quotes appear only where the result gave the exact words.

## WHAT I FOUND

1. **Production is triage, nothing else.** Dallas Dickinson (BioWare Austin), GDC 2012 Producer Bootcamp, "Triage: The Only Thing Production Does": the job differs by studio "but it all comes down to one thing: triage and prioritization"; the producer must "cut through the fog and identify what are your biggest challenges, biggest risks and biggest payoffs." Source: https://gdcvault.com/play/1016259/Producer-Boot
   Why it matters here: the sweep is 26 lane rows of equal weight, not a ranked list of the three risks that can sink the game.

2. **Risk belongs in preproduction, and the proof is one polished playable piece.** Mark Cerny's Method (DICE 2002): preproduction and production are "as different as night and day"; "You can't plan when inspiration will strike and you can't schedule the date when you'll have worked out all your seemingly intractable problems." The exit is a "publishable first playable," one level that shows "exactly the game you were trying to make, and once you had the level, then you make 30 of them." Sources: https://www.videogameschronicle.com/features/who-is-mark-cerny/ and https://giantbomb.com/wiki/People/Mark_Cerny
   Why it matters here: by Cerny's test Bohemia is still in preproduction. There is no single fight-plus-map slice finished to the bar the whole game must meet, and volume before that slice is the classic failure.

3. **The producer empowers; granular control does not scale.** Travis George (Riot), GDC Online 2011, "Letting Go: Creating Self-Managed, Self-Directed Teams": a producer's role is to empower the team rather than manage its members, and granular involvement cannot scale to a larger team. Source: https://gdcvault.com/play/1015041/Letting-Go-Creating-Self-Managed
   Why it matters here: 26 lanes cannot be steered by one chat rewriting their jobs two words at a time. The coordinator's leverage is the rules and the one build, not the rows.

4. **The servant leader fixes the system, not the person.** Clinton Keith (Agile Game Development): "Servant leaders should start with finding what led the system to beat someone, rather than blame them. They need to be a student of system thinking and to continually explore better ways of working." Source: https://www.goodreads.com/author/quotes/4080560.Clinton_Keith. His 2007 GDC tutorial slides define the product owner as the keeper of the vision who establishes what "done" means. Source: https://www.mountaingoatsoftware.com/uploads/presentations/Agile-Game-Development-Tutorial-Game-Developers-Conference-2007.pdf
   Why it matters here: when a lane read a rule backwards (the s13 reversal), the fix was a note in the rule. Keith's answer: find what in the board let that happen, and change the board.

5. **A build cadence like a TV series, each episode with a headline.** Greg Kasavin on Hades Early Access: the team "sees it more like a serial television series with each update being like an episode of a show"; they aimed for monthly, then settled on Major Updates "dropping approximately every two months" because bigger updates worked better. Each one "had to offer new content, including a highlight like a new god." Sources: https://onlysp.escapistmagazine.com/greg-kasavin-hades-interview/ , https://gamingbolt.com/hades-creative-director-sees-early-access-schedule-like-serial-tv-series , https://www.pcgamer.com/a-journey-through-early-access-helped-make-hades-a-masterpiece/
   Why it matters here: we push to main every thirteen minutes and Paolo twice said "I didn't see nothing new." A named episode with one visible headline is the fix, not more pushes.

6. **Read all of it, play all of it, measure all of it.** Amir Rao on Hades: players could upvote requests on Discord "but we actually read all of it"; the team also played the game extensively, watched streamers, and for the first time had "large amounts of anonymous player data" to see where players got stuck. Source: https://www.pcgamer.com/a-journey-through-early-access-helped-make-hades-a-masterpiece/
   Why it matters here: the vote tab is our upvote board. The other two legs, the coordinator playing the build and the deed ledger as data, are weaker than the tab.

7. **Overhype wrote one dev blog a week for years, went quiet when there was nothing new, and then said "done."** The Battle Brothers blog ran weekly past #112; after launch the studio said the weekly blogs would not resume for update 1.1 because it added no new game mechanics to discuss. The team had started as a hobby in the evenings and on the weekends (Jan Taaks), and the final post announced update 1.1, development concluded, team moving on. Sources: https://battlebrothers.fandom.com/wiki/News_and_Dev_Blogs , https://www.gamebanshee.com/kdpqu , https://turnbasedlovers.com/10-turns-interview/with-battle-brothers-developer/ , https://gamebanshee.com/kdarp
   Why it matters here: one cadence, one voice, silence when there is nothing to show, and a clear "finished." Our records folder has a page for every thought and our board has never closed a lane as done.

8. **Tynan Sylvester does not pre-announce.** In "What's Next for RimWorld" (March 2, 2020) he says he rarely wrote about the future because release dates and game designs often change unexpectedly during development, and he was only talking now so the community and modders could plan. Before Alpha 17 he polled players on a refinement build (bugs and balance only) versus waiting for a big content drop. Sources: https://ludeon.com/blog/2020/03/whats-next-for-rimworld , https://www.gameskinny.com/news/rimworld-alpha-17-being-worked-on/
   Why it matters here: the coordinator says "coming" a lot. The producer rule is: in the build, or not mentioned.

9. **Klei publishes dates as estimates and names the fix update in the plan.** The Shipwrecked roadmap promised content every three weeks, then a bug-fix and balance update, with the line "all of these times are just estimates of our schedule and may very well change." In 2024 Klei slowed updates and promised to communicate more between them "so people can see the game is being worked on." Sources: https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/dont-starve-shipwrecked-gets-a-roadmap-for-future-content , https://kleiforums.com/forums/topic/154074-dont-starve-together-2024-roadmap/
   Why it matters here: a refinement round (plumber, tuning, bugs) is a legitimate episode and should be announced as one.

10. **The producer owns the miss in public, by name.** Hinterland's "About the Countdown" (May 4, 2017), Raphael van Lierop: "The countdown -- that was my idea." "Please remember that the community team at Hinterland works under my guidance, so if you are angry and want to vent, please feel free to direct it at me, and not at them." Elsewhere: "you need to be open to feedback but also firm in your vision." Sources: https://kotaku.com/the-long-dark-pisses-off-players-with-unsatisfying-teas-1794963893 , https://siliconangle.com/?p=232806
   Why it matters here: when a lane ships something Paolo hates, the coordinator's reply should say "I let that through," not "lane X did it."

11. **The solo path costs you the producer.** Eric Barone worked about 10 hours a day, every day, for over four years, redoing "pretty much everything in the game multiple times" as his skills improved. Sources: https://destructoid.com/?p=204027 , https://pcgamer.com/stardew-valley-interview
   Why it matters here: 26 lanes with nobody triaging is 26 Barones redoing everything. The fourth version of a feature is what a producer exists to catch.

12. **Producers play the game every day and do not hide behind the spreadsheet.** A Bethesda producer (Fallout 3 "Inside the Vault"): producers play the game every day to stay informed about where the project stands. Nick Laing, GDC Europe 2015, "How to be a Producer the Hard Way": against producers who hide behind spreadsheets and schedules; the top producer is the internal and external face of the product. Sources: https://www.gamebanshee.com/news/85087-fallout-3-inside-the-vault-qa-v15-85087.html , https://gdcvault.com/play/1022785/How-to-be-a-Producer
   Why it matters here: the coordinator reads commits and the board; it does not open the alpha on a phone before the sweep. VAMILY.md is our spreadsheet.

## WHAT THE BEST DO THAT WE DON'T

- **They triage before they report.** Dickinson's producer opens with the biggest risks and payoffs. Our sweep opens with 26 lane rows of equal weight, so the one thing that can kill the game (no finished fight-plus-map slice) sits in the same font as a haircut gate.
- **They ship episodes, not pushes.** Supergiant: one named update, one headline. We push to main every thirteen minutes and the director cannot tell what changed. A push is not a release until it has a name and a thing he can see.
- **They play their own build first.** Bethesda's producers play every day; Rao's team played, watched and measured. The coordinator reads commits about the game more than it plays the game.
- **They say less about the future.** Sylvester rarely pre-announced; Overhype went silent when there was nothing to show. We write "coming" and "OWED" across dozens of laws and records, and he reads it as broken promises.
- **They close things.** Overhype said "development concluded." Cerny: one perfect level, then thirty. No lane in Bohemia has ever been declared finished, so nothing is safe from a fourth rewrite.
- **They own the miss by name.** Van Lierop: my idea, direct it at me. Keith: fix the system that beat the person. Our replies put the blame on a lane and fix it with a note in a rule, which is a note, not a gate.

## THE CHANGES FOR THE COORDINATOR

1. **Triage at the top of every sweep.** The first block of every sweep record and every reply to Paolo is THREE RISKS, THREE PAYOFFS, ranked, in plain words, before any lane row. Test: open the next sweep record; if line one is a lane row and not a risk, the change did not land.
2. **Play before you read.** Before reading a single commit, the coordinator opens the alpha link on a phone, plays five minutes, and writes a "WHAT I PLAYED" line: one thing better, one thing broken, the build stamp. Test: the next sweep record carries that line and the risks in change 1 cite it.
3. **One named episode per round.** Every round gets a name and one headline Paolo can see in a named tab ("ROUND 41: THE MOUND"); the build stamp carries the name; a lane whose work is not in the episode is told so in its block; refinement rounds are named as refinement rounds, Klei style. Test: the build stamp on the live alpha matches the round name in the sweep record.
4. **In the build or not mentioned.** "Coming," "soon" and any future-tense feature are banned from the body of replies to Paolo. Anything not on the live link today is either a risk in change 1 or absent. Test: grep the next reply body for "coming," "soon," "OWED"; zero hits.
5. **Fix the board, not the lane.** When a lane misreads a rule or rebuilds a dead feature, the sweep writes one line, "what in the board let this happen," and changes the board (a gate, a shorter rule, a split job), never only a note, and the coordinator signs the miss as its own in the reply. Test: the next misread produces a diff to a gate or to VAMILY's front page, not a paragraph added to a law.

## ONE RULE
**THE PRODUCER TRIAGES, THEN PLAYS, THEN REPORTS.** Every sweep opens with the three biggest risks and the coordinator's own five minutes in the live build, and no lane's report is read before both are written down.

## FOR PAOLO, IN PLAIN WORDS
The best small studios have one person whose whole job is to pick the three things that matter most, play the game every day, and ship one named update at a time that everyone can see. I was sweeping 26 chats like a spreadsheet instead of doing that job. From the next round on, I play the build first, name the round, and tell you the three biggest risks before anything else.
