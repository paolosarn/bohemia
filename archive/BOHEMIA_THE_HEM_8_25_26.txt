WHAT A HEM STITCH IS
--------------------
Cloth is hemmed and the stitching is ONE pixel. At 56 a shirt hem is one row and that
row IS the edge, so there was nowhere to put it. At 112 there is a row to spare.
ONE HELPER, THREE GENERATORS -- genTop, genCoat and genHat all hem, so it lives in one
place rather than three.

*** IT SHIPPED AS A PURE NO-OP FIRST, AND THE AUDIT SAID SO ***
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The first cut put the stitch on the BOTTOM ROW in r.dk, and the numbers did not move by a
single garment. Measured before guessing: all 8 candidate pixels on a long-sleeve shirt
were ALREADY r.dk, because a garment darkens its own hem edge. PAINTING DARK OVER DARK IS
A NO-OP, and an audit that reports no change looks exactly like a mark that did not help.
Two fixes, and the correct placement and the visible one turned out to be the same thing:
  - ONE CELL ABOVE THE FOLD, which is where real hem stitching runs anyway.
  - CONTRAST WITH WHAT IS THERE: dark cloth takes a mid thread, light cloth takes a dark
    one. A stitch you cannot see is not a stitch.
base 50 -> 65, outer 20 -> 27 the moment it could actually be seen.

*** AND THE HELPER TOOK FOUR GATES RED AT ONCE ***
----------------------------------------------------
Calling hemStitch directly turned HEADWEAR, HOODIE, OPEN-COAT and STRUCTURE red together,
all reporting that genTop, genCoat and genHat "do not render". They were THROWING: every
one of those gates builds the generators in isolation with its own short list of helpers,
and a helper added today is in none of them.
THIS IS THE SAME SHAPE AS THE `window is not defined` BREAK TWO DAYS AGO. A GENERATOR THAT
DEPENDS ON SOMETHING OUTSIDE ITSELF BREAKS EVERY HARNESS THAT DOES NOT KNOW ABOUT IT, AND
THERE ARE MORE HARNESSES THAN ANYONE REMEMBERS. Fixed at the call site with a typeof
guard, which repairs all four and any future one for free -- rather than editing four
helper lists and waiting to discover the fifth.
Where the helper is absent the garment draws without its hem stitch. That is correct for
gates measuring SHAPE, and the gate that measures the detail does include it.

*** THE PICTURE LIED A THIRD TIME, SO THE TOOL NOW REFUSES TO ***
------------------------------------------------------------------
Three pictures this week showed two identical halves, for three different reasons:
    8/23  a flag one generator had never been taught to read
    8/24  a garment name that was not in the catalogue, so nothing drew at all
    8/25  a crop framed at the chest when the change is at the HEM
Every one looked like an honest "the change did nothing". The tool now COUNTS THE
DIFFERING PIXELS INSIDE THE CROP and prints NO DIFFERENCE IN THIS CROP on any row where
that count is zero. A before/after picture can no longer quietly claim a null result it
did not measure. All four rows of this one differ.
