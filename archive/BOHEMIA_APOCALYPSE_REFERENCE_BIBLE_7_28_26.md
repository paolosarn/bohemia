# THE APOCALYPSE REFERENCE BIBLE (7/28/26, WORLD lane — research, commissioned)

Paolo: *"all the districts need revamping, bro I need you to like do like three terms of
research on the most memorable apocalyptic versions of these buildings."*

Three passes, as asked: **(1) the craft** — how the games that nailed it actually did it;
**(2) the real ruins** — because Bohemia is grounded in the real; **(3) the mechanics of
decay** — what actually happens to a building nobody maintains, and the Nevada-specific
version of it.

NOTHING WAS BUILT OFF THIS YET. It is a reference bible to build the revamp on, so the
next 32 districts are made from something instead of from vibes. Every claim is sourced.

---

# THE ONE IDEA THAT SHOULD RUN THE WHOLE REVAMP

The Division is set in New York at **Christmas**. Decorations still hanging, fairy lights
still blinking in shop windows — *"the city is dressed for warmth and celebration while
everything underneath has collapsed."* That contrast is why it reads as apocalypse
instead of as a grey ruin, and reviewers name it as the single reason the world works.

**Las Vegas is that idea by default.** Every building in this valley is *celebration
architecture* — it exists to promise you a good time. Neon, signs, porte-cocheres,
fountains, marquees, a giant fibreglass thing on a pole. A city built entirely out of
invitations, with nobody left to invite.

So the rule for every district we revamp:

> **THE BUILDING IS STILL TRYING TO SELL YOU SOMETHING. NOBODY IS BUYING.**

The sign still stands. The awning still shades an empty patio. The lot lines still tell
you where to park. The invitation outlives the business, and that gap is the horror. A
generically crumbled building has no gap in it, which is exactly why our current 32 read
as nothing — they are *decayed* but they are not *disappointed*.

**And the corollary, which is where our current districts go wrong:** an empty parking
lot is not content, it is an absence. His two harshest layout notes — commercial *"way to
many parking spots"* and ballpark *"the parking is fucked"* — are the same note. Pavement
reads as nothing. The FEATURE is what people came for; the pavement is what they crossed.

# PASS 1 — THE CRAFT: WHAT THE GAMES THAT NAILED IT ACTUALLY DID

### 1. Three visible landmarks per grid square (Fallout: New Vegas)
Obsidian's own rule: **there must be at least three visible landmarks, that are real
in-game locations, from any grid space**, so the player can orient without a map. This is
a hard, checkable design law and we can gate it. Our valley currently has landmark
districts scattered as single cells with nothing tall in them — you cannot navigate by
our skyline because our skyline is flat.
→ **Bohemia action:** every district needs ONE thing tall enough to be seen from the next
cell. The town has its water tower already. Most have nothing.

### 2. The environment answers a question the player already asked
The Last of Us: *"every room, corridor, and even outside area seems like it was designed
with a story and a purpose in mind."* The technique named repeatedly is **environmental
clues that tell you what happened without telling you** — plus **anti-affordances** (the
world visibly showing you what you cannot do).
→ **Bohemia action:** each district needs ONE frozen event, not general decay. Not "an
abandoned school" — a school on the day the buses did not come.

### 3. The palette is doing the work
TLOU is deliberately grey with washed-out greens and muted browns; the decay reads
because the palette is disciplined, not because objects are broken. **Our act-1 palette is
already this** — which means our problem is not colour, it is that the SHAPES underneath
are generic.

### 4. Density of small true detail beats big broken shapes
The Division's believability is credited to *"an unprecedented amount of detail"* —
subway interiors, the inside of an office, the back alley. And the single most-cited shot
is Madison Square Garden with **a tarp thrown over the sign rechristening it Madison
Field Hospital**. One tarp. One re-labelled sign. That is the whole story of the
apocalypse told in a prop.
→ **Bohemia action:** the memorable beat is almost always a SMALL OBJECT IN THE WRONG
PLACE, not a big broken building.

# PASS 2 — THE REAL RUINS, BECAUSE OURS IS AN ECONOMIC APOCALYPSE

This is the pass that matters most, and it re-frames everything: **Bohemia's collapse is
economic. There is no radiation** (his ruling). So Chernobyl/Pripyat — the reference
everyone reaches for — is the WRONG well. Nobody fled Bohemia in an afternoon leaving
soup on the table. **People left over years, because they could not afford to stay.** That
is a totally different ruin, and America has documented it exhaustively.

### THE MASTER REFERENCE: LAS VEGAS'S OWN 2008
This actually happened, here, to this city. Builders walked off half-finished suburbs,
leaving **"zombie subdivisions" ringing the city** — and the detail that should be in our
game verbatim:

> **"freshly paved streets and lamp posts with no homes"** — only weeds and rusting
> for-sale signs. (Hidden Ridge; and Desert Mesa in North Las Vegas, boarded up with
> warning signs, trash piled outside.)

At the peak **more than 80% of Vegas mortgages were underwater.** Nevada was the most
foreclosed state in the nation.
→ **Bohemia action:** THE EMPTY SUBDIVISION THAT WAS NEVER FINISHED is our single most
authentic district and we do not have it. Streets, kerbs, lamp posts, fire hydrants,
driveways to nothing. Utterly free of "and then it decayed" — it never lived at all.
That is more haunting than any ruin and it is our city's real history.

### THE DEAD MALL: Rolling Acres, Akron
The most photographed dead mall in the world. The image everyone knows: **the glass
skylight collapsed inward**, letting snow and rain fall into the concourse, with
**sun-faded neon and outdated mall maps still in place.** Two storeys, glass elevators, an
arboretum. Photographer Seph Lawless made it famous in *Black Friday: The Collapse of the
American Shopping Mall*.
→ **Bohemia action (mall, "both looking like shit"):** the memorable mall is not a broken
box, it is **weather getting in through the roof and a garden growing under the hole**,
with the directory still telling you where Sears is.

### THE DEAD INDUSTRY: the Packard Plant, Detroit
*"For three decades, the single most famous abandoned building in America."* 3.5 million
sq ft, 40 acres, designed by Albert Kahn — too expensive to even demolish. Detroit lost
~63% of its population. The photographs out of Detroit in the 2000s-2010s **defined the
entire visual language of post-industrial abandonment.**
→ **Bohemia action (industrial, warehouse, battery, substation):** the note is SCALE and
EMPTINESS INSIDE, not rubble. A vast floor plate with the machines gone and the roof
partly open. What makes Packard iconic is that it is *too big to remove* — the corpse
nobody can afford to bury. That is an economic-apocalypse idea, exactly ours.

### THE DEAD RESORT IN OUR OWN DESERT: Bombay Beach, Salton Sea
120 miles from Vegas. A 1950s celebrity resort town — Sinatra, the Beach Boys, half a
million visitors a year — that died when the water turned toxic. Now: broken windows,
graffiti, **weeds through foundation cracks, dead fish along the shore**, and a handful of
houses in varying stages of destruction. Sun-bleached, salt-eaten, no vegetation to
soften it.
→ **Bohemia action:** THIS is what our desert decay should look like, not green
overgrowth. TLOU's ruins are wet and green; ours is dry, bleached and cracked. **We should
not be copying the overgrown look at all** — it is the single most common apocalypse
cliché and it is wrong for the Mojave.

### THE DEAD TOWN: Rhyolite / Goldfield, Nevada
Nevada has **over 600 ghost towns.** Rhyolite: 10,000 people, banks, a stock exchange, an
opera house — **alive for twelve years, 1904 to 1916.** Boom to nothing inside a decade,
because the money left. The Cook Bank Building is *the most photographed ghost-town
building in the West*: a masonry shell standing alone with its windows gone.
→ **Bohemia action (town):** the memorable version is **a grand building that is far too
good for the town around it** — a two-storey bank with a stone facade in a place with
nine buildings. Optimism that outlived the reason for it. Our town is currently all one
storey and all the same.

### AND THE TERM WE SHOULD STEAL: **ARRESTED DECAY**
The actual preservation doctrine used at Bodie: you do not restore, and you do not let it
fall — **you freeze it at the moment you found it.** That is precisely what our act-1
dead world is, and it gives the art direction a real name and a real rule:
*everything looks exactly as it did the day it stopped, plus weather.*

# PASS 3 — WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENS TO A BUILDING NOBODY MAINTAINS

From Alan Weisman's *The World Without Us*, the standard reference:
- **Immediately:** with no heating or air conditioning, strange temperature gradients form
  inside and **mould starts on the walls.**
- **~20 years:** lightning rods rust and snap, **roof fires** leap between buildings; gas
  lines ignite and blow out windows; rain and snow get in, and **poured concrete floors
  freeze, thaw and buckle.** Water-soaked steel supporting streets corrodes and buckles.
- **~500 years:** everything is half-fallen.

**THE ORDER MATTERS AND WE HAVE IT BACKWARDS.** Decay starts at the ROOF and the WATER,
not at the walls. A building dies top-down: roof membrane fails → water gets in → floor
buckles → walls go last. Our districts currently show damage as *edge chipping on walls*,
which is the last thing that happens, not the first.

**The Mojave amendment:** all of the above assumes rain and freeze-thaw. Vegas gets ~4
inches of rain a year and does not freeze much. So in OUR world:
- **UV and heat do the killing, not water.** Paint chalks and fades off the south and west
  faces. Plastic goes brittle and yellow, then shatters. Asphalt greys, then cracks in a
  crocodile pattern. Vinyl siding warps. Rubber roofs shrink and split at the seams.
- **Wind and sand polish and scour** — the bottom metre of everything gets sandblasted.
- **Almost no green.** What grows is tumbleweed, creosote and salt crust in the low spots.
- **Interiors survive far better than exteriors** — which is a gift for us: outside is
  bleached and dead, inside is eerily intact. That contrast is free drama every time the
  player steps through a door.
- **Flash floods** do concentrated, local damage in the washes — the one place water wins.

## THE THREE THINGS I THINK THIS RESEARCH SAYS WE SHOULD ACTUALLY CHANGE

1. **STOP DRAWING DECAY, START DRAWING ABANDONMENT.** Our districts are worn. They are not
   *left*. The difference is a story — an event frozen — and it is almost always told with
   one small wrong object, not with damage.
2. **KILL THE PAVEMENT.** It is his loudest layout note, twice, and the research agrees:
   an empty lot is an absence. Every square metre of asphalt we can turn into the thing
   people came for is a square metre that starts reading.
3. **GO DRY, NOT GREEN.** The overgrown-ruin look is the default apocalypse and it is
   wrong for the Mojave. Bleached, cracked, sandblasted at the ankles, interiors intact.
   This is a real point of difference and nobody else's apocalypse looks like it.

**AND THE ONE I WILL NOT DECIDE:** how long since the collapse? Every number in Pass 3 is
keyed to it — 5 years and 20 years are completely different-looking worlds, and it sets
the damage level for all 45 districts at once. [PENDING Paolo]
