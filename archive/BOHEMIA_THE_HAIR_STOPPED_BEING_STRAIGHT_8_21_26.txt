THE RULING THIS SERVES (Paolo 8/1, LOCKED, craft law clause 3)
--------------------------------------------------------------
    "a lot about hair is about just the little off shapes that it makes ... I'm
     seeing you make like a lot of straight lines and that's not realistic at all"

THE FIX FROM 8/1 WORKED AND THEN QUIETLY STOPPED WORKING
--------------------------------------------------------
A deterministic per-row wobble went in the same day: hash the row, push the edge out,
same input same wobble forever so an NPC never shimmers. Correct, and still correct.

But it hashes the CELL row and its only amplitude is S. At 56 that is one pixel and it
is the finest mark available. THE MOMENT THE RIG WENT TO 112 (8/20) IT BECAME A TWO
PIXEL STEP ON A CELL-SIZED GRID -- the same silhouette as 56, drawn twice as big. Four
times the pixels bought a SHARPER hair edge and not a FINER one, which is the whole
difference between a resolution bump and actual detail.

NOTHING IN THE MACHINE NOTICED, and the reason is the important part: craft_law_gate
asserts clause 3 BY FINDING THE WOBBLE IN THE SOURCE. The wobble was there, hashed,
deterministic, exactly as the law says. A source check cannot tell you the edge it
produces is dead straight. THAT IS A MENTION CHECK WEARING A LAW'S NAME.

MEASURED, BEFORE ANYTHING CHANGED (tools/bohemia_hair_straightness.js)

Walk every canon style's silhouette on all 8 facings, count consecutive rows sitting at

Half of every head of hair in the game was a machine-drawn line.

THE CHANGE, AND WHY IT CANNOT BECOME STATIC
--------------------------------------------
The cell-level step is UNTOUCHED, so the coarse silhouette is the one he approved.
Where that step says "stay put", the individual PIXEL row may now move by ONE. His own
clause 5 is the argument: "ONE PIXEL not three at 56px" -- a finer grid is for finer
marks, not bigger ones.

The failure the cell-hash exists to prevent is the edge turning to television static,
and the sub-step cannot cause it: IT ONLY FIRES WHERE THE COARSE STEP DID NOT, so the
edge's total travel is unchanged at S. At S===1 it never fires at all, which is why all
1,744 pinned 56 hashes are byte-identical.

    AFTER:  18.5%  of edge rows in a straight run of 4+   (was 50.7%)
                6  longest straight edge anywhere          (was 16)

TWO RATES WERE TRIED AND THE LOSER IS WORTH RECORDING. Firing 1-in-4 per pixel row gave
28.0% and a 12-row longest -- WORSE ON BOTH AXES than 1-in-2, and it still peaked at
single-row runs. There was no trade-off to tune; 1-in-2 dominated it outright.

*** THE GATE HAD TO BE WIDENED, AND THAT IS THE UNCOMFORTABLE PART ***
----------------------------------------------------------------------
clothes_4x_gate's claim 1 says the 112 output IS the 56 output scaled. That is the right
default and it is what catches a hardcoded distance. IT IS ALSO, the moment any authored
detail exists at 112, A CLAIM THAT FORBIDS THE ENTIRE POINT OF THE FLIP. Step 5 is
DEFINED as detail the finer grid can express and the coarse one cannot.

A GATE MUST NEVER OUTRANK A RULING (8/1). So a generator may carry @SUBCELL-DETAIL and
the gate widens THAT generator's bound by exactly the size of a sub-cell mark -- one
pixel, 12% coverage -- and nothing else's.

AND I TRIED TWICE TO MAKE THE MARKER SOMETHING THE MACHINE VERIFIES, AND FAILED TWICE:
    ATTEMPT 1  compare the 112 output to the 56 output block-doubled. USELESS -- a
               resolution-native generator differs from that anyway just by drawing
               natively, so genBag passed on a bare pasted comment.
    ATTEMPT 2  count cells whose TWO PIXEL ROWS differ, since only finer-than-a-cell
               logic should split a cell. MEASURED, AND IT DOES NOT SEPARATE THEM:
                   genHair    4   <- the one generator that really does author sub-cell
                   genCoat   13      detail scores LOWEST of the three
                   genGear   17
               Native edge shading splits cells all by itself.
A third attempt would have been the fourth version of something nobody asked for
(STOP PRODUCING, 7/26), so THE CHECK IS NOT THERE and the gate says so out loud.

WHY THAT IS ACCEPTABLE, AND IT IS MEASURED NOT ASSUMED: the marker moves the bound from
1 pixel to 2 and from 4% coverage to 12%. THE BUG THIS GATE EXISTS FOR IS NOT IN THAT
RANGE. Mutation-tested WITH the wider bound in force: put the backpack's hardcoded
distances back and it still fires on three facings at 31% of proper coverage. A false
marker can excuse a small honest difference; it cannot hide a mis-scaling bug.

AND CLAUSE 3 NOW HAS A GATE THAT LOOKS AT PIXELS
-------------------------------------------------
hair_gate walks the real silhouette and ratchets both numbers, only ever downward:

of two rather than the only one.
