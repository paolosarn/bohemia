# RESEARCH — THE DEATH ONLY LANDS IF YOU PLAYED THE PERSON (8/13/26,
# coordinator sweep 7 catch; doctrine §4b — both aisles, anti-yes-man)

## THE FALSIFIABLE QUESTION
The demo's emotional core is the cold open: the sibling dies in the
family-defense tutorial. As built (engine/bohemia_scene.js, 8/9), the
player's total attachment time before that death is a 13.5-second WATCHED
wordless match-cut. Question: does a scripted death land when the
attachment before it is watched-only and seconds long — or does it need
PLAYED attachment, and what is the minimum effective dose?

## AISLE 1 — GAMES (practitioner primary)
1. THE LAST OF US (Druckmann, primary interviews): the opening was
   originally Joel's POV and was SCRAPPED as predictable ("I can tell
   what's going to happen before it happens"). The shipped fix: you PLAY
   Sarah for a few minutes before she dies in Joel's arms — Druckmann and
   a decade of postmortems credit the death's impact to exactly that.
   Played minutes, then the loss. The most-cited emotional opening in the
   medium is a controlled experiment on this question and the watched
   version LOST.
2. BROTHERS: A TALE OF TWO SONS: the attachment is IN THE HANDS — one
   stick per brother, and the famous ending beat works because the
   CONTROLS carry the relationship (the player physically is the bond).
   Lesson: mechanics are the strongest attachment channel a game has,
   stronger than any cutscene.
3. RETENTION DATA (Zukowski's demo practice + first-10-minutes studies):
   unskippable/long intro cutscenes are a TOP cause of early quits;
   time-to-first-fun rules the opening; tutorials should be 1-3 minutes
   and INTEGRATED INTO GAMEPLAY, not shown. So the fix is NOT a longer
   cutscene — a 4-minute watched intro would buy attachment and pay for
   it in demo abandonment.

## AISLE 2 — OUTSIDE GAMES
4. UP's "Married Life" (Pixar, Docter): the gold-standard watched
   attachment-before-loss takes ~4.5 minutes of SPECIFIC shared moments,
   wordless on purpose (Docter: less dialogue = more emotion). Film's
   minimum effective dose is minutes of specificity — and film has no
   interactive channel; we do, and it is cheaper per second.
5. IDENTIFIABLE VICTIM EFFECT (Small & Loewenstein; Schelling 1968): one
   NAMED person with specific personal detail moves people far more than
   any abstraction; specificity of identity is what triggers the
   affective response. A sibling who is "the sibling" is a statistic; a
   sibling with a name, one quirk, and one shared moment is a person.

## THE CHALLENGE FINDING (anti-yes-man — this challenges the cold open
## as currently built, not the ruling)
Both aisles converge: WATCHED attachment is the weak form and SECONDS is
below any measured effective dose — but adding watched minutes violates
the demo-retention aisle. The only move that satisfies both: THE TUTORIAL
IS THE ATTACHMENT MACHINE. The family-defense fight is already the
combat tutorial (locked 7/19 shape, untouched); make the SIBLING the
tutorial's mechanism, so the 1-3 tutorial minutes double as played
attachment at zero added runtime:
- The sibling TEACHES you the beat: I-MOVE-YOU-MOVE with your sibling as
  your first dance partner — the game's core combat idea, learned from
  the person you are about to lose. (Their death then costs the player
  their teacher — mechanical grief, the Brothers pattern.)
- One PROTECT/ASSIST beat mid-fight (they board a window while you hold
  the line, or hand you the weapon) — cooperation, not escort.
- The sibling has a NAME and ONE specific quirk before the fight (drafts
  exist — family cast is drafted; IVE says the name+quirk is load-
  bearing, not flavor).
- The match-cut stays EXACTLY as ruled (7/19, "without a word") — this
  adds played beats INSIDE the existing tutorial, zero new scenes, zero
  added minutes.

## CONFIDENCE
TLOU/Brothers/Up craft accounts: published primary/secondary, high.

— the effect concentrates in affect-driven giving; direction uncontested).
Retention numbers: practitioner aggregates, medium. The transfer to our
cold open is my inference, flagged as such.
