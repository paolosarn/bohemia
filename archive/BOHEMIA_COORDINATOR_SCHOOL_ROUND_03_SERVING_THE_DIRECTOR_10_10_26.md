# BOHEMIA COORDINATOR SCHOOL, ROUND 3 OF 10: SERVING THE DIRECTOR (10/10/26)

## THE QUESTION (one paragraph)
How do the best producers, first ADs and studio heads serve a creative director: how they turn his raw notes into work, protect his vision from drift and from their own taste, and spend as little of his attention as possible while still getting every real decision out of him. Paolo is the auteur, the coordinator is the only chat he talks to, and his notes arrive as garbled voice-to-text. This round looks at Pixar's Braintrust, Nintendo's producer and director split, director-led studios (Yoko Taro and his producer, Larian), Disney's sweatbox and dailies, Hollywood's first AD, Amazon's door model, and the research on decision fatigue, interruption and choice overload. Page fetching was blocked in this session, so quotes are only the wording a search result returned verbatim; the rest is paraphrased.

## WHAT I FOUND

1. The Braintrust has no authority. Catmull wrote that "The Braintrust has no authority"; notes go to the director and it is up to him to decide what to do with them. Source: https://www.supersummary.com/creativityinc/background/ and https://review.firstround.com/spark-creativity-with-these-tips-from-pixars-president/ . Why it matters here: the 26 lanes are Paolo's Braintrust, not his bosses; a lane's better idea never overrides a ruling.

2. A good note is specific. Catmull describes a good note as one that says what is wrong, what is missing, what isn't clear, what makes no sense, arrives in time to fix it, and need not propose a fix; "I'm bored" is the example of a bad note. Source: https://www.motionpictures.org/2014/05/lessons-gleaned-from-pixar-co-founder-ed-catmulls-creativity-inc/ . Why it matters here: "this is all bad" (Paolo 7/26) is an "I'm bored" note; the coordinator turns it into the four specifics before any lane hears it.

3. The Braintrust was forged in the Toy Story 2 crisis and works on ugly early reels, not polished late ones. Source: https://mindsetonline.com/ed-catmull-pixar-30-years-braintrust-ugly-work-early/ . Why it matters here: COOK EVERY ROUND (9/21) is correct; a rough thing he sees this round beats a finished thing in three.

4. Miyamoto upends the tea table late and the team re-sets the plates. On Twilight Princess his change (three days in Ordon Village instead of one) landed after the script went to localization; on Super Mario Galaxy director Koizumi says he upended the table himself and Miyamoto "realigned the plates." Source: https://zeldadungeon.net/wiki/Upending_the_Tea_Table and https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/super_mario_galaxy/0/3 . Why it matters here: a late reversal (9/28 killing the walk 9/27 built) is normal auteur practice; build so plates re-set cheaply, which is the case for the data-file line.

5. Aonuma's three-times rule. He privately decided that if Miyamoto pointed out the same thing three times a change was definitely needed; a second mention made him suspect Miyamoto meant it. On Twilight Princess the team "didn't have that luxury" and changed things on the first mention. Source: https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/twilight_princess/0/5 . Why it matters here: Paolo repeating himself ("I want to repeat this," 8/24) is the signal the first mention was dropped; nobody counts the repeats.

6. The producer builds the quiet room; the director translates the auteur. On Super Mario Galaxy producer Shimizu says his main task was to create a work environment where staff could focus on development; director Koizumi says that because Shimizu handled outside communication he could focus, and "it was also my job to 'translate' what Miyamoto-san was saying to the developers." Source: https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/super_mario_galaxy/0/0/ . Why it matters here: the coordinator is both at once, the wall that keeps noise off the lanes and the translator of VAMILY plus a garbled sentence into a buildable job.

7. Disney's sweatbox notes were taken by a stenographer, typed, and handed to everyone present; Cartoon Brew calls them marching orders from generals to troops. Source: https://www.cartoonbrew.com/classic/john-canemaker-unlocks-the-secrets-of-fantasias-sweatbox-notes-109312.html . Why it matters here: "every sentence becomes a job" is 90 years old, except Disney kept the director's words verbatim beside the order and we keep only the two-word job.

8. Dailies show the shot cut into the reel so it is judged in context, the director approves or requests changes, and group sessions are preferred so every artist hears every note. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dailies and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweat_box . Why it matters here: SHOW IT FROM THE GAME'S CAMERA (9/23) is dailies practice, and a ruling given to one lane must be readable by all 26.

9. The first AD runs the set so the director only does the creative. On features the 1st AD works "FOR the Director WITH the Producers," managing crew and schedule so the director can focus on actors and camera; the DGA agreement lets the director pick the 1st AD. Source: https://www.fusionmagazine.org/the-first-assistant-director-communicator-in-chief-on-a-movie-set/ and https://dga.org/Contracts/Creative-Rights/Basic-Agreement-Article-7 . Why it matters here: no scheduling or ownership question reaches Paolo; NEVER ASK HIM A TECHNICAL QUESTION (8/15) is the 1st AD rule in his words.

10. Two-way doors do not go upstairs. Bezos split decisions into one-way doors (hard to reverse, decided slowly) and two-way doors (reversible, decided fast and low); AWS's Garman says "part of my job is to make the one-way door decisions." Source: https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2016-letter-to-shareholders and https://www.businessinsider.nl/amazon-web-services-ceo-explains-the-decision-making-framework-he-uses-for-moving-fast . Why it matters here: with content in data files almost everything is a two-way door and should never be an ask; only names, identity, reference games, the ending and law reversals are one-way.

11. Decide at 70 percent, then disagree and commit. The 2016 letter says most decisions should be made with around 70 percent of the information you wish you had, and gives Bezos's own example of approving a show he doubted with "I disagree and commit and hope it becomes the most watched thing we've ever made." Source: https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2016-letter-to-shareholders . Why it matters here: a lane that disagrees with a ruling writes one line of dissent in the record and builds it the same round; it never goes back to Paolo for a re-vote.

12. Decisions degrade in a marathon and interruptions are expensive. Danziger's parole study found 65 percent favorable rulings right after a break falling toward zero before the next (the cause is contested, the pattern is not); Gloria Mark found interrupted work resumed on average 23 minutes and 15 seconds later with about two tasks in between. Source: https://nocamels.com/2011/05/fatigue-negatively-influences-the-rulings-of-judges/ , https://voices.uchicago.edu/oysteindaljord/files/2019/12/Status-Quo-Theory-Revisited-3.pdf and https://news.gallup.com/businessjournal/23146/too-many-interruptions-work.aspx . Why it matters here: 83 verdicts in one vote session (9/21) means the late ones are worth less than the early ones, and a technical question inside a lore yap has a measured price.

13. Fewer options, more decisions, and show them. Iyengar and Lepper: 24 jams drew more browsers but 3 percent bought, 6 jams and about 30 percent bought; Hick's law has choice time rising with the log of the options; Tompson and Tomitsch found physical mock-ups gave "a clearer articulation of stakeholder constraints" than talk. Source: https://faculty.washington.edu/jdb/345/345%20Articles/Iyengar%20&%20Lepper%20(2000).pdf and https://rossy.ruc.dk/index.php/pdc/article/view/4587 . Why it matters here: the A/B/C law (8/4) and THE VOTE TAB SHOWS THE THING (9/21) have a research floor; a text list of six is the 24-jam table.

14. The hands-off producer. Yoko Taro's producer Yosuke Saito says he doesn't really put in requests as far as content goes and the two talk about once a month; Larian's Vincke credits studio managers with team management and says if the team could handle a feature it went in. Source: https://www.siliconera.com/yoko-taro-and-yosuke-saito-on-nier-replicants-new-additions/ and https://www.shacknews.com/article/137186/larian-studios-ceo-interview-bg3-2023 . Why it matters here: the producer keeps his taste out of the content and his questions off the auteur's calendar; BUILD IT, DON'T ASK (9/28) is Vincke's rule. (Kojima's producer practice turned up no usable interview, so it is left out rather than guessed.)

## WHAT THE BEST DO THAT WE DON'T

- They keep the director's words verbatim next to the order. Disney typed Walt's sentences and handed them out; we compress a garbled note into two words per job and the sentence lives only in chat scrollback, so when a lane reads the job backwards (the 9/28 house-tile reading) there is nothing to check against.
- They count repeats as the failure signal. Aonuma knew a second mention meant he had missed the first. Paolo has had to say "I want to repeat this" in the laws, and no record counts how many times a ruling was given before it held.
- They sort doors before anything reaches the top. Our WHAT I NEED FROM YOU block has no door test, so a two-way door (a number, a tile size, a lane boundary) can still end up as his question.
- They turn bad notes into good notes before the crew hears them. "He hates it" is what the lanes get now, and a lane given a mood builds a fourth version of the same thing (STOP PRODUCING, 7/26).
- They review in context with the whole room listening. Our judge pages show a thing alone, and a verdict given to one lane is not necessarily read by the other 25.
- They keep their own taste out of the content. Saito makes no content requests; the coordinator still proposes design inside the ask block, which is how pending-verdict queues came back after they were banned.

## THE CHANGES FOR THE COORDINATOR

1. The ruling record carries the sentence, not just the job. Every job written into VAMILY or a record quotes Paolo's raw sentence (garbles and all) on the line above the two-word job and the coordinator's reading. Test: any job line without a quoted sentence above it fails.

2. Every ask passes the door test. Before an item enters WHAT I NEED FROM YOU the coordinator names which one-way door it is: a name or identity he reserved, a reference game, the ending, or reversing a locked law. Anything else is decided, built and listed as "decided for you, correct me." Test: an ask that names no one-way door is a violation; count per round, target zero.

3. The repeat log. When Paolo says a thing that matches an earlier ruling, the coordinator writes one line in a repeats record (first date, second date, which lane dropped it, why) and the fix ships the same round. Test: a round where he repeats and the record does not grow is a miss.

4. Bad notes become good notes before dispatch. A verdict without a specific is rewritten into the four Catmull lines (what is wrong, missing, unclear, makes no sense) from the repo and the game before any lane gets it; only if one line cannot be filled does ONE bolded question go to him. Test: a dispatch containing "he didn't like it" with no specific after it fails.

5. Options are things, at most three, in the game's camera. Any A/B/C put to him exists in the VOTE tab as a picture, animation or sound before the reply mentions it; text-only options are allowed only when the thing cannot be made in one round, and the reply says so. Test: every A/B/C in a reply has a vote-tab entry the same round.

## ONE RULE
TRANSLATE, DON'T RELAY. Keep the director's sentence, turn it into a specific job, decide every door that can be walked back through yourself, and bring him only the one-way doors, shown as a thing he can see or hear, never as a list.

## FOR PAOLO, IN PLAIN WORDS
The best game and film teams write down the boss's exact words, then turn them into clear jobs so nobody guesses. They only bring him the few choices that cannot be undone, and they show those as pictures or sounds, two or three at a time. I am changing how I read your messages so you say things once, see them in the game, and never get asked a nerd question again.
